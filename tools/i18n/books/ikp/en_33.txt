s7005 6.3. Natural course of aortic stenosis and principles of treatment
s7006 Aortic stenosis gradually progresses over the years: the orifice area decreases on average by **0.1 cm^2^** per year, and the gradient increases by **7 mm Hg** per year. The asymptomatic stage lasts a long time, but the prognosis worsens sharply once symptoms appear.
s7007 **Treatment:** there is no effective drug therapy; in severe symptomatic stenosis — **valve replacement**: surgical (open-heart) or **transcatheter (TAVI)** — in elderly patients and those at high surgical risk; according to the ESC/EACTS 2025 guideline, the choice is based on age and risk.
s7008 **Precautions:** in severe aortic stenosis, potent vasodilators (high-dose nitrates) and strenuous physical exertion are dangerous — they may cause syncope and sudden death.
s7009 In severe aortic stenosis there is a risk of **sudden cardiac death** (mainly in symptomatic patients). That is why, if an elderly patient has syncope or angina on exertion and a harsh systolic murmur is heard over the aorta, echocardiography should be performed immediately.
s7010 7. Mixed and multivalvular aortic defects
s7011 **A mixed aortic defect** (stenosis + regurgitation) occurs in rheumatic and degenerative diseases. On auscultation, **systolic and diastolic murmurs** are heard together over the aorta (a “sawing” quality). Determining the predominant lesion: if stenosis predominates — the pulse is slow and small, the pulse pressure narrow, the thrill distinct; if regurgitation predominates — the pulse is rapid and high, the diastolic pressure low, the peripheral signs pronounced.
s7012 **Multivalvular mitral-aortic defects** (often rheumatic): for example, when mitral stenosis and aortic regurgitation coexist, the peripheral signs of aortic regurgitation are poorly expressed because the mitral stenosis limits the blood flow into the left ventricle — each defect “masks” the signs of the other. In such cases the diagnosis is established by echocardiography.

s7065 12. Principles of treatment of aortic defects (in brief)
s7066 **Aortic regurgitation:** in severe regurgitation, if symptoms appear or, even without symptoms, if there is left ventricular dilation (end-systolic diameter more than 50 mm) and a fall in the ejection fraction (50 % or less) — **valve replacement**; if the aortic root is dilated — together with the aorta. Drugs (vasodilators) reduce symptoms in inoperable patients.
s7067 **Aortic stenosis:** in severe symptomatic stenosis — **surgical or transcatheter (TAVI)** valve replacement; in asymptomatic severe stenosis — regular follow-up and exercise testing, and in some cases early surgery.
s7068 **Infective endocarditis:** intravenous bactericidal antibiotics for **4–6 weeks** according to the blood culture result; if indicated — early surgery. Treatment is managed by the “endocarditis team” (cardiologist, cardiac surgeon, infectious disease specialist, microbiologist).
