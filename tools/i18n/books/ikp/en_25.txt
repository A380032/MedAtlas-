s5348 Normal borders of relative cardiac dullness: **right — IV intercostal space, 1–1.5 cm lateral to the right sternal edge; upper — III rib; left — V intercostal space, 1–1.5 cm medial to the midclavicular line**. The transverse diameter of the heart is **11–13 cm** (the right part 3–4 cm, the left part 8–9 cm).
s5349 Relative dullness: **“4-3-5”** — the right border in the IV space, the upper border at the III rib, the left border in the V space. Absolute dullness: **“Sternum-4”** — the right border is the left sternal edge, the upper border the IV rib.
s5350 Percussion assesses the size of the heart only approximately: the error is large in obesity, emphysema and chest deformities. **Echocardiography** and radiography are used to assess the size of the cardiac chambers precisely.
s5351 **In aortic regurgitation** the pulse is rapid, high and large (pulsus celer, altus, magnus); **in aortic stenosis** it is slow and small (pulsus tardus, parvus). **Pulse deficit** is a typical sign of atrial fibrillation; to detect it, one person counts the heartbeats on auscultation and another counts the pulse at the same time for one minute.
s5352 The normal pulse rate differs between sources: in Russian and Uzbek textbooks it is **60–80** (or 60–90), and in international cardiology guidelines (ESC) **60–100** beats. More than 100 per minute is tachycardia. The manual gives 60–80 as normal, but the range 80–100 should not be regarded as pathological.
s5353 **The cardiothoracic ratio** (CTR) is the ratio of the greatest transverse diameter of the cardiac shadow to the greatest internal transverse diameter of the chest. Normally it **does not exceed 0.5** (50 %); more than 0.5 is cardiomegaly.
s5354 **The “auscultatory gap”** is a temporary disappearance of the sounds between phases I and II (most often in hypertension and atherosclerosis). If the cuff is not first checked by the palpatory method, the lower limit of the gap is taken as the systolic pressure by mistake, and the systolic pressure is underestimated by 20–40 mm Hg. **The “infinite sound”** — the sounds are heard down to zero (aortic regurgitation, thyrotoxicosis, pregnancy, children).
s5355 The ESC 2024 guideline proposed a simplified classification: **non-elevated BP** — below 120/70; **elevated BP** — 120–139/70–89; **hypertension** — 140/90 or higher. In both classifications the threshold of hypertension is **140/90 mm Hg** when measured in the clinic and **135/85 mm Hg** when measured at home.
s5356 The pulses in the carotid arteries must not be palpated **on both sides at the same time** or with strong pressure: this may reduce cerebral blood flow and, through the carotid sinus reflex, lead to bradycardia and syncope (especially in the elderly). When the signs of clinical death are being determined, the pulse is checked **in the carotid artery** (within 10 seconds).
s5357 By the displacement of the contrast-filled esophagus: **“small radius — Mitral stenosis, large radius — Mitral regurgitation”**. In mitral stenosis the left atrium is moderately enlarged (the esophagus is displaced along an arc of small radius), whereas in mitral regurgitation it may be very large (an arc of large radius).
s5358 **The ejection fraction** (EF) is the ratio of the blood ejected from the left ventricle with each systole to the diastolic volume. By EF, heart failure is divided into three types (ESC, 2021): **reduced EF** (40 % or less), **mildly reduced EF** (41–49 %) and **preserved EF** (50 % or more).
s5359 Physical signs characteristic of essential hypertension: a **resistant** apex beat slightly displaced to the left, a widened left cardiac border, an **aortic configuration**, an **accentuated II sound** over the aorta (sometimes with a metallic tinge), a **tense (hard)** pulse.
s5360 The cardiac borders and the chambers: **“Right — Right atrium, Upper — Left atrium, Left — Left ventricle”**. Whichever border is displaced, that chamber is enlarged.
s5361 1. Percussion of the heart
s5362 Part of the heart is covered by the lungs, and part is in direct contact with the chest wall. That is why two kinds of dullness are distinguished:
s5363 **Relative cardiac dullness** is the **true projection** of the heart onto the anterior surface of the chest (including the parts covered by the lungs). It is determined by percussion of moderate strength, by the change from the clear lung note to an impaired note.
s5364 **Absolute cardiac dullness** is the part of the heart not covered by the lungs and in direct contact with the chest wall (mainly the right ventricle). It is determined by **the quietest** percussion, by the change from an impaired note to a completely dull note.
s5365 1.1. Rules of percussion
s5366 The patient stands or lies on the back (always in the same position, so that the results can be compared).
s5367 The pleximeter finger is placed **parallel** to the border being determined.
s5368 Percussion proceeds from the resonant note toward the dull note.
s5369 The border is marked along the edge of the pleximeter facing the resonant note.
s5370 Sequence: the right border is determined first, then the upper border, then the left border.
s5371 1.2. Determining the borders of relative cardiac dullness
s5372 **The right border.** First the lower border of the right lung is determined along the midclavicular line (normally the VI rib). Then the pleximeter is moved one intercostal space higher — to the **IV intercostal space** — and percussion proceeds medially with the pleximeter parallel to the sternum.

s5394 1.3. Determining the width of the vascular bundle
s5395 Percussion is performed **in the II intercostal space** from the right and left midclavicular lines toward the sternum.
