s4440 A cavity is detected by physical methods only under the following conditions: it is **more than 4–5 cm** in diameter; it lies **close to the chest wall (superficially)**; it **communicates with a bronchus** and is filled with air (the pus has drained); the lung tissue around it is consolidated (inflammatory infiltrate). A small, deep-seated or pus-filled cavity gives the picture of consolidation or no change at all on physical examination.
s4441 The improvement of the condition and the fall in temperature after an abscess has ruptured into a bronchus may be deceptive. If the cavity drains poorly (a narrow or oblique draining bronchus), the fever and intoxication increase again. An abscess that has not healed in 2–3 months becomes a **chronic abscess**.
s4442 The main signs of bronchiectasis — **“Pus, Blood, Clubs”**: a large amount of purulent sputum (in the morning), hemoptysis, clubbing.
s4443 Typical errors in the case history: listing complaints without analysis; breaking the chronology of the history; writing a general “normal” in the objective examination (in fact, each parameter is written with an exact figure: respiratory rate, pulse, BP, borders); not justifying the syndromic diagnosis with signs; “copying” the diagnosis from the medical record before talking to the patient.
s4444 In a smoker over 40, any cavity in the lung, especially one with a thick, irregular wall, is considered a **cavitating lung cancer** until proven otherwise: bronchoscopy and biopsy are performed. “Treating an abscess” with antibiotics may lead to a loss of time.
s4445 It is convenient to write progress notes and conclusions according to the **SOAP** system of the English-language literature: **S**ubjective — the patient’s complaints; **O**bjective — objective findings; **A**ssessment — assessment (dynamics, diagnosis); **P**lan — plan (investigations and treatment).
s4446 Postural drainage is **contraindicated** in pulmonary hemorrhage, severe heart failure, high arterial hypertension, acute cerebrovascular events and in the elderly in a severe condition.
s4447 The most important risk factor for lung cancer is **smoking** (80–90 % of cases). In a smoker over 40 with a prolonged (more than 3 weeks) cough, hemoptysis or pneumonia recurring in the same place, chest CT and bronchoscopy are mandatory.
s4448 The **“5 parameters”** followed daily in the progress notes: **temperature, respiratory rate, SpO~2~, sputum, auscultation**. Their improvement shows that the treatment is effective.
