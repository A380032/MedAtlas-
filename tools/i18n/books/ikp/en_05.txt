s1055 13. Inspection of the neck: assessment of the central venous pressure
s1056 The filling of the neck veins reflects the pressure in the right atrium (the central venous pressure — CVP). Its assessment is important in detecting heart failure and pericarditis and in judging fluid balance.
s1057 **Method:** the patient lies at an angle of 45° with the head turned slightly to the left. The highest point of pulsation of the right internal jugular vein is identified. The vertical distance from it to the **sternal angle** (angle of Louis) is measured. Because the sternal angle lies about 5 cm above the right atrium, CVP ≈ the measured distance + 5 cm.

s1188 20. Clinical part
s1189 **General condition** moderately severe. Consciousness clear (GCS 15), oriented in time and place. Position forced — sitting with the legs lowered (orthopnea). Body build hypersthenic, epigastric angle 100°. The face is puffy, the lips and tip of the nose are cyanotic (acrocyanosis). The sclerae are not subicteric, the conjunctivae pink. The tongue is moist, covered with a white coating. The neck is symmetrical, the thyroid gland is not visible, and the neck veins are distended and pulsate even in the upright position.
s1190 **Conclusion:** orthopnea, acrocyanosis and distended neck veins raise suspicion of heart failure with congestion in the systemic and pulmonary circulations.
