s6564 6.3. Mitral valve prolapse
s6565 **Mitral valve prolapse** is bulging of the mitral valve leaflets (most often the posterior leaflet) into the cavity of the left atrium by more than 2 mm during systole. It occurs in 2–3 % of the population, more often in asthenic women and in connective tissue dysplasia (Marfan syndrome). In most cases it is asymptomatic; sometimes there is stabbing pain in the heart region, palpitations, weakness. **Auscultation:** a **midsystolic “click”** at the apex followed by a **late systolic murmur**; on standing and during the Valsalva maneuver the click moves closer to the I sound and the murmur lengthens. Complications: significant mitral regurgitation, arrhythmias, infective endocarditis (if there is regurgitation).

s6581 7. Principles of treatment of mitral defects (in brief)
s6582 Knowing the principles of treatment helps the student understand the essence of the disease:
s6583 **Mitral stenosis:** in severe symptomatic stenosis — **percutaneous balloon mitral commissurotomy** (if the leaflets are suitable) or surgical valve replacement; slowing the heart rate with drugs (beta-blockers), diuretics; in atrial fibrillation — **anticoagulants** (warfarin) are mandatory.
s6584 **Mitral regurgitation:** in severe primary regurgitation — valve **repair** (plasty) or replacement; in the secondary type — full treatment of heart failure, sometimes transcatheter edge-to-edge repair of the leaflets.
s6585 In all patients — secondary prevention of rheumatism, sanitation of foci of infection, dental hygiene.
s6586 8. Chronic rheumatic heart disease and pregnancy
s6587 Rheumatic defects often occur in young women, so pregnancy has a great effect on their course. During pregnancy the circulating blood volume increases by **30–50 %**, and the heart rate and cardiac output rise.
s6588 **In mitral stenosis** pregnancy is most dangerous: the heart rate increases and diastole shortens, the pressure in the left atrium rises — **pulmonary edema** (most often in the II–III trimester and during delivery), atrial fibrillation. In severe mitral stenosis, balloon commissurotomy may be performed before or during pregnancy.
s6589 **In mitral and aortic regurgitation** pregnancy usually goes relatively well (the fall in peripheral resistance reduces the regurgitation).
s6590 **In severe aortic stenosis** the risk is high.

s6624 12. Natural course of mitral defects
s6625 **Mitral stenosis:** it usually takes **10–20 years** after ARF for the defect to form; symptoms often appear at the age of 30–40. Once symptoms have appeared, the 10-year survival of untreated patients is about 50–60 %. Causes of death: progressive heart failure, thromboembolism (stroke), pulmonary edema, infective endocarditis.
s6626 **Chronic mitral regurgitation** remains compensated for many years; left ventricular dilation and a fall in the ejection fraction (below 60 %) are indications for surgery, because the results of the operation are good if it is performed before irreversible myocardial dysfunction develops.

s6741 Analyze according to the Jones criteria. What is the diagnosis?

s6743 In a 45-year-old man, the apex beat is in the VI space, 2 cm lateral to the midclavicular line, diffuse and strong. The cardiac borders are widened upward and to the left. At the apex the I sound is inaudible; a blowing murmur merging with the I sound and occupying the whole of systole radiates to the left axillary region. Accentuation of the II sound over the pulmonary artery.
s6744 Which defect is this? Which cardiac chambers are enlarged, and why?
s6745 Mitral valve regurgitation. Because blood returns to the left atrium in systole, the left atrium dilates (displacement of the upper border); because excess blood enters the left ventricle in diastole, left ventricular dilation and hypertrophy develop (displacement of the apex beat and the left border to the left). Accentuation of the II sound shows raised pressure in the pulmonary circulation.
