s1566 8. Palpation of the thyroid gland
s1567 The thyroid gland is located on the anterior surface of the neck, below the thyroid cartilage of the larynx, and consists of two lobes and the isthmus connecting them. Normally a lobe is no larger than the terminal phalanx of the patient’s thumb.
s1568 **Method of palpation (from in front of the patient):** the patient sits or stands with the head bent slightly forward. Standing opposite the patient, the physician places the thumbs of both hands below the thyroid cartilage at the inner edge of the sternocleidomastoid muscles. The patient is asked to swallow a sip of water: during swallowing the gland slides upward under the fingers, and its lobes and isthmus are palpated.
s1569 **Method of palpation (from behind the patient):** the physician stands behind the patient, places fingers 2–4 of both hands over the gland and palpates the lobes during swallowing.
s1570 The following are assessed: size, consistency (soft, firm, hard), surface (smooth, uneven), the presence of nodules, tenderness, mobility. A systolic bruit heard on auscultation over the gland indicates the increased blood flow of diffuse toxic goiter.

s1683 12.1. Pathogenesis of fever
s1684 Exogenous pyrogens (bacterial endotoxin, viruses) and tissue breakdown products activate leukocytes and macrophages → **endogenous pyrogens** (interleukin-1, interleukin-6, tumor necrosis factor alpha) are released → **prostaglandin E~2~** is formed in the hypothalamus → the “set point” of the thermoregulatory center rises. Antipyretic drugs (paracetamol, NSAIDs) act precisely by blocking prostaglandin synthesis.
s1685 **The protective and harmful sides of fever:** fever enhances the immune response and slows the multiplication of many microbes; however, high fever increases oxygen consumption and places a burden on the heart (tachycardia), the nervous system (seizures in children) and metabolism.
s1686 12.2. Hypothermia and fever of unknown origin
s1687 **Hypothermia** — a core temperature below 35 °C: exposure to cold, hypothyroidism (myxedema coma), the severe stage of sepsis, alcohol intoxication, adrenal insufficiency.
s1688 **Fever of unknown origin** — a temperature above 38.3 °C lasting more than 3 weeks whose cause has not been found even after one week of investigation in hospital. The main groups of causes: **infections** (tuberculosis, infective endocarditis, occult abscesses), **tumors** (lymphomas, kidney cancer), **connective tissue diseases** (SLE, vasculitides, Still’s disease), **drug fever**.

s1716 Palpation is one of the oldest methods of propaedeutics, in which the physician’s hand serves as a “measuring instrument”.
