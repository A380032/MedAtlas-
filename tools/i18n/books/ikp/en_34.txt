s7163 A 38-year-old man who had rheumatism in childhood is being followed up for mitral regurgitation. A tooth was extracted a month ago. For two weeks his temperature has risen to 38.5–39 °C in the evenings, with chills, profuse sweating and weakness; he has lost 4 kg. On examination the skin is pale yellowish, there are petechiae on the left conjunctiva and painful red nodules on the fingertips. A diastolic murmur that was not present before has appeared at the Botkin–Erb point. The spleen is palpable. Hb 98 g/l, ESR 62 mm/h.
s7164 Which disease is suspected? Which signs have been found? What should be done to confirm the diagnosis?
s7165 Infective endocarditis (probably viridans streptococcal, after a dental procedure, on the background of a rheumatic defect). Signs: fever, weight loss, “café-au-lait” skin and anemia, the Lukin–Libman sign (conjunctival petechiae), Osler nodes, a new murmur of aortic regurgitation (destruction of the aortic valve), splenomegaly, raised ESR. To confirm: at least 3 blood cultures before antibiotics and echocardiography (transthoracic, then transesophageal) to detect vegetations; assessment according to the Duke-ISCVID criteria.
s7166 A 70-year-old man reports squeezing retrosternal pain on climbing stairs over the past year and two episodes of fainting on exertion. A harsh systolic murmur is heard in the II space to the right of the sternum, radiating to the carotid arteries, with a systolic thrill at the same site. The II sound over the aorta is markedly weakened. Pulse 58/min, slow and small. BP 105/80 mm Hg.
s7167 Which defect is this? Calculate and interpret the pulse pressure. Which echocardiographic parameters indicate severe stenosis?
s7168 Aortic stenosis (in old age most often degenerative calcification) — symptomatic (angina, syncope). Pulse pressure 105 − 80 = 25 mm Hg — narrow (limited stroke volume); the pulse is slow and small (tardus et parvus). Severe stenosis: orifice area less than 1.0 cm^2^, peak velocity 4 m/s or more, mean gradient 40 mm Hg or more. Symptomatic severe aortic stenosis is an indication for valve replacement (surgical or transcatheter TAVI).
s7169 A 28-year-old man (tall, with long limbs and long thin fingers) was referred because of a BP of 150/45 mm Hg found incidentally at a medical examination. Strong pulsation of the carotid arteries, Quincke pulse positive. A diastolic murmur over the aorta.
s7170 Which defect is this, and what is its probable cause? Why is the diastolic pressure low?
s7171 Aortic valve regurgitation; the body build (arachnodactyly, tall stature) suggests Marfan syndrome — valve regurgitation resulting from dilation of the aortic root. The diastolic pressure is low because in diastole blood returns from the aorta to the left ventricle and the pressure in the aorta falls sharply; the large stroke volume raises the systolic pressure — the pulse pressure is wide. On echocardiography the size of the aortic root and the severity of the regurgitation are assessed.
