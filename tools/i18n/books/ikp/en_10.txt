s2209 6. Pathogenesis of the main complaints
s2210 6.1. Mechanisms of dyspnea
s2211 Dyspnea is a multifactorial sensation that arises through the following mechanisms:
s2212 **Impaired gas exchange** — hypoxemia and hypercapnia stimulate the chemoreceptors and excite the respiratory center.
s2213 **Impaired respiratory mechanics** — increased bronchial resistance (obstruction) or decreased lung compliance (fibrosis, congestion) requires greater work of the respiratory muscles; the “mismatch between the effort required and the result obtained” is felt as dyspnea.
s2214 **Stimulation of receptors in the lungs** — the interstitial J receptors (pulmonary edema, fibrosis) and stretch receptors.
s2215 **Metabolic acidosis** — Kussmaul breathing.
s2216 **Psychological factors** — anxiety, panic attacks (hyperventilation syndrome: “unable to take a full breath”, sighing).

s2261 7. Additional aspects of the inspection of the chest
s2262 7.1. Topographic lines of the chest
s2263 Conventional vertical lines are used to localize changes on the chest precisely: the **anterior midline**, the **sternal line**, the **parasternal line** (midway between the sternal and midclavicular lines), the **midclavicular line** (through the middle of the clavicle), the **anterior, middle and posterior axillary lines**, the **scapular line** (through the angle of the scapula), the **paravertebral line** and the **posterior midline**.
s2264 **Counting the ribs:** in front — the II rib joins the sternum at the level of the sternal angle (the angle of Louis, where the manubrium joins the body of the sternum), and counting proceeds downward from it; behind — with the arms lowered, the angles of the shoulder blades correspond to the VII rib; the spinous process of the VII cervical vertebra is the most prominent when the head is bent forward.

s2328 10. Detailed analysis of cough and sputum
s2329 10.1. Mechanism of the cough reflex
s2330 When the cough receptors are stimulated (mechanically, chemically, thermally, by inflammation), impulses travel along the vagus nerve to the cough center in the medulla oblongata. A cough consists of three phases: **a deep inspiration** → **closure of the glottis and contraction of the expiratory muscles** (the intrathoracic pressure rises to 100–300 mm Hg) → **sudden opening of the glottis** and expulsion of air at high speed (hundreds of km per hour) together with the secretions.

s2341 10.2. The amount of sputum and its significance
s2342 **A small amount** (a few spittings a day) — acute bronchitis, bronchial asthma, the onset of pneumonia;
s2343 **a moderate amount** (50–100 ml) — chronic bronchitis, pneumonia;
s2344 **a large amount** (more than 100–150 ml, sometimes up to 1 l) — lung abscess (after rupture), bronchiectasis, lung gangrene, pulmonary edema, cavitary tuberculosis.
s2345 11. Measuring the dimensions of the chest
s2346 The chest circumference is measured with a centimeter tape: behind, the tape passes below the angles of the shoulder blades, and in front, at the level of the nipples in men and at the junction of the IV rib with the sternum in women. Normally the circumference equals half the height or is slightly greater. The **anteroposterior** and **transverse** diameters of the chest are determined with a special caliper (pelvimeter); normally the ratio of the anteroposterior to the transverse diameter (the chest index) is **0.65–0.75**; in emphysema it approaches 1.0.

s2360 13. Features of the respiratory system in the elderly
s2361 With age, the chest becomes rigid (the costal cartilages calcify), the strength of the respiratory muscles and the elasticity of the lungs decrease, mucociliary clearance weakens and the cough reflex diminishes. As a result:
s2362 chest excursion decreases and resistance increases;
s2363 the lower borders of the lungs descend slightly (“senile emphysema”), and vesicular breathing is diminished;
s2364 pneumonia often runs an **asymptomatic or atypical** course: without fever, with only weakness, confusion, loss of appetite, falls;
s2365 isolated crackles in the lower parts of the lungs that disappear after coughing may be normal (basal atelectasis).

s2385 Skill: counting the respiratory rate
s2386 Lay or seat the patient in a comfortable position.
s2387 Hold the patient’s hand as if counting the pulse (so that the patient does not consciously change his or her breathing).
s2388 Place your other hand on the lower part of the chest or on the abdominal wall.
s2389 Count the respiratory movements for one minute (one inspiration and one expiration — one movement).
s2390 At the same time, assess the type, depth and rhythm of breathing.
s2391 Record the result on the temperature chart.
s2392 Skill: determining vocal fremitus
s2393 Undress the patient to the waist, standing or sitting.
s2394 Place the palms of both hands lightly on symmetrical areas of the chest, with the fingers directed along the ribs.
s2395 Ask the patient to say “thirty-three” or “ninety-nine” in a low voice.
s2396 Compare the fremitus on the two sides; then swap the hands and examine again.
s2397 Examine the areas in turn: supraclavicular, infraclavicular, lateral surfaces, suprascapular, interscapular, infrascapular.
s2398 Interpret the result: equal, increased or decreased (in which area).
s2399 Skill: measuring chest excursion
s2400 Pass the centimeter tape behind below the angles of the shoulder blades, and in front at the level of the nipples in men and below the breasts in women.
s2401 Measure the chest circumference during quiet breathing, at maximal inspiration and at maximal expiration.
s2402 The difference between the circumferences at maximal inspiration and expiration is the excursion, normally 6–8 cm. It is reduced in emphysema, pleural adhesions and ankylosing spondylitis.
