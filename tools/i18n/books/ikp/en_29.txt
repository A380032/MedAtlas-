s6264 16. Useful rules for recording and teaching murmurs and sounds
s6265 Listen **to the sounds first, then to the murmurs**: the phase of a murmur cannot be determined without placing it relative to the I and II sounds.
s6266 **Attend to one sound at a time**: listen “selectively” first only to the I sound, then only to the II sound, then to systole, then to diastole.
s6267 **In tachycardia** the duration of systole and diastole becomes equal — determine the phases by the carotid pulse.
s6268 **In atrial fibrillation** the loudness of a murmur varies with the cycle: in the cycle after a long pause the murmur of aortic stenosis becomes louder, while the murmur of mitral regurgitation does not change.
s6269 Always record the result in the same form: **phase — epicenter — radiation — intensity (Levine) — shape — timbre — relation to the sounds — dynamic maneuvers**.
s6270 17. Auscultatory signs of tricuspid valve defects
s6271 **Tricuspid regurgitation** is most often **relative** (right ventricular dilation due to mitral stenosis, pulmonary hypertension or cor pulmonale), less often organic (infective endocarditis — in injecting drug users, rheumatism, Ebstein anomaly). Signs: a **pansystolic murmur** at the base of the xiphoid process and the lower part of the left sternal edge that **becomes louder on inspiration (Rivero-Carvallo sign)**; a **positive venous pulse**, systolic pulsation of the liver, hepatomegaly, ascites, edema.
s6272 **Tricuspid stenosis** is rare and almost always rheumatic and combined with mitral stenosis; a diastolic murmur at the base of the xiphoid process that becomes louder on inspiration, large “a” waves in the neck veins, marked peripheral edema and ascites (even though pulmonary congestion is slight).
s6273 **Auscultation.** The heart sounds are regular, 78 per minute. At the apex the I sound is weakened, and a systolic murmur is heard at the apex: it merges with the I sound, occupies the whole of systole, has a blowing timbre, is grade 3 on the Levine scale, radiates to the left axillary region and becomes louder in the left lateral position. Accentuation of the II sound over the pulmonary artery. The II sound over the aorta is preserved. No extra sounds.
s6274 **Conclusion:** weakening of the I sound at the apex, a systolic murmur radiating to the left axilla and accentuation of the II sound over the pulmonary artery — characteristic of mitral valve regurgitation.

s6389 The older literature used the term “rheumatism”. International and CIS classifications now use the terms **“acute rheumatic fever”** and **“chronic rheumatic heart disease”**. The term “rheumatism” in the course syllabus is understood in this sense.
s6390 Diagnosis of **initial ARF**: **2 major** or **1 major + 2 minor** criteria together with evidence of a preceding group A streptococcal infection. **Recurrent ARF** (with a history of ARF or rheumatic heart disease): 2 major, 1 major + 2 minor, or **3 minor** criteria. Evidence of streptococcal infection — a raised or rising ASO titer, a positive throat culture for streptococcus or a positive rapid antigen test. Chorea alone is also sufficient for the diagnosis.
s6391 The major Jones criteria — **“JONES”**: **J** — Joints (polyarthritis); **O** — the heart-shaped “O” (carditis); **N** — Nodules (subcutaneous nodules); **E** — Erythema marginatum; **S** — Sydenham chorea.
s6392 The textbook gives three grades of activity of the rheumatic process (maximal — III, moderate — II, minimal — I) (A.I. Nesterov classification). This classification is partly retained in CIS practice, but in international practice the diagnosis is made by the Jones criteria, and carditis is assessed by echocardiography (including subclinical carditis).
s6393 The auscultatory triad of mitral regurgitation: **weakening of the I sound at the apex + a systolic murmur merging with the I sound and radiating to the left axilla + accentuation of the II sound over the pulmonary artery**.
s6394 The auscultatory signs of mitral stenosis: **snapping I sound + mitral opening snap (“quail rhythm”) + a diastolic murmur at the apex increasing in presystole + accentuation of the II sound over the pulmonary artery**.
s6395 Mitral stenosis — **“Face, Blood, Quail”**: facies mitralis, hemoptysis, the “quail rhythm”. Mitral regurgitation — **“Soft I sound, murmur to the Axilla”**.
s6396 Streptococcal **pharyngitis and tonsillitis** lead to ARF, whereas streptococcal **skin infections** (impetigo) usually do not (they are associated more with glomerulonephritis). That is why, in a child with a sore throat and fever, detecting the streptococcal infection and giving a full 10-day course of antibiotics — **primary prevention** — is extremely important.
s6397 Duration of secondary prevention (AHA): without carditis — **5 years or until the age of 21** (whichever is longer); carditis without residual valve disease — **10 years or until the age of 21**; carditis with residual valve disease — **10 years or until the age of 40**, sometimes lifelong.
s6398 To remember the changes in the cardiac chambers in mitral defects: **“In stenosis — the left atrium and the right heart; in regurgitation — the left atrium and the left ventricle”**. The left atrium enlarges in both defects; the difference lies in the ventricles.
s6399 Every pregnant woman with a heart defect should be followed jointly by **a cardiologist and an obstetrician** and monitored by echocardiography. In pregnant women with a mechanical prosthetic valve, anticoagulant therapy is managed according to a special protocol.
s6400 In mitral stenosis, the shorter the **“II sound — opening snap” interval**, the more severe the stenosis (the pressure in the left atrium is high, and the valve opens earlier). If this interval on the PCG is less than 0.07 s, the stenosis is severe.
s6401 Complications of mitral stenosis — **“RASH”**: **R**ight ventricular failure, **A**trial fibrillation, **S**troke and other thromboembolism, **H**emoptysis and pulmonary edema.
