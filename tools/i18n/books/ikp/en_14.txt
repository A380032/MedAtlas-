s3047 3.2. Bronchial (laryngotracheal) breathing
s3048 **Mechanism of production:** when air passes through the narrow glottis in the larynx, the airflow becomes turbulent and a sound is produced. Because the glottis is narrower during expiration, the expiratory phase of bronchial breathing is louder, harsher and longer.
s3049 **Features:** harsh and loud, resembling the sound made when saying the letter “h”; it is heard **throughout** inspiration and expiration, and the expiratory phase is stronger and longer.
s3050 **Pathological bronchial breathing** is heard when the lung tissue has come to conduct sound well.

s3064 3.3. Mixed (bronchovesicular) breathing
s3065 It combines the features of vesicular and bronchial breathing: the inspiratory phase resembles vesicular and the expiratory phase bronchial breathing. It is heard when the lung tissue is consolidated in small foci, deep down or unevenly (focal pneumonia, tuberculous infiltrate, pneumosclerosis). In a healthy person it may also be heard over the right apex.

s3067 The added breath sounds include **rales** (dry and moist), **crepitation** and the **pleural friction rub**. They are heard against the background of the main breath sound and are always considered pathological.

s3100 4.3. Crepitation (crepitatio)
s3101 **Mechanism:** when there is a sticky secretion (exudate) on the inner surface of the alveoli, the alveolar walls stick together during expiration; at the **very end** of a deep inspiration, air enters and separates them simultaneously — a faint “crackling” is heard. This sound is likened to rubbing a lock of hair between the fingers next to the ear or to throwing salt onto a fire.
s3102 **Causes:** stages I (crepitatio indux) and III (crepitatio redux) of lobar pneumonia, pulmonary infarction, pulmonary congestion and the onset of pulmonary edema, fibrosing alveolitis.
s3103 4.4. Pleural friction rub (frictio pleurae)
s3104 **Mechanism:** normally the smooth, moist layers of the pleura slide silently during breathing. In inflammation of the pleura (fibrin deposits), its drying (dehydration, uremia), tumors and tuberculous tubercles, the surfaces of the layers become rough — and a sound is produced when they rub together. This sound resembles the crunching of snow underfoot or the creaking of new leather.
s3105 **Causes:** dry (fibrinous) pleurisy, lobar pneumonia (the pleura is also involved), pulmonary infarction, pleural tumors and tuberculosis, uremia, severe dehydration. In exudative pleurisy, it reappears during the period of fluid resorption.
s3106 The pleural friction rub is most often heard **in the lower part of the axillary region** (where the lung moves most during breathing). When the pleura next to the heart is inflamed, a **pleuropericardial rub** related to both the heartbeat and breathing is heard.

s3134 6. Bronchophony
s3135 **Bronchophony** is the audibility with a stethoscope, over the chest wall, of words whispered by the patient. It has the same mechanism as vocal fremitus but is more sensitive in detecting smaller foci.
s3136 **Method:** the stethoscope is placed on symmetrical points, and the patient is asked to whisper words containing hissing sounds (“cup of tea”, “sixty-six”, “hush”). Normally the words are not heard clearly; only an indistinct murmur is perceived.
s3137 **Increased bronchophony** (the words are heard clearly) — in consolidation of the lung tissue and in a cavity communicating with a bronchus (the same conditions as increased vocal fremitus). **Decreased** — fluid or air in the pleural cavity, obstructive atelectasis, emphysema.
s3138 English-language textbooks distinguish two types of bronchophony: **whispered pectoriloquy** — clear audibility of whispered words, and **egophony** — the sound “ee” heard like “ay”, resembling the bleating of a goat; egophony is observed at the upper border of fluid (over the compressed lung) and in consolidation.

s3178 8.1. Conditions for the occurrence of pathological bronchial breathing
s3179 For bronchial breathing to be heard over the chest wall, three conditions must be met:
s3180 the lung tissue must be **consolidated** (airless);
s3181 the consolidated area must be connected with a **patent bronchus**;
s3182 the area of consolidation must be **close** to the chest wall and sufficiently **large**.
s3183 That is why bronchial breathing is rarely heard in focal pneumonia (small, deep foci), whereas in lobar pneumonia it is always heard.

s3200 9.2. Wheezes and stridor
s3201 **Polyphonic** wheezes (of different pitches at the same time) — diffuse narrowing of many bronchi (bronchial asthma, COPD).
s3202 **Monophonic** (a single pitch, always in the same place) — local narrowing of a single bronchus (tumor, foreign body). If such a wheeze does not change after coughing, bronchoscopy is needed.
s3203 **Stridor** is a loud, harsh sound heard at a distance, mainly **during inspiration**, in narrowing of the upper airways (larynx, trachea). It is a life-threatening sign: angioedema, foreign body, laryngeal tumor, epiglottitis, false croup.
s3204 9.3. Other added sounds
s3205 **Pleuropericardial rub** — a sound related to the heartbeat and to breathing when the pleura next to the heart is inflamed; it diminishes when the breath is held.
s3206 **“Falling drop” sound** — a resonant dripping sound heard when the patient changes position in a large cavity containing air and fluid (hydropneumothorax, a large cavern).
s3207 **Hippocratic succussion splash** — heard in hydropneumothorax when the patient is shaken by the shoulders.
s3208 **“Mediastinal crunch”** (Hamman’s sign) — a crunching sound heard in time with the heartbeat in mediastinal emphysema.

s3233 12. Standard form for recording auscultatory findings
s3234 The result of auscultation is recorded in the following order:
s3235 The type and intensity of the main breath sound (vesicular, diminished, increased, harsh, bronchial, mixed, amphoric).
s3236 The ratio of the respiratory phases (whether expiration is prolonged or not).
s3237 Added sounds: type (dry — whistling or humming; moist — fine-, medium- or coarse-bubbling, consonating or nonconsonating; crepitation; pleural friction rub), amount (isolated, numerous), location, phase of respiration, change after coughing.
