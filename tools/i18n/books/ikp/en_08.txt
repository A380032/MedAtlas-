s1773 17. Additional methods of assessing body weight
s1774 **Broca index** — ideal weight = height (cm) − 100 (for people 155–165 cm tall); at 165–175 cm, − 105; above 175 cm, − 110. Exceeding the ideal weight by more than 10 % is overweight.
s1775 **Skinfold thickness** is measured with a special instrument (caliper) at several points (the back of the upper arm — triceps, below the shoulder blade, next to the umbilicus, over the iliac crest), and the proportion of fat in the body is calculated. Modern methods are **bioimpedance analysis** and **DEXA**.

s1963 When purulent sputum is left to settle in a container, it separates into layers. According to the textbook, **two layers** (pus and serous fluid) are characteristic of lung abscess, and **three layers** (pus at the bottom, serous fluid in the middle, frothy mucus on top) of bronchiectasis and cavitary tuberculosis.
s1964 In Russian-language textbooks (Grebenev, Mukhin), three-layered sputum is given as a common sign of **lung abscess, lung gangrene and bronchiectasis**. Hence the number of layers does not strictly distinguish an abscess from bronchiectasis; the decisive investigation is radiography and CT.
s1965 In a healthy adult at rest, the respiratory rate is **16–20** per minute (12–14 during sleep), and in a newborn 40–45. A respiratory rate of more than 20 per minute is **tachypnea**, less than 12 is **bradypnea**, and cessation of breathing is **apnea**. The ratio of the respiratory rate to the pulse rate is normally **1:4**.
s1966 **“Kussmaul — Ketoacidosis”**: deep, noisy breathing in acidosis. **“Cheyne–Stokes — Crescendo-decrescendo wave + Cessation”**. **“Biot — Bursts of equal Breaths, Blank pauses”**.
s1967 Vocal fremitus is **increased over solid tissue and dampened by fluid and air**: “Solid — Strong, Fluid and Air — Faint”.
s1968 For vocal fremitus to be increased, bronchial patency must be preserved. If the bronchus of the consolidated area is blocked by a tumor or a foreign body (obstructive atelectasis), the fremitus is **decreased** even though the tissue is dense.
s1969 Loss of **more than 100–200 ml** of blood per day (500 ml in some sources), or bleeding accompanied by airway obstruction and hemodynamic instability, is **massive pulmonary hemorrhage**; it is life-threatening and requires urgent bronchoscopy and bronchial artery embolization. The patient is laid on the bleeding side (so that blood does not flow into the healthy lung).
s1970 **Pulse oximetry** is the painless measurement of the oxygen saturation of arterial blood (SpO2) and the pulse with a sensor placed on a finger. Normal is **95–98 %**. An SpO2 below 90 % is hypoxemia (an indication for oxygen therapy). In patients with COPD, the target SpO2 is 88–92 %.
s1971 What is examined by palpation — **“TREV”**: **T**enderness, **R**esistance, **E**xpansion (participation in breathing), **V**ocal fremitus.
s1972 Vocal fremitus is easy to examine in men with a low voice, whereas in women and children with a high-pitched voice it is felt only weakly. That is why **comparing the two sides** is important: it is not the absolute strength of the fremitus but its **asymmetry** that has diagnostic significance.
s1973 The production of a large amount of sputum at once (“a mouthful”) is a sign of the **rupture of a cavity in the lung into a bronchus** (abscess) or of **bronchiectasis**; an increase in sputum in the morning and its dependence on position are characteristic of bronchiectasis.
