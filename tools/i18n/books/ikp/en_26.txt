s5568 6. Arterial pressure and its measurement
s5569 **Arterial pressure** (BP) is the pressure exerted by the blood on the walls of the arteries. **Systolic** (upper) pressure is the highest pressure during ventricular systole; **diastolic** (lower) pressure is the lowest pressure in diastole; **pulse pressure** is the difference between them (normally 40–50 mm Hg); **mean arterial pressure** ≈ diastolic + 1/3 of the pulse pressure.

s5576 6.1. Rules for measuring BP by the Korotkoff method
s5577 The patient sits quietly for 5 minutes; for 30 minutes beforehand he does not smoke, drink coffee or exert himself physically; the bladder is empty.
s5578 The patient sits leaning back, legs uncrossed, arm resting on a table, the middle of the upper arm **at the level of the heart**.
s5579 The cuff is wrapped around the upper arm, its lower edge **2–3 cm above** the elbow crease, so that one finger fits between the cuff and the skin. The cuff width should cover **40 %** of the arm circumference, and the bladder length **80 %** (a large cuff for obese patients).
s5580 First, by the palpatory method, air is pumped in until the pulse in the radial artery disappears; then the pressure is raised a further **20–30 mm Hg** above this level.
s5581 The membrane of the stethoscope is placed over the brachial artery in the cubital fossa.
s5582 The air is released at a rate of **2–3 mm Hg per second**.
s5583 The reading at which **the first Korotkoff sound** appears is the systolic pressure; the reading at which the sounds **disappear completely** (phase V) is the diastolic pressure.
s5584 The measurement is repeated 2–3 times at intervals of 1–2 minutes, and the average is taken; at the first visit BP is measured on both arms (the difference should not exceed 10–15 mm Hg).
