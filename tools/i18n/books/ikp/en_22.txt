s4657 9. Postural drainage
s4658 **Postural drainage** is placing the patient in a special position so that sputum drains out of the affected bronchi. It is used in bronchiectasis, lung abscess and chronic purulent bronchitis. s4672 10. Principles of treating lung abscess and bronchiectasis
s4673 **Lung abscess:** prolonged (4–6 weeks or more) antibiotic therapy (drugs active against anaerobes); postural drainage; bronchoscopic sanitation; if ineffective — percutaneous drainage or surgery (removal of a lobe of the lung).
s4674 **Bronchiectasis:** ensuring airway clearance (physiotherapy, drainage, mucolytics), antibiotics during exacerbations, long-term macrolides for frequent exacerbations; surgery for localized bronchiectasis; vaccination (influenza, pneumococcus).

s4849 The atrioventricular (mitral and tricuspid) valves close **at the beginning of systole** (the I sound), and the semilunar (aortic and pulmonary) valves close **at the beginning of diastole** (the II sound). This rule is the key to understanding heart sounds and murmurs.
s4850 The three features of anginal pain — **“Squeezes, on Exertion, eased by Nitro”**. If all three are present, it is typical angina; if two, atypical; if one or none, cardialgia (non-anginal pain).
s4851 When pulsation is seen in the neck, arterial and venous pulsation must be distinguished. **Venous pulsation** is soft, undulating, biphasic and visible but not palpable; it disappears when the vein is pressed above the clavicle and changes with the patient’s position and breathing. **Arterial pulsation** is abrupt, monophasic, easily palpable and does not disappear with pressure.
s4852 Displacement of the apex beat to the left and its widening are signs of **left ventricular dilation**, and its being resistant is a sign of **hypertrophy**. In aortic regurgitation the left ventricle is both dilated and hypertrophied — the impulse is **displaced to the left and downward, broad, high, forceful and resistant** (“dome-shaped”).
s4853 The 5 features of the apex beat — **“Location–Area–Height–Force–Resistance”**: “**L**azy **A**nts **H**ate **F**ast **R**unning”.
s4854 The stages of chronic heart failure (the classification of N.D. Strazhesko and V.Kh. Vasilenko): **I** — latent, signs only on physical exertion; **IIA** — congestion in one circulation, signs even at rest; **IIB** — congestion in both circulations; **III** — the final, dystrophic stage: irreversible changes in the organs, cachexia. This classification is used together with the NYHA functional classes.
s4855 Syncope **during physical exertion**, palpitations preceding it, syncope in the lying position and sudden death of young people in the family are signs of cardiogenic syncope and are life-threatening; such a patient urgently undergoes ECG, echocardiography and Holter monitoring.
s4856 Extracardiac causes of displacement of the apex beat — **“fluid and air push, adhesions and atelectasis pull”**: fluid or air in the pleura — the impulse shifts to the healthy side; pleural adhesions and atelectasis — to the affected side; a high diaphragm (ascites, pregnancy, meteorism) — upward and to the left; a low diaphragm (emphysema, ptosis) — downward and medially.
