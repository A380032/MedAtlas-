s5083 8. Heart failure: pathogenesis and signs
s5084 **Heart failure** is a clinical syndrome in which, as a result of a disturbance of the structure or function of the heart, the heart cannot meet the oxygen demands of the tissues or does so only at the cost of raised filling pressures (ESC, 2021).
s5085 **Compensatory mechanisms:** the Frank–Starling mechanism (increased filling strengthens contraction), activation of the sympathetic nervous system (tachycardia, vasoconstriction), the renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system (retention of sodium and water, edema), myocardial hypertrophy and remodeling. These mechanisms help at first but later aggravate the disease — modern treatment is aimed precisely at blocking them.

s5162 13. Examination of the peripheral arteries and veins
s5163 **Signs of atherosclerosis of the leg arteries:** intermittent claudication (calf pain after walking a certain distance that passes on stopping), pale, cold, dry skin of the legs with hair loss, thickened nails, trophic ulcers; weakened or absent pulses in the dorsalis pedis and posterior tibial arteries. **The ankle-brachial index** (the ratio of the systolic pressure at the ankle to the systolic pressure in the arm) is normally **1.0–1.4**; **below 0.9** indicates peripheral arterial disease.
s5164 **Veins:** varicose veins of the legs, signs of chronic venous insufficiency (edema, hyperpigmentation, trophic ulcers — most often in the region of the medial malleolus); signs of deep vein thrombosis — swelling and pain of the calf, a calf circumference more than 3 cm larger than that of the healthy leg.

s5233 Skill: palpation of the apex beat
s5234 Undress the patient to the waist, lying on the back (with the head raised by 30°) or standing.
s5235 Place the palm of the right hand over the region of the III–VI ribs, with the base of the palm toward the sternum and the fingers toward the axillary line.
s5236 Place the tips of 2–3 fingers (bent) perpendicularly over the place where the impulse is felt and find its exact site — the most lateral and lowest point.
s5237 Count the intercostal space (starting from the II rib — next to the sternal angle) and measure the distance from the midclavicular line.
s5238 Assess its area, height, force and resistance.
s5239 If the impulse is not detected, turn the patient onto the left side (the impulse shifts 2–3 cm to the left) and examine again; ask the patient to breathe out deeply and hold the breath.
s5240 Record the result.
s5241 Skill: detecting the “cat’s purr”
s5242 Place the palm in turn over the cardiac apex, the II intercostal space to the right and left of the sternum, and the left sternal border (III–IV space).
s5243 At the same time, palpate the pulse in the carotid artery with the other hand.
s5244 Determine the presence, site and phase (systolic or diastolic) of the thrill.
s5245 To feel the thrill better in mitral stenosis, lay the patient on the left side.
