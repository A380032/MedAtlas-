s3900 If the bronchus of the consolidated area is blocked by a tumor or a foreign body (**obstructive atelectasis**), then even though the percussion note is dull, vocal fremitus, bronchophony and breath sounds are **decreased or absent**, bronchial breathing is not heard, and the mediastinum shifts to the affected side. It is very important to distinguish this condition from pneumonia (especially in elderly smokers — lung cancer).
s3901 The stages of lobar pneumonia: **“Crepitation — Bronchial — Crepitation”** (indux — hepatization — redux), and on percussion **“Dull-tympanic — Dull — Dull-tympanic”**.
s3902 By CURB-65, **0–1 point** — outpatient treatment is possible; **2 points** — hospital admission is recommended; **3–5 points** — severe pneumonia, intensive care is likely. Where urea cannot be measured, the **CRB-65** variant is used.
s3903 In a **transudate** (hydrothorax), the fluid moves freely and its upper border is usually **horizontal**; the border also shifts when the patient changes position. In an **exudate**, because of adhesion of the pleural layers and fibrin, the border is **oblique** (the Damoiseau line) and depends little on position. This difference is relative; they are distinguished precisely by pleural puncture and fluid analysis.
s3904 **Light’s criteria** (1972): the fluid is an **exudate** if **at least one** of the following is present: 1) the ratio of pleural fluid protein to serum protein is **more than 0.5**; 2) the ratio of fluid LDH to serum LDH is **more than 0.6**; 3) the fluid LDH is **more than 2/3** of the upper limit of normal for serum LDH.
s3905 The dynamics of pleurisy: **“The pain goes — the dyspnea comes”** — when dry pleurisy turns into exudative pleurisy, the fluid separates the pleural layers and the pain lessens, but the lung is compressed and the dyspnea increases.
s3906 Usually **no more than 1–1.5 liters** of fluid are removed in a single procedure: rapid removal of a large amount of fluid may lead to an abrupt mediastinal shift, collapse and “re-expansion” pulmonary edema. The fluid obtained is sent for physicochemical, cytological and bacteriological examination.
s3907 The term “atypical” does not mean a mild or unusual course of pneumonia but refers to the intracellular pathogens **insensitive to the usual antibiotics (beta-lactams)** (Mycoplasma, Chlamydophila, Legionella). Macrolides or respiratory fluoroquinolones are used for them.
s3908 The rule of mediastinal shift: **“Volume added — it moves away, volume lost — it is pulled toward”**. Fluid, air or a large tumor in the pleura (volume added) — the mediastinum shifts to the **healthy** side; obstructive and contraction atelectasis, pneumonectomy (volume lost) — to the **affected** side.
s3909 The most common cause of a transudate is **heart failure** (often bilateral or right-sided), and of an exudate, **pneumonia, tumor and tuberculosis**. A unilateral, large, rapidly reaccumulating hemorrhagic exudate raises suspicion of a **tumor** first of all.
s3910 Signs of tension pneumothorax: severe dyspnea and cyanosis, tachycardia, **hypotension**, distended neck veins, deviation of the trachea to the **healthy** side, a tympanic note and absent breath sounds on the affected side. This condition requires urgent **needle decompression** without waiting for an X-ray (the II intercostal space in the midclavicular line or the IV–V space in the anterior axillary line).
s3911 If there are clinical signs but no infiltrate is found on X-ray (the initial stage, dehydration, neutropenia, a very small focus), pneumonia is considered “probable”, and the X-ray is repeated after 24–48 hours or CT is performed. Conversely, an infiltrate on X-ray is not always pneumonia: it must be distinguished from pulmonary infarction, tumor, tuberculosis and eosinophilic infiltrate.
s3912 The **“4C”** order of pleural fluid analysis: **C**olor (appearance), **C**hemistry (Rivalta test and protein — transudate or exudate), **C**ells (neutrophils, lymphocytes, eosinophils, atypical cells), **C**ulture (microbiology: bacteria, tuberculosis).
s3913 The key to distinguishing the three syndromes is **vocal fremitus**: it is **increased in consolidation and decreased in fluid and air**. Fluid and air, in turn, are distinguished by **percussion**: fluid — dull, air — tympanic.

s3960 3.2. Clinical picture
s3961 **Sudden onset**: severe chills (shivering), the body temperature rises to 39–40 °C within a few hours.
