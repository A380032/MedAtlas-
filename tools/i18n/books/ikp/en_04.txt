s0705 The epigastric angle — **“Hyper — Wide, Asthenic — Narrow”**: in the HYPERsthenic type the angle is wide (>90°), in the ASTHENIC type it is narrow (<90°), and in the normosthenic type it is a right angle (90°).
s0706 When assessing distension of the neck veins, the patient lies at an angle of 45°. If the upper level of the internal jugular venous pulsation is **more than 3 cm above** the sternal angle, the central venous pressure is considered raised.
s0707 Normally, the venous pulsation rises no higher than **3 cm above** the sternal angle (CVP up to 8 cm H2O). At 4 cm or more the CVP is raised: right ventricular failure, tricuspid regurgitation, cardiac tamponade, constrictive pericarditis, hypervolemia. If the neck veins are full with the patient sitting upright, the CVP is markedly raised.
s0708 Sudden confusion and restlessness in an elderly patient are often not “old age” but a sign of **pneumonia, urinary tract infection, hypoglycemia, dehydration or an adverse drug effect**. In such a situation the cause must be actively sought.
s0709 Inspection of the hands is **“a mirror that tells a great deal”**: the nails (clubbing, koilonychia, Terry’s nails), the palms (erythema, pallor, Dupuytren’s contracture), the fingers (Osler’s nodes, Heberden’s and Bouchard’s nodes, rheumatoid deformities), tremor (thyrotoxicosis, asterixis), temperature and moisture. That is why it is convenient to begin the objective examination by taking the patient’s hand and counting the pulse.
s0710 1. Rules for performing the general inspection
s0711 However advanced laboratory and instrumental methods become, the general inspection (inspectio) remains one of the main methods of examination. An experienced physician often draws an important conclusion at the very first sight of the patient: a patient with heart failure sitting in the orthopnea position, a thyrotoxic patient with protruding eyes, and so on.
s0712 Conditions of the inspection:
s0713 the room should be warm (so that the patient does not get chilled) and quiet;
s0714 the light should be **natural, diffuse daylight**: jaundice and pallor are poorly seen in artificial (electric) light;
s0715 the light should fall from the side: protrusions and depressions on the body surface and pulsation are then well seen;
s0716 the patient’s body is uncovered step by step, and the parts not being examined are kept covered;
s0717 the symmetrical parts of the body are always compared.
s0718 The inspection is performed according to a plan: first the **general inspection** (general condition, consciousness, position, habitus — body build, posture and gait), then the **inspection of the body regions** (head, face, neck, trunk, limbs), and then the skin, subcutaneous fat, lymph nodes, muscles and joints are assessed.

s0804 4. The patient’s position
s0805 The patient’s position may be of three kinds: **active**, **passive** and **forced**.
s0806 **Active position** — the patient can assume any position he or she wishes and moves freely (mild diseases).
s0807 **Passive position** — the patient cannot change the position independently; the head and limbs hang down (coma, extreme weakness, spinal cord injury).
s0808 **Forced position** — a position the patient assumes himself or herself to relieve his or her condition (to lessen pain, dyspnea or cough).

s0880 6. Inspection of the head
s0881 The shape and size of the head are assessed. **Macrocephaly** (enlargement of the head) is seen in hydrocephalus, and **microcephaly** in developmental delay. In acromegaly, the skull bones, lower jaw, nose and ears enlarge. Rhythmic **nodding** of the head in time with the heartbeat — **de Musset’s sign** — is characteristic of aortic regurgitation. Head tremor is seen in Parkinson’s disease.

s0970 9. Inspection of the neck
s0971 On inspection of the neck, attention is paid to the following:
s0972 **Shape and symmetry** — enlargement of the thyroid gland (goiter), enlargement of the lymph nodes, tumors.
s0973 **Neck veins** — distension and pulsation (right ventricular failure, tricuspid regurgitation, pericarditis).
s0974 **Strong pulsation of the carotid arteries** — the “carotid dance” (aortic regurgitation).
s0975 **Use of the accessory muscles** — tensing of the sternocleidomastoid muscles during breathing (bronchial asthma, COPD).
s0976 **“Stokes’ collar”** — edema of the neck, face and shoulder girdle with dilated veins (compression of the superior vena cava, tumors of the clavicular region).
s0977 To assess the thyroid gland on inspection, the patient is asked to swallow a sip of water: during swallowing the gland rises together with the larynx. If a swelling in the neck moves with swallowing, it belongs to the thyroid gland; a lymph node, by contrast, hardly moves on swallowing.
