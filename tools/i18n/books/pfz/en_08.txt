s1276 Blood flow through a vessel obeys Poiseuille's law: flow is directly proportional to the pressure difference between the beginning and the end of the vessel and inversely proportional to vascular resistance. Vascular resistance is inversely proportional to the fourth power of the radius — that is, doubling the radius of an arteriole increases blood flow about 16 times.
s1277 Factors regulating the microcirculation:
s1278 **neurogenic** — sympathetic vasoconstrictor nerves (norepinephrine, alpha-adrenergic receptors) and vasodilator nerves in some regions;
s1279 **humoral** — epinephrine, angiotensin II, vasopressin (constrict); histamine, bradykinin, prostacyclin (dilate);
s1280 **local metabolic** — adenosine, CO2, H+, K+, lactate, oxygen deficiency (dilate);
s1281 **endothelial** — nitric oxide (NO) and prostacyclin (dilate), endothelin (constricts);
s1282 **myogenic** — the response of smooth muscle to stretching of the vessel wall by contraction (the Bayliss effect).

s1296 **Hyperemia** (Greek hyper — excess, haima — blood) is increased blood filling of an organ or tissue. When the inflow of blood increases, **arterial (active)** hyperemia develops; when the outflow of blood is impeded, **venous (passive, congestive)** hyperemia develops.
s1297 3. Arterial hyperemia
s1298 **Arterial hyperemia** is an increased inflow of blood through the arteries into an organ or tissue and increased blood filling as a result of arteriolar dilatation, while the outflow of blood remains normal.
s1299 Types of arterial hyperemia
s1300 **Physiological arterial hyperemia** is a normal phenomenon observed when the activity of an organ increases:
s1301 **working (functional)** — in a working muscle, in the gastrointestinal tract during digestion, in the brain during mental work;
s1302 **reflex** — reddening of the skin under the action of heat;
s1303 **emotional** — reddening of the face from embarrassment or anger.
s1304 **Pathological arterial hyperemia** develops under the action of pathogenic factors, is not related to increased activity of the organ and is disproportionate to the stimulus.

s1342 Outcomes of arterial hyperemia
s1343 **Beneficial outcomes:** the supply of the tissue with oxygen and nutrients improves, metabolism, regeneration and phagocytosis increase, and metabolites are rapidly removed. That is why arterial hyperemia is used in physiotherapy (hot compresses, UHF, mustard plasters, cups).
s1344 **Harmful outcomes:** rupture of the wall of altered (atherosclerotic, aneurysmal) vessels and hemorrhage (for example, cerebral hemorrhage in a hypertensive crisis); increased intracranial pressure (headache); relative ischemia in other organs when a large part of the blood collects in one region.
s1345 Arterial hyperemia is often observed in the clinic: redness and heat in an inflammatory focus (rubor, calor), warm and moist skin in hyperthyroidism, “hot flashes” in menopause, flushing in carcinoid syndrome. Red, warm, pulsating tissue is always a sign of increased blood inflow.

s1431 6. Experimental part
s1432 Experiment 1: neuroparalytic arterial hyperemia in the rabbit ear (C. Bernard's experiment)
s1433 **Aim:** to observe the development of arterial hyperemia when the sympathetic vasoconstrictor influence is lost.
s1434 **Equipment:** a white (albino) rabbit, surgical instruments, a local anesthetic, a thermometer (electric thermometer), a light source.
s1435 Fix the rabbit on a special table; hold both ears against the light and compare the vascular network, color and temperature.
s1436 Under local anesthesia, incise the skin and tissues on one side of the neck, find the cervical sympathetic nerve lying next to the carotid artery and transect it.
s1437 After 5–10 minutes, compare both ears: in the ear on the side of transection, the vessels dilate, the ear reddens, more small vessels become visible and the temperature rises.
s1438 Record the results in the protocol.
s1439 **Analysis:** when the cervical sympathetic nerve is transected, the constant vasoconstrictor impulses to the vessels of the ear stop, the tone of arteriolar smooth muscle decreases and the arterioles dilate — **neuroparalytic arterial hyperemia** develops.
s1440 Experiment 2: venous hyperemia in the rabbit ear
s1441 Place a rubber ring or ligature at the base of the rabbit ear (where the vessels pass) so that it compresses only the veins and not the artery.
s1442 Observe for 10–15 minutes: the ear becomes bluish, the veins dilate and fill with blood, the temperature falls and the ear swells.
s1443 Remove the ring and observe the restoration of circulation.
s1444 Experiment 3: changes in the microcirculation in the frog mesentery
s1445 Under anesthesia (or after destruction of the spinal cord), fix the frog on a cork board, incise the abdominal wall from the side, bring out an intestinal loop, spread the mesentery over the opening and place it under the microscope.
s1446 In the normal state, observe blood flow in the arterioles, capillaries and venules: in the arterioles blood flows rapidly and pulsates, in the venules slowly and evenly; red blood cells are in the axis of the vessel, and there is a plasma layer next to the wall.
s1447 **Arterial hyperemia:** apply a drop of xylene (or warm physiological saline) to the mesentery — the arterioles dilate, previously invisible capillaries open and blood flow accelerates.
s1448 **Venous hyperemia:** tie a mesenteric vein with a ligature — the venules and capillaries dilate, blood flow slows, pendulum-like movement and stasis appear, and diapedesis of red blood cells is observed.
