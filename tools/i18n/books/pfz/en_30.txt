s6157 A 24-year-old woman has frequent palpitations, trembling hands, sweating and weight loss. Pulse 118 beats/min, regular. ECG: a positive P before each QRS, PQ 0.14 s, QRS 0.08 s, RR 0.51 s. Blood thyroid hormones are raised.
s6158 Analyze the ECG. What are the type and mechanism of the arrhythmia? What is the danger for the heart?
s6159 Heart rate = 60 / 0.51 ≈ 118 beats/min; a P before each QRS, normal PQ, narrow QRS — sinus rhythm. This is sinus tachycardia (a nomotopic disturbance of automaticity). The cause is thyrotoxicosis: thyroid hormones increase the number of beta-adrenoceptors in the sinus node cells and their sensitivity to catecholamines, and act directly on ion channels to accelerate diastolic depolarization; in addition, metabolism increases. In tachycardia diastole shortens, ventricular filling and coronary blood flow decrease, and myocardial oxygen demand increases; if prolonged, atrial fibrillation and tachycardia-induced cardiomyopathy may develop.
s6160 On day 2 of myocardial infarction, a 58-year-old man's cardiac monitor shows frequent premature wide QRS complexes: they have two different shapes, sometimes come in pairs, and some fall on the T wave of the preceding complex.
s6161 What arrhythmia is this? Why is it dangerous? What is the mechanism of its development?
s6162 Multifocal, paired and early (“R on T”) ventricular extrasystoles. In the ischemic myocardium of the infarct zone and its border, the membrane potential changes, conduction slows and refractoriness becomes uneven — conditions for re-entry are created; potassium leakage from damaged cells, increased catecholamines and reperfusion enhance triggered activity. Early extrasystoles falling into the relative refractory period of the ventricles (the “vulnerable phase”) can trigger ventricular fibrillation. Therefore the patient should be under monitor observation, electrolytes (potassium, magnesium) should be corrected and a defibrillator should be ready.
s6163 A 72-year-old woman has had several attacks of sudden loss of consciousness, each lasting a few seconds and preceded by dizziness. 24-hour ECG monitoring revealed a heart rate of 38–42 beats/min, pauses of 4–5 seconds and episodes of atrial fibrillation between them.
s6164 What syndrome can be suspected? What is the mechanism of the fainting?
s6165 Sick sinus syndrome (the “tachy-brady” variant): as a result of organic damage to the sinus node (fibrosis in old age, ischemia), automaticity is reduced — persistent bradycardia and sinus arrest pauses, alternating with atrial tachyarrhythmia. During long pauses, if the escape centers do not start in time, cardiac output stops and cerebral ischemia develops — Morgagni–Adams–Stokes attacks (fainting). Treatment — implantation of a permanent pacemaker.

s6203 1. The conduction system and impulse spread
s6204 The impulse arises in the sinus node and reaches the **AV node** through the atrial myocardium and internodal pathways. In the AV node the impulse is physiologically **delayed** (about 0.1 s) — this is necessary so that atrial contraction is completed and the ventricles fill with blood. The impulse then spreads rapidly to the ventricular myocardium through the **bundle of His**, its right and left bundle branches (the left branch divides into anterior and posterior fascicles) and the **Purkinje fibers**.
s6205 **Conduction disturbances** are of two kinds:
s6206 **slowing or arrest (block)** — the impulse is delayed in some part of the conduction system or does not pass at all;
s6207 **acceleration** — the impulse reaches the ventricles prematurely via accessory (abnormal) pathways (pre-excitation syndromes).
s6208 Causes of conduction disturbances:
s6209 organic — ischemic heart disease and myocardial infarction, myocarditis (rheumatic, diphtheritic), cardiosclerosis, cardiomyopathies, heart defects, idiopathic fibrosis of the conduction system (Lenègre and Lev diseases), surgical injuries;
s6210 functional — a sharp increase in vagal tone;
s6211 toxic and drug-induced — cardiac glycosides, beta-blockers, verapamil, antiarrhythmic drugs;
s6212 electrolyte — hyperkalemia.
s6213 **Mechanism:** reduction of the resting potential of the cells (ischemia, hyperkalemia) leading to inactivation of sodium channels and reduced rate of rise and amplitude of the action potential; prolongation of the refractory period; replacement of conducting fibers by fibrosis.

s6278 Pre-excitation syndrome (Wolff–Parkinson–White, WPW)
s6279 There is an accessory conducting pathway (the **Kent bundle**) between the atria and ventricles. The impulse passes through this pathway without the physiological delay of the AV node and excites part of the ventricles early.
s6280 **ECG:** shortened PQ (below 0.12 s), a **delta wave** (sloping upstroke) at the beginning of the QRS, widened QRS, discordant ST–T changes.
s6281 **Significance:** the accessory pathway and the AV node form a closed loop — attacks of paroxysmal tachycardia by **re-entry**; if atrial fibrillation develops, impulses can pass very rapidly to the ventricles via the accessory pathway and trigger ventricular fibrillation.
s6282 5. Atrial fibrillation
s6283 **Atrial fibrillation** (“ciliary arrhythmia”) — uncoordinated, very rapid (350–700 per minute) excitation of the atria; the atria do not contract effectively, and the ventricles contract irregularly. Two types are distinguished: **atrial fibrillation** and **atrial flutter**.
s6284 **Causes:** mitral defects (especially mitral stenosis — dilation of the left atrium), ischemic heart disease, arterial hypertension, thyrotoxicosis, cardiomyopathies, myocarditis, alcohol intoxication (“holiday heart”), chronic lung diseases, old age.
s6285 Mechanism:
s6286 many small **re-entry** circuits in the atrial myocardium (the “multiple wavelet” theory) — excitation waves circulate chaotically in a dilated and fibrosed atrium;
s6287 rapid impulses from **ectopic foci** at the mouths of the pulmonary veins (trigger);
s6288 the AV node acts as a “filter” — it conducts only some of the impulses to the ventricles, which is why the ventricular rhythm is irregular.
s6289 **ECG:** no P waves; instead, small irregular **f waves** on the isoelectric line, completely irregular RR intervals, QRS usually narrow.
