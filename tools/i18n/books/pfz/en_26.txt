s5346 The general signs of anemia are related to hypoxia and compensatory reactions: weakness, fatigue, dizziness, tinnitus, “floaters” before the eyes, dyspnea, palpitations, tachycardia, pallor of the skin and mucous membranes, a functional systolic murmur at the cardiac apex (reduced blood viscosity and increased flow velocity).
s5347 In the first hours of acute blood loss, hemoglobin and hematocrit may be normal, because plasma and erythrocytes are lost in the same proportion. Relying on these values and underestimating the volume of blood loss is dangerous: at this time pulse, arterial pressure, the shock index (pulse / systolic pressure) and clinical signs are more important.
s5348 The reticulocyte count is the simplest indicator of the erythropoietic activity of the bone marrow. **Reticulocytosis** occurs after hemolysis and acute blood loss (from day 4–5); **reticulocytopenia** occurs in aplastic anemia and in iron, B12 and folate deficiency (before treatment). The “**reticulocyte crisis**” seen on day 5–7 of treatment of B12 deficiency is a sign of treatment effectiveness.
s5349 The old Sahli method (converting hemoglobin to hematin with hydrochloric acid and comparing it visually with a standard color) is subjective and of low accuracy; today the hemiglobincyanide photometric method and automated analyzers are the standard. The reagents of the hemiglobincyanide method are toxic (cyanides) — observe safety precautions when working with them.
s5350 1. Erythropoiesis and its regulation
s5351 **Erythropoiesis** — the formation of erythrocytes; in adults it takes place in the red bone marrow. s5397 3. Erythrocytoses (polyglobulias)
s5398 **Erythrocytosis** — an increase in the erythrocyte count and hemoglobin in the blood.
s5399 **Primary (polycythemia vera, Vaquez disease)** — a myeloproliferative neoplastic disease (JAK2 mutation), independent of erythropoietin; accompanied by leukocytosis and thrombocytosis.

s5462 Hereditary spherocytosis (Minkowski–Chauffard disease)
s5463 Inherited in an autosomal dominant manner. As a result of a defect in erythrocyte membrane proteins (spectrin, ankyrin), membrane is lost and the erythrocyte becomes spherical (a **spherocyte**). Spherocytes are small, lack central pallor and have low deformability; they cannot pass through the splenic sinuses and are destroyed by macrophages (extravascular hemolysis).
s5464 Signs: anemia, jaundice, splenomegaly, gallstones; microspherocytes and reticulocytosis in the blood; **decreased osmotic resistance** of erythrocytes (they hemolyze quickly in hypotonic solutions). Treatment — splenectomy (almost stops the hemolysis, but the membrane defect remains).
s5465 G6PD deficiency
s5466 Inherited in an X-linked manner. With the enzyme deficiency, little NADPH and reduced glutathione are formed in erythrocytes, and the erythrocytes become unable to withstand oxidative stress. **Hemolytic crises** develop after oxidizing drugs (primaquine, sulfonamides, nitrofurans), infections and **eating legumes (fava beans)** (favism). Heinz bodies (precipitated hemoglobin) are found in the blood.
s5467 Sickle cell anemia
s5468 A point mutation of the beta-globin gene (glutamic acid is replaced by valine at position 6) produces HbS. When oxygen falls, HbS polymerizes, the erythrocytes become sickle-shaped, block small vessels (vaso-occlusive crises, pain, infarcts) and undergo hemolysis. Heterozygous carriers are more resistant to falciparum malaria — which is why the mutation is common in areas where malaria is widespread.
s5469 **Hemolytic disease of the newborn** — when the mother is Rh-negative and the fetus Rh-positive (or with ABO incompatibility), maternal IgG antibodies cross the placenta and destroy fetal erythrocytes (a type II allergic reaction). In severe cases indirect bilirubin accumulates in the brain nuclei — **bilirubin encephalopathy (kernicterus)**. Prevention — giving anti-D immunoglobulin to the Rh-negative woman within 72 hours after delivery.
