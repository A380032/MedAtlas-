s3488 The largest share of blood buffer capacity is provided by the **hemoglobin** buffer system, but clinically the most important system, and the one that is assessed, is the **bicarbonate buffer system**, because both of its components are physiologically regulated (CO~2~ — by the lungs, HCO~3~^−^ — by the kidneys), and it is exactly this system that is measured in blood gas analysis.
s3489 Causes of high anion gap metabolic acidosis: **“MUDLES”** (a short form of the classic MUDPILES) — **M**ethanol, **U**remia (kidney failure), **D**iabetic and other ketoacidosis, **L**actic acidosis, **E**thylene glycol (antifreeze), **S**alicylates.
s3490 A rule of ABB analysis: **the primary change and the compensatory change go in the same direction** (pCO~2~ and HCO~3~^−^ both increase or both decrease). If they change in opposite directions (e.g. pCO~2~ raised, HCO~3~^−^ decreased), this indicates a **mixed disorder** (respiratory and metabolic acidosis together) — for example, in cardiac arrest.
s3491 1. Acid–base balance and its parameters
s3492 **Acid–base balance** — the relative constancy of the hydrogen ion concentration (pH) in the internal environment of the body. Arterial blood pH is kept within very narrow limits — 7.35–7.45. A pH below 6.8 or above 7.8 is incompatible with life.
s3493 Acids are constantly produced in the body: the **volatile acid** — carbon dioxide (about 15,000 mmol per day, excreted by the lungs) — and **nonvolatile (fixed) acids** — sulfuric, phosphoric, lactic and keto acids (50–100 mmol per day, excreted by the kidneys).

s3532 Role of the lungs
s3533 The respiratory center is very sensitive to pH and pCO~2~. In acidosis (increased H^+^) pulmonary ventilation increases, more CO~2~ is exhaled and pH rises; in alkalosis ventilation decreases. Pulmonary compensation starts within minutes to hours, but it is incomplete.
s3534 Role of the kidneys
s3535 The kidneys regulate ABB most powerfully, but slowly (over hours and days):
s3536 **Bicarbonate reabsorption** — almost all filtered HCO~3~^−^ is reabsorbed (85 percent of it in the proximal tubules).
s3537 **Acidogenesis** — secretion of H^+^ ions and their excretion bound to the phosphate buffer (**titratable acidity**).
s3538 **Ammoniogenesis** — ammonia (NH~3~) is formed from glutamine in the tubular cells, binds H^+^ and is excreted as ammonium (NH~4~^+^).
s3539 In these processes a new HCO~3~^−^ is formed for each H^+^ excreted and returned to the blood.
s3540 In acidosis the kidneys can excrete up to 500 mmol of acid per day, with **ammoniogenesis** playing the main role, because it can increase many times over within a few days. Therefore, in kidney failure ammonia formation decreases and metabolic acidosis inevitably develops.
s3541 Other organs
s3542 Bone tissue (in prolonged acidosis, through calcium carbonate and phosphates — risk of osteoporosis), the liver (converts lactic acid to glucose, urea synthesis) and the stomach (HCl secretion) also take part in regulating ABB.

s3549 4. Acidosis
s3550 Respiratory (gas) acidosis
s3551 **Cause** — reduced elimination of CO~2~ through the lungs (alveolar hypoventilation):
s3552 depression of the respiratory center (opiates, barbiturates, anesthesia, head injury);
s3553 damage to the respiratory muscles and neuromuscular apparatus (myasthenia, poliomyelitis, paralysis of the respiratory muscles);
s3554 diseases of the lungs and airways (chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, severe asthma attack, pulmonary edema, extensive pneumonia);
s3555 chest deformity, pneumothorax;
s3556 breathing CO~2~-rich air in a closed room.
s3557 **Compensation:** buffer systems (mainly intracellular and hemoglobin) and the kidneys — H^+^ secretion and bicarbonate reabsorption increase, and blood HCO~3~^−^ rises (in the chronic state this is fully developed in 3–5 days).
s3558 Nonrespiratory (metabolic) acidosis
s3559 **Causes** are divided into two groups according to the anion gap.
s3560 Table 11.3. Causes of metabolic acidosis
s3561 High anion gap
s3562 Excess production or intake of acids
s3563 Ketoacidosis (diabetes mellitus, starvation, alcohol), lactic acidosis (hypoxia, shock, metformin), kidney failure (retention of sulfates and phosphates), poisoning with methanol, ethylene glycol, salicylates

s3567 **Compensation:** buffer systems (HCO~3~^−^ is consumed), **lungs** — hyperventilation, CO~2~ elimination increases (deep, noisy **Kussmaul breathing** in severe ketoacidosis), **kidneys** — acidogenesis and ammoniogenesis increase (unless the kidneys themselves are the cause).
s3568 Effects of acidosis on the body
s3569 **Cardiovascular system:** myocardial contractility decreases, vessels lose their sensitivity to catecholamines (hypotension), and the risk of arrhythmias increases.
s3570 **Nervous system:** depression, weakness, drowsiness, coma in severe cases (especially in respiratory acidosis — CO~2~ crosses the blood–brain barrier rapidly; “CO~2~ narcosis”).
s3571 **Electrolytes: hyperkalemia** — H^+^ enters the cells and K^+^ leaves them; ionized calcium increases.
s3572 **Oxyhemoglobin dissociation:** the curve shifts to the right (Bohr effect) — oxygen delivery to tissues is facilitated.
s3573 **Bone:** in prolonged acidosis calcium leaves bone and osteoporosis develops.
s3574 5. Alkalosis
s3575 Respiratory (gas) alkalosis
s3576 **Cause** — alveolar hyperventilation and excessive elimination of CO~2~:
s3577 psychogenic hyperventilation (hysteria, fear, pain);
s3578 hypoxemia (altitude, lung diseases, anemia) — reflex hyperventilation;
s3579 brain injury and inflammation (stimulation of the respiratory center);
s3580 fever, early sepsis, early stage of salicylate poisoning;
s3581 liver failure, pregnancy (progesterone stimulates the respiratory center);
s3582 incorrect settings of mechanical ventilation.
s3583 **Compensation:** the kidneys reduce bicarbonate reabsorption and H^+^ secretion, and more HCO~3~^−^ is excreted in the urine.
s3584 Nonrespiratory (metabolic) alkalosis
s3585 Causes:
s3586 **loss of H^+^** — profuse vomiting (HCl is lost with gastric juice), prolonged gastric drainage through a tube;
s3587 **renal loss of H^+^ and K^+^** — thiazide and loop diuretics, hyperaldosteronism (Conn syndrome), Cushing syndrome;
s3588 **hypokalemia** — K^+^ leaves the cells, H^+^ enters them;
s3589 **excessive intake of alkali** — excessive administration of sodium bicarbonate, “milk-alkali syndrome”.
s3590 **Compensation:** the lungs — hypoventilation (CO~2~ retention), but limited by the risk of hypoxemia; the kidneys — increase bicarbonate excretion (provided there is no hypovolemia, hypokalemia or hypochloremia).
s3591 Effects of alkalosis on the body
s3592 **Increased neuromuscular excitability:** ionized calcium decreases (more of it binds to albumin) — paresthesias, muscle cramps, tetany (“obstetrician's hand”), laryngospasm.
s3593 **Brain:** in respiratory alkalosis, hypocapnia constricts the cerebral vessels — dizziness, fainting.
s3594 **Electrolytes: hypokalemia** (K^+^ enters the cells and is lost through the kidneys), hypochloremia.
s3595 **Oxyhemoglobin dissociation:** the curve shifts to the left — hemoglobin gives up oxygen to the tissues less readily.
s3596 **Heart:** arrhythmias (especially together with hypokalemia).

s3617 Experiment 1: determining titratable acidity and ammonia in urine
s3618 **Aim:** to assess the role of the kidneys in regulating ABB.
s3619 Determining titratable acidity:
s3620 Put 10 ml of urine and 1–2 drops of phenolphthalein indicator into a flask.
s3621 Titrate with 0.1 N sodium hydroxide solution until a pale pink color appears.
s3622 Record the volume of alkali used and recalculate it for the daily urine volume.
s3623 Normal titratable acidity is about 10–30 mmol per day (part of the H^+^ excreted in the urine).
s3624 Determining ammonia (formol titration method):
s3625 Add neutralized formalin solution to the titrated urine — ammonium ions react with formalin, an equivalent amount of acid is released and the pink color disappears.
s3626 Titrate again with 0.1 N sodium hydroxide until the pink color returns.
s3627 The volume of alkali used in the second titration corresponds to the amount of ammonium.
s3628 Normally about 30–50 mmol of ammonium is excreted in the urine per day.

s3638 Experiment 2: determining the alkaline reserve of blood (Van Slyke method)
s3639 **Alkaline reserve** — the amount of CO~2~ that blood plasma can bind as bicarbonate when saturated with carbon dioxide (pCO~2~ = 40 mm Hg). Normally 50–65 vol % (22–29 mmol/l).
s3640 Saturate the plasma at a CO~2~ pressure close to that of alveolar air.
s3641 In the Van Slyke apparatus, add acid to the plasma and measure the volume of CO~2~ released from bicarbonate.
s3642 Correct the result for temperature and pressure and express it in volume percent.
s3643 **Interpretation:** in metabolic acidosis the alkaline reserve decreases (bicarbonate is used up neutralizing acids), and in metabolic alkalosis it increases. In modern practice, standard bicarbonate and BE measured on a blood gas analyzer are used instead of this parameter.
s3644 Skill: determining an ABB disorder from blood gases
s3645 **Assess pH:** below 7.35 — acidosis, above 7.45 — alkalosis; if normal, there may be a compensated or mixed disorder.
s3646 **Assess pCO~2~:** if it has changed in the opposite direction to pH (pH decreased, pCO~2~ increased), the primary disorder is respiratory.
s3647 **Assess HCO~3~^−^ and BE:** if they have changed in the same direction as pH (pH decreased, HCO~3~^−^ decreased), the primary disorder is metabolic.
s3648 **Assess compensation:** has the second component changed in the same direction as the first?
s3649 **In metabolic acidosis, calculate the anion gap:** Na^+^ − (Cl^−^ + HCO~3~^−^).
