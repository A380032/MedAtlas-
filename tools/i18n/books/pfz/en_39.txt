s7747 9. Renal function tests. The Zimnitsky test
s7748 **Zimnitsky test** — a method of assessing the **concentrating and diluting** ability of the kidneys under an ordinary drinking regimen.
s7749 Procedure:
s7750 The patient follows the usual diet and drinking regimen (diuretics are stopped); the fluid intake is recorded.
s7751 At 6 a.m. the bladder is emptied (this urine is discarded).
s7752 Then urine is collected every 3 hours into separate containers: at 9, 12, 15, 18, 21, 24, 3 and 6 o'clock — 8 portions in total.
s7753 The volume and specific gravity (with a urometer) of each portion are determined.
s7754 Day (9–18) and night (21–6) diuresis and daily diuresis are calculated.
s7755 Normally:
s7756 daily diuresis is 70–80 percent of the fluid intake;
s7757 day diuresis is about 2/3 of the daily total, night diuresis — 1/3;
s7758 portion volumes vary within 40–350 ml;
s7759 the difference between the highest and lowest specific gravity is 8–10 or more (e.g. 1008–1025), and the maximum specific gravity is above 1020.

s7869 What syndrome and disease is this? Explain the pathogenesis. What is the mechanism of the edema and hypertension?
s7870 Nephritic syndrome — acute poststreptococcal glomerulonephritis. Group A streptococcal antigens and antibodies against them form immune complexes that are deposited on the glomerular basement membrane (a type III allergic reaction); complement is activated (C3 is consumed and decreases) and neutrophils accumulate and damage the capillaries. Membrane permeability increases and erythrocytes (altered, with casts — glomerular hematuria) and protein pass into the urine. Filtration falls in the inflamed glomeruli — oliguria, azotemia; because tubular sodium reabsorption is preserved, sodium and water are retained — hypervolemia: edema (on the face and eyelids — loose tissue) and hypertension (increased circulating blood volume, RAAS activation).
s7871 A 56-year-old woman has had diabetes mellitus for 20 years. Weakness, loss of appetite, nausea, skin itching, frequent urination at night. The skin is pale-yellowish and dry. BP 170/100. s7872 What condition has developed? Interpret the laboratory values. What is the mechanism of the anemia and bone disorders?
s7873 Chronic kidney failure resulting from diabetic nephropathy, uremic stage. The number of functioning nephrons is sharply reduced — azotemia (raised creatinine and urea), hyperkalemia, metabolic acidosis (reduced excretion of H^+^ and ammonium), hyperphosphatemia. Isosthenuria and nocturia in the Zimnitsky test — the remaining nephrons work in an osmotic diuresis mode and cannot concentrate the urine. Anemia — reduced erythropoietin production, the effect of uremic toxins on erythropoiesis and shortened erythrocyte lifespan. Calcitriol synthesis decreases and calcium absorption falls, phosphates are retained — hypocalcemia stimulates parathyroid hormone (secondary hyperparathyroidism) — renal osteodystrophy. Nausea and itching — the effect of uremic toxins. Hypertension — sodium and water retention, RAAS activation. The patient needs hemodialysis or kidney transplantation.
s7874 A 35-year-old man's legs were trapped for a long time in a car accident. After rescue his BP fell, his urine became brown-red, and over the following days daily diuresis was 150 ml. Blood: creatinine 480 µmol/l, K^+^ 6.9 mmol/l, CK sharply raised, myoglobinuria. ECG: tall peaked T waves.
s7875 What condition has developed? Explain the mechanism of the acute kidney failure. Which disturbance is the most life-threatening?
s7876 Acute kidney failure (renal, acute tubular necrosis) due to crush syndrome, oligoanuric stage. Myoglobin, potassium, phosphates and toxins pass from the damaged muscles into the blood (rhabdomyolysis — raised CK). Myoglobin precipitates in the tubules in an acidic environment, obstructs them and has a direct toxic effect on the epithelium; hypovolemia and shock aggravate renal ischemia (spasm of the afferent arterioles). As a result filtration falls sharply — anuria, azotemia. The most dangerous is hyperkalemia (release of potassium from muscles and failure of renal excretion): myocardial excitability and conduction are disturbed, there are tall peaked T waves on the ECG and the risk of cardiac arrest is high. Emergency measures: calcium preparations, glucose–insulin, hemodialysis.

s7910 The feedback principle is the key to determining the level of a disorder. In **primary** (peripheral gland) insufficiency the peripheral hormone is low and the tropic hormone is **high** (the inhibition is lost). In **secondary** (pituitary) insufficiency both are **low**. For example: in primary hypothyroidism T~4~ ↓, TSH ↑; in secondary hypothyroidism T~4~ ↓, TSH ↓.

s7912 1. Hierarchy of endocrine regulation
s7913 The endocrine system is organized **hierarchically**: cerebral cortex and limbic system → **hypothalamus** (liberins and statins) → **pituitary** (tropic hormones) → **peripheral glands** (adrenal cortex, thyroid, gonads) → target tissues. Glands that are independent of the pituitary (pancreatic islets, parathyroid glands, adrenal medulla) are regulated mainly by the controlled variable itself (glucose, calcium) or by the nervous system.
