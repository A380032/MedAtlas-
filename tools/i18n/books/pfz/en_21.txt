s4149 1. Factors that injure cells
s4150 **Cell injury** — a change in the structure, metabolism, physicochemical properties and function of a cell that disrupts its vital activity. Every disease is ultimately linked to cell injury.
s4151 Table 13.1. Factors that injure cells
s4152 Hypoxia (the most common cause)
s4153 Ischemia, anemia, respiratory failure, carbon monoxide poisoning
s4154 Mechanical trauma, high and low temperature, ionizing radiation, electric current
s4155 Poisons (cyanides, heavy metals), drugs (a large dose of paracetamol), alcohol, excess oxygen
s4156 Viruses, bacteria and their toxins, parasites
s4157 Immune
s4158 Autoimmune reactions, allergic reactions, cytotoxic T lymphocytes
s4159 Genetic
s4160 Enzyme defects, mutations of structural proteins, chromosomal abnormalities
s4161 Nutritional disorders
s4162 Deficiency or excess of protein, vitamins, trace elements
s4163 Injury can be **primary** (direct action of the factor on the cell) or **secondary** (the effect of changes resulting from the primary injury, e.g. hypoxia or accumulation of toxins). **Specific** changes (characteristic of a particular factor) and **nonspecific** changes (repeated in any injury) are also distinguished.
s4164 2. General mechanisms of cell injury
s4165 Figure 13.1
s4166 General mechanisms of cell injury (based on Novitskiy V.V., 2009, figure 3.15)
s4167 Disturbance of energy supply
s4168 The main source of cell energy is ATP, formed by oxidative phosphorylation in the mitochondria. Energy supply can be disturbed at the following stages:
s4169 **reduced ATP formation** — hypoxia, inhibitors of the respiratory chain (cyanides block cytochrome oxidase), uncoupling of oxidation and phosphorylation (excess thyroid hormones, 2,4-dinitrophenol), lack of substrates (hypoglycemia);
s4170 **impaired ATP transport** — damage to the creatine phosphokinase system;
s4171 **impaired ATP use** — reduced ATPase activity.
s4172 Consequences of ATP deficiency: the Na+/K+ pump stops → Na+ and water enter the cell (cell swelling) and K+ leaves; anaerobic glycolysis increases → lactate accumulates and pH falls; Ca2+ pumps stop → cytoplasmic Ca2+ rises; protein synthesis decreases (ribosomes detach).

s4187 Free-radical oxidation of lipids and antioxidant defense
s4188 **Free radicals** — highly reactive molecules with an unpaired electron in the outer orbital. The main oxygen radicals are the superoxide anion (O2−) and the hydroxyl radical (OH•); hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) and singlet oxygen are also reactive oxygen species.
s4189 Sources of radicals: the mitochondrial respiratory chain (electron “leakage”, especially during reperfusion), NADPH oxidase of phagocytes, xanthine oxidase (accumulates in ischemia), ionizing radiation, metabolism of drugs and poisons.

s4206 Disturbance of ion and water balance
s4207 Entry of Na+ and water into the cell → **cell swelling** (hydropic degeneration) and swelling of organelles;
s4208 Loss of K+ from the cell → change in membrane potential, impaired excitability;
s4209 **Rise of cytoplasmic Ca^2+^** — the “final common pathway” of cell injury.
s4210 Damage to the genetic apparatus
s4211 DNA damage (mutations, breaks, chromosomal aberrations) occurs under the action of ionizing radiation, chemical mutagens, viruses and free radicals. Consequences: impaired protein synthesis, arrest of cell division, apoptosis (via the p53 protein), tumor transformation. The cell has DNA repair systems; their hereditary defects (e.g. xeroderma pigmentosum) sharply increase the risk of tumors.
s4212 Disturbance of regulatory mechanisms
s4213 Disturbance of the systems that regulate cell activity: changes in the number and sensitivity of receptors, impaired G-protein function (e.g. cholera toxin permanently activates the Gs protein, raising cAMP in intestinal cells and causing severe secretory diarrhea), impaired function of second messengers and protein kinases.

s4233 4. Reversible and irreversible injury. Cell death
s4234 Signs of **reversible injury**: swelling of the cell and organelles, blebs on the membrane surface, detachment of ribosomes, accumulation of fat droplets (fatty degeneration). If the injuring factor is removed, the cell recovers.
s4235 Signs of **irreversible injury**: marked swelling of mitochondria with dense calcium deposits in the matrix, extensive damage to the plasma membrane, rupture of lysosomes, nuclear changes (pyknosis, karyorrhexis, karyolysis).
s4236 When the cell membrane is damaged, the enzymes and proteins inside it are released into the blood — they are used diagnostically as **markers of cell injury**: troponins and the MB fraction of creatine phosphokinase in myocardial injury, aminotransferases (ALT, AST) in the liver, creatine phosphokinase in skeletal muscle, amylase and lipase in the pancreas.
