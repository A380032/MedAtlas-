s0189 7. The doctrine of etiology
s0190 **Etiology** (Greek aitia — cause, logos — study) is the doctrine of the causes and conditions of the origin of diseases.
s0191 **The cause of disease (etiologic factor)** is the factor that produces the disease and gives it its specific features. There is no disease without a cause, and without the action of the cause the disease does not develop.
s0192 **Conditions** are factors that do not themselves produce the disease but promote or hinder the realization of the action of the cause.

s0250 8. The doctrine of pathogenesis
s0251 **Pathogenesis** (Greek pathos — disease, genesis — origin) is the doctrine of the mechanisms of development, course and resolution of diseases and pathological processes. Etiology answers the question “why?”, whereas pathogenesis answers the question “how?”.
s0252 The role of the etiologic factor in pathogenesis
s0253 The etiologic factor takes part in pathogenesis in different ways:
s0254 **it acts only at the onset of the disease** and then disappears (burns, mechanical trauma, ionizing radiation) — the disease continues on its own according to the laws of pathogenesis;
s0255 **it acts throughout the disease** (most infectious diseases, helminthic infestations) — in this case removing the cause leads to recovery.

s0283 The unity of structure and function in pathogenesis
s0284 Any functional change is based on a certain structural change — it may be at the molecular or subcellular level and may not be detectable by ordinary methods. The concept of “functional” diseases (for example, neuroses) only means that no structural change has been found with current research methods.
s0285 The role of the nervous and endocrine systems in pathogenesis
s0286 The nervous system plays a dual role in pathogenesis: it regulates protective reactions (reflexes, stress), and at the same time it can itself be the source of a pathological process (neuroses, pathological reflexes, degenerative processes). The endocrine system regulates the stress reaction (the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal system), metabolism and reactivity.

s0305 10. Practical part: pathophysiological analysis of a disease
s0306 No experiment is performed in this session; the main practical task is to learn to analyze a clinical situation from the standpoint of etiology and pathogenesis. The analysis is carried out according to the following plan.
s0307 Plan for analyzing a disease
s0308 Identify the etiologic factor (cause) and state its type (physical, chemical, biological, etc.).
s0309 Distinguish the conditions (external and internal) that promoted and those that hindered the development of the disease.
s0310 Identify the initial injury (at which level and in which structure).
s0311 Write down the cause-and-effect chain of pathogenesis in sequence.
s0312 Mark the main link and, if present, the vicious circle.
s0313 Distinguish local and systemic, specific and nonspecific changes.
s0314 State the protective-adaptive and compensatory reactions.
s0315 Determine the period of the disease and its probable outcome.
s0316 Justify the directions of etiotropic, pathogenetic and symptomatic treatment.
