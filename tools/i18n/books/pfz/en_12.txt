s2314 1. Emigration of leukocytes
s2315 **Emigration** is the exit of leukocytes from a blood vessel into the inflammatory focus. Emigration takes place mainly in **postcapillary venules**, because here blood flow is slow, the endothelium has many adhesion molecules, and the junctions between endothelial cells are looser.

s2335 **Leukocyte adhesion deficiency** is a rare disease associated with a hereditary defect of integrins (CD18). In such children leukocytes cannot leave the vessels: there are many neutrophils in the blood, but no pus is formed, the umbilical stump separates late, and recurrent severe bacterial infections are observed. This example clearly shows the protective significance of emigration.
s2336 Sequence of emigration
s2337 As I.I. Mechnikov established, in acute inflammation leukocytes leave the vessels in a certain order:
s2338 **Neutrophils** predominate in the first 6–24 hours (the most mobile, numerous and fast-responding).
s2339 **Monocytes (which turn into macrophages)** predominate after 24–48 hours.
s2340 **Lymphocytes** come later, especially in chronic and immune inflammation.
s2341 This sequence is not always maintained: in viral infections lymphocytes, and in allergic inflammation and helminthic infestations eosinophils, predominate early.

s2367 3. Phagocytosis
s2368 **Phagocytosis** (Greek phagein — to devour, kytos — cell) is the active engulfment and digestion of foreign particles, microorganisms and dead cells by specialized cells (phagocytes). Phagocytosis was discovered by I.I. Mechnikov in 1883, who regarded it as the main protective mechanism of inflammation.
s2369 Phagocytes:
s2370 **microphages** — neutrophil granulocytes (mainly phagocytose bacteria);
s2371 **macrophages** — monocytes and tissue macrophages (histiocytes, Kupffer cells, alveolar macrophages, microglia, osteoclasts); they engulf large particles and dead cells and present antigen.

s2385 Mechanisms of killing microbes
s2386 **Oxygen-dependent mechanism (“respiratory burst”):** oxygen consumption rises sharply during phagocytosis; NADPH oxidase forms the superoxide anion (O2−), from which hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) and the hydroxyl radical arise; myeloperoxidase forms a very powerful bactericidal substance, hypochlorous acid (HOCl), from H2O2 and chloride ion.
s2387 **Oxygen-independent mechanism:** lysozyme (breaks down the bacterial wall), defensins (form pores in the membrane), lactoferrin (binds iron and stops bacterial growth), cationic proteins, proteases, the acidic environment in the phagolysosome.
s2388 Completeness of phagocytosis
s2389 **Complete phagocytosis** — the engulfed microbe is killed and fully digested.
s2390 **Incomplete phagocytosis** — the engulfed microbe does not die; it survives and even multiplies inside the phagocyte (Mycobacterium tuberculosis, gonococcus, Brucella, Leishmania). In this case the phagocyte may spread the microbe throughout the organism.

s2406 4. Proliferation and repair
s2407 **Proliferation** is the multiplication of cells in the inflammatory focus; it is the final stage of inflammation and ensures the restoration of the injured tissue.
s2408 Proliferation mainly involves **local stem (cambial) cells** (fibroblasts, endothelium, epithelium) and monocytes arriving from the blood. Macrophages release growth factors: platelet-derived growth factor (PDGF), fibroblast growth factor (FGF), transforming growth factor beta (TGF-beta) and vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF).
s2409 **Granulation tissue** is young connective tissue consisting of newly formed capillaries, fibroblasts and inflammatory cells. It fills the bottom of the wound, and later, as collagen fibers increase, it turns into a scar.

s2432 6. Outcomes of inflammation and chronic inflammation
s2433 Outcomes of acute inflammation:
s2434 **complete resolution** — the phlogogenic factor disappears, the exudate is absorbed and the tissue is restored;
s2435 **scar formation (fibrosis)** — in extensive injury;
s2436 **abscess formation** — a delimited collection of pus in purulent infection;
s2437 **transition to chronic inflammation** — if the phlogogenic factor persists.
s2438 **Chronic inflammation** is long-lasting inflammation in which alteration, inflammation and repair proceed simultaneously. Macrophages, lymphocytes and plasma cells predominate in its cellular composition; fibrosis and angiogenesis are marked. Causes: microbes that are difficult to kill (Mycobacterium tuberculosis, fungi), long-acting exogenous substances (silica dust — silicosis), autoimmune reactions.
s2439 **Granulomatous inflammation** is a special type of chronic inflammation in which **granulomas** consisting of activated macrophages (epithelioid cells) and multinucleated giant cells are formed (tuberculosis, sarcoidosis, around foreign bodies).
