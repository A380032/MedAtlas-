s6501 2. Compensatory mechanisms of the heart
s6502 Cardiac compensatory mechanisms
s6503 Rapid (emergency) mechanisms:
s6504 **Heterometric mechanism (Frank–Starling law)** — if myocardial fibers are stretched more in diastole (increased preload), the next contraction is stronger. It is effective up to a certain limit; with excessive stretching (myogenic dilation) the force of contraction decreases.
s6505 **Homeometric mechanism (Anrep phenomenon)** — without a change in fiber length, the force of contraction gradually increases when aortic resistance rises (through greater release of calcium ions).
s6506 **Tachycardia** — resulting from sympathetic influence and the Bainbridge reflex (stretching of the venae cavae and right atrium). If the rate rises excessively, diastole shortens, filling and coronary blood flow decrease, and the effect is lost.
s6507 **Inotropic effect** — catecholamines increase myocardial contractility.
s6508 **Long-term mechanism — myocardial hypertrophy.** With constant overload, cardiomyocyte size (hypertrophy) and heart mass increase; this normalizes the work and wall stress per unit mass.
s6509 **Concentric hypertrophy** — with pressure overload (aortic stenosis, hypertension): the wall thickens, the cavity is unchanged or smaller (sarcomeres are added in parallel).
s6510 **Eccentric hypertrophy** — with volume overload (valvular insufficiency): the cavity dilates and the wall thickens relatively (sarcomeres are added in series).

s6513 **Cardiac reserve mechanisms of compensation** — increases in heart rate, stroke volume and oxygen extraction; athletes show physiological hypertrophy (balanced growth).
s6514 Extracardiac compensatory mechanisms
s6515 activation of the **sympathoadrenal system** — tachycardia, inotropic effect, constriction of arterioles (maintaining pressure), increased venous tone (increasing venous return), redistribution of blood in favor of vital organs;
s6516 **renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system** — vasoconstriction, sodium and water retention, increased blood volume;
s6517 **antidiuretic hormone** — water retention;
s6518 **natriuretic peptides** (atrial and brain natriuretic peptides) — released from the stretched atria and ventricles, they enhance natriuresis and vasodilation (a counter-regulatory system; their blood level is used in the diagnosis of heart failure);
s6519 **increased erythropoiesis** (due to hypoxia), increased oxygen extraction in the tissues, increased anaerobic glycolysis.

s6521 Neurohumoral activation and the vicious circle in heart failure
s6522 At first, neurohumoral mechanisms maintain blood pressure and perfusion of vital organs, but with prolonged activation they increase preload and afterload on the heart and have a direct harmful effect on the myocardium (remodeling, fibrosis, apoptosis) — a **vicious circle** forms. This is why the basis of treatment of chronic heart failure is drugs that block these systems (ACE inhibitors, angiotensin receptor blockers, beta-blockers, aldosterone antagonists).

s6540 4. Heart defects
s6541 **Heart defect** — a permanent change in the structure of the heart valves, septa or great vessels that leads to disturbed blood flow. Defects are **congenital** (atrial and ventricular septal defects, patent ductus arteriosus, coarctation of the aorta, tetralogy of Fallot) and **acquired** (mainly rheumatic fever, infective endocarditis, atherosclerosis, degenerative changes).
s6542 Two types of valvular defect:
s6543 **stenosis (narrowing)** — the orifice narrows and resistance to blood flow increases → the preceding chamber is overloaded **by pressure** (concentric hypertrophy);
s6544 **insufficiency** — the cusps do not close completely and part of the blood flows back (regurgitation) → the receiving chambers are overloaded **by volume** (eccentric hypertrophy and dilation).

s6571 5. Coronary insufficiency and myocardial infarction
s6572 **Coronary insufficiency** — a mismatch between the oxygen demand of the myocardium and the oxygen delivered by coronary blood flow.
s6573 reduced coronary blood flow — atherosclerosis of the coronary arteries (the main cause), spasm, thrombosis, embolism, fall in diastolic pressure (aortic insufficiency, hypotension), shortening of diastole in tachycardia;
s6574 increased myocardial oxygen demand — physical and emotional stress (catecholamines), hypertrophy, tachycardia, thyrotoxicosis;
s6575 reduced oxygen-carrying capacity of the blood — anemia, hypoxemia, carbon monoxide poisoning.
s6576 **Consequences of myocardial ischemia:** within seconds aerobic oxidation stops and is replaced by anaerobic glycolysis, lactate accumulates, ATP falls — contractility decreases; ion pumps fail, intracellular Na+ and Ca2+ increase and K+ leaves — electrical instability and arrhythmias; after 20–40 minutes irreversible injury — **necrosis (infarction)**. Pain arises from the action of ischemic metabolites (adenosine, lactate, bradykinin, K+).

s6592 6. Arterial hypertension and hypotension
s6593 **Arterial hypertension** — a persistent arterial pressure of 140/90 mm Hg or higher. Arterial pressure = cardiac output × total peripheral resistance.
s6594 **Primary (essential) hypertension** — 90–95 percent of cases; hereditary predisposition, high salt intake, stress, obesity, insulin resistance, reduced renal sodium excretion, increased sympathetic and RAAS activity, endothelial dysfunction (decreased NO, increased endothelin).
s6595 **Secondary (symptomatic) hypertension** — renal (glomerulonephritis, renal artery stenosis — renovascular), endocrine (primary hyperaldosteronism, Cushing syndrome, pheochromocytoma, thyrotoxicosis), hemodynamic (coarctation of the aorta), neurogenic, drug-induced.
s6596 **Target organ damage:** heart (left ventricular hypertrophy, ischemic heart disease, heart failure), brain (stroke, encephalopathy), kidney (nephrosclerosis), retina (retinopathy), vessels (atherosclerosis, aortic aneurysm).
s6597 **Arterial hypotension** — pressure below 100/60 (95/60 in women) mm Hg: physiological (athletes), acute (shock, collapse, blood loss), chronic (adrenal insufficiency, hypothyroidism, neurocirculatory dystonia), **orthostatic** (a sharp fall in pressure on standing — autonomic nervous system disorders, hypovolemia, drugs).
s6598 **Collapse** — acute vascular insufficiency resulting from an acute fall in vascular tone and a decrease in circulating blood volume: arterial and venous pressure fall, with hypoxia of the brain, heart and other organs.
s6599 7. Pericardial pathology
s6600 **Pericardial tamponade** — rapid accumulation of fluid (blood, exudate) in the pericardial cavity, compressing the heart. The ventricles cannot expand in diastole — filling and stroke volume decrease sharply and venous pressure rises. **Beck's triad:** arterial hypotension, distended neck veins, muffled heart sounds. **Pulsus paradoxus** — a fall in systolic pressure of more than 10 mm Hg on inspiration. Care — emergency pericardiocentesis.
