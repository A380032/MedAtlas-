s6309 Consequences of atrial fibrillation
s6310 **Loss of atrial systole** — ventricular filling decreases by about 20–30 percent; cardiac output falls significantly, especially in mitral stenosis and myocardial hypertrophy.
s6311 **Tachysystole** — diastole shortens, the ventricles do not fill adequately, coronary blood flow decreases and myocardial oxygen demand increases; if prolonged — heart failure.
s6312 **Pulse deficit** — contractions with a small stroke volume do not produce a pulse wave at the periphery: the number of contractions at the cardiac apex exceeds the radial pulse.
s6313 **Thromboembolism** — blood stagnates in the non-contracting atrium (especially the left atrial appendage) and a thrombus forms; a fragment of the thrombus enters the systemic circulation and causes **ischemic stroke** and embolism of the renal, intestinal and leg arteries.
s6314 Ventricular fibrillation and flutter
s6315 **Ventricular fibrillation** — chaotic, uncoordinated excitation and contraction of individual fibers of the ventricular myocardium; the pumping function of the heart stops — this is the most common mechanism of **clinical death**. The ECG shows chaotic waves of varying shape and amplitude, with no distinguishable QRS or T. **Ventricular flutter** — regular, high-amplitude “sinusoidal” waves (200–300 per minute), which quickly turn into fibrillation.
s6316 **Causes:** acute myocardial infarction and ischemia, electric shock, hyper- and hypokalemia, glycoside toxicity, long QT syndrome, hypothermia, severe hypoxia.
s6317 **Care:** immediate cardiopulmonary resuscitation and **electrical defibrillation** — a strong electrical impulse depolarizes all myocardial cells simultaneously, after which the sinus node may restore the rhythm.

s6430 A 66-year-old man has had several sudden losses of consciousness; when he fell his face turned pale, then convulsions occurred, and he came round after 20–30 seconds. Pulse 34 beats/min, regular. ECG: P waves 76 per minute, QRS 34 per minute, wide (0.14 s), no constant relationship between P and QRS.
s6431 What is the ECG conclusion? Explain the mechanism of the fainting attacks. What treatment is needed?
s6432 Third-degree (complete) AV block; the escape rhythm is ventricular (wide QRS, 34 beats/min — idioventricular rhythm). The atria are excited by the sinus node and the ventricles independently by their own center (AV dissociation). When the block appears or deepens, the ventricular center starts a few seconds late; during this time cardiac output stops and cerebral ischemia develops — Morgagni–Adams–Stokes attacks (loss of consciousness, then hypoxic convulsions). Persistent bradycardia reduces cardiac output and leads to heart failure. Treatment — a permanent pacemaker.

s6472 The hypertrophied myocardium is “imperfect” — **unbalanced growth**: the mass of cardiomyocytes increases, but the number of capillaries, nerve endings, mitochondria and the sarcoplasmic reticulum lag behind the growth of the myocardium. s6474 1. Circulatory failure and its forms
s6475 **Circulatory failure** — a state in which the cardiovascular system is unable to supply tissues and organs with blood in accordance with their needs. It develops for cardiac, vascular or mixed reasons.
s6476 **Heart failure** — a clinical syndrome in which, because of impaired pumping function of the heart, cardiac output cannot meet the needs of the tissues, or can do so only at the cost of increased ventricular filling pressure.
