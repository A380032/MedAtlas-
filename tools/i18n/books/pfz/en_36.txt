s7244 A 32-year-old man has had epigastric pain on an empty stomach and at night for 3 years; the pain decreases after eating; heartburn, sour belching, constipation. He smokes heavily. Endoscopy: an ulcer in the duodenal bulb, positive urease test. Gastric juice: acidity increased.
s7245 Which aggressive and protective factors were involved in the development of the ulcer? Explain the constipation and the reduction of pain after eating.
s7246 Duodenal ulcer. Aggressive factors: Helicobacter pylori (antral gastritis, loss of D cells → decreased somatostatin and increased gastrin → hyperacidity), hypersecretion of hydrochloric acid and pepsin, smoking (reduces prostaglandin and bicarbonate secretion and worsens blood supply). The protective factors (mucus–bicarbonate barrier, blood supply) are weakened. On an empty stomach, highly acidic contents enter the duodenum and cause pain; food neutralizes the acid — the pain decreases. Hyperacidity causes pyloric spasm and reflex slowing of intestinal motility — constipation. Treatment — eradication of Helicobacter pylori and reduction of acid secretion.
s7247 A 4-year-old child at a kindergarten has contracted cholera: watery stools resembling “rice water” 15–20 times a day and vomiting. The skin is dry, the skin fold returns slowly, the eyeballs are sunken, urine is scanty, BP is decreased, the pulse rapid and weak. Blood: hematocrit raised, K^+^ decreased, pH 7.26, bicarbonate decreased.
s7248 Explain the mechanism of the diarrhea. Interpret the water–electrolyte and acid–base disturbances. Why is this condition especially dangerous in children?
s7249 Cholera toxin permanently activates adenylate cyclase in the intestinal epithelium and raises cAMP — chloride and bicarbonate ions, and water with them, are secreted into the intestinal lumen in large amounts and sodium absorption decreases — secretory diarrhea (the mucosa is not damaged, which is why there is no blood in the stool). The result is isotonic dehydration (hypovolemia — raised hematocrit, oliguria, hypotension, hypovolemic shock), potassium loss (hypokalemia), and metabolic acidosis due to loss of bicarbonate and lactate accumulation with tissue hypoperfusion. In children the proportion of body water and of extracellular fluid is higher, water turnover is faster and the concentrating ability of the kidneys is lower — dehydration and shock develop very quickly. The main treatment is oral or intravenous administration of water–electrolyte solutions (rehydration).
s7250 After a festive meal (much fatty food, alcohol), a 48-year-old man developed severe girdle-like epigastric pain and repeated vomiting. BP 90/60 mm Hg, pulse 120 beats/min, bluish patches on the abdominal skin. Blood amylase and lipase are sharply raised, calcium decreased, leukocytosis.
s7251 What is the disease? Explain its pathogenesis. By what mechanism did the hypotension and hypocalcemia develop?
s7252 Acute pancreatitis. Alcohol and fatty food stimulate the gland's secretion, cause spasm of the sphincter of Oddi and increased duct pressure, and premature activation of trypsinogen in the acinar cells — trypsin activates the other enzymes and self-digestion of the gland (autolysis) begins. Lipase and phospholipase A~2~ cause fat necrosis; in the necrotic foci fatty acids form soaps with calcium — hypocalcemia (a sign of a severe course). Elastase damages vessel walls and causes hemorrhages (the patches on the abdominal skin). Activation of the kallikrein–kinin system leads to vasodilation and increased vascular permeability, accumulation of fluid in the abdominal cavity and retroperitoneal tissue, and water loss with vomiting — hypovolemia, which together with pain and enzymemia causes shock (hypotension, tachycardia). The enzymes pass into the blood (raised amylase and lipase) and may damage the lungs and kidneys.

s7320 2. Liver failure
s7321 **Liver failure** — a condition with impairment of one or more functions of the liver.
s7322 **infectious** — viral hepatitis (B, C, D, A, E), leptospirosis, echinococcosis, alveococcosis, opisthorchiasis;
s7323 **toxic** — alcohol, hepatotropic poisons (carbon tetrachloride, phosphorus, poisonous mushrooms — death cap), drugs (a large dose of paracetamol, antituberculosis and cytostatic drugs);
s7324 **circulatory disturbance** — chronic heart failure (“nutmeg liver”), shock, hepatic vein thrombosis (Budd–Chiari syndrome);
s7325 **impaired bile outflow** — gallstones, tumors (secondary biliary cirrhosis);
s7326 **hereditary metabolic disorders** — hemochromatosis (iron), Wilson–Konovalov disease (copper), alpha1-antitrypsin deficiency;
s7327 **autoimmune** — autoimmune hepatitis, primary biliary cholangitis;
s7328 **tumors** — hepatocellular carcinoma, metastases;
s7329 **nutritional** — protein deficiency, obesity (fatty hepatosis).

s7340 3. Hepatic coma (hepatic encephalopathy)
s7341 **Hepatic encephalopathy** — impaired function of the central nervous system under the action of toxic substances that are not neutralized in liver failure; its severe degree is **hepatic coma**.
s7342 Types:
s7343 **endogenous (hepatocellular)** — extensive necrosis of the liver parenchyma (fulminant hepatitis, poisoning);
s7344 **exogenous (portosystemic, bypass)** — in portal hypertension, blood from the intestine bypasses the liver through portocaval anastomoses and enters the systemic circulation directly;
s7345 **mixed**.
s7346 Pathogenesis:
s7347 **Ammonia** — formed mainly from the breakdown of proteins by bacteria in the intestine; it must be converted to urea in the liver. In the brain, excess ammonia binds alpha-ketoglutarate to form glutamate and then glutamine — **alpha-ketoglutarate in the Krebs cycle decreases and ATP formation falls**; glutamine accumulates in astrocytes and causes their swelling (cerebral edema); GABA-ergic inhibition increases.
s7348 **False neurotransmitters** — aromatic amino acids (phenylalanine, tyrosine, tryptophan) increase and branched-chain amino acids (valine, leucine, isoleucine) decrease; **octopamine** and **phenylethylamine** are formed in the brain and replace the true transmitters (dopamine, norepinephrine).
s7349 Other toxins — mercaptans (“fetor hepaticus” from the mouth), short-chain fatty acids, phenols, endogenous benzodiazepines.
s7350 Additional factors — hypoglycemia, hypokalemia, alkalosis (ammonia passes into the brain easily), hypoxia.
s7351 Factors that precipitate hepatic encephalopathy: gastrointestinal bleeding (blood in the intestine is a source of protein — ammonia increases), a high-protein diet, constipation, infections, diuretics (hypokalemia and alkalosis), sedatives, alcohol. Clinical signs: disturbed sleep rhythm, behavioral changes, “flapping” tremor (asterixis), stupor, coma.

s7366 Pathogenesis of ascites
s7367 Portal hypertension — hydrostatic pressure in the hepatic sinusoids and intestinal capillaries rises and fluid passes into the abdominal cavity; hepatic lymph increases.
s7368 Hypoalbuminemia — oncotic pressure decreases.
s7369 Dilation of the visceral arteries (increased NO) — the effective blood volume decreases → **activation of RAAS, the sympathetic system and ADH** → sodium and water are retained by the kidneys; aldosterone inactivation in the liver decreases (secondary hyperaldosteronism).
s7370 **Liver cirrhosis** — a chronic diffuse disease of the liver characterized by death of hepatocytes, fibrosis and replacement of the normal architecture by **regenerative nodules**. s7371 Fibrosis develops as a result of activation of hepatic stellate cells (Ito cells) and their production of collagen. Cirrhosis combines the syndromes of liver failure and portal hypertension.
