s7589 **Mechanisms of oliguria in acute tubular necrosis:** spasm of the afferent arterioles (RAAS, tubuloglomerular feedback — the macula densa senses an increased sodium load and reduces filtration), obstruction of the tubules by dead cells and casts (intratubular pressure rises and filtration falls), back-leak of filtrate through the damaged tubular wall into the blood, interstitial edema.
s7590 Other functional tests: the **Rehberg–Tareev test** (determining GFR and tubular reabsorption from endogenous creatinine clearance), the **Nechiporenko test** (number of leukocytes, erythrocytes and casts in 1 ml of urine), the concentration test (dry diet) and the dilution test (water load). In modern practice GFR is usually calculated from blood creatinine using formulas (CKD-EPI).
s7591 1. Renal functions and their assessment
s7592 **Main functions of the kidneys:** excretory (excretion of nitrogenous waste, drugs and toxins), homeostatic (water–electrolyte balance, osmotic pressure, acid–base balance), endocrine (renin, erythropoietin, calcitriol — the active form of vitamin D, prostaglandins, kinins), metabolic (gluconeogenesis, breakdown of protein hormones).
s7593 **Urine formation** consists of three processes:
s7594 **Glomerular ultrafiltration** — primary urine is filtered from the capillaries into Bowman's capsule (about 180 l per day). Effective filtration pressure = capillary hydrostatic pressure – (plasma oncotic pressure + intracapsular pressure) ≈ 70 – (30 + 20) = 20 mm Hg.
s7595 **Tubular reabsorption** — 99 percent of the water and solutes are returned to the blood.
s7596 **Tubular secretion** — certain substances (H^+^, K^+^, ammonia, drugs) are transferred from the blood into the urine.
s7597 **Clearance** — the volume of plasma cleared of a given substance by the kidneys in 1 minute: C = (U × V) / P, where U is the urine concentration of the substance, V is the minute diuresis and P is the plasma concentration.
s7598 The clearance of a substance that is only filtered and neither reabsorbed nor secreted (inulin; in practice **endogenous creatinine** — the Rehberg–Tareev test) equals the **glomerular filtration rate (GFR)**.

s7675 Pathological changes in urine composition:
s7676 **Proteinuria** — protein in the urine: **glomerular** (increased membrane permeability; selective — mainly albumin, nonselective — larger proteins as well), **tubular** (impaired reabsorption of low-molecular-weight proteins), **overflow** (increased small proteins in the blood — Bence Jones protein in myeloma), **postrenal** (inflammation of the urinary tract). s7680 **Glucosuria** — when blood glucose exceeds the renal threshold (about 8.9–10 mmol/l) or in a tubular defect.
s7681 5. Extrarenal signs
s7682 **Nephrotic edema** — massive proteinuria → hypoalbuminemia → oncotic pressure falls → fluid moves into the tissues → circulating blood volume decreases → RAAS and ADH are activated → sodium and water are retained. The edema is large and soft, starting on the face and progressing to anasarca.
s7683 **Nephritic edema** — reduced filtration and primary retention of sodium and water (hypervolemia), increased capillary permeability. The edema is on the face and eyelids, more in the morning.
s7684 Renal hypertension
s7685 **Renovascular** — renal ischemia → increased renin → angiotensin II (vasoconstriction) and aldosterone (sodium and water retention).
s7686 **Renoparenchymal** — sodium and water retention (hypervolemia), RAAS activation, reduction of the renal depressor systems (prostaglandins, kinins).
s7687 Renal anemia
s7688 Reduced **erythropoietin** production as renal parenchyma decreases; additional factors — the effect of uremic toxins on the bone marrow, shortened erythrocyte lifespan, bleeding.
s7689 Renal osteodystrophy
s7690 Reduced calcitriol synthesis (hypocalcemia) and phosphate retention (hyperphosphatemia) → increased parathyroid hormone (**secondary hyperparathyroidism**) → release of calcium from bones, osteomalacia, osteoporosis.

s7714 7. Kidney failure
s7715 Acute kidney failure (acute kidney injury)
s7716 **Acute kidney failure** — a sudden (within hours or days) sharp decline in renal function, characterized by a rise in blood creatinine and (or) a decrease in diuresis.
s7717 Figure 25.1
s7718 Types of acute kidney failure
s7719 Stages:
s7720 **Initial** — action of the cause (shock, poisoning), hours or days.
s7721 **Oligoanuric** (1–3 weeks) — oliguria or anuria; **azotemia**, **hyperkalemia** (risk of arrhythmia and cardiac arrest — the most dangerous complication), metabolic acidosis, hyperhydration (pulmonary and cerebral edema), hyperphosphatemia, hypocalcemia, uremic intoxication.
s7722 **Polyuric (recovery of diuresis)** — filtration recovers, but the tubular epithelium is still immature, reabsorption and concentration are low — polyuria, hyposthenuria; risk of dehydration, **hypokalemia** and hyponatremia.
s7723 **Recovery** — from several months to a year.
s7724 Chronic kidney failure (chronic kidney disease)
s7725 **Chronic kidney failure** — a progressive decline in renal function resulting from a gradual, irreversible reduction in the number of functioning nephrons (GFR below 60 ml/min or signs of kidney damage for more than 3 months).
s7726 **Causes:** diabetes mellitus (diabetic nephropathy), arterial hypertension (nephrosclerosis), chronic glomerulonephritis, chronic pyelonephritis, polycystic disease, urinary tract obstruction, amyloidosis.
s7727 **Pathogenesis:** as nephrons are lost, the remaining nephrons work in a hyperfiltration mode (**adaptive hyperfiltration**) — glomerular pressure rises and they gradually become sclerotic (**progressive nephrosclerosis**) — a “vicious circle”. Proteinuria and angiotensin II enhance interstitial fibrosis.
s7728 **Stages (by GFR):** latent (GFR 90–60) → compensated → polyuria, nocturia, isosthenuria (GFR 60–30; osmotic diuresis in the surviving nephrons) → increasing azotemia (GFR 30–15) → **terminal (uremic)** stage (GFR below 15) — oliguria; hemodialysis or kidney transplantation is required.
s7729 Pathogenesis of uremia
s7730 **Uremia** — poisoning of the body by metabolic products in end-stage kidney failure:
s7731 **uremic toxins** — urea, creatinine, guanidines, phenols, indole, “middle molecules”, parathyroid hormone (one of the most important “uremic toxins”);
s7732 **nervous system** — uremic encephalopathy (weakness, drowsiness, coma), polyneuropathy;
s7733 **cardiovascular** — hypertension, uremic pericarditis (pericardial friction rub), heart failure;
s7734 **respiratory** — Kussmaul breathing (acidosis), uremic pneumonitis, ammonia odor from the mouth;
s7735 **digestive** — uremic gastroenteritis (urea is broken down to ammonia in the intestine and irritates the mucosa), nausea, vomiting;
s7736 **blood** — anemia, platelet dysfunction (bleeding), immunodeficiency;
s7737 **skin** — dryness, itching, “uremic frost” (urea crystals excreted through the skin);
s7738 **bone** — renal osteodystrophy;
s7739 **water–electrolyte** — hyperkalemia, metabolic acidosis, hyperphosphatemia, hypocalcemia.
s7740 8. Features of renal disorders in children
s7741 In infants GFR and concentrating ability are low (they mature by 1–2 years) — they are sensitive to dehydration and to excessive fluid administration.
s7742 Tubular bicarbonate reabsorption and H^+^ secretion are low — a tendency to metabolic acidosis.
