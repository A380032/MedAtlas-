s0966 4. Biological barriers
s0967 **External barriers:** the skin (mechanical protection, acidic environment, bactericidal action of fatty acids, desquamation), mucous membranes (mucus, ciliated epithelium, lysozyme, secretory IgA), the acidic environment of gastric juice, and the normal microflora (competition with pathogenic microbes).
s0968 **Internal (blood-tissue) barriers:** the blood-brain, blood-ocular, blood-testis and blood-placental barriers, lymph nodes, and the liver (detoxifies toxic substances coming from the intestine).
s0969 5. The role of the nervous system in reactivity
s0970 The nervous system is the main system that shapes and regulates reactivity. Its state sharply changes the organism's response to pathogenic influences.
s0971 **In ontogenesis:** in newborns the nervous system is immature, so their fever and inflammatory reactions are weak and infection rapidly becomes generalized.
s0972 **Inhibition of the central nervous system** (sleep, anesthesia, hibernation) reduces metabolism and oxygen demand — resistance to hypoxia and certain poisons increases.
s0973 **Excitation of the central nervous system** (stress, caffeine, amphetamine, hyperthyroidism) increases metabolism — resistance to hypoxia decreases.
s0974 **Injury of the nervous system** (spinal cord transection, denervation) leads to degenerative changes in tissues and impaired inflammation and regeneration.

s0996 6. The role of the endocrine system in reactivity
s0997 The endocrine system regulates reactivity together with the nervous system.
s0998 **The hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenocortical system** provides the stress reaction. According to H. Selye's doctrine, the organism responds to strong stimuli with the **general adaptation syndrome**: the alarm stage, the stage of resistance and the stage of exhaustion. Glucocorticoids suppress inflammatory and allergic reactions, but in excess they lower immunity.
s0999 **Thyroid hormones** increase metabolism: in hyperthyroidism resistance to hypoxia decreases, whereas in hypothyroidism reactivity is weakened in general.
s1000 **Insulin deficiency** (diabetes mellitus) weakens phagocytosis and regeneration — susceptibility to purulent infections increases and wounds heal slowly.
s1001 **Sex hormones** determine sex differences in reactivity.

s1084 9. The effect of age and sex on reactivity
s1085 **Infancy and early childhood:** the nervous, endocrine and immune systems are immature, and the biological barriers are weak. Inflammatory and febrile reactions are weak, and infections quickly become generalized (predisposition to sepsis). At the same time, newborns are more resistant to hypoxia than older people (high activity of anaerobic glycolysis; fetal hemoglobin binds oxygen well).
s1086 **Maturity:** reactivity is at its highest level.
s1087 **Old age:** the immune response weakens, barriers become weaker, and the reserve capacity of organs decreases; diseases often run a hypoergic, asymptomatic course; neoplastic diseases become more frequent.
s1088 **Sex:** the female organism is more resistant to blood loss, hypoxia and starvation; autoimmune diseases are more common in women, whereas ischemic heart disease occurs more often at an early age in men (the protective effect of estrogens). In women, reactivity changes during the menstrual cycle, pregnancy and menopause.
s1089 10. Experimental part: development of hypoxic hypoxia in different states of the central nervous system
s1090 **Aim:** to observe how an internal condition — the functional state of the central nervous system — changes the development and outcome of hypoxia.
s1091 **Equipment:** white mice (3, of the same weight), a Komovsky pressure chamber and a manometer, a solution of a narcotic agent (the drug and dose adopted at the department, for example urethane), 1% caffeine solution, physiological saline, syringes, a stopwatch.

s1104 **The mouse in narcotic sleep** is the most resistant to hypoxia: the signs of hypoxia appear at a much higher “altitude”, and convulsions are weak or absent. The reason is that the central nervous system is inhibited, and metabolism and the oxygen demand of neurons are reduced.
s1105 **The mouse given caffeine** is the least resistant to hypoxia: the signs appear at a lower “altitude” and sooner. The reason is that excited neurons and muscles consume much oxygen.
s1106 **Conclusion:** the same cause (a decreased partial pressure of oxygen in the air) gives different results in different states of the central nervous system. Inhibition of the nervous system lowers reactivity but increases resistance to hypoxia, whereas excitation does the opposite. This confirms the role of internal conditions in the development of a pathological process.
