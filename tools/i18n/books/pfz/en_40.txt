s7947 Extraglandular (peripheral)

s7950 4. Disorders of the hypothalamic–pituitary system
s7951 Hyperfunction of the adenohypophysis
s7952 **Excess somatotropin (growth hormone)** (usually a somatotroph adenoma) before closure of the bone growth plates (childhood and adolescence) — **gigantism** (height above 200 cm in men, above 190 cm in women).
s7953 **Excess growth hormone after closure of the growth plates** — **acromegaly**: the hands, feet, nose, lower jaw, ears and tongue enlarge, the internal organs enlarge (splanchnomegaly), and the facial features coarsen; counter-insulin effect — **hyperglycemia** (secondary diabetes), arterial hypertension, cardiomyopathy. The effects are mediated mainly by **IGF-1**, produced in the liver.
s7954 **Excess ACTH** — **Cushing disease** (pituitary adenoma): secondary hypercortisolism.
s7955 **Excess prolactin** (prolactinoma) — in women galactorrhea, amenorrhea, infertility; in men impotence, gynecomastia (prolactin inhibits GnRH).
s7956 Hypofunction of the adenohypophysis
s7957 **Pituitary dwarfism** — growth hormone deficiency in childhood: marked growth retardation, but body proportions are preserved and mental development is normal (unlike hypothyroid dwarfism); sexual development is also often delayed.
s7958 **Panhypopituitarism** — deficiency of all hormones of the adenohypophysis: tumors, trauma, surgery, radiation, postpartum pituitary necrosis (**Sheehan syndrome** — ischemia of the pituitary, enlarged during pregnancy, due to heavy blood loss at delivery), **Simmonds disease** (pituitary cachexia). Signs: secondary hypothyroidism, secondary adrenal insufficiency, hypogonadism (amenorrhea, loss of libido, loss of body hair), failure of lactation, weakness, hypoglycemia, hypotension.
s7959 Disorders of the neurohypophysis
s7960 **Diabetes insipidus** — ADH deficiency (**central**: tumors of the hypothalamus and pituitary, trauma, infections, surgery) or insensitivity of the kidney to ADH (**nephrogenic**). Water reabsorption in the collecting ducts decreases — **polyuria** (5–20 l per day), very low urine specific gravity (1001–1005), intense thirst (polydipsia), risk of dehydration and hypernatremia.
s7961 **Syndrome of inappropriate ADH secretion** (Parhon syndrome) — tumors (small cell lung cancer — ectopic ADH), CNS diseases, lung diseases, drugs: water is retained — **water intoxication**, dilutional **hyponatremia**, cerebral edema; the urine specific gravity is high, and there is usually no edema.

s7985 Hypofunction of the adrenal cortex
s7986 **Primary chronic insufficiency (Addison disease)** — destruction of more than 90 percent of the adrenal cortex: autoimmune adrenalitis (the main cause), tuberculosis, metastases, hemorrhage.
s7987 glucocorticoid deficiency — weakness, weight loss, **hypoglycemia**, hypotension, intolerance of stress;
s7988 mineralocorticoid deficiency — loss of sodium and water (hypovolemia, hypotension), **hyperkalemia**, craving for salty food;
s7989 **hyperpigmentation of the skin and mucous membranes** (“bronze disease”) — the fall in cortisol, through feedback, leads to an increase in **ACTH and melanocyte-stimulating hormone** (whose common precursor is proopiomelanocortin). In secondary (pituitary) insufficiency there is no hyperpigmentation.
s7990 **Acute adrenal insufficiency (Addisonian crisis)** — Waterhouse–Friderichsen syndrome (adrenal hemorrhage in meningococcal sepsis), sudden withdrawal of glucocorticoids, stress in Addison disease: collapse, hypoglycemia, hyperkalemia, dehydration — life-threatening.
s7991 Hyperfunction of the adrenal medulla
s7992 **Pheochromocytoma** — a tumor of the chromaffin cells with excess catecholamines: paroxysmal (crisis) or persistent **arterial hypertension**, tachycardia, headache, sweating, pallor, hyperglycemia, weight loss.

s8021 7. Disorders of the parathyroid glands
s8022 **Hyperparathyroidism** — excess parathyroid hormone: primary (adenoma), secondary (chronic kidney failure, vitamin D deficiency — in response to hypocalcemia). In the primary form: **hypercalcemia**, hypophosphatemia, release of calcium from bones (osteoporosis, osteitis fibrosa, fractures), kidney stones, peptic ulcer, muscle weakness, polyuria. To remember: “**bones, stones, abdominal groans and psychic moans**”.
s8023 **Hypoparathyroidism** — most often damage to the parathyroid glands during thyroid surgery: **hypocalcemia** → increased neuromuscular excitability → **tetany** (cramps, “obstetrician's hand”, laryngospasm), positive Chvostek and Trousseau signs, QT prolongation.
s8024 8. Disorders of the gonads
s8025 **Hypogonadism** — primary (damage to the gonads: Klinefelter syndrome 47,XXY; Shereshevsky–Turner syndrome 45,X0; orchitis, trauma, radiation — LH and FSH increased) and secondary (damage to the hypothalamus–pituitary — LH and FSH decreased).
s8026 Androgen deficiency before puberty — **eunuchoidism** (long limbs, undeveloped secondary sexual characteristics).
s8027 **Precocious puberty** — true (hypothalamic tumors, early activation of GnRH) and pseudo- (adrenal and gonadal tumors, adrenogenital syndrome).
s8028 9. Methods of examining the endocrine system
s8029 **Determining hormone levels** — radioimmune, enzyme immunoassay and chemiluminescent methods (in blood, urine, saliva).
s8030 **Stimulation tests** — when hypofunction is suspected (ACTH test — adrenal reserve; insulin hypoglycemia test — growth hormone and ACTH reserve).
s8031 **Suppression tests** — when hyperfunction is suspected (dexamethasone test — distinguishing Cushing disease from Cushing syndrome; suppression of growth hormone by a glucose load — acromegaly).
s8032 **Glucose tolerance test** (topic 10).
s8033 **Imaging** — US, CT, MRI (pituitary adenoma), scintigraphy (thyroid gland).
s8034 **Detecting autoantibodies** (to thyroid peroxidase, TSH receptors, the adrenal cortex).
s8035 **Biological tests** — assessing hormone activity in animals (historical methods; e.g. the Galli Mainini test).
s8036 **Experimental methods** — removal of the gland (extirpation), administration of a gland extract or hormone, transplantation of glandular tissue.
s8037 The Galli Mainini test
s8038 **The Galli Mainini test** (1947) — a biological method for the early detection of pregnancy. The urine of a pregnant woman contains **chorionic gonadotropin** (produced by the placenta); it acts on the germ cells of a male frog (or toad) and stimulates the **release of spermatozoa**.
s8039 Examine the fluid from the cloaca of a male frog (toad) under the microscope and confirm that it contains no spermatozoa.
s8040 Inject a few milliliters of the morning urine of the woman being tested into the dorsal lymph sac of the frog.
s8041 After 1–2 hours, take fluid from the cloaca with a pipette and examine it under the microscope.
s8042 **If spermatozoa are found, the test is positive** (chorionic gonadotropin is present in the urine — pregnancy is likely); if not — negative.
s8043 **Diagnostic significance:** chorionic gonadotropin rises sharply in pregnancy, as well as in **trophoblastic tumors** (hydatidiform mole, choriocarcinoma) and some testicular tumors. The test is of historical importance; today it has been replaced by **immunological detection of β-chorionic gonadotropin** in blood and urine (test strips, enzyme immunoassay) — these are more sensitive and faster.
s8044 Skill: determining the level of an endocrine disorder by the feedback principle
s8045 Assess the level of the peripheral hormone (increased or decreased).
s8046 Assess the level of the corresponding tropic hormone.
s8047 Peripheral hormone low, tropic hormone high — primary (glandular) insufficiency.
s8048 Peripheral hormone and tropic hormone both low — secondary (pituitary or hypothalamic) insufficiency.
s8049 Peripheral hormone high, tropic hormone low — primary hyperfunction (gland tumor or autoimmune stimulation).
s8050 Both high — central (pituitary) hyperfunction or ectopic secretion.
