s2868 9 days after receiving antitetanus horse serum, a patient developed fever, skin rashes, joint pain and enlarged lymph nodes. Protein was found in the urine, and the blood complement level was decreased.
s2869 What condition has developed? Which type of allergic reaction does it belong to? Why did the signs appear after 9 days?
s2870 Serum sickness — a type III (immune complex) allergic reaction. Foreign (horse) proteins persist in the body for a long time; within about a week antibodies (mainly IgG) are formed against them and create soluble immune complexes with the antigen still in the blood. These complexes deposit in vessel walls, renal glomeruli and joint synovium, activate complement (which is why blood complement falls), attract neutrophils and cause vasculitis, glomerulonephritis (proteinuria) and arthritis. The signs appeared after the time needed to form antibodies (7–14 days); because there was no prior sensitization, the reaction developed on the very first injection.
s2871 A 32-year-old patient has had weight loss, prolonged fever, oral candidiasis and recurrent pneumonias over a year. Pneumocystis was found in the latest pneumonia. Blood CD4^+^ lymphocytes are 120 cells/µl.
s2872 What condition can be suspected? Explain the mechanism of the immunodeficiency.
s2873 The AIDS stage of HIV infection (secondary immunodeficiency). HIV enters T helper cells via the CD4 molecule and coreceptors, inserts its genome into the cell DNA by means of reverse transcriptase, replicates and kills the cells. Loss of CD4^+^ T helper cells impairs cellular immunity (macrophage activation, cytotoxic T cells) and humoral immunity (help to B lymphocytes). When CD4^+^ cells fall below 200/µl, opportunistic infections develop — pneumocystis pneumonia, candidiasis, tuberculosis. The diagnosis should be confirmed by detecting antibodies to HIV and viral RNA, and antiretroviral therapy should be started.

s2908 The main systems regulating water–salt homeostasis: **antidiuretic hormone (ADH, vasopressin)** — formed in the hypothalamus when osmoreceptors sense a rise in plasma osmolarity and released from the posterior pituitary; it increases water reabsorption in the renal collecting ducts; thirst appears at the same time; **renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system (RAAS)** — renin is released when renal blood flow falls, angiotensin II constricts vessels and increases aldosterone secretion; aldosterone retains sodium and excretes potassium; **natriuretic peptides** — released when the atria and ventricles are stretched, they increase sodium and water excretion; **thirst mechanism** — regulates water intake.
s2909 Water always moves towards the side of **higher osmolarity**: “**water follows salt**”. If plasma salt increases, the cell “dries out” (hyperosmolar state); if plasma salt decreases, the cell “swells” (hypoosmolar state).
s2910 Most edemas develop with the participation of **several factors**, one of which is leading (initiating). For example, in cardiac edema the leading factor is hydrostatic, in nephrotic edema — oncotic, in inflammatory edema — membranogenic, in lymphedema — lymphogenic.
s2911 Edema is sometimes life-threatening: **laryngeal edema** leads to asphyxia, **cerebral edema** to herniation of the brainstem into the foramina of the skull, **pulmonary edema** to severe hypoxemia. Such edemas require emergency treatment.

s3008 4. Edema and its pathogenetic factors
s3009 **Edema** — excess accumulation of fluid in the interstitial space. Accumulation of fluid in serous cavities is called **dropsy**: in the abdominal cavity — ascites, in the pleura — hydrothorax, in the pericardium — hydropericardium, in the testicular membranes — hydrocele. Widespread edema of the whole body is called **anasarca**.
s3010 Starling forces
s3011 Fluid exchange between capillary and tissue obeys E. Starling's law. The forces that **move** fluid out of the capillary into the tissue are capillary hydrostatic pressure and the oncotic pressure of tissue fluid; the forces that **return** it to the capillary are plasma oncotic pressure and tissue hydrostatic pressure.
