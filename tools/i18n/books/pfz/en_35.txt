s7051 1. General etiology of digestive disorders
s7052 **Digestive insufficiency** — a state in which digestion and absorption of nutrients do not meet the needs of the body.
s7053 **dietary factors** — irregular eating habits, poor-quality, very hot or spicy food, alcohol, dry food;
s7054 **infections and parasites** — Helicobacter pylori, intestinal infections (Shigella, Salmonella, rotaviruses), Giardia, helminths;
s7055 **drugs and toxins** — nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs), glucocorticoids, antibiotics (dysbiosis), cytostatics, radiation;
s7056 **disturbances of nervous and humoral regulation** — stress, autonomic dysfunction, endocrine diseases (diabetes, thyrotoxicosis, hypothyroidism);
s7057 **hereditary factors** — enzyme deficiencies (lactase, disaccharidases), celiac disease (gluten enteropathy), cystic fibrosis;
s7058 **diseases of other organs** — liver, kidney (uremic gastroenteritis), heart failure (venous congestion), autoimmune processes;
s7059 **tumors, surgical interventions** (gastric resection, intestinal resection).
s7060 2. Digestive disorders in the oral cavity and esophagus
s7061 Disorders of salivation:
s7062 **hypersalivation (ptyalism)** — stomatitis, helminths, toxemia of pregnancy, mercury poisoning, parkinsonism; large amounts of swallowed saliva reduce gastric acidity;
s7063 **hyposalivation (xerostomia)** — fever, dehydration, diabetes mellitus, Sjögren syndrome, atropine; dry mouth, difficulty chewing and swallowing, development of caries and stomatitis (loss of the bactericidal properties of saliva).
s7064 Disorders of the esophagus:
s7065 **dysphagia** — difficulty swallowing: mechanical (stenosis, tumor, post-burn scar, diverticulum) or functional (achalasia — failure of the lower sphincter to relax, esophageal spasm);
s7066 **gastroesophageal reflux** — incompetence of the lower esophageal sphincter, increased intragastric pressure, hiatal hernia; acidic contents flow back into the esophagus — **heartburn**, reflux esophagitis, and if prolonged, Barrett esophagus (metaplasia, a precancerous condition).
s7067 3. Disorders of gastric secretory function
s7068 Gastric juice secretion is regulated in three phases: **complex reflex (cephalic)** — via the vagus nerve; **gastric (neurohumoral)** — gastrin, histamine (H~2~ receptors), acetylcholine; **intestinal** — secretin, cholecystokinin and somatostatin inhibit gastric secretion.

s7092 4. Disorders of gastric motor-evacuatory function
s7093 **Hypertonus and hyperkinesia** — rapid emptying of food from the stomach, strong contractions and pain; **pylorospasm** — delayed evacuation.
s7094 **Hypotonus and hypokinesia** — dilation of the stomach, stagnation of food, fermentation and putrefaction.
s7095 **Disturbed evacuation**: accelerated (achylia, after resection) and delayed (pyloric stenosis — ulcer scar, tumor; pyloric spasm in hyperacidity; diabetic gastroparesis).
s7096 **Belching (eructation)** — expulsion of air or food from the stomach through the mouth; in cardiac incompetence and increased gastric pressure. Belching smelling of “rotten eggs” — putrefaction of proteins in pyloric stenosis (hydrogen sulfide).
s7097 **Nausea and vomiting** — a protective reflex resulting from stimulation of the **vomiting center** in the medulla oblongata. The center is stimulated via the **trigger (chemoreceptor) zone** in the floor of the fourth ventricle by blood-borne toxins, drugs (cytostatics, morphine, glycosides), uremia and ketoacidosis, as well as by impulses from the gastrointestinal tract, vestibular apparatus and cerebral cortex, and by raised intracranial pressure.
s7098 **Consequences of repeated vomiting:** dehydration (hypovolemia), **hypochloremic metabolic alkalosis** due to loss of hydrochloric acid, hypokalemia (renal potassium loss), possible alkalotic tetany; tearing of the mucosa at the esophagogastric junction (**Mallory–Weiss syndrome**), aspiration pneumonia.
s7099 5. Pathogenesis of peptic ulcer disease
s7100 **Gastric and duodenal peptic ulcer disease** — a disease characterized by the formation of a chronic recurrent ulcer in the mucosa. Its pathogenesis is explained by an **imbalance between aggressive and protective factors**.
s7101 Figure 23.2
s7102 Pathogenesis of peptic ulcer disease: balance of aggressive and protective factors
s7103 **Role of Helicobacter pylori:** using the enzyme **urease**, the bacterium breaks down urea, forms a “cloud” of ammonia around itself and survives in the acidic environment; with its flagella it penetrates the mucus layer and produces cytotoxins (CagA, VacA) — inflammation (gastritis); somatostatin-releasing D cells in the antrum decrease → gastrin increases → hyperacidity → gastric metaplasia in the duodenum and ulcer.
s7104 **NSAIDs** inhibit cyclooxygenase and reduce the synthesis of protective prostaglandins — mucus, bicarbonate and blood supply decrease.
s7105 **Stress ulcers** (severe burns — Curling, brain trauma — Cushing, shock) — mucosal ischemia and the effects of catecholamines and glucocorticoids.
s7106 **Complications:** bleeding, perforation (peritonitis), penetration (into a neighboring organ), pyloric stenosis, malignant transformation (in gastric ulcer).
s7107 6. Disorders of intestinal digestion and absorption
s7108 Luminal and membrane digestion
s7109 **Luminal digestion** — breakdown of large molecules in the intestinal lumen by the enzymes of pancreatic and intestinal juice and by bile.
s7110 **Membrane (parietal) digestion** — the stage associated with final breakdown and absorption by enzymes located on the surface of the microvilli of the intestinal epithelium, in the glycocalyx (disaccharidases, peptidases) (A. M. Ugolev).
s7111 **Maldigestion syndrome** — impaired digestion: pancreatic insufficiency, failure of bile acids to reach the intestine (obstructive jaundice), disaccharidase deficiency (lactase — milk intolerance), change in intestinal pH.
s7112 **Malabsorption syndrome** — impaired absorption: damage to the intestinal mucosa (celiac disease — villous atrophy, Crohn disease, enteritis), reduced absorptive surface (intestinal resection — “short bowel” syndrome), impaired lymph flow, impaired blood circulation in the intestinal wall.
s7113 **Consequences:** steatorrhea (fat in the stool), creatorrhea (undigested muscle fibers), amylorrhea (starch), weight loss, hypoproteinemic edema, anemia (iron, B~12~, folate), deficiency of fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K) — hypocalcemia, osteomalacia, impaired blood clotting.
s7114 Disorders of intestinal motility
s7115 **Diarrhea** — frequent passage of liquid stools. Mechanisms:
s7116 **secretory** — increased secretion of water and electrolytes into the intestinal lumen (cholera toxin activates adenylate cyclase and raises cAMP — several liters of fluid are lost per day);
s7117 **osmotic** — non-absorbable osmotically active substances in the intestine (lactase deficiency, magnesium sulfate);
s7118 **exudative (inflammatory)** — inflammation and ulceration of the mucosa, with discharge of blood, mucus and protein (dysentery, ulcerative colitis);
s7119 **motor (hyperkinetic)** — accelerated peristalsis (thyrotoxicosis, irritable bowel syndrome).
s7120 **Consequences:** dehydration, hypokalemia, **metabolic acidosis** due to loss of bicarbonate, hypovolemic shock (especially in children).
s7121 **Constipation** — retention of stool for more than 3 days: alimentary (little fiber and fluid), hypokinetic (physical inactivity, hypothyroidism, old age), spastic (autonomic dysfunction, lead poisoning), mechanical obstruction (tumor, scar), proctogenic (anal fissure, hemorrhoids — reflex inhibition due to pain). Consequences: intoxication (absorption of putrefaction products — indole, skatole, phenol), hemorrhoids.
s7122 Intestinal obstruction
s7123 **Intestinal obstruction (ileus)** — arrest of the movement of intestinal contents.
s7124 **Mechanical** — obturation (tumor, gallstone, a ball of worms, foreign body), strangulation (volvulus, knotting, incarcerated hernia — the mesenteric vessels are also compressed, rapid necrosis), intussusception (more common in children).
s7125 **Dynamic** — paralytic (peritonitis, after abdominal surgery, hypokalemia) and spastic (lead poisoning, hysteria).
s7126 **Pathogenesis:** above the obstruction the intestine is distended and the circulation in its wall is impaired; fluid and electrolytes accumulate in the intestinal lumen and are lost with vomiting — **dehydration and hypovolemia**; bacteria multiply, and toxins and bacteria pass through the wall into the blood (**endotoxemia**), peritonitis; pain impulses — pain shock. The result is water–electrolyte disturbances, intoxication and shock.
s7127 7. Disorders of pancreatic function
s7128 **Exocrine pancreatic insufficiency** — chronic pancreatitis, cystic fibrosis, resection and tumors of the gland. Lipase deficiency manifests first: **steatorrhea** (fatty, shiny, bulky stools), creatorrhea, amylorrhea, weight loss.
s7129 **Acute pancreatitis** — activation of the gland's enzymes within the gland and digestion of its own tissue (autolysis).
s7130 **Causes:** gallstones (obstruction of the common bile–pancreatic duct and bile reflux), alcohol, large fatty meals, trauma, hypertriglyceridemia, hypercalcemia, infections, drugs.
s7131 **Pathogenesis:** trypsinogen is converted to trypsin within the gland → it activates other enzymes: **phospholipase A~2~** and **lipase** — fat necrosis (steatonecrosis, formation of soaps with calcium — hypocalcemia), **elastase** — damage to vessel walls and bleeding, activation of the **kallikrein–kinin system** — vasodilation, increased vascular permeability, pain, shock. The enzymes pass into the blood (**enzymemia** — amylase and lipase rise) and damage distant organs (lungs, kidneys).
s7132 8. Features of digestive disorders in children
s7133 The acidity of digestive juices and enzyme activity are low and the permeability of the intestinal mucosa is high — infections and allergens pass through easily.
s7134 The lower esophageal sphincter is immature and the stomach lies horizontally — **regurgitation** is frequent.
s7135 **Pyloric stenosis** — congenital hypertrophy of the pyloric muscle: projectile (“fountain”) vomiting from 2–4 weeks of age, hypochloremic alkalosis, weight loss.
s7136 **In diarrhea** water and electrolyte balance is disturbed quickly — exsiccosis and toxicosis; the risk of hypovolemic shock is much higher than in adults.
s7137 Hereditary enzymopathies (lactase deficiency, celiac disease, cystic fibrosis) often manifest when switching to formula feeding or introducing complementary foods.
s7138 Intussusception is the most common cause of intestinal obstruction in children.
s7139 9. Experimental part
s7140 Determining the acidity of gastric juice (titration method)
s7141 **Aim:** to determine the total acidity and free hydrochloric acid of gastric juice.
s7142 Put 5 ml of filtered gastric juice into a flask.
s7143 Add the indicators: dimethylaminoazobenzene (for free hydrochloric acid) and phenolphthalein (for total acidity).
s7144 Titrate with 0.1 N sodium hydroxide solution: when the red color turns yellowish-orange — free hydrochloric acid; when a pink color appears — total acidity.
s7145 Multiply the milliliters of alkali used by 20 (calculating per 100 ml of juice) and express the result in titration units.
s7146 Compare the result with the normal values and write a conclusion (hyperacid, normacid, hypoacid, anacid).
s7147 Studying the activity of digestive enzymes
s7148 In test tubes, observe the breakdown of starch by salivary amylase (iodine test) and the digestion of protein (fibrin, egg white) by gastric juice, and the effect of various factors (temperature, pH, bile) on them.
s7149 Skill: interpreting gastric juice analysis
s7150 Compare the volumes of basal and stimulated secretion with the normal values.
s7151 Assess total acidity and the amount of free hydrochloric acid.
s7152 No free hydrochloric acid and very low total acidity — anacid state; if pepsin is also absent — achylia.
s7153 Determine the type of secretion from the shape of the secretion curve.
s7154 Relate the result to the clinical signs (ulcer, atrophic gastritis, anemia) and write a conclusion.
s7155 Skill: determining the mechanism of diarrhea
s7156 Assess the volume and character of the stools (watery, mixed with blood and mucus, fatty).
s7157 Does the diarrhea stop with fasting? If it stops — osmotic mechanism; if not — secretory.
s7158 If blood, mucus and leukocytes are present — exudative (inflammatory).
s7159 Fatty, shiny, bulky stools — maldigestion or malabsorption (steatorrhea).
s7160 Assess the degree of dehydration and electrolyte disturbance.
