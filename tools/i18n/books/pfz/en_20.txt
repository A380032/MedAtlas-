s3955 **Regulation of appetite and body weight:** the arcuate nucleus of the hypothalamus regulates appetite. **Leptin** (secreted by adipocytes) and insulin give the satiety signal and reduce appetite; **ghrelin** (stomach) increases appetite. s4095 A 72-year-old woman has type 2 diabetes mellitus and takes oral agents. She has had diarrhea for a week and drank little fluid. She was brought to hospital with clouded consciousness. The skin and mucous membranes are very dry, breathing is rapid and shallow, there is no acetone odor. Blood: glucose 48 mmol/l, Na+ 152 mmol/l, K+ 4.8 mmol/l, urea 18 mmol/l, pH 7.36; no ketone bodies in the urine.
s4096 What coma has developed? Calculate the plasma osmolarity. Why is there no ketoacidosis?
s4097 Hyperosmolar (hyperglycemic, non-ketoacidotic) coma. Plasma osmolarity = 2 × (152 + 4.8) + 48 + 18 ≈ 380 mOsm/kg (normal 280–295) — markedly increased. Diarrhea and low fluid intake started the dehydration, hyperglycemia aggravated it through osmotic diuresis, and reduced renal perfusion cut glucose excretion, raising glycemia further. Hyperosmolarity led to dehydration of neurons and impaired consciousness. In type 2 diabetes the residual insulin secretion, although insufficient to ensure glucose uptake by tissues, is enough to suppress lipolysis in adipose tissue and ketogenesis in the liver — which is why there are no ketone bodies and no acidosis.
s4098 A 48-year-old man, height 175 cm, weight 100 kg, waist circumference 112 cm. Arterial pressure 145/90 mm Hg. Blood: fasting glucose 6.3 mmol/l, triglycerides 2.6 mmol/l, total cholesterol 6.2 mmol/l, HDL 0.9 mmol/l.
s4099 What syndrome does the patient have? Calculate the atherogenic index. Explain the pathogenesis.
s4100 Metabolic syndrome: abdominal obesity (waist circumference 112 cm; BMI ≈ 32.7 kg/m2 — class I obesity) and four additional criteria — raised triglycerides, low HDL, arterial hypertension and raised fasting glycemia. Atherogenic index = (6.2 − 0.9) / 0.9 ≈ 5.9 — the risk of atherosclerosis is high. Pathogenesis: visceral adipose tissue releases large amounts of free fatty acids and inflammatory cytokines, which cause insulin resistance; compensatory hyperinsulinemia increases renal sodium retention and sympathetic activity, causing arterial hypertension; in the liver, synthesis of VLDL and triglycerides increases and HDL decreases. Because the beta-cell reserve is beginning to be exhausted, glycemia is rising — the risk of type 2 diabetes is high.
s4101 A 30-year-old patient has had type 1 diabetes mellitus for 12 years, with poor glycemic control (HbA1c 10.5 %). Recently his vision has deteriorated, he has numbness in his feet and reduced sensation in a “stocking” distribution, and persistent protein is found in the urine.
s4102 What complications have developed in the patient? What is their common pathogenetic mechanism?
s4103 Late microangiopathic and neuropathic complications of diabetes: diabetic retinopathy (deteriorating vision), diabetic nephropathy (proteinuria) and distal symmetric polyneuropathy (sensory loss in a “stocking” distribution). The common mechanism is many years of poorly controlled hyperglycemia (HbA1c 10.5 %): non-enzymatic glycation of proteins and accumulation of advanced glycation end products (thickening of basement membranes), activation of the polyol pathway and sorbitol accumulation in cells that take up glucose independently of insulin, activation of protein kinase C, oxidative stress and endothelial dysfunction. Good glycemic control slows the development of these complications.

s4146 **Oxidative stress** — a shift in the balance between free radical formation and antioxidant defense in favor of the radicals. It plays an important role in reperfusion injury, atherosclerosis, the complications of diabetes mellitus, aging, radiation injury and many inflammatory diseases.
s4147 The “**4P**” effects of calcium overload: **P**hospholipases (break down the membrane), **P**rotein- and DNA-cleaving enzymes — proteases (disrupt the cytoskeleton) and endonucleases (cleave DNA), **P**hosphorylation uncoupling (mitochondria are damaged, ATP falls), **P**ulling of fibrils (contracture of myofibrils).
s4148 Both **decreased** and **increased** apoptosis cause disease. Insufficient apoptosis — tumors (p53 mutation, Bcl-2 excess), autoimmune diseases (autoreactive lymphocytes are not eliminated). Excessive apoptosis — neurodegenerative diseases (Alzheimer, Parkinson), death of CD4+ lymphocytes in AIDS, part of ischemic injury.
