s8151 What is the disease? Determine the level of the disorder. Explain the pathogenesis of the main signs.
s8152 Cushing disease — central (pituitary) hypercortisolism: a pituitary corticotroph adenoma produces excess ACTH, which stimulates both adrenal cortices (cortisol and ACTH both raised; the adenoma is partly sensitive to feedback — a high dose of dexamethasone suppresses it). Centripetal obesity — different sensitivity of adipose tissue in different regions to cortisol and insulin; striae, thin skin, thin limbs — protein catabolism; steroid diabetes — increased gluconeogenesis and insulin resistance; hypertension and hypokalemia — the mineralocorticoid effect of cortisol and increased vascular sensitivity; lymphopenia — immunosuppression; hirsutism and menstrual disturbance — increased adrenal androgens under the action of ACTH.

The gland itself is destroyed — cortisol is low and, through feedback, ACTH is sharply raised (primary level). Aldosterone deficiency leads to loss of sodium and water (hyponatremia, hypovolemia, hypotension) and potassium retention (hyperkalemia); craving for salt — sodium deficiency. With stress, infection or surgery an Addisonian crisis (collapse) may develop — the patient needs lifelong hormone replacement therapy.

s8199 **Multiple organ failure syndrome** — the main cause of death in shock: **shock lung** (acute respiratory distress syndrome — damage to the alveolar–capillary membrane, pulmonary edema, hypoxemia), **shock kidney** (acute kidney failure — prerenal, then acute tubular necrosis), shock liver (cytolysis, jaundice), intestine (mucosal ischemia, translocation of bacteria and endotoxins), heart (reduced coronary perfusion, myocardial depression), CNS (encephalopathy), blood (DIC).
s8200 Collapse is not shock, but prolonged collapse can progress to shock through tissue hypoxia and impaired microcirculation. **Fainting (syncope)** — a brief (seconds, minutes) loss of consciousness with spontaneous recovery, resulting from short-term cerebral hypoperfusion (vasovagal, orthostatic, cardiogenic — arrhythmias).
s8201 The duration of clinical death: “**cold prolongs it, heat shortens it, a long agony steals it**”.
s8202 1. Extreme and terminal states
s8203 **Extreme state** — a state resulting from extraordinarily strong influences on the body (injury, blood loss, infection, poisoning, etc.) with excessive strain and exhaustion of the adaptive mechanisms and a risk of profound disturbance of vital functions. These include **shock, collapse, coma**, crush syndrome and acute organ failure.
s8204 **Terminal state** — states on the border between life and death that do not resolve spontaneously and lead to death without special help: preagony, terminal pause, agony and clinical death.
s8205 Common features of extreme states:
s8206 disturbance of vital functions (circulation, respiration, CNS activity);
s8207 initial hyperactivity of the adaptive mechanisms followed by their exhaustion (stress reaction → exhaustion);
s8208 **hypoxia** — the common pathogenetic link of all extreme states;
s8209 metabolic acidosis, endogenous intoxication;
s8210 formation of **vicious circles** and multiple organ failure.
s8211 2. Shock
s8212 **Shock** — an acute, severe generalized pathological process developing as a result of extremely strong influences, characterized by a sharp decrease in the blood supply (perfusion) of organs and tissues, cellular hypoxia and metabolic disturbance, followed by multiple organ failure.

s8231 Stages of shock (according to N. I. Pirogov):
s8232 **Erectile (excitation) stage** — short (minutes), especially marked in traumatic and burn shock: strong pain impulses, excitation of the CNS and the sympathoadrenal system — the patient is restless, agitated and talkative, BP normal or raised, tachycardia, pale skin.
s8233 **Torpid (inhibition) stage** — inhibition of the CNS: the patient is apathetic, immobile and shows no interest in the surroundings, BP decreased, pulse rapid and weak (thready), skin pale-gray, cold sweat, oliguria.
s8234 Stages by circulatory changes:
s8235 **Compensated shock** — under the action of catecholamines, angiotensin II and vasopressin the vessels of the skin, muscles and internal organs (intestine, kidney) constrict and blood is directed mainly to the brain and heart — **centralization of the circulation**; BP is maintained.
s8236 **Decompensated (progressive) shock** — with prolonged ischemia, lactate, H^+^, adenosine and histamine accumulate in the tissues; the **precapillary sphincters**, being sensitive to them, relax, while the **postcapillary venules** remain constricted — blood enters the capillaries but cannot leave (**stasis**, “sequestration”); hydrostatic pressure rises and plasma leaks into the tissue, the blood thickens, erythrocytes aggregate (**sludge**) and DIC develops. Venous return decreases and BP falls — **decentralization**: the brain and heart also become ischemic.
s8237 **Irreversible shock** — cells die en masse, and lysosomal enzymes, myocardial depressant factor (formed in pancreatic ischemia) and endotoxins (which enter the blood when the intestinal barrier fails) accumulate; treatment is ineffective.
s8238 **Shock index (Allgöwer index)** = pulse / systolic BP. Normally about 0.5–0.7; around 1.0 — moderately severe shock (about 20–30 percent of blood volume lost); 1.5 or higher — severe shock.
s8239 Some types of shock
s8240 **Hemorrhagic shock** — when more than 20–25 percent of the circulating blood volume is lost rapidly (topic 17).
s8241 **Burn shock** — with deep burns of more than 15–20 percent of the body surface (more than 10 percent in children): severe pain, **plasma loss** due to a sharp increase in capillary permeability — hemoconcentration (hematocrit and hemoglobin rise), hypovolemia; toxins from the burned tissues (burn toxemia).
s8242 **Septic shock** — bacterial endotoxin (lipopolysaccharide) activates macrophages → cytokines (TNF-alpha, IL-1, IL-6) → activation of **inducible NO synthase** → excess NO formation → marked vasodilation and hypotension; endothelial damage, increased capillary permeability, DIC, mitochondrial dysfunction. There is an initial “warm” phase (hyperdynamic — warm skin, increased cardiac output) and a later “cold” phase (hypodynamic).
s8243 **Anaphylactic shock** — a type I allergic reaction: histamine, leukotrienes and platelet-activating factor are released from mast cells and basophils → marked vasodilation, increased vascular permeability (plasma leakage), bronchospasm, laryngeal edema (topic 8). Care — immediate epinephrine.
s8244 **Cardiogenic shock** — when more than 40 percent of the left ventricular myocardium is damaged (topic 21): cardiac output falls, with pulmonary congestion (pulmonary edema) together with peripheral hypoperfusion.
s8245 3. Collapse
s8246 **Collapse** — acute vascular insufficiency resulting from an acute fall in vascular tone and (or) a decrease in circulating blood volume: arterial and venous pressure fall sharply, with hypoxia of the brain, heart and other organs.
s8247 **Causes:** infections and intoxications (critical fall of fever, typhoid fever, pneumonia), blood loss, orthostatic position (standing up quickly, antihypertensive drugs), adrenal insufficiency, severe hypoxia, poisoning.
