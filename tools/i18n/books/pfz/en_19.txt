s3731 A 19-year-old girl with type 1 diabetes mellitus has not taken insulin for 2 days. Nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, deep noisy breathing, acetone odor on the breath. Blood: glucose 26 mmol/l, pH 7.12, pCO~2~ 22 mm Hg, HCO~3~^−^ 7 mmol/l, BE −20 mmol/l, Na^+^ 136, Cl^−^ 100 mmol/l, K^+^ 5.6 mmol/l.
s3732 Determine the type of ABB disorder. Calculate and interpret the anion gap. Explain the changes in breathing and potassium.
s3733 Decompensated metabolic acidosis (pH 7.12; HCO~3~^−^ and BE markedly decreased) with partial respiratory compensation (pCO~2~ 22 mm Hg — hyperventilation). Anion gap = 136 − (100 + 7) = 29 mmol/l — increased: in insulin deficiency, lipolysis and the formation of ketone bodies (acetoacetic and beta-hydroxybutyric acids) are sharply increased, and these are unmeasured anions. Deep noisy Kussmaul breathing is compensatory hyperventilation resulting from stimulation of the respiratory center by H^+^; CO~2~ elimination increases. Hyperkalemia — in acidosis H^+^ enters the cells and K^+^ leaves them; in addition, in insulin deficiency potassium does not move into the cells. During treatment with insulin, potassium returns to the cells and there is a risk of hypokalemia, so potassium is monitored.
s3734 A 45-year-old patient has had profuse vomiting for 1 week due to narrowing of the gastric outlet (pyloric stenosis). The patient is weak and has cramps in the hands (“obstetrician's hand”). Blood: pH 7.52, pCO~2~ 48 mm Hg, HCO~3~^−^ 36 mmol/l, K^+^ 2.9 mmol/l, Cl^−^ 85 mmol/l.
s3735 Determine the type of ABB disorder. Explain its pathogenesis and the cause of the cramps.
s3736 Metabolic alkalosis (pH and HCO~3~^−^ increased) with partial respiratory compensation (pCO~2~ 48 — hypoventilation). With vomiting, HCl (H^+^ and Cl^−^) is lost in the gastric juice — hypochloremia. Dehydration and hypovolemia increase aldosterone secretion: the kidneys retain sodium and excrete K^+^ and H^+^ — hypokalemia and maintenance of the alkalosis. In hypokalemia K^+^ leaves the cells and H^+^ enters them, which deepens the alkalosis. The cramps (“obstetrician's hand”) are due to increased neuromuscular excitability, because in alkalosis more calcium binds to albumin and ionized calcium decreases. Treatment — restore water and electrolyte balance with sodium chloride and potassium chloride solutions.
s3737 A 62-year-old patient has had chronic obstructive pulmonary disease for many years. His condition is stable. Blood: pH 7.37, pCO~2~ 60 mm Hg, HCO~3~^−^ 34 mmol/l.
s3738 Determine the type of ABB disorder. Why is the pH normal? Why is it dangerous to give such a patient high-concentration oxygen?
s3739 Compensated chronic respiratory acidosis. The primary disorder is a rise in pCO~2~ (60 mm Hg) due to hypoventilation. Over several days the kidneys have increased H^+^ secretion and bicarbonate reabsorption, raising HCO~3~^−^ to 34 mmol/l; the HCO~3~^−^/CO~2~ ratio has approached 20 : 1, so the pH is within the normal range. In such patients the respiratory center has adapted to prolonged hypercapnia and lost its sensitivity to CO~2~, and breathing is driven mainly by hypoxemia (via the carotid bodies). High-concentration oxygen removes the hypoxic stimulus, reduces ventilation further and creates the risk of a sharp rise in pCO~2~, decompensation of the respiratory acidosis and hypercapnic coma. Therefore oxygen is given to them under monitoring, at a low flow rate.

s3776 1. Diabetes mellitus: definition and classification
s3777 **Diabetes mellitus** — a group of metabolic diseases characterized by chronic hyperglycemia resulting from impaired insulin secretion, impaired insulin action, or both. It is accompanied by disturbed carbohydrate, fat and protein metabolism and damage to the vessels, nervous system, kidneys and eyes.

s3877 4. Late complications of diabetes mellitus
s3878 The main cause of late complications is prolonged hyperglycemia. The main mechanisms are:
s3879 **Non-enzymatic glycation of proteins** and accumulation of advanced glycation end products (AGE) — basement membranes thicken, the vessel wall stiffens, and inflammation and oxidative stress are enhanced via AGE receptors.
s3880 **Activation of the polyol (sorbitol) pathway** — in cells that take up glucose independently of insulin (Schwann cells, lens, endothelium), sorbitol accumulates, causing osmotic damage and NADPH deficiency (weakened antioxidant defense).
s3881 **Activation of protein kinase C** — impaired vascular permeability and contractility.
s3882 **Oxidative stress** and endothelial dysfunction.

s3902 5. Atherosclerosis
s3903 **Atherosclerosis** — a chronic disease characterized by accumulation of lipids (mainly cholesterol) in the intima of large and medium-sized arteries of the elastic and muscular-elastic type, connective tissue overgrowth and formation of atherosclerotic plaques. It underlies ischemic heart disease, stroke and peripheral arterial disease — the leading causes of death worldwide.
