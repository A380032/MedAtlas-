s0021 Caution
s0022 Animals and humans differ in structure, metabolism and reactivity (interspecies differences). Therefore the result of an experiment is not transferred to humans **directly** — it must be confirmed by clinical observations. For example, rats synthesize vitamin C, whereas humans do not; for this reason a model of scurvy cannot be created in rats.
s0023 The three main methodological principles of pathophysiology: **experiment** (modeling), **the systems approach** (disease is the response of the whole organism) and **cause-and-effect analysis** (determinism).
s0024 The main features of disease: it has a cause (there is no disease without a cause); it has two opposite sides: injury and protection-adaptation; it is a disease of the whole organism (even with local signs, the response is systemic); the adaptive capacity and working capacity of the organism decrease; it is a new qualitative state: the diseased organism is not a “reduced” copy of the healthy one.
s0025 Memory aid
s0026 **“R-P-S-D”** — Reaction → Process → State → Disease: complexity and duration increase. A reaction is “a moment”, a process is “a movement”, a state is “a trace”, a disease is “the whole organism”.
s0027 A protective-adaptive reaction can also become harmful when excessive. For example, fever is a protective reaction against infection, but above 41 °C the brain is injured; edema in inflammation delimits the focus, but laryngeal edema leads to asphyxia. This is **the dialectical unity of protection and injury**.
s0028 Conventional durations by course of disease: **hyperacute** — up to 4 days, **acute** — 5–14 days, **subacute** — 15–40 days, **chronic** — months and years. These durations are defined separately for each disease and are only approximate.
s0029 Textbooks give different durations of the course (the textbooks of N.H. Abdullaev and V.V. Novitsky differ slightly). In practice the durations for each disease are defined by the relevant clinical protocol.
s0030 A large part of the population is infected with Mycobacterium tuberculosis (according to the WHO, about one quarter of the world's population), but only 5–10% of them develop active tuberculosis during their lifetime. Thus, **the cause is necessary, but not always sufficient** — disease arises from the interaction of the cause and the conditions.
s0031 **The main link** is the link of the pathogenetic chain whose elimination breaks the whole chain and leads to recovery. The goal of pathogenetic treatment is precisely to find the main link and act on it. In blood loss the main link is the decrease in circulating blood volume, which is why the main treatment is transfusion of blood and blood substitutes.
s0032 **Vicious circle = “the snake bites its own tail”**: the consequence comes back and amplifies the cause. To break it, any link of the circle can be targeted, but the most effective is to act on the main link.
s0033 Do not confuse “etiology” and “pathogenesis”. “The etiology of pneumonia is the pneumococcus” (cause); “the pathogenesis of pneumonia is inflammation in the alveoli, accumulation of exudate, impaired gas exchange, hypoxemia” (mechanism). A common exam mistake is to answer a question on pathogenesis with a list of causes.
s0034 Symptomatic treatment alone can be dangerous: removing the signs makes diagnosis harder, while the disease continues. For example, if an analgesic is given for “acute abdomen” pain before a diagnosis is made, the signs of acute appendicitis may be masked and peritonitis may be missed.
s0035 1. Subject and tasks of pathophysiology
s0036 **Pathological physiology (pathophysiology)** is the science that studies the vital activity of the diseased organism and the general laws of the origin, development, course and outcomes of diseases and pathological processes. The term comes from the Greek words pathos — suffering, disease; physis — nature; logos — study.
s0037 The object of study of the discipline is the diseased organism. Pathophysiology studies disease at different levels of integration — molecular, subcellular, cellular, tissue, organ, system and whole-organism level. That is why it is considered an integrative science: on the one hand it relies on biology, physiology, biochemistry, histology and pathological anatomy, and on the other hand on the clinical disciplines.

Structure and function form an inseparable unity — there is no functional disorder without a change in structure, and vice versa.
s0058 2. Research methods of pathophysiology
s0059 The main method of pathophysiology is **experiment**, that is, modeling of diseases and pathological processes in animals. Experiment makes it possible to study the cause of a disease, its mechanism of development and methods of treatment without risk to humans, in dynamics and at any level of integration.

s0078 3. History of the discipline
s0079 Views on the nature of disease developed together with medicine. Hippocrates (5th–4th centuries BC) regarded disease as an imbalance of four humors (blood, phlegm, yellow bile and black bile) (the humoral theory). In “The Canon of Medicine”, Avicenna (Ibn Sina) divided the causes of diseases into external and internal factors, suggested that infectious diseases are spread by invisible creatures, and divided medicine into theoretical and practical parts.

s0099 4. General nosology. Health and disease
s0100 **Nosology** (Greek nosos — disease, logos — study) is the doctrine of disease. **General nosology** studies the general laws of the nature, causes, development and classification of disease, whereas **special nosology** studies individual diseases (nosological entities).
s0101 **Health**, according to the definition of the World Health Organization (WHO, 1948), is a state of complete physical, mental and social well-being and not merely the absence of disease or infirmity.
s0102 **The norm** is the state of the organism that is optimally adapted to the conditions of the external environment. The concept of the norm is relative: an increased heart rate during physical work is normal, but at rest it may be a sign of disease. The reference ranges of laboratory parameters cover 95% of the healthy population, that is, in 5% of healthy people a parameter may fall “outside the norm”.
s0103 **Disease** is a complex systemic response of the organism to the action of a harmful factor, in which injury (alteration) and protective-adaptive reactions appear at the same time; as a result, the adaptation of the organism to the external environment and its working capacity decrease.
s0104 The main features of disease:
s0105 it has a cause (there is no disease without a cause);
s0106 it has two opposite sides: injury and protection-adaptation;
s0107 it is a disease of the whole organism (even with local signs, the response is systemic);
s0108 the adaptive capacity and working capacity of the organism decrease;
s0109 it is a new qualitative state: the diseased organism is not a “reduced” copy of the healthy one.
s0110 **The predisease (prenosological) state** is an intermediate state between health and disease: the adaptive capacity of the organism is reduced, but there are no definite signs of disease yet. Examples are intermittent rises in arterial pressure, impaired glucose tolerance (prediabetes) and chronic fatigue. Preventive measures are most effective during this period.

s0129 Protective-adaptive reactions in disease
s0130 In disease, the organism activates a number of reactions in response to injury:
s0131 **protective reactions** — removal and neutralization of the pathogenic factor: barriers (skin, mucous membranes, the blood-brain barrier), protective reflexes (cough, sneezing, vomiting, lacrimation), phagocytosis, the immune response;
s0132 **adaptive reactions** — adjustment of the organism's activity to changed conditions (for example, an increase in the number of red blood cells at high altitude);
