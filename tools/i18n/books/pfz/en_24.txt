s5041 Interpret the leukogram. Calculate the nuclear shift index. What complication can be suspected?

s5043 An 8-year-old child has itchy skin, restless sleep and abdominal pain. Blood test: leukocytes 8.2 × 10^9^/l; eosinophils 18 %, other values close to normal. Ascaris eggs were found in the stool.
s5044 Assess the change in the leukogram. What is the mechanism of the eosinophilia?
s5045 Absolute eosinophilia: 8.2 × 18 / 100 ≈ 1.48 × 10^9^/l (normal up to 0.3 × 10^9^/l). Helminth antigens induce a Th2-type immune response: Th2 lymphocytes secrete IL-5, which enhances the production and maturation of eosinophils in the bone marrow; IL-4 and IL-13 increase IgE synthesis. Eosinophils adhere to helminths coated with IgE and IgG and damage them by releasing major basic protein and other toxic substances. The itching is the allergic (IgE-mediated) component.
s5046 A 60-year-old woman is taking thiamazole for an overactive thyroid. In week 6 of treatment she developed a fever of 39.5 °C, severe sore throat and necrotic ulcers on the tonsils. Blood test: leukocytes 1.1 × 10^9^/l, granulocytes 0.2 × 10^9^/l, erythrocytes and platelets normal.
s5047 What condition has developed? What are its mechanism and danger?
s5048 Drug-induced immune agranulocytosis (granulocytes far below 0.75 × 10^9^/l; erythrocytes and platelets normal — not a myelotoxic but an immune mechanism). Thiamazole, acting as a hapten, binds to proteins on the neutrophil surface and causes the formation of antibodies against them and destruction of neutrophils in the blood and bone marrow. Because of the absence of phagocytes, the oral microflora has caused necrotic tonsillitis; the infection can quickly progress to sepsis. The drug is stopped immediately, the patient is placed in an isolated (aseptic) ward, and broad-spectrum antibiotics and granulocyte colony-stimulating factor are prescribed.

s5080 The three main features of leukemia: **neoplastic nature** — leukemic cells have all the properties of tumor cells (uncontrolled proliferation, atypia, metastasis — leukemic infiltrates); **clonal origin** — all leukemic cells are descendants of a single mutated cell; **location** — the tumor starts in the bone marrow and spreads to the blood (leukemic cells do not have to be numerous in the blood).
s5081 The terms “acute” and “chronic” leukemia refer not to the duration of the disease but to the degree of maturity of the tumor cells. Acute leukemia never turns into chronic leukemia, whereas chronic myeloid leukemia may pass into an “acute leukemia-like” state with a blast crisis in its final stage.
