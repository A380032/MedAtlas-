s5893 At the standard paper speed of 25 mm/s: 1 mm (small square) = 0.04 s, 5 mm (large square) = 0.2 s. Heart rate = 60 / RR (in seconds) or 300 / the number of large squares between R waves. **Criteria of sinus rhythm:** a P wave before each QRS, P positive in lead II, constant PQ (0.12–0.20 s), equal RR intervals (difference below 10 percent), rate 60–90 beats/min.
s5894 “Dangerous” signs of ventricular extrasystoles: multifocal, paired and grouped, early (“R on T”) extrasystoles, especially against the background of acute myocardial infarction — they indicate a risk of progression to ventricular fibrillation. The contraction that follows the prolonged pause after an extrasystole is stronger (Frank–Starling law) — the patient feels this as “the heart stopped and then beat hard”.

s5980 4. Disturbances of automaticity
s5981 Sinus tachycardia
s5982 An increase in the rate of impulses from the sinus node at rest above **90 (100) beats/min**.
s5983 **Causes:** physiological — physical exertion, excitement; pathological — fever (about 8–10 beats/min for every 1 °C), anemia, hypoxia, hypovolemia, hypotension, thyrotoxicosis, heart failure, myocarditis, pheochromocytoma, drugs (atropine, adrenomimetics, caffeine).
s5984 **Mechanism:** increased sympathetic or decreased parasympathetic influence — diastolic depolarization in the sinus node cells accelerates.
s5985 **ECG:** sinus rhythm preserved, RR intervals shortened, rate above 90.
s5986 Figure 19.2
s5987 **Consequences:** diastole shortens → ventricular filling and coronary blood flow (which occurs mainly in diastole) decrease, myocardial oxygen demand increases → attacks of angina in patients prone to ischemia.
s5988 Sinus bradycardia
s5989 A decrease in the rate of sinus node impulses below **60 beats/min**.
s5990 **Causes:** physiological — in athletes (vagotonia), during sleep; pathological — increased vagal tone (raised intracranial pressure, obstructive jaundice — effect of bile acids), hypothyroidism, hypothermia, sick sinus syndrome, myocardial infarction (inferior wall), drugs (beta-blockers, cardiac glycosides, verapamil).
s5991 **ECG:** sinus rhythm, RR intervals prolonged, rate below 60.
s5992 Figure 19.3
s5993 Sinus arrhythmia
s5994 **Irregular** discharge of impulses from the sinus node: the difference between RR intervals exceeds 0.1–0.15 s (or 10 percent).
s5995 **Respiratory arrhythmia** — the rate increases on inspiration and decreases on expiration (variation of vagal tone with the phases of respiration); physiological in children and adolescents.
s5996 **Non-respiratory arrhythmia** — organic myocardial changes, intoxications.
s5997 Figure 19.4
s5998 **Sick sinus syndrome** — reduced automaticity resulting from organic damage to the sinus node (ischemia, fibrosis, cardiomyopathies): persistent bradycardia, sinoatrial blocks, sinus arrest, alternation of bradycardia with tachyarrhythmias (“tachy-brady syndrome”). During long pauses there are attacks of fainting due to cerebral ischemia (**Morgagni–Adams–Stokes**); treatment — a permanent pacemaker.
s5999 Escape (replacement) rhythms
s6000 When sinus node activity is depressed or impulses fail to be conducted, lower-order centers begin to generate impulses: AV junctional rhythm (40–60 beats/min, P inverted or merged with QRS), idioventricular rhythm (20–40 beats/min, wide deformed QRS). This is a protective (compensatory) mechanism — it prevents asystole.
s6001 5. Disturbances of excitability. Extrasystole
s6002 **Extrasystole** — a premature (out-of-turn) contraction of the heart or part of it; the impulse arises from an ectopic focus (outside the sinus node).
s6003 **functional** — excitement, smoking, strong tea and coffee, alcohol, autonomic dysfunction, gastrointestinal diseases (reflex);
s6004 **organic** — ischemic heart disease, myocardial infarction, myocarditis, cardiomyopathies, heart defects, arterial hypertension;
s6005 **toxic and electrolyte** — cardiac glycoside toxicity, hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, adrenomimetics, thyrotoxicosis.
s6006 **Theories and mechanisms of extrasystole:** re-entry of the excitation wave, increased automaticity of an ectopic focus, triggered activity (afterdepolarizations).
s6007 **Compensatory pause** — the prolonged interval after an extrasystole until the next normal contraction:
s6008 **full compensatory pause** — the sum of the RR intervals before and after the extrasystole equals two normal RR intervals; typical of ventricular extrasystoles (the sinus node is not “discharged” and its rhythm is not disturbed; the sinus impulse following the extrasystole reaches refractory ventricles);
s6009 **incomplete compensatory pause** — the sum is less than two RR intervals; typical of atrial and AV extrasystoles (the ectopic impulse also excites the sinus node and “resets” its cycle).

s6028 Types of extrasystole: **single**, **paired** (two in succession), **grouped** (three or more — “runs”, progressing to paroxysmal tachycardia); **allorhythmia** — regularly repeating: **bigeminy** (an extrasystole after every normal contraction), **trigeminy** (after every two); **monofocal** (from one focus) and **multifocal** (of different shapes, from different foci); early (“R on T” — falling on the T wave of the preceding contraction).
s6029 **Hemodynamic consequences:** during an extrasystole the ventricles do not fill adequately with blood — the stroke volume is small, and the pulse wave may not be felt at the periphery (**pulse deficit**); frequent extrasystoles reduce cardiac output and coronary blood flow.
s6030 6. Experimental part: recording and analyzing the ECG
s6031 **Aim:** to record a normal ECG and analyze the ECGs of patients with disturbances of automaticity and excitability.
s6032 **Equipment:** electrocardiograph, electrodes, contact gel, calipers (measuring tool), a set of ECG samples of various arrhythmias.
s6033 ECG recording procedure
s6034 Lay the subject on their back, with relaxed muscles and calm breathing.
s6035 Moisten the electrode sites with gel.
s6036 Apply the limb electrodes: red — right arm, yellow — left arm, green — left leg, black (ground) — right leg.
s6037 Apply the chest electrodes: V~1~ — fourth intercostal space at the right sternal border; V~2~ — fourth space at the left sternal border; V~4~ — fifth space on the left midclavicular line; V~3~ — midway between V~2~ and V~4~; V~5~ — anterior axillary line at the level of V~4~; V~6~ — midaxillary line at the same level.
s6038 Check the calibration signal (1 mV = 10 mm) and the paper speed (25 or 50 mm/s).
s6039 Record all 12 leads; record lead II for longer (for rhythm analysis).
