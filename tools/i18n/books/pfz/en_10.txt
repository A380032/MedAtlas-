s1910 A 58-year-old man has smoked for 20 years and has diabetes mellitus. He complains of pain in the left calf that appears when walking and disappears when he stops (“intermittent claudication”). On examination, the left foot is pale and cold, and no pulse is detected in the foot arteries. Ultrasound revealed atherosclerotic narrowing of the femoral artery.
s1911 What circulatory disorder has developed? Why does the pain appear only when walking? What factors are important in the development of the disease?
s1912 Obturative ischemia has developed because of an atherosclerotic plaque. At rest, the blood arriving through the narrowed artery and the collaterals covers the needs of the muscle, but during walking the oxygen demand of the muscle increases while the inflow of blood does not — relative ischemia; products of anaerobic metabolism (lactate, H+, K+) accumulate, stimulate the nerve endings and cause pain. When the patient stops, demand decreases and the pain passes. Smoking (endothelial injury, vascular spasm) and diabetes mellitus (endothelial dysfunction, accelerated atherosclerosis) are important as etiologic factors and conditions. Because the disease developed slowly, the collaterals have had time to develop partially — that is why there is no necrosis at rest.
s1913 A 67-year-old woman underwent surgery for a fracture of the femoral neck. On day 6, when she got up for the first time, she suddenly developed severe shortness of breath and chest pain, her lips turned blue, and arterial pressure fell to 80/50 mm Hg. The left calf is swollen and painful on palpation.
s1914 What complication has developed? What is the source of the embolus? Which factors of Virchow's triad took part in thrombus formation?
s1915 Pulmonary embolism has developed. The source of the embolus is a thrombus in the deep veins of the left leg (swelling and pain of the calf). All three factors of Virchow's triad took part: endothelial injury (trauma and surgery), slowing of blood flow (prolonged bed rest, old age) and hypercoagulability (the state after surgery and trauma). When the patient got up and her muscles contracted, the thrombus detached and was carried with venous blood through the right heart into the pulmonary artery. Occlusion of the pulmonary artery led to right ventricular strain, decreased cardiac output and arterial hypotension, and the ventilation-perfusion mismatch led to hypoxemia and cyanosis.
s1916 A 70-year-old patient has atrial fibrillation (permanent form) and does not take anticoagulants. Paralysis of the right arm and leg and a speech disorder suddenly appeared.
s1917 What condition has developed? Where did the embolus come from and by what path? Could this condition have been prevented?
s1918 Ischemic stroke has developed as a result of thromboembolism of a cerebral artery (the territory of the left middle cerebral artery). The detached thrombus passed through the left ventricle and aorta into the arteries of the systemic circulation — into a cerebral artery. Because the cerebral arteries are “end” arteries with insufficient collaterals, ischemic necrosis developed. In atrial fibrillation, anticoagulant therapy significantly reduces the risk of such embolism.

s1966 1. The concept of inflammation and its biological significance
s1967 **Inflammation** (Latin inflammatio) is a typical pathological process formed during evolution that manifests in response to injury by changes in local circulation and microcirculation in the tissue, exudation, emigration of leukocytes and proliferation of cells. Its goal is to delimit and eliminate the injurious factor and to clean and restore the injured tissue.
s1968 **The protective significance** of inflammation: delimiting the pathogenic factor and the injured tissue from the rest of the organism (forming a barrier), neutralizing and eliminating it, and creating conditions for regeneration. I.I. Mechnikov regarded inflammation as a protective reaction based on phagocytosis.
s1969 **The harmful side** of inflammation: it may itself intensify injury (secondary alteration), impair the function of vital organs (laryngeal edema, cerebral edema, injury of the heart valves), produce scars, and underlie chronic diseases (rheumatoid arthritis, atherosclerosis).

s1998 3. Components and pathogenesis of inflammation
s1999 Inflammation consists of three interrelated components: **alteration** (injury), **exudation** (disturbed microcirculation, exit of fluid and cells from the vessels) and **proliferation** (multiplication of cells and repair). These components begin in sequence but then proceed simultaneously.
