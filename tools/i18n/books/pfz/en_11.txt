s2002 4. Alteration
s2003 **Alteration** (Latin alteratio — change) is injury of the structure, metabolism and function of a tissue. Alteration is the initial stage of inflammation.
s2004 **Primary alteration** is the direct injurious action of the phlogogenic factor on the tissue. Its extent depends on the strength, nature and duration of action of the factor.
s2005 **Secondary alteration** is the spread and deepening of injury under the action of factors formed as a result of primary alteration:
s2006 release of hydrolytic enzymes (proteases, lipases, glycosidases) from the lysosomes of injured cells;
s2007 release of reactive oxygen species and enzymes from activated leukocytes;
s2008 hypoxia due to disturbed microcirculation;
s2009 acidosis and hyperosmia;
s2010 excessive formation of mediators.
s2011 Disturbances of metabolism in the inflammatory focus
s2012 In the inflammatory focus, metabolism initially increases and oxygen consumption rises; then, as a result of disturbed microcirculation and mitochondrial injury, **anaerobic glycolysis** predominates.

s2094 Exudation
s2095 **Exudation** is the exit of the liquid part of blood (plasma), proteins and blood cells from the vessels into the inflammatory focus. The fluid that leaves the vessels is called **exudate**.
s2096 Mechanisms of exudation:
s2097 **Increased permeability of the vessel wall** — the main mechanism. Under the action of mediators (histamine, bradykinin, leukotrienes), endothelial cells contract and gaps form between them (mainly in postcapillary venules); the endothelium is injured directly; activated leukocytes injure the endothelium.
s2098 **Increased hydrostatic pressure in capillaries and venules** — as a result of hyperemia.
s2099 **Increased osmotic and oncotic pressure in the tissue** — hyperosmia and hyperonkia draw fluid into the focus.

s2139 7. Experimental part: Cohnheim's experiment
s2140 **Aim:** to observe under the microscope the stages of the changes in local circulation in the inflammatory focus in the frog mesentery.
s2141 **Equipment:** a frog, ether or urethane for anesthesia (or a needle for destroying the spinal cord), a cork board with an opening, pins, surgical instruments, a microscope, physiological saline.
s2142 Under anesthesia (or after destruction of the spinal cord), fix the frog on its back on the board with an opening.
s2143 Make a 1–1.5 cm incision on the side of the abdominal wall and carefully pull out an intestinal loop.
s2144 Spread the mesentery over the opening in the board and fix it with pins (do not compress the vessels).
s2145 At low magnification, find the arterioles, capillaries and venules; describe normal blood flow (fast, axial flow, a plasma layer next to the wall).
s2146 Because the mesentery is in contact with air, inflammation gradually develops. Observe the changes every 5–10 minutes and record them in the protocol (1–1.5 hours in total).
s2147 Apply drops of physiological saline from time to time so that the mesentery does not dry.
s2148 Sequence of the changes observed
s2149 **Arterial hyperemia** (the first 10–20 minutes): arterioles and capillaries dilate, previously invisible capillaries open and blood flow accelerates.
s2150 **Venous hyperemia**: venules dilate, blood flow slows and the plasma layer disappears.
s2151 **Margination of leukocytes**: leukocytes roll along the venular wall and adhere to it.
s2152 **Emigration of leukocytes**: leukocytes pass through the venular wall into the surrounding tissue (in detail in the next topic).
s2153 **Prestasis and stasis**: in some capillaries blood moves in a pendulum-like manner and stops; diapedesis of red blood cells.

s2261 With which process is redness (rubor) in the inflammatory focus associated?
s2262 A 25-year-old man got a thorn in his finger. Two days later the fingertip became red, swollen and warm, severe “throbbing” pain appeared, and bending the finger is difficult. Body temperature is 37.8 °C.
s2263 What pathological process has developed? Explain the mechanism of each local sign. What does the systemic sign indicate?
s2264 Acute inflammation of the finger (paronychia/felon) has developed; the phlogogenic factor is the microbes introduced with the thorn and the foreign body. Redness and heat — arterial hyperemia (dilatation of arterioles under the action of mediators, inflow of warm blood, increased metabolism). Swelling — exudation: increased permeability of the vessel wall, hyperosmia and hyperonkia. Pain — stimulation of nerve endings by bradykinin, prostaglandin E~2~, H^+^ and K^+^ ions, and compression of the nerve endings by exudate in the dense tissue of the fingertip (hence “throbbing”). Loss of function — due to pain and swelling. Fever — a systemic reaction under the action of cytokines (IL-1, IL-6, TNF-alpha) released from the inflammatory focus; it indicates that the local process is affecting the whole organism.
s2265 Students performed Cohnheim's experiment. After 40 minutes, one student saw that blood flow in the venules had slowed and leukocytes had accumulated along the wall. In the second student's preparation, stasis was observed in all vessels after only 10 minutes, and the stage of arterial hyperemia was not seen at all.
s2266 Which stage of inflammation did the first student observe? What does the second student's result indicate?
s2267 The first student observed the stage of venous hyperemia and the margination of leukocytes — the regular development of inflammation. In the second student's preparation, rapidly developing stasis and the absence of arterial hyperemia indicate a fault in the experimental technique: the mesentery was stretched and the vessels were compressed by the pins, or the mesentery dried. Such stasis developed not because of inflammation but as a result of a mechanical factor, so the preparation must be made again.
s2268 Fluid was obtained by puncture of the pleural cavity in a 40-year-old patient. The fluid is turbid, protein content 42 g/L, specific gravity 1.024, many neutrophils, positive Rivalta test.
