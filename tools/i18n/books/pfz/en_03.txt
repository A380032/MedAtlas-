s0450 Case 1
s0451 Four members of one family fell ill with influenza at the same time. The 25-year-old son recovered in 5 days. The 70-year-old grandmother, who has diabetes mellitus, was hospitalized with secondary bacterial pneumonia against the background of influenza. The father (45 years old, smokes 20 cigarettes a day) was treated for a long time for the complication of bronchitis. The mother had previously been vaccinated against influenza and had a mild form of the disease.
s0452 What is the cause of the disease? What factors can explain the different courses of the disease? Which rule of etiology does this example confirm?
s0453 The cause is the influenza virus (a biological etiologic factor); it is the same in all family members. The different courses of the disease depend on the conditions and the reactivity of the organism: in the grandmother, old age and diabetes mellitus (reduced immunity and regeneration) are promoting internal conditions; in the father, smoking has impaired the mucociliary clearance of the bronchi; in the mother, the immunity produced by vaccination is a hindering condition. The example confirms the modern view that “the cause is necessary, but the course and outcome of the disease depend on the interaction of the cause, the conditions and the reactivity of the organism” and shows the limitations of monocausalism.
s0454 Case 2
s0455 A 40-year-old patient lost 1.5 liters of blood as a result of bleeding from a gastric ulcer. Arterial pressure is 80/50 mm Hg, pulse 120 beats/min, the skin is pale and covered with cold sweat. The ECG shows signs of myocardial ischemia.
s0456 Indicate the main link of pathogenesis. What vicious circle has formed in the patient? What should pathogenetic treatment be aimed at?
s0457 The main link is a sharp decrease in circulating blood volume (hypovolemia). Vicious circle: hypovolemia → decreased venous return to the heart → decreased cardiac output and arterial pressure → decreased coronary blood flow → myocardial hypoxia → decreased contractility → further decrease in cardiac output. Tachycardia and spasm of the skin vessels are compensatory reactions. Pathogenetic treatment is aimed at the main link: restoration of circulating blood volume by transfusion of blood and blood substitutes; this also breaks the vicious circle. Etiotropic treatment is stopping the bleeding (endoscopic or surgical hemostasis).
s0458 Case 3
s0459 At an exam, a student answered: “The pathogenesis of type 1 diabetes mellitus is hereditary predisposition, viral infections and stress.” The teacher marked the answer as wrong.
s0460 What mistake did the student make? Briefly state the correct pathogenesis of type 1 diabetes mellitus and indicate the main link.
s0461 The student confused etiology (causes and conditions) with pathogenesis (the mechanism of development): hereditary predisposition, viruses and stress are etiologic factors and conditions. Pathogenesis: autoimmune injury of the pancreatic beta cells → insulin deficiency → decreased entry of glucose into tissues, increased gluconeogenesis and lipolysis → hyperglycemia, glucosuria, polyuria, ketoacidosis. The main link is insulin deficiency; therefore the pathogenetic treatment is insulin replacement therapy.

s0504 Do not confuse **hyperthermia** and **fever**! In fever, the thermoregulatory center is “reset” by pyrogens to maintain the temperature at a higher level (an active process). In hyperthermia, the thermoregulatory center tries to maintain the normal level, but heat loss is insufficient (passive heat accumulation). That is why antipyretics (paracetamol (acetaminophen), ibuprofen) are ineffective in hyperthermia — the body must be cooled physically.
s0505 The **“rule of nines”** for estimating the burned area: head and neck — 9%, each arm — 9%, front of the trunk — 18%, back of the trunk — 18%, each leg — 18%, perineum — 1%. The patient's palm (with the fingers) equals about 1% of the body surface.
s0506 Prevention of decompression sickness: slow, stepwise ascent from high pressure (according to decompression tables) and use of a helium mixture for breathing instead of nitrogen. Treatment: immediate placement of the patient in a pressure chamber, raising the pressure again (recompression) and then lowering it slowly.
s0507 **The law of Bergonié and Tribondeau:** the radiosensitivity of a tissue is directly proportional to the mitotic activity of its cells and inversely proportional to their degree of differentiation. The most sensitive tissues are hematopoietic tissue (bone marrow), lymphoid tissue, intestinal epithelium and germ cells. The most resistant are nervous tissue, muscle and bone.
s0508 The time of onset of the primary reaction (vomiting) gives an approximate idea of the dose: the earlier vomiting begins, the higher the dose. The patient's feeling of well-being in the latent period is deceptive — it is precisely during this period that the reserve of bone marrow cells is being depleted.
s0509 The pathogenicity of an external factor depends on three things: **the factor itself** (its nature, strength and duration), **the conditions of action** (in what environment and by what route it acts) and **the reactivity of the organism**. The same factor can be harmless under one condition and fatal under another.
s0510 The effect of cold has two stages: with brief chilling (while body temperature has not yet fallen), thermogenesis in the mouse — shivering and metabolism — increases, oxygen demand **rises**, and resistance to hypoxia may decrease. Resistance increases only **after body temperature has fallen** (hypothermia). That is why in the experiment it is important to check by body temperature that the mouse has been cooled sufficiently.
s0511 1. External conditions and their classification
s0512 As discussed in the previous topic, disease develops from the interaction of the cause and the conditions. Conditions are **external** (related to the environment) or **internal** (related to the organism itself). The same factor may be a cause in one case and a condition in another: for example, low temperature is the **cause** of frostbite but a **condition** of pneumonia (chilling lowers the organism's resistance to infection).

s0535 2. The pathogenic action of temperature
s0536 Humans are homeothermic (warm-blooded) organisms: their body temperature is kept at about 36–37 °C by the balance between heat production (thermogenesis) and heat loss (thermolysis). The thermoregulatory center is located in the hypothalamus. When the external temperature is very high or very low, this balance is disturbed and **hyperthermia** or **hypothermia** develops.
