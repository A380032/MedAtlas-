s6703 A 58-year-old man has had arterial hypertension for 15 years without regular treatment. Over the last year he has had dyspnea when walking, and at night he wakes up suffocating and has to sit up. On examination: moist rales in the lower parts of the lungs, BP 170/105 mm Hg. Echocardiography: left ventricular wall thickened, cavity dilated, ejection fraction 35 %.
s6704 What pathological condition has developed? By what mechanism was compensation achieved and why did decompensation develop? Explain the pathogenesis of the signs.
s6705 Chronic left ventricular (systolic) failure resulting from many years of arterial hypertension. The left ventricle was overloaded by pressure and was compensated for a long time by concentric hypertrophy. In the hypertrophied myocardium the capillaries, mitochondria and innervation lagged behind, and relative coronary insufficiency and fibrosis developed; in addition, prolonged activation of RAAS and the sympathetic system enhanced remodeling — the stage of exhaustion: the ventricle has dilated and its contractility has fallen. The weak left ventricle cannot pump the blood out — congestion in the pulmonary circulation: dyspnea, moist rales in the lungs; when lying down venous return increases and congestion worsens — orthopnea and nocturnal attacks of suffocation (cardiac asthma).
s6706 A 34-year-old woman had rheumatic fever in childhood. She has dyspnea on exertion, cough and sometimes blood-streaked sputum. On her face there is a “mitral flush” (bluish-red cheeks), and her heart beats irregularly. Leg edema, liver enlarged by 4 cm.
s6707 Which defect can be suspected? Explain the sequence of hemodynamic changes.
s6708 Rheumatic mitral stenosis. The passage of blood from the left atrium to the left ventricle is impeded — the left atrium, overloaded by pressure, has hypertrophied and dilated (the dilated atrium is a substrate for atrial fibrillation, which is why the heart beats irregularly). Because the left atrium is weak, it cannot compensate for long — the pressure is transmitted to the pulmonary veins: pulmonary congestion (dyspnea, cough), rupture of capillaries (hemoptysis). Reflex spasm of the pulmonary arterioles (Kitaev reflex) increases pulmonary hypertension — the right ventricle, overloaded by pressure, hypertrophies and then decompensates: congestion in the systemic circulation (enlarged liver, leg edema). The left ventricle, for its part, does not receive enough blood — cardiac output is reduced.
s6709 A 45-year-old man was brought in after a car accident with a chest injury. His condition is deteriorating: BP 75/50 mm Hg, pulse 130 beats/min and weak, neck veins distended, heart sounds very muffled, the pulse almost disappears on inspiration.
s6710 What condition has developed? Explain the mechanism of the hemodynamic disturbance. What care is needed?
s6711 Acute pericardial tamponade due to hemopericardium. Blood accumulates rapidly in the pericardial cavity and compresses the heart — the ventricles cannot expand in diastole (impaired diastolic filling), stroke volume and cardiac output fall sharply — hypotension, compensatory tachycardia. Venous blood cannot return to the heart — the neck veins are distended and central venous pressure rises; the fluid damps the sound — muffled heart sounds (Beck's triad). On inspiration the right ventricle fills more and pushes the interventricular septum to the left, further reducing left ventricular filling — pulsus paradoxus. Emergency care — pericardiocentesis (pericardial puncture) and surgical control of the source of bleeding.

s6833 5. Disturbances of diffusion and perfusion
s6834 Disturbance of diffusion
s6835 Passage of gases across the alveolar–capillary membrane is impeded. According to Fick's law, diffusion is directly proportional to the membrane area and the gas pressure difference and inversely proportional to the membrane thickness.
s6836 **Causes:** thickening of the membrane (interstitial pulmonary edema, pulmonary fibrosis, pneumoconioses, alveolitis), reduction of the diffusion area (emphysema, lung resection, atelectasis), too rapid passage of blood through the capillary (physical exertion).
s6837 **Feature:** CO2 crosses the membrane about 20 times more easily than O2 — which is why in diffusion disturbances **hypoxemia** develops first, while pCO2 remains normal for a long time or falls because of hyperventilation.
s6838 Disturbance of perfusion
s6839 Reduced blood flow through the pulmonary capillaries: pulmonary thromboembolism, pulmonary hypertension, heart failure, shock, right-to-left shunts in congenital defects.
s6840 Disturbance of the ventilation–perfusion relationship
s6841 Normally the ratio of alveolar ventilation to blood flow (V/Q) is about **0.8–1.0**.
s6842 **Decreased V/Q** (alveoli that are not ventilated but are perfused — atelectasis, pneumonia, bronchial obstruction) — the **shunt** effect: venous blood not saturated with oxygen is added to arterial blood; hypoxemia is poorly corrected even by breathing oxygen.
s6843 **Increased V/Q** (ventilated but not perfused — pulmonary embolism) — **dead space** increases: ventilation is wasted.
s6844 6. Hypoxia: definition and classification
s6845 **Hypoxia** — a typical pathological process resulting from inadequate oxygen supply to tissues or impaired use of oxygen by them. **Hypoxemia** — a decrease in the oxygen content of the blood.

s6848 Exogenous hypoxia
s6849 A decrease in the partial pressure of oxygen in the inspired air.
s6850 **Hypobaric** — on ascent to altitude (mountain sickness, in pilots): total atmospheric pressure and pO2 decrease.
s6851 **Normobaric** — little oxygen in the air at normal pressure (mine, well, closed room, malfunction of a breathing apparatus).
s6852 **Blood gases:** arterial pO2 and SaO2 decreased; because of hyperventilation, **hypocapnia** and respiratory alkalosis (inhibition of the respiratory center, spasm of the cerebral vessels).
s6853 Respiratory hypoxia
s6854 Resulting from failure of external respiration: disturbances of ventilation, diffusion and perfusion and V/Q mismatch (sections 3 and 5). **Blood gases:** arterial pO2 and SaO2 decreased, often **hypercapnia** and respiratory acidosis (if ventilation is impaired).
s6855 Hemic (blood) hypoxia
s6856 The oxygen capacity of the blood decreases:
s6857 **anemias** — decreased hemoglobin;
s6858 **carbon monoxide (CO) poisoning** — carboxyhemoglobin is formed; CO binds to hemoglobin about 200–300 times more strongly than oxygen and also shifts the oxyhemoglobin dissociation curve to the left, impeding oxygen release; the skin is “cherry-red”;
s6859 **methemoglobin-forming agents** (nitrites, nitrates, aniline, sulfonamides) — the iron passes into the Fe3+ state and does not carry oxygen; the blood is brown, the skin gray-bluish.
s6860 **Blood gases:** arterial pO2 is **normal**, but the oxygen capacity and oxygen content of the blood are decreased; the arteriovenous difference is decreased.
