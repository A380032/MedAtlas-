s0756 Weight gain:
s0757 in the first half-year — on average **800 g** per month (25–30 g per day);
s0758 in the second half-year — on average **400 g** per month;
s0759 at 1 year the weight is on average **10.5 kg** — **3 times** the birth weight; the weight **doubles by 4.5–5 months**.
s0760 **Formula for calculating weight in the first year:** the weight of a 6-month-old child ≈ **8200 g**. For a child younger than 6 months, 800 g are subtracted for each missing month; for a child older than 6 months, 400 g are added for each subsequent month. Another method: 800 g for each month of the first half-year and 400 g for each month of the second half-year are added to the birth weight.

s0771 Head circumference
s0772 At birth the head circumference is **34–36 cm**. In the first year it grows by 2 cm per month at 0–3 months, by 1 cm per month at 4–6 months and by 0.5 cm per month at 7–12 months. At 1 year it is ≈ **46 cm**, at 5 years ≈ **50 cm**, at 10 years ≈ **55 cm**.
s0773 **Formulas:** in a 6-month-old child the head circumference is **43 cm**; 1.5 cm are subtracted for each missing month, and 0.5 cm are added for each subsequent month. In a child over 1 year it is **50 cm** at 5 years; 1 cm is subtracted for each missing year, and 0.6 cm is added for each subsequent year.
s0774 Chest circumference
s0775 At birth the chest circumference is **32–34 cm** — **1–2 cm smaller** than the head circumference. **At 3–4 months** the two circumferences become equal, after which the chest circumference grows faster. After 1 year the chest circumference exceeds the head circumference by approximately **n to 2n cm** (n — age).
s0776 **Formulas:** in a 6-month-old child the chest circumference is **45 cm**; 2 cm are subtracted for each missing month, and 0.5 cm are added for each subsequent month. At 10 years it is **63 cm**; 1.5 cm are subtracted for each missing year, and 3 cm are added for each subsequent year.
s0777 In a newborn, a head circumference **more than 2 cm larger** than the chest circumference may be a sign of hydrocephalus (for example, perinatal damage to the central nervous system). A head circumference small for age is microcephaly. When measuring, the molding of the skull bones during delivery (configuration), the caput succedaneum and edema are taken into account.
s0778 Proportionality indices and body proportions
s0779 **The weight–length ratio** is the birth weight (g) / length (cm). In a healthy term newborn it is **60–80** (most often 60–65). **Less than 60** indicates intrauterine growth restriction (hypotrophy).
s0780 **Body mass index (BMI, Quetelet index)** = weight (kg) / height (m)^2^ — used in school-age children to assess nutritional status and obesity; it is evaluated with centile tables for age and sex.
s0781 **Body proportions change with age:** the height of the head is **1/4** of the body length in a newborn, **1/5** at 2 years, **1/6** at 6 years, **1/7** at 12 years and **1/8** in adults. The midpoint of the body lies above the umbilicus in a preterm newborn, at the umbilicus in a term newborn, between the umbilicus and the pubis at 6 years, and at the pubis in adults.
s0782 **Philippine test:** with the child's head upright, the child's right hand is placed across the top of the head, with the fingers reaching toward the left ear. If the fingertips reach the ear, the test is positive — the first “growth spurt” is complete and the child is biologically ready for school (usually at 5–8 years).
s0783 **The body surface area** of a child is large relative to that of an adult: in a newborn there are 0.06 m^2^ per 1 kg of weight, in an adult 0.02 m^2^. In a newborn the head accounts for 21% of the body surface, in an adult for 7.5%. That is why a child cools down and overheats quickly; the doses of many drugs are calculated per body surface area.

s0840 2.5. Semiotics of disorders of physical development
s0841 Changes in height
s0842 **Short stature** is a height markedly below that of children of the same age and sex (below the 3rd centile or −2 SD); severe short stature (**nanism, dwarfism**) is below −3 SD. Causes:
s0843 endocrine: growth hormone deficiency (pituitary dwarfism — usually detected at 2–4 years: a “doll face”, infantile body build), hypothyroidism, Cushing's disease, severe diabetes mellitus, precocious puberty (early closure of the growth zones);
s0844 chromosomal diseases: Shereshevsky–Turner syndrome and others;
s0845 skeletal dysplasias: achondroplasia, chondrodystrophy, severe rickets;
s0846 chronic somatic diseases (heart, lungs, kidneys, intestine), psychosocial deprivation;
s0847 constitutional (familial) short stature. **Tall stature (gigantism)** — growth hormone excess (pituitary tumor), hyperthyroidism, Marfan syndrome, Klinefelter syndrome (XXY); in families with tall parents, constitutional tall stature is considered normal.
s0848 Changes in weight
s0849 **Intrauterine growth restriction (IUGR)** — the newborn's weight and/or length is below the 10th centile for gestational age.
s0850 **Symmetric (hypoplastic)** — both weight and length are equally reduced; the adverse factors acted in the first and second halves of pregnancy, and the changes are more severe.
s0851 **Asymmetric (hypotrophic)** — length is normal and only weight is reduced; the adverse factors acted in the second half of pregnancy.

s0898 2.6. Anatomical and physiological features of the newborn
s0899 **The neonatal (newborn) period** is the period from birth to **28 days**; the period of the body's adaptation to extrauterine life. It is divided into the **early** (from tying of the cord to the end of day 7) and **late** (days 8–28) neonatal periods. **The perinatal period** — from the 22nd week of pregnancy to 7 days after birth.
s0900 Main definitions:
s0901 **Live born** — a baby that has breathed or shows other signs of life (heartbeat, pulsation of the umbilical vessels, voluntary muscle movement) (WHO).
s0902 **Term** — born from **37 completed weeks to 42 weeks** of gestation (37^0^–41^6^ weeks).
s0903 **Post-term** — born at **42 completed weeks or later**.
s0904 **Preterm (premature)** — born **before 37 completed weeks**.
s0905 **Gestational age** — the number of completed weeks from conception to birth.
s0906 **A healthy newborn** — a baby born to a mother whose pregnancy and delivery were uncomplicated, with an Apgar score of 8–10, an average weight of 3200–3600 g, a weight–length ratio of 60–80, a normal adaptation period, a weight loss not exceeding 6–8%, and fed with breast milk.

s0935 **Morphofunctional maturity** is the readiness of the newborn's organs and systems to support extrauterine life.
