s7434 12.4. Inspection
s7435 The following are assessed: the child's **general condition**, **position** (with abdominal pain — lying on the side with the legs drawn up to the abdomen; in peritonitis — lying motionless), the degree of **physical development** (hypotrophy — malabsorption, chronic diseases), the color of **the skin and mucous membranes** (pallor, **jaundice**, subicterus), rashes, hemorrhages, **spider angiomas**, **“liver palms”** (palmar erythema), striae, scars, a venous network on the anterior abdominal wall (“**caput medusae**” — portal hypertension).
s7436 Examination of the oral cavity
s7437 Examination of the oral cavity and pharynx is performed **last** so as not to upset the child. The corners of the mouth are gently pulled with a spatula, and the following are examined in turn:
s7438 **lips** — color, moisture, dryness, cracks, herpes, **angular cheilitis** (“perlèche” — deficiency of iron and B vitamins);
s7439 **oral mucosa** — color (pale, hyperemic, cyanotic, icteric), **aphthae** and ulcers (stomatitis), **candidiasis** (a white cheesy coating), **Filatov–Koplik spots** on the cheeks in measles;
s7440 **gums** — looseness, bleeding (gingivitis, leukemia, scurvy);
s7441 **teeth** — number (appropriate for age), position, bite, **caries**, enamel changes;
s7442 **tongue** — size (macroglossia — hypothyroidism, Down syndrome), moisture, color, state of the papillae, **coating** (white, yellowish, brown), **“geographic” tongue** (exudative diathesis), **“lacquered” tongue** (atrophy of the papillae — iron and B12 deficiency, liver diseases), **“raspberry” tongue** (scarlet fever), **short frenulum of the tongue**;
s7443 **hard and soft palate** — clefts, “gothic” palate;
s7444 **the ducts of the salivary glands** (hyperemia, edema — mumps);
s7445 **pharynx** and tonsils;
s7446 **breath odor** — acetone (ketoacidosis, acetonemic vomiting), “liver odor” (liver failure), putrid (caries, gastritis, tonsillitis).

s7451 Inspection of the abdomen
s7452 The abdomen is inspected with the child standing and lying down:
s7453 **shape and size** — normally slightly protuberant in infancy (weak muscles, relatively long intestine); it flattens after 3–5 years;
s7454 **symmetry** — uneven enlargement (hepato- or splenomegaly, tumor, hernia);
s7455 **participation in breathing** — lagging of one half (inflammation), **absence of abdominal breathing** (peritonitis, perforation);
s7456 **visible peristalsis** — obstruction to passage from the intestine or stomach (in pyloric stenosis, the “hourglass” sign in the epigastrium);
s7457 the state of **the umbilicus** (hernia, umbilical wound — in the newborn), **hernia of the linea alba**, separation of the muscles (diastasis);
s7458 **venous network** (“caput medusae”).

s7472 12.5. Palpation of the abdomen
s7473 **General rules:** a warm room, the doctor's hands warm and nails short; the child lies on the back with the head slightly raised, the arms along the body, the legs slightly bent at the knee and hip joints (the abdominal press relaxes); the doctor sits on the child's right side, facing the child. Talk to the child to distract attention; a small child may be on the mother's lap. A painful area is palpated **last**. During palpation the doctor watches the child's face (pain reaction).
s7474 **Abdominal regions:** two horizontal lines (through the 10th ribs and the anterior superior iliac spines) and two vertical lines (along the outer edges of the rectus muscles) divide the abdomen into **9 regions**: right and left hypochondrium, epigastrium; right and left lateral regions, umbilical region; right and left iliac regions, suprapubic region.
s7475 Figure 12.3
s7476 Abdominal regions and the projection of the organs: 1 — right hypochondrium; 2 — epigastrium; 3 — left hypochondrium; 4 — right lateral; 5 — umbilical; 6 — left lateral; 7 — right iliac; 8 — suprapubic; 9 — left iliac region (Navruzova, Geppe)
s7477 Superficial (orienting) palpation
s7478 The palm is placed flat on the abdominal wall and gentle pressure is applied with fingers II–V. Order: **starting from the left iliac region, counterclockwise** — left lateral region → left hypochondrium → epigastrium → right hypochondrium → right lateral region → right iliac region → periumbilical and suprapubic regions.
s7479 **Determined:** tenderness of the abdominal wall (localized or diffuse), **tension of the abdominal muscles** (local or diffuse — a “board-like” abdomen — peritonitis), separation of the rectus muscles, hernias (umbilical, inguinal, linea alba), superficial tumors, a markedly enlarged liver and spleen, zones of hyperesthesia (**Zakharyin–Head zones**), asymmetry in the thickness of the subcutaneous fat.
s7480 Muscle tension may be **active** (the child is afraid, ticklish — it disappears when attention is distracted) or **passive** (inflammation — it persists). In the newborn the umbilical ring, the periumbilical vessels and the umbilical wound (discharge, hyperemia) are palpated.

s7482 Superficial palpation of the abdomen: directions of hand movement (Navruzova, Geppe)
s7483 Peritoneal signs:
s7484 **Shchetkin–Blumberg sign** — a sharp increase in pain when the hand, slowly pressed into the abdominal wall, is **quickly withdrawn** — irritation of the peritoneum (peritonitis, acute appendicitis);
s7485 **Obraztsov's sign** — increased pain on palpation of the right iliac region while the straight right leg is raised (appendicitis);
s7486 **Rovsing and Sitkovsky** signs (appendicitis) — studied in detail in the surgery course.
s7487 Deep (methodical, sliding, topographic) palpation — according to Obraztsov–Strazhesko
s7488 **Four steps:** 1) place the fingers parallel to the organ being palpated; 2) move **a skin fold** perpendicular to the organ (toward the umbilicus); 3) during expiration, **plunge the fingers deep** into the abdominal cavity, to the posterior wall; 4) **slide perpendicular** over the organ.
s7489 **Order:** sigmoid colon → cecum (with the terminal ileum) → ascending colon → transverse colon → descending colon → liver and gallbladder → spleen → stomach and duodenum → pancreas → mesenteric lymph nodes.
s7490 **Sigmoid colon** — in the left iliac region, along the bisector of the left lower quadrant; normally a smooth, dense-elastic, painless, mobile, non-gurgling cylinder 1.5–2 cm in diameter.
s7491 **Cecum** — in the right iliac region, by the same method; soft-elastic, slightly gurgling, painless, mobile.
s7492 **Ascending and descending colon** — in the right and left lateral regions, with the other hand placed under the lower back as “support”.
s7493 **Transverse colon** — with both hands, 2–3 cm above the umbilicus, at the outer edges of the rectus muscles, during expiration.
s7494 **The small intestine** is usually not palpable (deep and mobile).
s7495 For each part of the intestine **the location, shape, size, consistency, surface, mobility, gurgling and tenderness** are assessed.

s7502 The greater curvature and pyloric part of **the stomach** are difficult to palpate; first its **lower border** is determined by percussion palpation (Obraztsov's “succussion splash”) or by **auscultofriction**, then the greater curvature is felt above the umbilicus as a soft thin fold by deep sliding palpation. Epigastric pain — a positive **Chernik's sign**.
s7503 Palpation of the liver
s7504 **Sliding palpation according to Strazhesko** — in infants and young children and when the liver is enlarged, only this method: the fingers of the right hand are placed in a line parallel to the lower edge of the liver and slide gently **from top to bottom**; the whole accessible surface of the liver is palpated.
s7505 **According to Obraztsov** (if the liver is not palpable): the right hand is placed flat on the right half of the abdomen at the level of the umbilicus or lower; the left hand encircles the lower right part of the chest (restricting respiratory movement). During expiration the right hand is plunged deep, then the child **takes a deep breath** — the descending edge of the liver strikes the fingers and “slips past” them. Palpation is repeated along the midclavicular, anterior axillary and midline.
s7506 **Assessed:** distance below the costal arch (cm), **edge** (sharp, blunt, rounded), **consistency** (soft-elastic, dense, hard), **surface** (smooth, uneven, bumpy), **tenderness**.

s7520 The gallbladder
s7521 The gallbladder is normally **not palpable** (it does not extend beyond the edge of the liver). Its projection is the point where the outer edge of the right rectus muscle crosses the costal arch (**Kehr's point**). Palpation of an enlarged painless gallbladder — **Courvoisier–Terrier sign**.
s7522 Gallbladder (“cystic”) signs:
s7523 **Kehr's sign** — pain when a finger is pressed into Kehr's point (on inspiration);
s7524 **Murphy's sign** — pain on deep inspiration while the right hypochondrium is pressed (the child stops breathing in);
s7525 **Ortner–Grekov sign** — pain on tapping the right costal arch with the edge of the palm (compared first on the left, then on the right);
s7526 **Georgievsky–Mussy (phrenicus) sign** — pain on pressure between the heads of the sternocleidomastoid muscle on the right;
s7527 **Razdolsky–Lepine sign** — pain on light percussion over the gallbladder projection;
s7528 **Boas sign** — pain on the right in the area of the transverse processes of ThXI–LI.

s7535 The pancreas and mesenteric lymph nodes
s7536 **Grott's method** — the child lies on the back, the clenched right fist placed under the lower back, the legs bent; the fingers are plunged during inspiration between the umbilicus and the hypochondria toward the spine at an angle of 45° (rarely used in practice — pain may occur even in a healthy child).
s7537 Pain points and zones:
s7538 **Chauffard's zone** — between the linea alba and the bisector of the right upper quadrant — the head of the pancreas and the duodenum (pyloroduodenal zone);
s7539 **Desjardins' point** — on the bisector of the right upper quadrant, 4–6 cm from the umbilicus (halfway) — the head of the gland;
s7540 **Mayo-Robson point** — on the bisector of the left upper quadrant, in the outer 1/3 of the distance between the umbilicus and the costal arch — the tail of the gland;
s7541 **Gubergrits–Skulsky zone** — on the left, symmetric to Chauffard's zone — the body of the gland;
s7542 **Kach's sign** — pain on the left in the area of the transverse processes of ThX–XI;
s7543 **Georgievsky–Mussy sign on the left** — pancreatitis.
s7544 **Mesenteric lymph nodes** are palpated in **Sternberg's zones** (in the left upper and right lower quadrants, along the outer edge of the rectus muscle); **Porges' point** — the duodenojejunal junction (to the left and above the umbilicus).

s7548 12.6. Percussion
s7549 Over the abdomen there is normally a **tympanic** sound almost everywhere (gas in the intestine); over the liver, the spleen, a stool-filled bowel (sigmoid) and a full bladder — a **dull** sound. Percussion is quiet.
s7550 Percussion of the liver
s7551 **Up to 5 years** the liver dimensions are determined along the right anterior axillary, midclavicular and midline. In children **over 5 years** — **three dimensions according to M.G. Kurlov**:
s7552 **The first dimension** — along the right midclavicular line: **the upper border** — from top to bottom, from the clear sound to the dull sound (normally rib VI), marked along the upper edge of the finger; **the lower border** — from the level of the umbilicus upward, from tympanitis to dullness (normally the edge of the costal arch), marked along the lower edge of the finger.
s7553 **The second dimension** — along the midline: the upper point is determined conventionally (because it merges with cardiac dullness) by dropping a perpendicular from the upper point of the first dimension to the midline; the lower border is found by percussion upward from the umbilicus (normally at the junction of the upper and middle thirds of the distance between the xiphoid process and the umbilicus).
s7554 **The third dimension** — along the left costal arch: the finger is placed perpendicular to the costal arch at the level of the attachment of rib VIII and percussion is performed along the arch toward the sternum; the border is marked along the outer edge of the finger. The distance from the upper point of the second dimension to this point is the third dimension.

s7566 Detecting free fluid in the abdominal cavity
s7567 **Fluctuation (fluid wave) sign** — the left palm is placed on the lateral surface of the abdomen and a short tap is given on the opposite side with the fingers of the right hand; the vibration of the fluid strikes the left hand as “a wave”. To exclude transmission through the abdominal wall, an assistant presses the edge of the palm on the middle of the abdomen.
s7568 **Percussion** — with the child lying on the back, percussion is performed from the umbilicus to the sides (finger parallel to the linea alba); **bilateral dullness in the lateral regions** — free fluid.
s7569 **With a change of position** — the child is turned on the side and percussion is repeated: if the dullness in the upper lateral region **disappears** — free fluid (the fluid has flowed down); if it persists — another cause (a full bowel, a tumor).
