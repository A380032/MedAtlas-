s0385 1.9. Clinical part
s0386 The history is half the diagnosis
s0387 Experienced pediatricians say that “a correctly taken history gives half the diagnosis”. The following examples show the practical value of the history:
s0388 **Relation to food.** If arrest of weight gain, an enlarging abdomen and large amounts of fatty stools began in a 9-month-old child 1–2 months after semolina porridge was introduced — celiac disease should be considered.
s0389 **Cough during feeding.** Attacks of coughing and cyanosis with every feed in a newborn are a sign of a tracheoesophageal fistula.
s0390 **Family history.** A dry night cough in a child whose parents and elder brother have allergic rhinitis and eczema is characteristic of bronchial asthma.
s0391 **Epidemiological history.** A child who had contact with a child with measles at the kindergarten and has developed fever and conjunctivitis is in the catarrhal period of measles; such a child is admitted to an isolation box ward.
s0392 **Birth history.** Delayed motor development later in a child born with severe asphyxia (Apgar 3 points) is a consequence of perinatal encephalopathy.
s0393 The importance of assessing severity correctly
s0394 Assessing the general condition is not just for the record — it determines where the patient is admitted, how quickly care is given and how he is monitored.
s0395 **In an acute disease** the main criterion is intoxication. For example, if a child with ARVI has a fever of 39.5 °C, lethargy, mottled skin and cold hands and feet, the condition is severe (“white hyperthermia”, impaired microcirculation). **In a chronic disease** the main criterion is functional failure.
s0396 For example, in a child with a congenital heart defect, even without fever, dyspnea at rest and an enlarged liver (grade II heart failure) make the condition severe.
s0397 The forced position — a key to the diagnosis
s0398 The patient's position often points to the diagnosis already in the admission room: a febrile child lying with the head thrown back and the legs bent — meningitis; a child sitting propped on the hands and breathing with a wheeze — an asthma attack; a cyanotic child squatting — tetralogy of Fallot.
s0399 Skill 1. Taking the history
s0400 Wash the hands, introduce yourself, establish contact with the parents (and the child) and obtain consent.
s0401 Establish the personal data.
s0402 Establish the main and general complaints; explore each complaint in detail (site, character, duration, aggravating and relieving factors).
s0403 Take the history of present illness: onset, cause, development, investigations, treatment and its effect.
s0404 Take the life history (appropriate to age): obstetric, neonatal, feeding, developmental, past illnesses, immunizations, allergic, hereditary, social and living conditions, epidemiological history.
s0405 Draw up a pedigree (at least three generations).
s0406 At the end of the conversation, give the parents the opportunity to ask further questions.
s0407 Draw a conclusion from the history: the presumed affected system, the nature and course of the disease, the risk factors.
s0408 Skill 2. Emergency assessment (triage)
s0409 Assess the child immediately on arrival (within 1–2 minutes) by ABCD.
s0410 **A** — patency of the airway: stridor, signs of a foreign body.
s0411 **B** — breathing: present or absent, respiratory distress (accessory muscles, nasal flaring, chest indrawing), central cyanosis.
s0412 **C** — circulation: feel the temperature of the hands, determine the capillary refill time (press on the nail for 3 seconds and release; if the color returns more slowly than in 3 seconds — pathology), pulse; consciousness (AVPU) and convulsions.
s0413 **D** — dehydration in a child with diarrhea: lethargy, sunken eyes, how fast the skin pinch goes back.
s0414 If there is an emergency sign — start treatment immediately and call a senior physician; if there is a priority sign — send for examination without waiting; if there are no signs — the general queue.
s0415 In a child aged 2 months to 5 years, ask about and check the general danger signs.
s0416 Skill 3. General inspection and assessment of severity
s0417 Ensure the conditions for examination: room temperature 20–22 °C, daylight, warm clean hands.
s0418 During the conversation, observe the child's behavior, movements, voice and response to the surroundings.
s0419 Undress the child appropriately for age and place him in a position convenient for examination.
s0420 Assess consciousness (answers to questions, response to voice and pain).
s0421 Determine the patient's position (active, passive, forced).
s0422 Assess the signs of intoxication: weakness, temperature, skin color, respiratory rate, pulse, vomiting, diuresis.
s0423 Identify the signs of functional impairment of organs (respiratory, heart failure and others).
s0424 Assess the body build, posture, gait and facial expression.
s0425 Look for and count the stigmas of dysembryogenesis in sequence: head, face, ears, mouth, neck, trunk, limbs.
s0426 Determine the grade of the general condition and record it in the case history.
s0427 Skill 4. Drawing up a pedigree (family tree)
s0428 The sick child (proband) is marked — he is indicated with an arrow.
s0429 Males are indicated by a square and females by a circle; the symbol of an affected person is shaded; a line is drawn through the symbol of a deceased person.
s0430 Members of one generation are placed in one horizontal row in birth order starting from the left; generations are indicated by Roman numerals (I — grandparents, II — parents and their brothers and sisters, III — the proband and his brothers and sisters).
s0431 Spouses are joined by a horizontal line and their children by a vertical line; a consanguineous marriage is shown by a double line.
