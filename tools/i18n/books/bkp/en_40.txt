s8032 Computed tomography, MRI and radioisotope investigation
s8033 **CT** — accurately assesses the state of the stomach, liver, gallbladder, pancreas, spleen and intestine without complex preparation; **40–50 times more sensitive** than classic radiography; it is performed after drinking a contrast medium (enhanced CT).
s8034 **MRI** — based on differences in the magnetic properties of tissue molecules; especially effective for studying dynamic processes such as blood flow; **MR cholangiopancreatography** — noninvasive imaging of the biliary and pancreatic ducts.
s8035 **Radioisotope scanning (scintigraphy)** — based on uptake of a radiopharmaceutical by hepatocytes, tumor or inflammatory cells (hepatobiliary scintigraphy, detection of Meckel's diverticulum).
s8036 13.4. Endoscopic investigations and biopsy
s8037 **Endoscopy** — viewing the digestive tube with an endoscope; it allows the diagnosis to be clarified, the depth and extent of the process to be assessed, and **a targeted biopsy** to be taken. In children it is performed with special preparation, often under **anesthesia**.
s8038 **Esophagogastroduodenoscopy (EGDS)** — viewing the esophagus, stomach, duodenum (and jejunum) in one examination. **Indications:** pain syndrome, recurrent vomiting, bleeding from the upper parts; **therapeutic endoscopy** — removal of foreign bodies, polypectomy, sclerotherapy of esophageal and gastric varices, dilatation of strictures.
s8039 **Rectosigmoidoscopy** — the rectum and the distal sigmoid colon; **colonoscopy** — the whole colon. The indication is justified by a specialist (gastroenterologist, proctologist, surgeon); at an early age under anesthesia.
s8040 **Endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP)** — during duodenoscopy the papilla of Vater is cannulated and the biliary and pancreatic ducts are contrasted; disadvantages — complexity, radiation dose, risk of the complication of **acute pancreatitis**.
s8041 **Video capsule endoscopy** — viewing the small intestine (Crohn disease, occult bleeding).

s8044 **Biopsy** — pathomorphological examination of altered tissue: determining the nature of space-occupying lesions, inflammatory and functional diseases (e.g., villous atrophy of the small intestine in celiac disease, detection of H. pylori). By fine-needle aspiration (cytology) or needle biopsy (a piece of tissue).
s8045 Detection of Helicobacter pylori infection
s8046 Detection of H. pylori determines the need for **etiotropic (eradication) therapy** in chronic gastric diseases.
s8047 **Bacteriological** — culture of a gastric biopsy on a special medium;
s8048 **morphological** — staining of histological preparations by Giemsa, toluidine blue, Warthin–Starry;
s8049 **cytological** — staining of biopsy smear-imprints by Giemsa, Gram;
s8050 **urea breath test** — labeled (13C or 14C) urea is drunk, and the isotope released by bacterial urease is detected in the exhaled air (noninvasive);
s8051 **rapid urease test** — the biopsy is placed in a medium with a substrate, buffer and indicator (color change);
s8052 **PCR** — the most reliable method;
s8053 **screening methods** — ELISA (antibodies in the blood), H. pylori antigen in the stool, rapid tests.

s8098 Regurgitation and rumination
s8099 **Regurgitation** — the **weak return, without straining of the abdominal press,** of swallowed food or secretions to the oral cavity and outside. Up to 6–7 months, if it is **infrequent** and occurs not later than 1 hour after feeding — **physiological**.
s8100 **Assessed:** time of onset (from birth, from 2–3 weeks, from 3–4 months), frequency (2–3 times a day, after each feed, more than 10 times), provoking factors (feeding, crying, swaddling), volume (small, “a mouthful”, **“projectile”**), the nature of the contents (fresh or curdled milk, bile, sour or sweetish smell), relation to feeding.
s8101 **Rumination** — for at least 3 months, gastric contents are returned to the mouth by periodic contractions of the abdominal press, diaphragm and tongue muscles, re-chewed and swallowed; in children aged 3–8 months, often with CNS pathology.

s8151 13.6. Main syndromes of digestive system involvement
s8152 Syndrome of impaired digestion in the oral cavity
s8153 Causes: disturbed sucking and swallowing (prematurity, CNS damage, cleft lip and palate, short frenulum of the tongue), stomatitis, dental pathology, salivary gland diseases. Manifestations — difficulty sucking and chewing, drooling, breath odor, refusal of food, the “oral syndrome” on coprology.
s8154 Gastritis syndrome
s8155 Classification: by course — acute, chronic; by extent — diffuse, focal; by secretion — with increased or reduced acidity; by depth — superficial, atrophic, erosive, hypertrophic.
s8156 **Clinical picture:** dull, distending, pressing or stabbing pain in the epigastrium related to food; night and seasonal pain favor increased acidity, an erosive process and antral location. Dyspepsia: nausea, vomiting after eating, belching of air, food and **with a rotten-egg smell** (hypoacid state), **heartburn** (hyperacid state), a feeling of heaviness, reduced appetite, unstable stool — **diarrhea** (hypoacid) or **constipation** (hyperacid). The tongue is coated with a white coating; epigastric tenderness on palpation.
s8157 **Investigations:** coprogram — many unchanged muscle fibers, digestible fiber and connective tissue “+++”; **EGDS** — hyperemia, edema, mucus, fasting fluid, refluxes; H. pylori tests.
s8158 **Functional disorder of the stomach (functional dyspepsia)** — short, inconstant, mild epigastric pains of various character, not related to food; no endoscopic, radiological or morphological changes.
s8159 Pylorospasm and pyloric stenosis
s8160 **Pylorospasm** — **a functional spasm of the gastric outlet** in young children. Predisposition: the cylindrical shape of the stomach, weakness of the cardiac muscles and **strong development of the pyloric muscles**, immaturity of the gastric fundus, pressure of the large liver, imperfect neuroregulatory mechanisms. **From the first days after birth**: inconstant, small-volume, frequent regurgitation and vomiting (immediately or after 15–20 minutes); the volume of the vomit is **less than the milk taken**; weight gain is slow but steady; no exsicosis or oliguria, no visible peristalsis; on X-ray passage is not impaired.
s8161 **Pyloric stenosis** — **a developmental defect** of the gastric outlet (hypertrophy of the muscle layer); in 1:2000 births, mainly **in boys**. The pyloric part is thickened in the shape of an olive (“olive”) (2–3.5 cm long, 1.5–3 cm wide), almost cartilage-like in density. The clinical picture appears **from week 2–3**: persistent **“projectile” vomiting**, large in volume, **the volume of the vomit is always greater than the volume of the feed**, immediately after feeding; the vomit **never contains bile**; the child loses weight quickly, dehydration, oliguria, stool retention (“**hunger stools**” — scanty stool resembling meconium); **“hourglass”**-shaped peristalsis in the epigastrium; a dense **“olive” of 2–4 cm** is palpable at the edge of the right rectus muscle, midway between the umbilicus and the xiphoid process. On X-ray barium is retained in the stomach for **24 hours or longer**, and emptying does not begin in the first 15–20 minutes. **Ultrasound** is the main diagnostic method (pyloric muscle thickness ≥3–4 mm, canal length ≥15–17 mm). Treatment — **surgical (pyloromyotomy)**.
