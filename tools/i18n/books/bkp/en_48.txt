s9578 15.6. Feeding children over 1 year
s9579 **Features:** stomach capacity increases, saliva and digestive juices increase, the chewing apparatus develops, and the number of teeth grows. **At 1–1.5 years** food is minced (ground, mushy), with a gradual transition to thicker food and pieces; the child is first spoon-fed, then taught **to eat independently**.
s9580 **Number of meals:** up to 1.5 years **4–5 times** a day, then **4 times** (breakfast, lunch, afternoon snack, dinner); intervals of 3.5–4 hours.
s9581 **Dairy products** are the main source of protein and calcium: at 1–3 years **≈600 ml** a day, at older ages **≈500 ml** (milk, kefir, yogurt, cottage cheese, cheese); for children over 1 year, **“growing-up” milk formulas** fortified with vitamins and trace elements are recommended.
s9582 **Meat** (beef, poultry, rabbit), **fish** and **eggs** are complete animal proteins; **sausage products** are not recommended.
s9583 **Cereal products** (buckwheat, rice, oats, corn) — giving buckwheat together with milk improves the amino acid balance.
s9584 **Vegetables, fruits, greens** provide vitamins, minerals and fiber; proteins eaten with vegetables are absorbed better (**85%** instead of 70%).
s9585 **Sugar and sweets** are limited; fast food and carbonated drinks are not recommended.

s9613 15.8. Writing and defending the case history
s9614 Structure of the case history
s9615 In pediatric propaedeutics, the educational case history reinforces the skill of **complete examination** of the child and **semiotic analysis** of the data obtained.
s9616 **Title page:** department, patient's full name, age, ward, curator (student) and teacher, period of supervision.
s9617 **Passport section:** full name, date of birth and age, sex, address, parents' full names, place of work and occupation, child-care institution, date of admission, referral diagnosis.
s9618 **Complaints** (in the words of the patient and parents): main and additional; review of systems.
s9619 **History of present illness (anamnesis morbi):** when and how it began, course of development, examinations and treatment received, condition before admission.
s9620 **Life history (anamnesis vitae):** antenatal period (mother's pregnancy — which one, course, illnesses, risk factors), **intranatal** (delivery — term, course, Apgar score), **postnatal** — birth weight and length, first breastfeeding, separation of the umbilical stump, neonatal period; **physical and neuropsychological development** (head holding, sitting, walking, first words, teething); **feeding history** (15.7); **past illnesses**; **preventive vaccinations** (according to the national schedule, reactions); **allergy history**; **epidemiological history** (contact with infectious patients); **family history** (genealogical tree); **social and living conditions**.
s9621 **Physical examination (status praesens):** general condition (degree of severity), consciousness, position, **anthropometry and assessment of physical development** (centile tables or z-score, harmony); then **by systems** — nervous system and sense organs, skin, subcutaneous fat, lymph nodes, musculoskeletal system, respiratory, cardiovascular, digestive, urinary and endocrine systems (results of inspection, palpation, percussion and auscultation, with figures).
s9622 **Semiotic conclusion (identifying pathological changes):** the pathological symptoms found are grouped by systems, combined into **syndromes**, and the **leading syndrome** is identified.
s9623 **Preliminary (provisional) diagnosis** and its justification (in the propaedeutics course — a syndromic diagnosis).
s9624 **Examination plan:** laboratory and instrumental tests and interpretation of their results (compared with age norms).
s9625 **Daily notes (diary)** — dynamics of the child's condition over the days of supervision (temperature, pulse, RR, BP, changes in condition).
s9626 **Epicrisis** — a brief summary.
s9627 **References used**, curator's signature.
s9628 Procedure for defending the case history
s9629 **Assessment criteria:** completeness and orderliness of the case history, correct use of medical terms, literate and precise writing; practical skill in performing the physical examination; correct identification and justification of syndromes; answers to theoretical questions.
s9630 Skill 1. Assessing breastfeeding technique
s9631 Help the mother choose a comfortable position (sitting, lying on her side, “football hold”).
s9632 Assess the child's position: head and body in one line, face toward the breast, body close to the mother, whole body supported.
s9633 Assess attachment: mouth wide open, lower lip turned outward, chin touching the breast, more of the areola visible above.
s9634 Assess sucking: slow, deep sucks with pauses, swallowing is heard.
s9635 Ask the mother about pain and the state of the breasts (cracks, milk stasis); explain mistakes.

s9637 Determine the child's age, current weight and type of feeding.

s9639 Divide the daily volume by the age-appropriate number of feeds to determine the single-feed volume.
s9640 Set the feeding times.
s9641 If complementary foods are given, replace one feed (then a second) with complementary food and draw up a daily menu.
s9642 Skill 3. Preparing an adapted formula
s9643 Wash hands; sterilize the bottle and nipple.
s9644 Pour the required volume of boiled water cooled to 40–50 °C into the bottle.
s9645 Add the indicated amount of dry formula using the scoop from the package (leveled off).
s9646 Shake until homogeneous; check the temperature by dripping onto the inner surface of the wrist (36–37 °C).
s9647 Feed the child in a semi-upright position; keep the bottle neck always full of milk (to avoid swallowing air); discard the leftover.
