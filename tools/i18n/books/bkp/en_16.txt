s3132 Detecting developmental dysplasia of the hip (newborn and infant)
s3133 All newborns are examined:
s3134 **Asymmetry of the skin folds** — the thigh, inguinal and gluteal folds are at different levels and of different depth.
s3135 **Relative shortening of the leg** — in a child lying on the back with the legs bent at the hips and knees, the knees are at different heights.
s3136 **Restricted spreading (abduction) of the thighs** — when the thighs flexed at 90° are spread apart, they do not reach the table surface (normally 80–90° at 1 month). At an early age physiological hypertonia also causes slight restriction.
s3137 **“Slipping” (dislocation-reduction) sign — the Marx–Ortolani test**: the child lies on the back with the legs flexed at 90° at the hips and knees; the doctor places the thumbs on the inner surface of the thighs and the other fingers on the outer (greater trochanter) surface and slowly spreads the thighs apart — as the dislocated femoral head enters the socket, **a “click”** is felt.
s3138 External rotation of the foot.
s3139 Confirmation — **hip ultrasound** (Graf method, at 1–3 months), and X-ray in older children.
s3140 Detecting developmental dysplasia of the hip **in the first weeks of life** and treating it with wide swaddling or special splints (the Pavlik harness) ensures full recovery of the joint. If it is detected late (after the child starts walking) — limping, “duck gait”, and surgical treatment is needed.
s3141 Additional methods of examination
s3142 **X-ray** — fractures, anomalies, inflammation, tumors, degenerative processes; **bone age** (wrist); in rickets **cup-shaped widening** of the metaphyses, osteoporosis.
s3143 **Ultrasound** (developmental dysplasia of the hip, intra-articular fluid), **CT, MRI**, **densitometry** (bone mineral density), scintigraphy.
s3144 **Biochemistry:** serum **calcium** (total 2.25–2.75 mmol/l; ionized 1.1–1.3), **phosphorus** (1.3–2.3 mmol/l at 1–2 years), **alkaline phosphatase** (raised in rickets), **25(OH)D**, parathyroid hormone; urinary calcium and phosphorus (Sulkowitch test).
s3145 **Immunological:** CRP, rheumatoid factor, ASO, antinuclear antibodies, HLA-B27.
s3146 Analysis of **synovial fluid**, **biopsy** of the synovial membrane and bone.

s3151 5.5. Semiotics of osteoarticular system involvement
s3152 Skeletal developmental anomalies
s3153 **Skull:** craniostenosis (premature closure of the sutures), “wolf's palate” (non-fusion of the maxillary and palatine bones), micro- and macrocephaly.
s3154 **Vertebrae:** non-fusion of the vertebral arch — **spina bifida** (most often lumbosacral), sacralization, lumbarization.
s3155 **Ribs:** an extra cervical rib, absent or fused ribs, clefts of the sternum.
s3156 **Limbs: congenital dislocation of the hip** (the most common), amelia (absence of a limb), phocomelia, poly- and syndactyly, **congenital clubfoot**, underdevelopment of the clavicle.
s3157 **Systemic skeletal dysplasias: achondroplasia** (short limbs, large head, depressed nasal bridge), **osteogenesis imperfecta** (brittle bones — multiple fractures, **blue sclerae**, deafness).
s3158 Bone and joint pain
s3159 **Ossalgia** (bone pain) — fracture, **osteomyelitis**, tumor, **leukemia** (night pain, pain on tapping).
s3160 **“Growing pains”** — at 4–12 years, in the evening and at night in the legs (shins, popliteal area), bilateral; they pass by morning; there are no local changes and no changes in the blood count.
s3161 **Arthralgia** — joint pain without objective changes (infections, onset of rheumatic diseases).
s3162 **Perthes disease** — aseptic necrosis of the femoral head: hip pain increasing with load, limping, the child feels well.
s3163 **Enthesopathies** — Osgood–Schlatter disease (tibial tuberosity), juvenile spondyloarthritis, reactive arthritis.
s3164 Rickets
s3165 **Rickets** — a disorder of bone formation and mineralization in the growing body due to **vitamin D deficiency** and disturbed phosphorus-calcium metabolism. Most often **from 2 months to 2 years**. The bones show signs of **softening (osteomalacia)** and **osteoid tissue hyperplasia**.

s3184 **Rickets-like diseases** (vitamin D-resistant rickets, de Toni–Debré–Fanconi syndrome, renal tubular acidosis) — tubulopathies developing after 2 years that do not respond to usual doses of vitamin D, with impaired intestinal absorption of calcium or urinary loss of calcium and phosphorus.
s3185 Spasmophilia (infantile tetany)
s3186 In the initial period of rickets or during recovery (most often in spring, from 6 months to 2 years), **neuromuscular excitability increases** as a result of **a fall in ionized calcium** in the blood.
s3187 Signs of **latent tetany**:
s3188 **Chvostek's sign** — contraction of the facial muscles (lip, nasal ala, eyelid) when the area between the zygomatic arch and the corner of the mouth (where the facial nerve emerges) is tapped with a hammer;
s3189 **Trousseau's sign** — when the neurovascular bundle of the upper arm is compressed (with a cuff) for 2–3 minutes, the hand goes into spasm and takes the **“obstetrician's hand”** position;
s3190 **Lust's sign** — outward turning and flexion of the foot when the area below the head of the fibula (n. peroneus) is tapped with a hammer;
s3191 **Maslov's sign** — a brief arrest of breathing when the skin is lightly pricked with a needle.
s3192 **Manifest tetany** — **laryngospasm** (crowing inspiration like a “rooster's crow”, cyanosis), **carpopedal spasm** (spasm of the hands and feet), generalized **eclamptic seizures**.
s3193 Emergency care for laryngospasm and seizures: lay the child down, splash cold water on the face, fresh air, clear the mouth and nose; **10% calcium gluconate** (or calcium chloride) slowly intravenously; if the seizures continue — diazepam. Vitamin D must not be prescribed without calcium preparations (it worsens the seizures).
s3194 Hypermobility
s3195 **Joint hypermobility** — a range of motion greater than normal: **connective tissue dysplasias** (Ehlers–Danlos and Marfan syndromes), muscle hypotonia (rickets, Down syndrome). It is assessed by the Beighton criteria (bending the little finger back more than 90°, touching the forearm with the thumb, hyperextension of the elbow and knee by more than 10°, placing the palms on the floor without bending the knees).
s3196 Arthritis
s3197 Signs of **arthritis**: **defiguration and/or deformity** of the joint, **swelling** of the soft tissues, local **hyperemia and hyperthermia**, **pain** on palpation, **restriction** of active and passive movements.

s3227 5.6. The muscular system
s3228 Anatomical and physiological features
s3229 In the newborn the number of muscles is the same as in adults (more than 600), but **the muscle mass** is **≈23–25%** of the body mass (27% at 8 years, **32–33%** at 15 years, **40–44%** in adults). The mass increases not through the number of muscle fibers but through their **thickening**.
s3230 The muscle fibers are thin and multinucleated, with few contractile proteins and preserved **fetal myosin**; they contain much water and little protein, fat and minerals.
s3231 The muscle relief is smooth and becomes defined at 5–7 years; **the fasciae** are delicate and the aponeuroses weak — in the newborn cephalohematoma, **umbilical hernia** (the umbilical ring is not fully closed), forward protrusion of the abdomen (until 3–5 years).
s3232 In the newborn the muscles **do not relax even during sleep** — this produces heat and stimulates the development of muscle tissue.
s3233 **Physiological hypertonia** — in the first 2–4 months **the tone of the flexor muscles** predominates over the extensors: it persists in the arms **until 2–2.5 months** and in the legs **until 3–4 months**. That is why the newborn lies in the “fetal position”.
s3234 In preterm babies (less than 30 weeks) — **generalized hypotonia**: the limbs are extended; at 30–34 weeks the legs flex, and flexion of the arms after 34 weeks — one of the criteria for assessing **gestational age**.
s3235 Muscle development is uneven: first the muscles of **the shoulder and forearm**, then the muscles of the hand (hand movements become well coordinated **at 6–7 years** — readiness for writing). The muscles holding the body upright grow rapidly from 7 years, especially **at 12–16 years**. Precision of movement improves from 10 years and speed at 14 years.
s3236 Muscle strength is higher in boys; relative strength changes little until 6–7 years and increases rapidly **at 13–14 years**.

s3241 Examination of the muscular system
s3242 **Complaints:** muscle pain (**myalgia**), weakness, rapid fatigue, reduced muscle strength, difficulty walking, frequent falls, inability to climb stairs. **History:** muscle diseases in the family, relation to infection, injury or medication.
s3243 **1. Degree of development** (inspection and palpation — symmetric areas at the same level are compared):
s3244 **good** — at rest the contours of the muscles of the trunk and limbs are clearly visible, the abdomen is drawn in, and the shoulder blades are close to the chest; on tension the relief becomes more pronounced;
s3245 **moderate** — the trunk muscles are moderately and the limb muscles well developed;
s3246 **poor** — at rest the contours are not visible and hardly change on tension; the lower abdomen sags, **the shoulder blades lag behind the chest (“winged scapulae”)**.
s3247 To detect asymmetry, the circumference of the limbs is measured with a tape at the same level.
s3248 **2. Muscle tone** — assessed by the resistance to passive movements (the limb should be maximally relaxed). The first impression is obtained from **the child's posture**.
