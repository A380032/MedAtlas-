s8884 The “critical periods” of immune system development
s8885 **Critical period 1 — the neonatal period (1st month):** **physiological depression** of the immune system; passive protection by maternal IgG; low phagocytosis, complement and interferon — susceptibility to pyogenic and gram-negative flora and viruses, a tendency of infection to **generalize** (sepsis). The first crossover of the leukocyte differential on day 4–5. **Critical period 2 — 3–6 months:** breakdown of maternal antibodies; the primary immune response (IgM) predominates, **immunological memory is not formed** — whooping cough and measles run an atypical course, reinfection is possible; local immunity is insufficient; allergic reactions and food allergy appear. **Critical period 3 — the 2nd year:** active exploration of the world, increasing contacts; local immunity remains insufficient; the response to the polysaccharide antigens of pneumococcus and Haemophilus is weak; most primary immunodeficiencies become manifest; diseases of the ENT organs and airways recur. **Critical period 4 — 4–6 years:** the second crossover of the leukocyte differential; IgG and
s8886 IgM reach adult levels, secretory IgA is still low; parasitic invasions, atopic and immune complex diseases, late immunodeficiencies. **Critical period 5 — puberty (12–16 years in boys, 11–15 years in girls):** involution of the thymus, the mass of the lymphoid organs decreases; the influence of sex hormones — autoimmune and lymphoproliferative diseases increase.

s8907 14.7. Examination of the immune system
s8908 **History and inspection:** frequent and severe infections, recurrences, opportunistic infections, reactions to vaccination (complications after BCG), allergic diseases, family history (death from infection at an early age); examination of the lymph nodes, tonsils and spleen (see topic 4); **absence or hypoplasia** of the tonsils and lymph nodes — a sign of humoral immunodeficiency.
s8909 Immunogram (laboratory methods):
s8910 **Cell-mediated immunity:** the number of lymphocytes and T lymphocytes (at 7–14 years T cells make up **≈70%** of lymphocytes, ≈1400/µl), the **CD3, CD4, CD8** subpopulations and **the CD4/CD8 ratio** (normal 1.5–2), the blast transformation reaction with phytohemagglutinin, cytokines; **skin tests** (Mantoux); chest X-ray (thymic hypoplasia or thymomegaly).
s8911 **Humoral immunity:** the number of B lymphocytes (CD19, CD20; ≈25%, ≈500/µl), **serum IgG, IgA, IgM, IgE**, secretory IgA (in saliva), circulating immune complexes, specific post-vaccination antibodies.
s8912 **Complement system:** total hemolytic activity (CH50), C3, C4, C1 inhibitor.
s8913 **Phagocytosis:** the neutrophil count (**neutropenia** — absolute count <1500/µl), the phagocytic index and number, **the NBT test** (nitroblue tetrazolium — bactericidal activity), chemotaxis, adhesion molecules (CD11/CD18).
s8914 14.8. Semiotics of immune system involvement
s8915 **3 types** of immune system involvement: 1) quantitative or functional insufficiency of a component of the immune system (**primary and secondary immunodeficiencies**); 2) impaired recognition of self antigens (**autoimmune diseases**); 3) a hyperreactive immune response (**allergic reactions**).
s8916 The 10 warning signs of primary immunodeficiency
s8917 **6–8 or more** episodes of otitis a year.
s8918 **4–6** severe sinusitis episodes a year.
s8919 **More than 2** severe pneumonias a year.
s8920 Recurrent **deep abscesses** of the skin or internal organs.
s8921 **More than 2** severe infections (osteomyelitis, sepsis, meningitis).
s8922 **Persistent oral candidiasis** or fungal infection of the skin in a child over 1 year.
s8923 The need for **2 months or more** of antibiotic treatment (ineffective).
s8924 The need for **intravenous antibiotics** to clear an infection.
s8925 **Primary immunodeficiency** or deaths from infection at an early age in the family history.
s8926 **Failure to gain weight and growth retardation** in an infant.
s8927 If there are two or more signs — an immunology consultation and an immunogram are needed. **Live vaccines** (BCG, oral poliomyelitis vaccine, measles) are **contraindicated** in cell-mediated and combined immunodeficiency — risk of generalized vaccine infection.

s8950 14.9. Clinical section
s8951 **1–3 weeks after tonsillitis, scarlet fever or pyoderma** — the “**nephritic triad**”: **edema** (face and eyelids in the morning), **arterial hypertension** (headache, vomiting; in a severe case — **eclampsia**: seizures, loss of consciousness), **urine the color of “meat washings”** (macrohematuria) and oliguria. Laboratory: proteinuria 1–2 g/l, erythrocyte casts; in the blood **raised ASO**, **reduced complement C3** (recovers in 6–8 weeks), raised ESR. Treatment: bed rest, restriction of salt and fluid, penicillin, antihypertensive drugs; the main dangers are hypertensive encephalopathy, acute heart failure and acute kidney injury.

s8959 Skill 2. Bimanual palpation of the kidneys (Obraztsov–Strazhesko)
s8960 The child lies on the back with the legs slightly bent; the doctor on the right; warm hands.
