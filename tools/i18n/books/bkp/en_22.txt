s4371 **The “traffic light” system: green zone** — ≥80% of the personal best (good control); **yellow** — 50–80% (step up treatment); **red** — <50% (emergency care).
s4372 Blood gases and pulse oximetry
s4373 **Blood gases** (capillary or arterial blood, micro-Astrup apparatus): **PaO~2~** (normal in children 80–100 mm Hg), **PaCO~2~** (35–45 mm), **pH** (7.35–7.45), buffer bases (BE).
s4374 **Pulse oximetry (oxyhemometry)** — continuous, painless transcutaneous measurement of blood oxygen saturation (**SpO~2~**); **94–98%** in a healthy child. **SpO~2~ <92–94%** is an indication for oxygen therapy (pneumonia, bronchiolitis, asthma attack).
s4375 7.2. Radiological methods of examination
s4376 **Chest X-ray** — the most widely used instrumental method; in frontal (anterior) and lateral projections. It detects pneumonia, pleurisy, pneumothorax, atelectasis, foreign bodies (radiopaque), thymomegaly and developmental defects.
s4377 **Fluoroscopy** — real-time observation of diaphragmatic movement and mediastinal shift (high radiation dose — rarely used).
s4378 **Tomography** (layer-by-layer) — the lung root, lymph nodes, cavities.
s4379 **Computed tomography** (spiral, high-resolution CT) — the most accurate method: bronchiectasis, interstitial changes, small foci, tumors; with contrast when necessary.
s4380 **Bronchography** — X-ray after injecting a contrast medium into the bronchi: **bronchiectasis**, bronchial defects, tumors (now mostly replaced by CT).
s4381 **Angiopulmonography**, **radioisotope scanning (scintigraphy)** — pulmonary circulation and ventilation.
s4382 **X-ray of the paranasal sinuses** — sinusitis.
s4383 **Ultrasound of the pleural cavity and lungs** — detecting fluid, marking the puncture site.
s4384 **Fluorography** — mass preventive screening (tuberculosis, tumor); because of the high radiation dose only **after 15 years**.
s4385 Order of reading an X-ray:
s4386 Determine the projection (frontal, lateral).
s4387 Correct positioning of the child — the clavicles at the same level, the spinous processes in the middle of the sternum.
s4388 Exposure of the X-ray — if the disc between the 3rd and 4th thoracic vertebrae is visible — normal.
s4389 The chest skeleton (ribs, spine, clavicles).
s4390 **Transparency** of the lung fields on both sides.
s4391 Position of the diaphragmatic domes, the costophrenic sinuses, the gastric air bubble.
s4392 **Lung roots** — normally from the anterior end of rib II to rib IV, structured.
s4393 **Lung markings** — normally visible in the inner 2/3 of the lung field; when increased they reach the periphery.
s4394 Pleura.
s4395 Mediastinum (cardiac shadow, thymus, position of the trachea).
s4396 Conclusion. **Reduced transparency (opacity)** — pneumonia, atelectasis, pleurisy, tumor; **increased transparency (lucency)** — emphysema, pneumothorax, bronchial obstruction.

s4399 7.3. Endoscopic methods
s4400 **Rhinoscopy** (anterior and posterior) and **pharyngoscopy** — the nose, adenoids, pharynx.
s4401 **Laryngoscopy** — at an early age direct laryngoscopy (often under anesthesia), in older children indirect (with a mirror); croup, laryngeal anomalies, papillomatosis.
s4402 **Bronchoscopy** — **up to 3–4 years** with a rigid bronchoscope **under anesthesia**, in older children **fiberoptic bronchoscopy** under local anesthesia. Its possibilities: viewing the inner surface of the trachea and bronchi; removing **a foreign body**; suctioning pus, blood and mucus (sanitation); detecting tumors and defects; **biopsy**; obtaining bronchial washings (bronchoalveolar lavage); direct administration of drugs.
s4403 **Thoracoscopy** — viewing the pleural cavity (pleural biopsy, treatment of empyema).

s4406 7.4. Laboratory methods
s4407 **Complete blood count:** in bacterial pneumonia **neutrophilic leukocytosis**, a left shift, raised ESR; in viral infection — leukopenia or normal, lymphocytosis; in bronchial asthma and helminthiasis — **eosinophilia**; in whooping cough — marked **leukocytosis and lymphocytosis**.
s4408 **Inflammatory markers:** C-reactive protein, procalcitonin (bacterial infection).
s4409 **Microbiological examination:** culture and bacterioscopy of nasopharyngeal swabs (including for diphtheria — Loeffler's bacillus), sputum, bronchial secretions and pleural fluid; antibiotic sensitivity; PCR and rapid tests for viruses; for tuberculosis — mycobacteria in sputum or gastric washings.
s4410 **Sweat test** — sweat electrolytes (chloride >60 mmol/l) — diagnosis of **cystic fibrosis**.
s4411 **Allergological:** skin tests (scarification, prick test, intradermal), provocation tests; total and specific **IgE** in the blood.
s4412 **Diagnosis of tuberculosis:** Mantoux test, Diaskintest, QuantiFERON test.

s4426 7.5. Semiotics of the main respiratory symptoms
s4427 **Main idea:** cough indicates **the level of the lesion** (pharynx, larynx, trachea, bronchus, pleura), while its timing and sputum indicate **the nature of the process**.
s4428 **Runny nose:** the discharge is serous, mucous, mucopurulent or blood-stained. Nasal congestion without discharge — viral and allergic disease, adenoids. **Unilateral purulent, fetid discharge** in a small child — **a foreign body in the nose**. **Nosebleeds** — hemorrhagic diatheses, leukemia, hypoplastic anemia, arterial hypertension, nasal polyps.

s4449 **Sputum:** “a mouthful” of purulent sputum — rupture of an abscess or a suppurating cyst into a bronchus, bronchiectasis. **Hemoptysis** — destructive pneumonia, tuberculous cavern, tumor, hemorrhagic diathesis, pulmonary hemosiderosis.
s4450 **Voice:** a weak squealing cry or no cry in a newborn — asphyxia, CNS damage; hoarse voice and aphonia — acute laryngitis, diphtheria; nasal speech — adenoids; a low “croaky” voice — hypothyroidism.
s4451 **Dyspnea:** inspiratory — narrowing of the upper airways (croup, foreign body, retropharyngeal abscess); expiratory — bronchial obstruction (asthma, obstructive bronchitis, bronchiolitis); mixed — pneumonia, pleurisy, respiratory failure, heart failure.
s4452 **Chest pain:** increased by deep breathing and coughing — pleurisy, lobar pneumonia (pleuropneumonia); localized, painful on pressure — myositis, intercostal neuralgia, rib injury; in diaphragmatic pleurisy and lower lobe pneumonia the pain **radiates to the abdomen** (risk of mistaking it for acute appendicitis).
s4453 **Cyanosis:** of the nasolabial triangle and around the eyes — mild to moderate respiratory failure; diffuse, gray-marbled — severe respiratory failure. **“Drumstick fingers”** and **“watch-glass” nails** — chronic hypoxia (bronchiectasis, cystic fibrosis).
s4454 7.6. Syndromes of consolidation (infiltration) of lung tissue and atelectasis
s4455 **Main idea: consolidation** (open bronchus) → vocal fremitus and bronchophony are **increased**, bronchial breathing; **atelectasis** (closed bronchus) → they are **weakened**, breathing is faint. On percussion the sound is shortened in both.
s4456 Infiltration syndrome
s4457 It occurs in: **pneumonia**, pulmonary infarction, pulmonary hemorrhage, tuberculous infiltrate, eosinophilic infiltrate.

s4490 7.7. Pleural syndromes
s4491 **Main idea:** in the pleura **both fluid and air** block sound — vocal fremitus, bronchophony and breathing are **weakened or absent**; the difference is in percussion: fluid — **dull**, air — **tympanic**. In both, the mediastinum shifts **toward the healthy side**.
s4492 Dry (fibrinous) pleurisy
s4493 The pleural layers are covered with fibrin. Signs: **stabbing pain** that increases with breathing and coughing; the child lies on the affected side (to restrict movement); the affected side lags behind during breathing; percussion is almost unchanged (mobility of the lower edge is restricted); breathing is weakened because of the pain; **pleural friction rub** (mainly low on the midaxillary line). In diaphragmatic pleurisy — pain radiating to the abdomen and shoulder, hiccups, **Mussy's sign**.
s4494 Syndrome of fluid accumulation in the pleural cavity
s4495 It occurs in: exudative pleurisy (parapneumonic, tuberculous), **empyema** (purulent), hydrothorax (heart failure, nephrotic syndrome), hemothorax, chylothorax.

s4534 7.8. Airway and other lung syndromes
s4535 **Main idea: narrowing of the upper airways** → **inspiratory** dyspnea (croup); **narrowing of the small bronchi** → **expiratory** dyspnea, box-like sound, whistling rales (obstruction).
