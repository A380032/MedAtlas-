s3705 **The respiratory center** (medulla oblongata and pons): the medullary part (alternation of inspiration and expiration), the apneustic part (inspiratory spasm), the pneumotaxic part (inhibits the apneustic part). The pneumotaxic part matures **by the end of the 1st year** — at an early age breathing is **arrhythmic**; **apnea** occurs in preterm babies. Up to 3 months, pauses in breathing of **up to 8–10 seconds** are physiological; apnea longer than 20 seconds or accompanied by bradycardia and cyanosis is pathological.
s3706 6.3. History taking
s3707 **Main idea:** the leading sign of respiratory disease is **cough**; its character often indicates the level of the lesion.
s3708 **Main complaints:** runny nose (nasal congestion, discharge), cough, sputum, dyspnea and attacks of suffocation, chest pain, hemoptysis, fever, weakness, headache, change of voice; in small children — refusal to feed, restlessness, vomiting.

s3733 6.4. Inspection
s3734 **Main idea:** five things are assessed on inspection — **skin color, chest shape, type of breathing, respiratory rate and rhythm, signs of dyspnea**.
s3735 **General inspection:** cyanosis (lips, nasolabial triangle, around the eyes), pallor; **flaring of the nasal alae** (pneumonia in an infant); froth at the corner of the mouth (pneumonia in the newborn); herpes on the lips (ARVI, lobar pneumonia); redness of one cheek (lobar pneumonia on that side); “**drumstick fingers**” and “**watch-glass**” nails (chronic hypoxia); forced position — **orthopnea** (in an asthma attack the child sits leaning on the hands), in pleurisy the patient lies on the affected side. Nasal discharge, nasal breathing. Inspection of the pharynx and tonsils is performed **last** so as not to upset the child.
s3736 **Chest shape.** In the newborn it is barrel-shaped (the sagittal dimension equals the transverse one); with age the transverse dimension increases. In an older child there are three types: **normosthenic** (conical, epigastric angle ≈90°), **asthenic** (long, narrow, angle <90°, winged scapulae), **hypersthenic** (cylindrical, angle >90°). Pathological shapes: **barrel-shaped (emphysematous)** — asthma, emphysema; **funnel chest** and “**pigeon chest**”, **Harrison's groove** — rickets; **asymmetric** — in exudative pleurisy the affected side bulges, in chronic pneumonia and pneumofibrosis it sinks.

s3782 6.5. Palpation of the chest
s3783 **Main idea:** palpation confirms the findings of inspection; it is performed with both hands over **symmetric** areas.
s3784 Palpation determines:
s3785 **Tenderness** — superficial (muscle, rib, nerve — myositis, neuralgia) or deep pleural. Pleural pain increases with breathing and decreases when the chest is compressed and when bending toward the affected side (in neuralgia it increases instead). **Mussy's sign** — pain on pressure between the heads of the sternocleidomastoid muscle — diaphragmatic pleurisy.
s3786 **Skin fold thickness** — thickening on the affected side (**Filatov's sign**) — exudative pleurisy, pneumonia.
s3787 **Resistance (elasticity)** — assessed by compressing the chest with the palms front-to-back and from the sides. If it compresses easily — elastic (normal; increased in rickets); if it does not compress — **rigid** (exudative pleurisy, tumor, ossification of the costal cartilages at an older age).
s3788 **Vocal fremitus (fremitus vocalis)** — transmission of the vibration of the vocal cords to the chest wall.
s3789 **In addition:** smoothing and bulging of the intercostal spaces (exudative pleurisy), rachitic “rosary”, subcutaneous **crepitus** (subcutaneous emphysema — crunching like snow), pleural friction felt by hand.
s3790 **Technique of testing vocal fremitus:** both palms are placed on symmetric areas of the chest and moved alternately from the apices to the lower parts. The child says **words with the letter “R”** (“tractor”, “thirty-three”, “ninety-nine”). In an infant who does not speak — during crying or screaming (low accuracy). Normally it is **the same** on both sides.

s3802 6.6. Percussion of the lungs
s3803 **Main idea:** in the child the stroke is **light (quiet)**, because the chest is thin and elastic — with a strong stroke distant tissues also vibrate and small foci are “lost”. Percussion is always performed **from the clear sound toward the dull sound**.
s3804 **General rules:** a quiet, warm room; the doctor's hands warm and dry, nails short. For anterior percussion the doctor stands on the child's right side, for posterior percussion on the left side. The child's position: over 2 years — standing or sitting; the mother holds an infant upright; up to 2–3 months — lying on the back for the front, and lying with the abdomen on the doctor's palm for the back. For posterior percussion the child bends the head slightly and crosses the arms in front (the shoulder blades move apart); for the lateral areas the hands are placed on the back of the neck. If the child cries — percuss during the deep inspiration between cries. The child's body must be straight and symmetric (otherwise the sound will be asymmetric).
s3805 Methods:
s3806 **Indirect (finger-to-finger)** — the 3rd finger of the left hand (pleximeter finger) is placed firmly in the intercostal space **parallel** to the ribs; the 3rd finger of the right hand (plessor finger) gives 2 short strokes on the middle phalanx of the pleximeter; movement only at the wrist joint.
s3807 **Direct** — the fingertip strikes the chest wall directly; used in infants and in hypotrophy. **Obraztsov's method** — the index finger of the right hand slips off the middle finger and strikes the chest.
s3808 Comparative percussion
s3809 The sound is compared at **symmetric** points of the chest; normally it is the same. A difference is a sign of pathology.
s3810 Order:
s3811 **From the front:** the supraclavicular fossa (finger parallel to the clavicle), directly over the clavicle, infraclavicular; along the midclavicular line in the 2nd–3rd intercostal spaces (lower down on the left is the heart — only on the right).
s3812 **From the sides:** along the midaxillary lines (hands on the back of the neck).
s3813 **From behind:** suprascapular (finger horizontal), interscapular (finger **parallel to the spine**), subscapular (finger horizontal along the intercostal spaces).

s3837 Topographic percussion
s3838 Purpose — to determine the lung borders. **Rules:** the pleximeter finger is placed **parallel** to the border sought; it is moved from the clear sound toward the dull sound; the border is marked along **the edge of the finger facing the clear sound**; quiet percussion is used.
s3839 **Height of the lung apices** (in children over 5–7 years; in younger children the apices lie behind the clavicle and cannot be determined):
s3840 **From the front** — the finger is placed in the supraclavicular fossa parallel to the clavicle and moved upward and medially. Normal: **2–4 cm above the clavicle**.
s3841 **From behind** — from the middle of the spine of the scapula toward the spinous process of **the 7th cervical vertebra**. Normal: **at the level of the C~VII~ process**.

s3866 **Changes in the borders:** downward displacement — emphysema, asthma attack, low position of the diaphragm (visceroptosis). s3867 **Mobility (excursion) of the lower edge** — in children who can hold their breath: the border is determined on the middle or posterior axillary line during quiet breathing → the child takes a deep breath and holds it, the border is found → the child breathes out deeply and holds it, it is found again. The distance between the two marks is the excursion, normally **2–6 cm** (depending on age). It is reduced in emphysema, asthma, pneumofibrosis, inflammation, pleural adhesions; it is absent with fluid or air in the pleura, complete fusion of the pleural layers, diaphragmatic paralysis.
