s9379 15.2. Breastfeeding
s9380 Physiology of lactation
s9381 **The prolactin reflex (milk production reflex):** when the baby sucks, the nerve endings in the nipple are stimulated → **prolactin** is released from the anterior pituitary → milk is produced in the alveoli. Prolactin is produced more **at night** — **night feeding** maintains lactation; frequent feeding increases milk.
s9382 **The oxytocin reflex (milk ejection reflex):** under the influence of sucking, seeing the baby and hearing its voice, **oxytocin** is released from the posterior pituitary → the myoepithelial cells around the alveoli contract → milk flows; oxytocin also accelerates **involution of the uterus**. Pain, stress and anxiety **inhibit** this reflex.
s9383 Composition of breast milk
s9384 **Colostrum** — at the end of pregnancy and in **the first 3–5 days** after delivery; yellowish, thick; rich in **protein** (mainly immunoglobulins — **secretory IgA**), leukocytes, vitamin A, carotene and trace elements; high in calories — even a small amount meets the newborn's needs; **“the first vaccination”**, it speeds up the passage of meconium (mild laxative effect).
s9385 **Transitional milk** — **from day 4–5 to week 2–3**; protein decreases, fat and lactose increase.
s9386 **Mature milk** — **from week 2–3**. Within one feed **“foremilk”** (watery, rich in lactose and water — quenches thirst) and **“hindmilk”** (rich in fat, high in calories — satisfies hunger) are distinguished; therefore the baby should **fully empty** one breast.

s9397 Advantages of breastfeeding
s9398 **Proteins** are finely dispersed (albumin, α-lactalbumin) and easily digested; amino acids (taurine, cystine) are in the optimal ratio for CNS development; there is no antigenic property — **low risk of allergy**.
s9399 **Fats** — many **unsaturated fatty acids** (omega-3, omega-6, long-chain — arachidonic and docosahexaenoic acids) — formation of the brain, retina and cell membranes; **breast milk lipase** breaks down fats already in the stomach.
s9400 **Carbohydrates** — mainly **β-lactose** and **oligosaccharides (bifidus factor)** — promote the growth of **bifidoflora** and improve calcium absorption.
s9401 **Enzymes** (amylase, lipase, proteases) — facilitate digestion.
s9402 **Trace elements** (iron, zinc, calcium) with **high bioavailability** (iron bound to lactoferrin is ≈50% absorbed); an optimal Ca:P ratio (2:1).
s9403 **Protective factors:** secretory IgA, IgG, IgM, **lactoferrin**, **lysozyme**, interferon, complement, macrophages, lymphocytes, neutrophils, bifidus factor — they reduce the risk of intestinal and respiratory infections, otitis, necrotizing enterocolitis, and later of obesity, diabetes mellitus and allergic diseases.
s9404 The milk is **sterile, warm and ready**, and its composition adapts to the baby's needs; it is economically cheap.
s9405 **A psychoemotional bond between mother and baby** forms; in the mother the risk of postpartum bleeding, breast and ovarian cancer and anemia decreases; **lactational amenorrhea** (natural contraception).

s9408 Rules of early breastfeeding and successful breastfeeding
s9409 Putting the baby to the breast **in the delivery room, within the first 30 minutes after delivery** (if there are no contraindications) and **“skin-to-skin” contact**:
s9410 stimulates the rapid onset and long duration of lactation;
s9411 ensures colonization of the skin and intestine with the mother's beneficial microflora; colostrum — immunological protection;
s9412 accelerates **involution of the uterus** and reduces postpartum bleeding;
s9413 prevents purulent-inflammatory diseases in mother and baby;
s9414 quickly establishes the psychoemotional bond.
s9415 **Breastfeeding technique — signs of a correct position:** the baby's head and body are in one line, the face is turned to the breast, the body is close to the mother, and the mother supports the baby's whole body. **Signs of correct attachment:** the mouth is wide open, **the lower lip is turned outward**, **the chin touches the breast**, more of the areola is visible above than below; the baby sucks slowly and deeply, swallowing is heard, and the mother feels no pain.
s9416 Feeding regimen
s9417 Today **“free feeding” — breastfeeding on demand** is recommended (not chaotic): 8–12 (≈10) times a day in the 1st month, including night feeds; later the baby settles into a regimen.

s9447 Hypogalactia
s9448 **Hypogalactia** — reduced secretory activity of the mammary glands during lactation. **Primary (true)** — hormonal disorders (deficiency of prolactin, oxytocin), endocrine diseases of the mother — rare (≈5%). **Secondary** — the majority: complications of pregnancy and delivery, maternal diseases, **stress**, unwillingness to breastfeed, **violation of the rules of breastfeeding** (late and infrequent breastfeeding, feeding on a strict schedule, a night break, pacifiers and teats, incorrect technique), inadequate nutrition and rest of the mother.
s9449 **Early** (within 10–14 days after delivery) and **late** (after 2–3 weeks).
s9450 **Degrees:** I — milk deficit <25%; II — 26–50%; III — 51–75%; IV — >75%.
s9451 **Lactation crises** — temporary unexplained decreases in milk (at 3–6 weeks, at 3, 4, 7, 8 months), lasting 3–4 days.
s9452 **Measures:** breastfeeding **frequently, on demand**, from **both breasts** at each feed (keeping night feeds); daytime rest and sleep for the mother, complete and varied nutrition, a warm drink and lactogenic teas 15–20 minutes before feeding; a positive emotional climate in the family; breast massage and a warm shower before feeding. Only when all possibilities have been exhausted, in **grade III–IV** hypogalactia, is **supplementary feeding** with an adapted formula (by spoon, cup or syringe — without a teat) prescribed.
s9453 15.3. Mixed and artificial feeding
s9454 Reasons for transfer to artificial feeding
s9455 Grade III–IV hypogalactia or agalactia; contraindications to breastfeeding; absence of the mother; the mother's own choice. **Artificial feeding is a metabolic stress**: protein load, changes in the intestinal microflora, increased risk of infection and allergy.
s9456 Breast milk substitutes
s9457 **Principles of adaptation:** reducing the protein content **by 2.5 times**, bringing the whey protein to casein ratio closer to that of breast milk (60:40), adding taurine and nucleotides; optimizing the fat composition with **unsaturated fatty acids** (vegetable oils, ARA, DHA); increasing carbohydrates **1.5–2 times** (lactose, prebiotics — galacto- and fructo-oligosaccharides); fortification with vitamins (12), iron, iodine, zinc, copper and selenium; reducing the content of Ca, K and Na.
s9458 **Starter (formula 1)** — 0–6 months (highly adapted);
s9459 **follow-on (formula 2)** — 6–12 months (more protein and calories);
s9460 **formula 3** — over 1 year;
s9461 **fermented adapted formulas** — from 3–4 weeks, in a 1:1 or 2:1 ratio with a sweet formula (probiotic effect, in dysbiosis, atopic dermatitis, intestinal diseases);
s9462 **therapeutic (special) formulas**.

s9479 Rules of artificial feeding
s9480 Feed with one adapted formula; **do not change the formula often**; switch gradually to a new formula over **5–7 days**.
s9481 The formula is prepared **fresh before each feed** according to the instructions on the package, at a temperature of **36–37 °C**; the teat hole is small (milk should drip when the bottle is turned over); the utensils are sterile.
s9482 The feeding interval is often **3–3.5 hours** (formula leaves the stomach more slowly), 6–7 times a day, with a 6-hour night break; overfeeding is avoided.
s9483 Complementary foods are also introduced to formula-fed children **at 4–6 months** (they may be introduced earlier than in breastfed children).
s9484 Rules of mixed feeding
s9485 First **breastfeed from both breasts**, and only after the breasts are fully emptied give supplementary feeding with formula (**top-up**) — to maintain lactation;
s9486 the top-up is given with **a spoon, cup** or a teat with a small hole (a teat weakens the baby's sucking — “nipple confusion”);
s9487 the volume of the top-up is determined by **test weighing** or by calculating the food volume;
s9488 measures to increase breast milk are continued.
s9489 15.4. Complementary foods
s9490 **Complementary foods** — new foods of higher concentration and density introduced into the diet in addition to breast milk (formula): they meet the growing body's increasing needs for protein, energy, iron, zinc and vitamins, develop the chewing apparatus and digestive system, and prepare the child for the family table.
s9491 **Timing of introduction:** according to the recommendations of WHO and the Ministry of Health of Uzbekistan — **from 6 months**; according to the Russian national program (Kildiyarova, Geppe) — individually, **at 4–6 months** (not before 4 months and not later than 6 months). **Signs of readiness:** holds the head independently, sits with support, the tongue-thrust reflex (pushing out the spoon) fades, shows interest in food, the weight has doubled since birth.

s9504 Rules for introducing complementary foods
s9505 Introduced only to a **healthy** child (no new food is introduced during illness or preventive vaccination);
s9506 each new food is started in a **small amount** (½–1 teaspoon) and increased to the required volume over **5–7 days**;
s9507 a new food is given **in the first half of the day** (to monitor tolerance);
s9508 move from **single-component** foods to multi-component ones; only **one** new food at a time;
s9509 food is **homogeneous** at first, then gradually thicker; given **from a spoon**;
s9510 complementary food is given **before breastfeeding**, then the breast is offered;
s9511 a new food is introduced only after the previous one is fully accepted;
s9512 until 1 year, **no salt or sugar is added** to food, and **honey and whole cow's milk** are not given.
s9513 **Choosing the first complementary food:** for children with adequate weight and a tendency to constipation — **vegetable puree** (cauliflower, zucchini, broccoli, pumpkin — soft fiber); for children with low weight and frequent stools — **cereal (porridge)** (first **gluten-free**: rice, buckwheat, corn; gluten-containing — semolina, oats, wheat — later).
