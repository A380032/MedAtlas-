s9374 **Whole cow's milk, goat's milk and kefir** are not given to a child under 1 year (according to Kildiyarova at least **until 8 months**, preferably until 2 years): much protein and salts — load on the kidneys; little iron and **diapedetic bleeding** from the intestinal mucosa — iron deficiency anemia; no vitamins or protective factors; risk of allergy. In artificial feeding only **adapted formulas** are used.
s9375 Sequence of complementary foods: **“Vegetables — Porridge — Meat — Cottage cheese — Egg — Fish — Kefir”**. Meat volume: 5–30 g at 6 months, 50 g at 8–9 months (meatballs), 60–70 g at 11–12 months (steamed cutlet). Meat puree should not be introduced **before 6 months**.
s9376 At the defense the student: 1) gives brief information about the patient (age, sex, when and with what complaints the patient was admitted); 2) presents **the main complaints and the history of the present illness**; 3) identifies the disease-related **risk factors** from the life history; 4) states **the main pathological findings of the objective examination** and must be able to demonstrate them in practice at the bedside; 5) assesses **physical and neuropsychological development**; 6) groups the symptoms into **syndromes** and justifies **the leading syndrome**; 7) interprets the results of laboratory and instrumental investigations by comparing them with the age norms; 8) answers questions from the teacher and the group (on anatomical and physiological features, methods of examination, semiotics).
s9377 The case history is **a medical document**: the **confidentiality** of patient data is preserved, and the rules of deontology are observed in communication with the patient and parents; the examination is carried out with the parents' consent and in their presence.

s9386 15.2. Breastfeeding
s9387 Physiology of lactation
s9388 **The prolactin reflex (milk production reflex):** when the baby sucks, the nerve endings in the nipple are stimulated → **prolactin** is released from the anterior pituitary → milk is produced in the alveoli. Prolactin is produced more **at night** — **night feeding** maintains lactation; frequent feeding increases milk.
s9389 **The oxytocin reflex (milk ejection reflex):** under the influence of sucking, seeing the baby and hearing its voice, **oxytocin** is released from the posterior pituitary → the myoepithelial cells around the alveoli contract → milk flows; oxytocin also accelerates **involution of the uterus**. Pain, stress and anxiety **inhibit** this reflex.
s9390 Composition of breast milk
s9391 **Colostrum** — at the end of pregnancy and in **the first 3–5 days** after delivery; yellowish, thick; rich in **protein** (mainly immunoglobulins — **secretory IgA**), leukocytes, vitamin A, carotene and trace elements; high in calories — even a small amount meets the newborn's needs; **“the first vaccination”**, it speeds up the passage of meconium (mild laxative effect).
s9392 **Transitional milk** — **from day 4–5 to week 2–3**; protein decreases, fat and lactose increase.
s9393 **Mature milk** — **from week 2–3**. Within one feed **“foremilk”** (watery, rich in lactose and water — quenches thirst) and **“hindmilk”** (rich in fat, high in calories — satisfies hunger) are distinguished; therefore the baby should **fully empty** one breast.

s9404 Advantages of breastfeeding
s9405 **Proteins** are finely dispersed (albumin, α-lactalbumin) and easily digested; amino acids (taurine, cystine) are in the optimal ratio for CNS development; there is no antigenic property — **low risk of allergy**.
s9406 **Fats** — many **unsaturated fatty acids** (omega-3, omega-6, long-chain — arachidonic and docosahexaenoic acids) — formation of the brain, retina and cell membranes; **breast milk lipase** breaks down fats already in the stomach.
s9407 **Carbohydrates** — mainly **β-lactose** and **oligosaccharides (bifidus factor)** — promote the growth of **bifidoflora** and improve calcium absorption.
s9408 **Enzymes** (amylase, lipase, proteases) — facilitate digestion.
s9409 **Trace elements** (iron, zinc, calcium) with **high bioavailability** (iron bound to lactoferrin is ≈50% absorbed); an optimal Ca:P ratio (2:1).
s9410 **Protective factors:** secretory IgA, IgG, IgM, **lactoferrin**, **lysozyme**, interferon, complement, macrophages, lymphocytes, neutrophils, bifidus factor — they reduce the risk of intestinal and respiratory infections, otitis, necrotizing enterocolitis, and later of obesity, diabetes mellitus and allergic diseases.
s9411 The milk is **sterile, warm and ready**, and its composition adapts to the baby's needs; it is economically cheap.
s9412 **A psychoemotional bond between mother and baby** forms; in the mother the risk of postpartum bleeding, breast and ovarian cancer and anemia decreases; **lactational amenorrhea** (natural contraception).

s9415 Rules of early breastfeeding and successful breastfeeding
s9416 Putting the baby to the breast **in the delivery room, within the first 30 minutes after delivery** (if there are no contraindications) and **“skin-to-skin” contact**:
s9417 stimulates the rapid onset and long duration of lactation;
s9418 ensures colonization of the skin and intestine with the mother's beneficial microflora; colostrum — immunological protection;
s9419 accelerates **involution of the uterus** and reduces postpartum bleeding;
s9420 prevents purulent-inflammatory diseases in mother and baby;
s9421 quickly establishes the psychoemotional bond.
s9422 **Breastfeeding technique — signs of a correct position:** the baby's head and body are in one line, the face is turned to the breast, the body is close to the mother, and the mother supports the baby's whole body. **Signs of correct attachment:** the mouth is wide open, **the lower lip is turned outward**, **the chin touches the breast**, more of the areola is visible above than below; the baby sucks slowly and deeply, swallowing is heard, and the mother feels no pain.

s9454 Hypogalactia
s9455 **Hypogalactia** — reduced secretory activity of the mammary glands during lactation. **Primary (true)** — hormonal disorders (deficiency of prolactin, oxytocin), endocrine diseases of the mother — rare (≈5%). **Secondary** — the majority: complications of pregnancy and delivery, maternal diseases, **stress**, unwillingness to breastfeed, **violation of the rules of breastfeeding** (late and infrequent breastfeeding, feeding on a strict schedule, a night break, pacifiers and teats, incorrect technique), inadequate nutrition and rest of the mother.
s9456 **Early** (within 10–14 days after delivery) and **late** (after 2–3 weeks).
s9457 **Degrees:** I — milk deficit <25%; II — 26–50%; III — 51–75%; IV — >75%.
s9458 **Lactation crises** — temporary unexplained decreases in milk (at 3–6 weeks, at 3, 4, 7, 8 months), lasting 3–4 days.
s9459 **Measures:** breastfeeding **frequently, on demand**, from **both breasts** at each feed (keeping night feeds); daytime rest and sleep for the mother, complete and varied nutrition, a warm drink and lactogenic teas 15–20 minutes before feeding; a positive emotional climate in the family; breast massage and a warm shower before feeding. Only when all possibilities have been exhausted, in **grade III–IV** hypogalactia, is **supplementary feeding** with an adapted formula (by spoon, cup or syringe — without a teat) prescribed.
s9460 15.3. Mixed and artificial feeding
s9461 Reasons for transfer to artificial feeding
s9462 Grade III–IV hypogalactia or agalactia; contraindications to breastfeeding; absence of the mother; the mother's own choice. **Artificial feeding is a metabolic stress**: protein load, changes in the intestinal microflora, increased risk of infection and allergy.
s9463 Breast milk substitutes
s9464 **Principles of adaptation:** reducing the protein content **by 2.5 times**, bringing the whey protein to casein ratio closer to that of breast milk (60:40), adding taurine and nucleotides; optimizing the fat composition with **unsaturated fatty acids** (vegetable oils, ARA, DHA); increasing carbohydrates **1.5–2 times** (lactose, prebiotics — galacto- and fructo-oligosaccharides); fortification with vitamins (12), iron, iodine, zinc, copper and selenium; reducing the content of Ca, K and Na.
s9465 **Starter (formula 1)** — 0–6 months (highly adapted);
s9466 **follow-on (formula 2)** — 6–12 months (more protein and calories);
s9467 **formula 3** — over 1 year;
s9468 **fermented adapted formulas** — from 3–4 weeks, in a 1:1 or 2:1 ratio with a sweet formula (probiotic effect, in dysbiosis, atopic dermatitis, intestinal diseases);

s9486 Rules of artificial feeding
s9487 Feed with one adapted formula; **do not change the formula often**; switch gradually to a new formula over **5–7 days**.
s9488 The formula is prepared **fresh before each feed** according to the instructions on the package, at a temperature of **36–37 °C**; the teat hole is small (milk should drip when the bottle is turned over); the utensils are sterile.
s9489 The feeding interval is often **3–3.5 hours** (formula leaves the stomach more slowly), 6–7 times a day, with a 6-hour night break; overfeeding is avoided.
s9490 Complementary foods are also introduced to formula-fed children **at 4–6 months** (they may be introduced earlier than in breastfed children).
s9491 Rules of mixed feeding
s9492 First **breastfeed from both breasts**, and only after the breasts are fully emptied give supplementary feeding with formula (**top-up**) — to maintain lactation;
s9493 the top-up is given with **a spoon, cup** or a teat with a small hole (a teat weakens the baby's sucking — “nipple confusion”);
s9494 the volume of the top-up is determined by **test weighing** or by calculating the food volume;
s9495 measures to increase breast milk are continued.
s9496 15.4. Complementary foods
s9497 **Complementary foods** — new foods of higher concentration and density introduced into the diet in addition to breast milk (formula): they meet the growing body's increasing needs for protein, energy, iron, zinc and vitamins, develop the chewing apparatus and digestive system, and prepare the child for the family table.
s9498 **Timing of introduction:** according to the recommendations of WHO and the Ministry of Health of Uzbekistan — **from 6 months**; according to the Russian national program (Kildiyarova, Geppe) — individually, **at 4–6 months** (not before 4 months and not later than 6 months). **Signs of readiness:** holds the head independently, sits with support, the tongue-thrust reflex (pushing out the spoon) fades, shows interest in food, the weight has doubled since birth.
