s4320 The key to distinguishing consolidation from atelectasis is **the state of the bronchus**: an open bronchus (pneumonia) — sound is conducted well (vocal fremitus ↑, bronchial breathing); a closed bronchus (atelectasis) — sound is not conducted (vocal fremitus ↓, breath sounds not heard). s4322 In Arzikulov's textbook the degrees of RF are also given by **blood oxygen saturation**: I — normal or down to 90%; II — 70–80%; III — <70%; IV (hypoxemic coma) — <50%. s4323 **Four rules:** 1) **Dense tissue + open bronchus** → sound is conducted well → vocal fremitus and bronchophony ↑, bronchial breathing. 2) **Fluid or air in the pleura, or an obstructed bronchus** → sound is blocked → everything ↓. 3) The mediastinum **moves away from what “pushes”** (fluid, air → toward the healthy side) and **toward what “pulls”** (atelectasis → toward the affected side). 4) **Upper airways → inspiration is difficult** (croup), **small bronchi → expiration is difficult** (asthma, bronchiolitis).

s4334 Spirometry
s4335 **Spirometry** — a method of determining **the vital capacity (VC)** (with a Hutchinson spirometer or a portable spirometer). The child, standing or sitting, takes a maximal breath, pinches the nose and slowly breathes all the air out into the spirometer through the mouthpiece. The measurement is repeated 2–3 times, the highest result is taken and compared with **the predicted value (for age, height and sex)**. A decrease of **more than 15–20%** from the predicted value is pathological.
s4336 Spirography
s4337 **Spirography** — graphic recording of respiratory movements, reflecting changes in lung volumes. It is performed in the morning on an empty stomach after 10–15 minutes of rest; the child breathes through a mouthpiece (with a nose clip) or a mask. First 3–5 minutes of quiet breathing and the VC are recorded, then the forced tests.
s4338 Main parameters:
s4339 **TV** — tidal volume (the volume of air in one respiratory cycle);
s4340 **MV** — minute volume of breathing = RR × TV; it increases in lung and heart diseases and thyrotoxicosis, and decreases when the respiratory center is depressed;
s4341 **VC** — vital capacity = TV + inspiratory reserve volume + expiratory reserve volume;
s4342 **FVC** — forced vital capacity (a sharp expiration after a maximal deep breath);
s4343 **FEV~1~** — forced expiratory volume in the 1st second; **Tiffeneau index** = FEV1 / VC × 100%, normally **≥70%**;
s4344 **MVV** — maximal voluntary ventilation of the lungs (the maximal volume of air that can be ventilated in 1 minute; calculated from a 10–15-second recording);
s4345 **Breathing reserve** = MVV – MV; it decreases in respiratory failure.
s4346 A deviation of **±20%** from the predicted values is considered acceptable.

s4365 Pneumotachometry
s4366 **Pneumotachometry** — assessment of bronchial patency: **the peak air flow rate** on inspiration and expiration is measured (from 5–6 years). First a tube 20 mm in diameter is used, then one of 10 mm; the test is repeated 3–4 times and the highest result is taken. A reduced expiratory rate indicates bronchial obstruction (bronchospasm, mucosal edema, secretions).
s4367 Peak flowmetry
s4368 **Peak flowmetry** — determining **the peak expiratory flow (PEF)** with a portable device, the peak flow meter. It is simple, cheap and done at home — the main method of **monitoring bronchial asthma** and its control. The child stands, takes a deep breath, seals the lips tightly around the mouthpiece and breathes out **sharply and quickly**; this is repeated 3 times and the highest result is recorded. Measurements are taken **2 times a day** — in the morning (before taking medication) and in the evening.
s4369 **Daily variability >20%** or a **“morning dip”** — bronchial **hyperreactivity** (uncontrolled asthma).
s4370 The result is compared with the norm for the child's height: 107 cm — ≈140, 130 cm — ≈250, 152 cm — ≈360, 175 cm — ≈480 l/min.
