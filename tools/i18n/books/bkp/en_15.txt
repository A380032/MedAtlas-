s2949 5.1. Anatomical and physiological features of bone tissue
s2950 **Development.** Bones form in two ways: **intramembranous osteogenesis** — directly from connective tissue (the bones of the cranial vault, the facial bones, part of the clavicle) and **endochondral osteogenesis** — first a cartilage model, then bone in its place (tubular bones, vertebrae, pelvis). The skeleton is mainly formed in **weeks 8–9** of intrauterine life.
s2951 **Growth.** Bone grows in length through the **epiphyseal (growth) cartilage** and in width through **the periosteum**.

s2980 5.2. Features of the parts of the skeleton
s2981 The skull
s2982 At birth the skull consists of many separate bones; **the sutures** (coronal, sagittal, lambdoid) are open — the head **molds** in the birth canal. The sutures begin to close at 3–4 months and fully close at **3–5 years**.
s2983 **The anterior fontanelle** — at the junction of the coronal and sagittal sutures, diamond-shaped; its size at birth is **from 1.5×2 to 3×3 cm** (measured between the midpoints of opposite edges); it closes at **1–1.5 years**.
s2984 **The posterior fontanelle** — between the sagittal and lambdoid sutures, triangular; it is closed at birth in **75%** of term babies, and closes at **1–2 months** in the rest.
s2985 **The lateral fontanelles** (sphenoid and mastoid) are closed in the term newborn.
s2986 **The cerebral part of the skull is 8 times larger than the facial part** (2 times in adults). The orbits are relatively wide, the frontal bone has two halves, there are no superciliary arches or frontal sinus, and the jaws are undeveloped.
s2987 In the 1st year the skull grows rapidly and the thickness of the bones increases 3 times; at 2–3 years growth of the facial part accelerates (teething, chewing); the skull base grows actively until 7 years.

s3010 The chest
s3011 In the newborn it is **conical (barrel-shaped)**; the anteroposterior dimension is greater than or equal to the transverse one; the ribs leave the spine **at a right angle, horizontally** — **breathing is abdominal (diaphragmatic)**, and chest excursion is small.
s3012 Once the child begins to walk, the sternum descends and the ribs become oblique; **at 3 years** the anteroposterior and transverse dimensions become equal; at school age the transverse dimension predominates and the chest flattens.
s3013 From school age its shape depends on the constitution: **conical** (hypersthenic), **cylindrical** (normosthenic), **flat** (asthenic).
s3014 The pelvis and limbs
s3015 At an early age the pelvis is **funnel-shaped** and narrow; sex differences become noticeable **from 9 years** (wider in girls). In the newborn **the acetabulum** is shallow and oval, and a large part of the femoral head lies outside the socket — **predisposition to developmental dysplasia and congenital dislocation of the hip**.
s3016 In the newborn the limbs are relatively **short**; later the legs grow faster than the arms.
s3017 **The foot** is flat until 1 year (fat pad); the arch forms with standing and walking — **flat feet are physiological up to 2 years**.
s3018 The joints
s3019 At birth the articular-ligamentous apparatus is anatomically formed. The epiphyses are cartilaginous, **the ligaments are extensible but not strong** — the range of motion in the joints is large, and **dislocations and subluxations** occur easily (at 2–4 years a sharp pull on the arm causes **subluxation of the radial head**). Full formation of the articular surfaces, capsule and ligaments is completed at **13–16 years**.

s3056 5.4. Methods of examining the osteoarticular system
s3057 **Complaints:** pain in the bones and joints (location, character, time of appearance, morning or evening, relation to movement, provoking factor); deformity; restricted movement and **morning stiffness**; change in gait; joint swelling.
s3058 **History:** osteoarticular, rheumatic and autoimmune diseases in relatives; relation to injury or infection (tonsillitis — rheumatic fever; intestinal and urinary tract infections — reactive arthritis); feeding in the 1st year and **vitamin D prophylaxis**, the age of walking.
s3059 Inspection is performed **from top to bottom**, with the child **lying, standing and moving**.
s3060 Head:
s3061 **shape** — round; **tower-shaped** (craniostenosis), “square” — enlarged **frontal and parietal bosses** (**rickets**), **flattening of the occiput** (rickets), **hydrocephalic** (overhanging forehead, small facial part), **microcephaly**;
s3062 **symmetry**;
s3063 **size** (head circumference — topic 2);
s3064 state of **the upper and lower jaws**, number and condition of the teeth, **the bite (occlusion)**: primary bite — **orthognathic**, permanent — orthognathic; pathological — progenia, prognathia, open bite; “**gothic palate**” (high, narrow).
s3065 Chest:
s3066 **shape** — conical, cylindrical, flat, **barrel-shaped** (emphysema, bronchial asthma);
s3067 **the epigastric angle**: **≈90°** — normosthenic; **obtuse** — hypersthenic; **acute** — asthenic;
s3068 **deformities**: **“pigeon chest”** (forward protrusion of the sternum — rickets), **funnel chest (“cobbler's chest”)** — inward depression of the lower sternum (congenital or rickets), **Harrison's groove** (an inward depression at the attachment of the diaphragm, with the costal arches flared forward — rickets), **cardiac hump** (congenital heart defect), **rachitic “rosary”** (thickenings at the costochondral junctions of the ribs).
s3069 Figure 5.10
s3070 Funnel chest (“cobbler's chest”) (Navruzova, Geppe)
s3071 **Spine:** physiological curves and how marked they are (hyperlordosis, hyperkyphosis, **rachitic kyphosis** — in the 1st year, prominent when sitting; **tuberculous spondylitis** — sharp-angled kyphosis — **gibbus**), **scoliosis** (lateral curvature), postural disorders. The child is examined from behind while standing: the symmetry of **the levels of the shoulders and the angles of the shoulder blades, the waist triangles** (the space between the arm and the trunk) and **the iliac crests**. For scoliosis, the line of the spinous processes and **the rib hump** are assessed with the child **bending forward**.

s3074 **Limbs:** straightness and symmetry of the contours (fracture, pseudarthrosis), relative length (proportions), shape — **X-shaped (valgus)** and **O-shaped (varus)** deformity of the legs (rickets), **saber-shaped** curvature of the tibia (rickets, congenital syphilis); **flat feet** (pathological after 2 years); **“bracelets”** (thickening of the distal metaphysis of the radius) and **“strings of pearls”** (finger phalanges) — rickets; **“drumstick fingers”** — chronic hypoxia.
s3075 Gait:
s3076 **antalgic** — the child quickly shifts from the painful leg to the healthy one (involvement of the hip, knee or foot, back pain);
s3077 **“duck gait”** (waddling) — congenital dislocation of **both hips**, myopathies;
s3078 **limping** — unilateral dislocation, Perthes disease, leg length discrepancy;
s3079 with heel pain (talalgia) — the child walks on tiptoe.
s3080 Palpation
s3081 **Head:** the sutures, the anterior fontanelle (size, edges, bulging or depression), **craniotabes** — softening of separate areas of the occipital and parietal bones (they bend like parchment when pressed with a finger) — **an initial sign of rickets** (3–6 months).
s3082 **Ribs:** in a healthy child the thickening at the costochondral junction is hardly noticeable; distinct hemispherical thickenings (ribs V–VIII) — **rachitic “rosary”**.
s3083 **Limbs:** “bracelets”, “strings of pearls”, pain (periostitis, osteomyelitis, tumor, leukemia), pathological fractures.
s3084 **Spine: enthesopathies** (pain at the sites where tendons and ligaments attach to bone), depression or protrusion of the spinous processes, pain.
s3085 **Tapping (percussion):** **pain** when the flat and tubular bones (sternum, ribs, vertebrae, tibia) are tapped with a finger — blood diseases (**leukemia**), bone metastases.
s3086 Examination of the joints
s3087 **On inspection:** shape, size, symmetry, color of the overlying skin; **swelling**, **defiguration** (a reversible change due to intra-articular fluid and thickening of the synovial membrane), **deformity** (a persistent change due to proliferative and destructive processes — subluxation, ankylosis, contracture), hyperemia, pigmentation.
s3088 **On palpation:** local temperature, pain, swelling of the soft tissues, intra-articular fluid (**ballottement** of the patella — on pressure over the patella it “floats” in the fluid), crepitus.

s3115 **Ankle:** passive flexion and extension; Achillobursitis (swelling in the area of the Achilles tendon).
