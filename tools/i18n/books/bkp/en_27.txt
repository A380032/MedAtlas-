s5481 The order of auscultation — **“Mitral – Aortic – Pulmonary – Tricuspid – Botkin”** = **“MAPTB”**: it starts with the valve most often affected by heart disease.
s5482 **A diastolic murmur is never functional.** A functional murmur is **“soft, short, systolic, variable, non-radiating”**. A functional systolic murmur is heard at various ages in 50–70% of healthy children.

s5484 9.1. Auscultation of the heart
s5485 a quiet, warm room; the doctor stands on **the child's right side**;
s5486 auscultation is performed **in different positions**: lying on the back, on the left side, standing; if necessary after physical exertion (at school age);
s5487 the results are compared during normal breathing and while holding the breath (after expiration);
s5488 a small-diameter stethoscope is used in small children; when the child cries, listen between breaths;

s5521 Age-related features of the heart sounds:
s5522 **Embryocardia** — a pendulum rhythm: S1 and S2 are identical and the pauses equal. In the newborn it is considered normal **in the first 2 weeks** of life (because of tachycardia); in an older child — **pathological** (acute heart failure, paroxysmal tachycardia, high fever).
s5523 In the neonatal period the sounds may be slightly muffled; later in children the sounds are **louder than in adults** (thin chest wall).
s5524 In **the first 2–3 days** of life S2 is louder than S1 at the apex and at Botkin's point, then they become equal; **from 2–3 months** S1 predominates at the apex.
s5525 In the 1st year S1 is heard better at the base of the heart, then they become equal; **from 2 years** S2 is stronger.
s5526 **From 2 to 12 years** S2 is stronger **over the pulmonary artery** than over the aorta — **a physiological accentuation of S2 over the pulmonary artery**; at 12 years they become equal, and later it is stronger over the aorta.
s5527 In children **physiological splitting of S1 and S2** (at the end of inspiration, changing with body position) and **S3** are frequently heard.
s5528 **Splitting and reduplication:** if there is an imperceptible pause between the two components of a sound — **splitting** (“TRA-ta”); if the pause is distinct — **reduplication**. Physiological splitting is variable and depends on body position and the phase of breathing.
s5529 Heart murmurs
s5530 **A murmur** is a longer sound, heard in addition to the heart sounds, resulting from turbulent blood flow. Turbulence depends on changes in **the diameter of the valve orifice or vessel**, **the blood flow velocity** and **the blood viscosity**.
s5531 Classification:
s5532 **intracardiac** and **extracardiac** murmurs (pericardial friction rub, pleuropericardial murmur);
s5533 **organic** (anatomical changes in the valves, septa and vessels — congenital and acquired defects, endocarditis) and **inorganic**;
s5534 according to P.S. Moshchich's classification: **organic**, **organic-functional** (impaired cardiac function in myocarditis and arrhythmias — relative valvular insufficiency), **functional** (in extracardiac diseases: anemia, fever, thyrotoxicosis, autonomic dysfunction) and **physiological (innocent)** — in a healthy child.
s5535 **Describing a murmur:** 1) phase of the cardiac cycle (systolic, diastolic; early, mid, late, pansystolic); 2) duration; 3) loudness (intensity, grade 1–6); 4) timbre (soft, blowing, harsh, scraping, musical); 5) the place where it is best heard (epicenter, punctum maximum); 6) radiation (axilla, neck, interscapular area); 7) relation to the heart sounds; 8) change with body position, breathing and exertion.

s5567 Auscultation of the blood vessels
s5568 In a healthy child two sounds are heard over **the carotid and subclavian arteries**, and sometimes S1 over **the femoral artery**; no sounds are heard over other arteries. Over the neck veins (especially on the right, with the head turned) a **“humming top”** is heard — a normal venous hum (increased in anemia). Murmurs are heard over vessels when arteries are narrowed or dilated and in aortic and valvular defects; a murmur over the abdominal aorta and renal arteries — renal artery stenosis (a cause of arterial hypertension).

s5571 9.2. The pulse
s5572 In children **over 2 years** the pulse is examined mainly at **the radial artery** (in younger children — at the temporal, carotid, brachial or femoral arteries). The most accurate information is obtained in the morning, after the child wakes up, at rest (**basal pulse**). First the pulse is examined **in both arms simultaneously** (synchrony and equality), then in one arm for 1 minute.

s5607 9.3. Arterial pressure
s5608 **Systolic BP** — the maximal pressure in the arteries during left ventricular systole (depends on the stroke volume and the elasticity of the aorta); **diastolic BP** — the minimal pressure in diastole (depends on the tone of the peripheral arterioles); **pulse pressure** — the difference between them (≈40 mm in the newborn, ≈50 mm at 14–15 years).
