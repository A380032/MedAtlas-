s5410 A 12-year-old boy complains of rapid fatigue, headache and leg pain when running. On examination: the shoulder girdle is well developed, the legs are thin. The neck vessels pulsate strongly. BP in the arms is 150/90 mm Hg, in the legs 100/70. The femoral pulses are weak and delayed. The apex beat is displaced to the left and increased. On X-ray “usuration” (notching) of the lower edges of the ribs is found.
s5411 1) Presumptive diagnosis? 2) Which signs confirm it? 3) Explain the change in the apex beat. 4) What is the normal ratio of BP in the arms and legs?
s5412 1) Coarctation of the aorta. 2) Disproportion between the upper and lower body, arterial hypertension in the arms, hypotension in the legs, weak femoral pulses, intermittent leg pain (ischemia), rib notching from the pressure of the intercostal collateral arteries. 3) Left ventricular hypertrophy (working against the narrowed aorta) — an increased apex beat displaced to the left. 4) Normally BP in the legs is 10–20 mm Hg higher than in the arms.

s5485 The order of auscultation — **“Mitral – Aortic – Pulmonary – Tricuspid – Botkin”** = **“MAPTB”**: it starts with the valve most often affected by heart disease.

s5488 9.1. Auscultation of the heart
s5489 a quiet, warm room; the doctor stands on **the child's right side**;
s5490 auscultation is performed **in different positions**: lying on the back, on the left side, standing; if necessary after physical exertion (at school age);

s5525 Age-related features of the heart sounds:
s5526 **Embryocardia** — a pendulum rhythm: S1 and S2 are identical and the pauses equal. In the newborn it is considered normal **in the first 2 weeks** of life (because of tachycardia); in an older child — **pathological** (acute heart failure, paroxysmal tachycardia, high fever).
s5527 In the neonatal period the sounds may be slightly muffled; later in children the sounds are **louder than in adults** (thin chest wall).
s5528 In **the first 2–3 days** of life S2 is louder than S1 at the apex and at Botkin's point, then they become equal; **from 2–3 months** S1 predominates at the apex.
s5529 In the 1st year S1 is heard better at the base of the heart, then they become equal; **from 2 years** S2 is stronger.
s5530 **From 2 to 12 years** S2 is stronger **over the pulmonary artery** than over the aorta — **a physiological accentuation of S2 over the pulmonary artery**; at 12 years they become equal, and later it is stronger over the aorta.
s5531 In children **physiological splitting of S1 and S2** (at the end of inspiration, changing with body position) and **S3** are frequently heard.
s5532 **Splitting and reduplication:** if there is an imperceptible pause between the two components of a sound — **splitting** (“TRA-ta”); if the pause is distinct — **reduplication**. Physiological splitting is variable and depends on body position and the phase of breathing.
s5533 Heart murmurs
s5534 **A murmur** is a longer sound, heard in addition to the heart sounds, resulting from turbulent blood flow. Turbulence depends on changes in **the diameter of the valve orifice or vessel**, **the blood flow velocity** and **the blood viscosity**.
s5535 Classification:
s5536 **intracardiac** and **extracardiac** murmurs (pericardial friction rub, pleuropericardial murmur);
s5537 **organic** (anatomical changes in the valves, septa and vessels — congenital and acquired defects, endocarditis) and **inorganic**;
s5538 according to P.S. Moshchich's classification: **organic**, **organic-functional** (impaired cardiac function in myocarditis and arrhythmias — relative valvular insufficiency), **functional** (in extracardiac diseases: anemia, fever, thyrotoxicosis, autonomic dysfunction) and **physiological (innocent)** — in a healthy child.
s5539 **Describing a murmur:** 1) phase of the cardiac cycle (systolic, diastolic; early, mid, late, pansystolic); 2) duration; 3) loudness (intensity, grade 1–6); 4) timbre (soft, blowing, harsh, scraping, musical); 5) the place where it is best heard (epicenter, punctum maximum); 6) radiation (axilla, neck, interscapular area); 7) relation to the heart sounds; 8) change with body position, breathing and exertion.

s5571 Auscultation of the blood vessels
s5572 In a healthy child two sounds are heard over **the carotid and subclavian arteries**, and sometimes S1 over **the femoral artery**; no sounds are heard over other arteries. Over the neck veins (especially on the right, with the head turned) a **“humming top”** is heard — a normal venous hum (increased in anemia). Murmurs are heard over vessels when arteries are narrowed or dilated and in aortic and valvular defects; a murmur over the abdominal aorta and renal arteries — renal artery stenosis (a cause of arterial hypertension).

s5575 9.2. The pulse
s5576 In children **over 2 years** the pulse is examined mainly at **the radial artery** (in younger children — at the temporal, carotid, brachial or femoral arteries). The most accurate information is obtained in the morning, after the child wakes up, at rest (**basal pulse**). First the pulse is examined **in both arms simultaneously** (synchrony and equality), then in one arm for 1 minute.
