s8196 **Duodenal ulcer** — hunger, night and late (2–4 hours) pains, **the Moynihan rhythm**, severe, recurrent, to the right of and above the umbilicus; heartburn, sour belching, nausea, vomiting, constipation; autonomic disturbances are more frequent. **Gastric ulcer** — early pain, slightly above the umbilicus along the midline; nausea and vomiting after eating. On examination: forced position, autonomic lability (distal hyperhidrosis, persistent red dermographism, bradycardia, hypotension), tension of the abdominal muscles, zones of skin hyperesthesia, a positive **Mendel's sign**, **Oppenhowski's points** (pain on percussion over the spinous processes of the thoracic vertebrae). Diagnosis — **EGDS** (the ulcer defect), the “niche” sign on contrast X-ray.
s8197 **Complications:** bleeding (“coffee grounds” vomiting, **melena** — black tarry stool), perforation (“dagger-like” pain, “board-like” abdomen, disappearance of liver dullness), penetration, stenosis.
s8198 Pancreatitis syndrome
s8199 In gross dietary errors, infections (mumps), abdominal trauma, gastrointestinal pathology. Sharp **girdle-like pain** above the umbilicus, nausea, **repeated vomiting** of gastric contents, intoxication, disturbed stool. In the blood — leukocytosis, raised ESR; increased **amylase, lipase, trypsin**; increased urinary diastase. Ultrasound — edema, enlargement and disturbed structure of the gland. In chronic pancreatitis — exocrine insufficiency (steatorrhea, reduced elastase-1).
s8200 Biliary tract dysfunction syndrome (biliary dyskinesia)
s8201 Causes: long intervals between meals, dry food, abuse of carbohydrate and fatty food, disturbed daily routine; anatomical defects of the gallbladder (kinks, constrictions); reflex, in diseases of other digestive organs. **Gallbladder dysfunction** and **dystonia of the sphincter of Oddi**; by functional state — **hypokinetic (hypomotor)** and **hyperkinetic (hypermotor)** forms.
s8202 The main sign is a dull or sharp pain in the right hypochondrium that **increases after eating and after physical exertion**; nausea, vomiting, **a bitter taste in the mouth**, signs of cholestasis, liver enlargement, pain in the right hypochondrium, epigastrium and Chauffard's zone, **positive gallbladder signs**. In the **hyperkinetic** form — short, paroxysmal, colicky pains; in the **hypokinetic** form — constant dull pain with a feeling of heaviness. Diagnosis — ultrasound with a cholekinetic breakfast, duodenal intubation.
s8203 Syndromes of liver involvement
s8204 **Cytolysis syndrome** — in viral, infectious and noninfectious, parasitic (echinococcus), toxic (drugs, heavy metal salts, benzene) influences, anomalies of the biliary tract, congenital metabolic disorders. **Clinical picture:** pain and heaviness in the right hypochondrium, dyspepsia (reduced appetite, nausea, bilious vomiting, meteorism, unstable stool), astheno-vegetative signs (weakness, rapid fatigue, sweating, headache), signs of cholestasis (itching, subicterus). The skin is pale, sometimes icteric, dry; scratch marks, ecchymoses, petechiae; **spider angiomas** (telangiectasias), **palmar erythema** (“liver palms”); **hepatosplenomegaly** — the liver dense, tender, smooth. **Dark urine (“beer-colored”)**, discolored stool. Laboratory: increased ALT, AST, LDH, bilirubin mainly at the expense of the direct fraction, dysproteinemia, increased IgM; prolonged detection of HBsAg — a sign of chronicity.
s8205 **Cholestasis syndrome** — disturbed bile flow: **itching of the skin**, pigmentation, **xanthomas**, inconstant jaundice, dyspepsia, astheno-vegetative syndrome; scratch marks, hepatosplenomegaly. Laboratory: high cholesterol, bile acids, β-lipoproteins, total lipids, **alkaline phosphatase**, leucine aminopeptidase; increased direct bilirubin.
s8206 **Hepatocellular insufficiency syndrome** — when only **20–25%** of the liver parenchyma remains functional. The **acute** form (more often at an early age) — viral hepatitis, hepatotropic poisons (death cap, mushrooms, barbiturates) — hepatic necrosis and coma; **chronic** — as a result of cirrhosis, congenital infections, hereditary diseases. **Clinical picture:** change in behavior — lethargy, stupor, adynamia, drowsiness (less often agitation), then confusion and **coma**; intoxication, pale-yellow skin, dystrophy of the muscles and subcutaneous fat, avitaminosis, liver signs (“lacquered” tongue, spider angiomas, palmar erythema), abdominal enlargement (meteorism, hepatosplenomegaly, **ascites**), edema of the legs, **hemorrhagic syndrome** (bleeding from the nose, gums, gastrointestinal tract); laboratory — hypoalbuminemia, reduced prothrombin. **When coma develops the liver begins to shrink** — a poor prognostic sign.
s8207 **Portal hypertension syndrome** — increased pressure in the portal venous system; prehepatic, intrahepatic and posthepatic forms. The main signs — **varicose dilatation of the esophageal and gastric veins** (X-ray, endoscopy), **splenomegaly**, hepatomegaly, **ascites**, a venous network on the anterior abdominal wall (“caput medusae”).
s8208 Syndromes of impaired digestion in the small intestine
s8209 **Enteritis syndrome** — against a background of intestinal infections (coli infection, salmonellosis), food allergy, operations on the small intestine, gross dietary errors, extraintestinal diseases (influenza, pneumonia, otitis). **Diarrhea** (painless, with passage of gas, followed by relief), meteorism, gurgling, pain **around the umbilicus** after eating; signs of intestinal toxicosis and dehydration (gray skin, the fold smooths out slowly). Coprogram: the stool watery, copious, unformed, pale yellow, with a **sour** (fermentation) or **fetid** (putrefaction) odor, mucus mixed with the stool, altered muscle fibers, fatty acids, soaps.
s8210 **Dyspeptic syndrome of infants (simple dyspepsia)** — disturbed feeding routine (overfeeding, too frequent feeding), a diet inappropriate for age, too rapid transfer to artificial feeding. Diarrhea (6–8 times a day, liquid, yellow-green, sour-smelling, with a little mucus and **white flakes** — magnesium and calcium soaps of fatty acids), regurgitation, reduced appetite; the general condition is not disturbed, temperature normal, no dehydration, steady weight gain; no pathogenic flora grows.
s8211 **Malabsorption syndrome** — **impaired absorption** in the small intestine: recurrent diarrhea, **polyfecalia** and progressive dystrophy. **Causes:** congenital and hereditary diseases (**celiac disease**, **cystic fibrosis**, disaccharidase (lactase) deficiency, exudative enteropathy), food allergy (cow's milk proteins), chronic inflammation (enterocolitis, pancreatitis), tumors, intestinal resection (“short bowel” syndrome), long-term antibiotics, massive giardiasis; secondary — against a background of severe diseases.
s8212 **Clinical picture:** the stool watery or frothy, **large in volume**, **gray and clay-like**, fetid (**steatorrhea**), **greasy and shiny**, leaving greasy stains on diapers that are difficult to wash, the potty is hard to clean; vomiting, progressive **anorexia**, abdominal distension, spastic pains, **abdominal enlargement**, emaciation, **lagging physical development** (slowed growth, weight deficit); signs of vitamin and trace element deficiency (anemia, rickets-like changes, hypoproteinemic edema).

s8215 **Celiac disease** — intolerance of gluten (the protein of wheat, rye, barley); symptoms begin 1–2 months **after cereals (porridge, bread) are introduced**. Diagnosis: **IgA antibodies to tissue transglutaminase** in the blood (with total IgA), antibodies to endomysium, duodenal biopsy (villous atrophy). Treatment — **a lifelong gluten-free diet**. **Lactase deficiency** — frothy, sour-smelling diarrhea and meteorism after milk intake; stool pH <5.5, carbohydrates >0.25%. **Cystic fibrosis** — fatty diarrhea + recurrent bronchopulmonary infections; sweat chloride >60 mmol/l.
s8216 Impaired digestion in the colon (colitis syndrome)
s8217 Infections (dysentery, food toxicoinfections), parasitoses (amebiasis), helminthiases (enterobiasis, hymenolepiasis, trichuriasis), candidomycosis, noninfectious diseases (**ulcerative colitis, Crohn disease**). Colicky pains in the lower abdomen increasing with defecation, **tenesmus** (false urges), defecation **brings no relief**; frequent stools (10–12 times a day, 20–30 times in severe cases), **mucus, pus, blood** in the stool; in severe cases the stool loses its fecal character (“rectal spit”). Coprogram: much mucus, leukocytes, erythrocytes; pathogenic flora on bacteriological examination.
s8218 Irritable bowel syndrome
s8219 **A functional** pathology: abdominal pain or discomfort (usually **relieved by defecation**), dyspepsia (meteorism, gurgling, diarrhea, constipation or their alternation), a feeling of incomplete emptying of the bowel, imperative urges; lasting **at least 3 months**. It is **a diagnosis of exclusion**. **“Red flag” signs** that exclude it: unexplained weight loss, predominance of symptoms **at night**, constant severe pain, fever, hepatosplenomegaly, **blood in the stool**, leukocytosis, anemia, raised ESR, biochemical changes.
s8220 13.7. Clinical section
s8221 Algorithm for examining a child with abdominal pain
s8222 Exclude **an “acute abdomen”** (see topic 12) — surgical examination. 2) Look for **“red flag”** signs (weight loss, night pain and diarrhea, bloody stools, fever, growth retardation, inflammatory bowel diseases in the family). If they are absent and the pain lasts more than 2 months — most often **functional pain** (functional dyspepsia, irritable bowel syndrome, abdominal migraine). 3) Minimal investigation: CBC and urinalysis, coprogram, helminths and Giardia, **abdominal ultrasound**, biochemistry (ALT, AST, amylase, bilirubin), and if necessary **EGDS** and an H. pylori test.
