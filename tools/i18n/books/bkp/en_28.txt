s5609 Technique of measurement (Korotkov method)
s5610 The child sits quietly for **5 minutes** (small children lying down); there should be no food or active play for 30 minutes before the measurement.
s5611 **The cuff size must be appropriate for age**: the width of the rubber bladder should cover **≈40%** of the arm circumference and its length **80–100%** (newborn — 3–5 cm, infant — 5–8 cm, preschool — 9–10 cm, school age — 12–13 cm, adolescents — adult cuff). A narrow cuff **overestimates** BP, a wide cuff **underestimates** it.
s5612 The arm on the table, palm up, muscles relaxed; the heart, the cuff and the zero point of the manometer **at the same level**.
s5613 The deflated cuff is placed on the upper arm **2 cm above** the antecubital fossa so that 1–2 fingers can pass under it.
s5614 The brachial artery is found by palpation in the antecubital fossa, and the stethoscope is placed over it.
s5615 Air is pumped into the cuff to **30–40 mm Hg** above the level at which the radial pulse disappears.
s5616 The pressure is released slowly (**2–3 mm** per second): **the first clear sound** — systolic BP (Korotkov phase I), **disappearance of the sounds** — diastolic BP (phase V). In small children the sounds may be heard down to zero — then the point of sharp muffling of the sounds (phase IV) is taken.
s5617 The measurement is repeated **2–3 times** at 2–3-minute intervals and the average is taken; the first time it is measured **in both arms** and **in the legs** (in the leg — with a wide cuff on the thigh, listening over the popliteal artery).

s5633 9.4. Functional tests
s5634 Functional tests are performed to determine the adaptation of the cardiovascular system to exertion, the possibility of extending the regimen, and the physical education group.
s5635 **Martinet test (orthostatic).** The child lies for 5–10 minutes and the pulse and BP are measured; then they are measured again standing or after **20 squats** (within 30 seconds), and recovery is observed every minute. **Favorable reaction:** the pulse rises by **≤25% (–50%)**, systolic BP rises by **5–10 mm** (up to 20 mm), diastolic is unchanged or falls slightly, the parameters recover **in 3 minutes**, no complaints.
s5636 **Graded exercise test according to N.A. Shalkov.** HR, RR and BP are measured at rest; the child is given a load (loads 1–3 — sitting up and lying down in bed for patients on bed rest; 4–6 — 5, 10, 20 deep squats or climbing 10–30 steps; 7–8 — exercise and sports loads), and the reaction is assessed.

s5654 **Bicycle ergometry, treadmill test** — in older children, to assess physical working capacity and exercise-related arrhythmias and ischemia.

s5693 Other electrophysiological methods:
s5694 **Holter monitoring** — continuous ECG recording for 24 hours (or longer): HR dynamics, arrhythmias and their associations, ischemic changes. Indications: fainting, life-threatening arrhythmias, any arrhythmia at an early age, sudden death at a young age in relatives.
s5695 **24-hour ambulatory BP monitoring (ABPM)** — with a portable monitor under natural conditions.
s5696 **Cardiointervalography (CIG)** — mathematical analysis of 100 cardiac cycles; assessment of autonomic tone (sympathetic or parasympathetic predominance) and reactivity.
s5697 **Phonocardiography (PCG)** — graphic recording of heart sounds and murmurs; objective determination of the phase, shape and frequency of a murmur.
s5698 **Echocardiography** (see topic 8) and **Doppler echocardiography** — the direction and velocity of blood flow, regurgitation, gradients.
s5699 **Rheovasography, Doppler ultrasound** — the state of the peripheral vessels.
s5700 **CT, MRI, angiocardiography, cardiac catheterization** — complex defects and preoperative examination.
s5701 **Laboratory methods:** markers of myocardial damage (CK-MB, troponins, LDH), natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP — heart failure), CRP, ASO (streptococcal infection), blood culture (infective endocarditis), lipid profile.

s5752 ECG semiotics
s5753 Arrhythmias:
s5754 **Sinus tachycardia** — HR above the age norm, the rhythm source is the sinus node; causes: exertion, fever, anemia, hypoxia, thyrotoxicosis, myocarditis, heart failure, “drop” heart.
s5755 **Sinus bradycardia** — HR below the age norm; vagotonia, athletes, hypothyroidism, raised intracranial pressure, sick sinus syndrome.
s5756 **Sinus (respiratory) arrhythmia** — the RR intervals change with the phase of breathing; it disappears when the breath is held — normal.
s5757 **Extrasystole** — premature contractions: **atrial** (deformed P, narrow QRS, incomplete compensatory pause), **AV nodal**, **ventricular** (wide, deformed QRS without P, discordant ST and T, **complete compensatory pause**). In children it is often functional (autonomic), but also occurs in myocarditis and cardiomyopathy.
s5758 **Paroxysmal tachycardia** — a sharp increase in HR that begins and ends suddenly (160–200 in older children, up to 200–300 at an early age); **supraventricular** (narrow QRS) and **ventricular** (wide QRS — dangerous) forms; the child is restless and pale, and heart failure may develop quickly.
s5759 **Atrial flutter and fibrillation** — rare in children (heart defects, after surgery).
s5760 **WPW syndrome** (Wolff–Parkinson–White) — an accessory pathway: short PQ (<0.1 s), **delta wave** at the start of QRS, widened QRS; predisposition to paroxysmal tachycardia.
s5761 **Blocks:** sinoatrial; **AV blocks** — 1st degree (prolonged PQ), 2nd degree (some QRS complexes are dropped — Mobitz I/II), 3rd degree (complete — the atria and ventricles contract independently, marked bradycardia, Morgagni–Adams–Stokes attacks; congenital — associated with maternal SLE); **bundle branch blocks**.
s5762 **Long QT syndrome** — congenital or drug-induced; risk of fainting and sudden death.
s5763 Signs of hypertrophy:
s5764 **left ventricle** — left axis deviation (relative to the age norm), tall R in V5–6, deep S in V1–2, ST depression and negative T in the left chest leads;
s5765 **right ventricle** — marked right axis deviation, tall R (qR) in V1–2, deep S in V5–6;
s5766 **left atrium** — wide, double-humped P (“**P mitrale**”) in I, II, aVL;
s5767 **right atrium** — tall, pointed P (“**P pulmonale**”) in II, III, aVF.
s5768 **Signs of myocardial damage:** reduced voltage of the waves, QRS deformation, arrhythmias and blocks, ST depression of more than 1 mm below the isoline, flattening or inversion of the T wave.
s5769 Changes in arterial pressure
s5770 **Arterial hypertension** — **primary (essential)** — most often in adolescents, with obesity and hereditary predisposition; **secondary (symptomatic)** — kidney diseases (glomerulonephritis, pyelonephritis, polycystic disease, renal artery stenosis — the most common cause in children), **coarctation of the aorta**, endocrine diseases (Itsenko–Cushing, pheochromocytoma, thyrotoxicosis, hyperaldosteronism), CNS damage (tumor, injury, encephalitis), drugs (glucocorticoids).
s5771 **Arterial hypotension** — primary (constitutional, autonomic dysfunction — vagotonia) and secondary: adrenal insufficiency, hypothyroidism, pituitary insufficiency, aortic stenosis, shock, collapse, blood loss, dehydration, anaphylaxis.
