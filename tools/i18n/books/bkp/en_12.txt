s2326 Anatomical and physiological features
s2327 Adipose tissue consists mainly of **white fat** and partly of **brown fat**. In brown fat cells oxidation is uncoupled from phosphorylation — energy is converted not into ATP but into **heat** (**non-shivering thermogenesis**).

s2360 Semiotics of changes in the subcutaneous fat
s2361 **Deficiency of the fat layer.** In young children — **hypotrophy** (topic 2), at an older age — **dystrophy**, the most severe degree — **cachexia**. Causes: constitutional (asthenic type), insufficient or unbalanced nutrition, digestive diseases (malabsorption), prolonged intoxication, chronic infections (tuberculosis), helminthic invasion, CNS pathology, endocrine diseases (thyrotoxicosis, diabetes mellitus), malignant tumors.
s2362 **Excessive deposition (obesity).** If the body mass exceeds the mass appropriate for height by **more than 15%**, it is obesity (the degrees are in topic 2; it is now also assessed by BMI centiles: **≥95th centile** or, by WHO, **Z-score > +2**). It most often develops **before 4 years and at 7–11 years**.
s2363 **Primary (exogenous-constitutional)** — overeating, low activity; the fat is evenly distributed, the height is appropriate for age or tall.
s2364 **Secondary** — endocrine (hypothyroidism, Itsenko–Cushing, hypogonadism), hypothalamic (tumor, craniopharyngioma, after a neuroinfection), hereditary syndromes (Prader–Willi, Laurence–Moon–Bardet–Biedl, Down).
s2365 **In Itsenko–Cushing syndrome** fat accumulates on **the face** (“moon face”), **the back of the neck** (“buffalo neck”), the upper body and the abdomen, while the limbs are thin; pink-purple striae, hyperemic cheeks.
s2366 **Paratrophy** — with incorrect (carbohydrate-rich) feeding: the mass is excessive, but the turgor is reduced, the tissues are hydrophilic and pale.

s2382 Edema is the accumulation of fluid in the interstitial spaces. s2398 4.6. Anatomical and physiological features of the lymphatic system
s2399 The lymphatic system includes **the thymus, the spleen, the lymph nodes**, circulating lymphocytes and the lymphoid aggregates of the mucous membranes (tonsils, adenoids, Peyer's patches of the ileum, the appendix). The thymus and bone marrow are **central**, and the rest **peripheral**, lymphoid organs.
s2400 The thymus
s2401 It lies in the upper mediastinum behind the sternum; it develops **from the 6th week** of intrauterine life from the 3rd–4th pairs of pharyngeal pouches; it forms **before the other lymphoid organs**.
s2402 In the newborn its mass is **10–15 g** (the largest relative to body mass); the cortex predominates over the medulla.
s2403 Its absolute mass increases **until puberty (9–12 years)** (≈30–40 g), after which **age-related involution** begins — the lymphoid tissue is replaced by fat, but the gland persists throughout life.
s2404 Its function is **the differentiation of T lymphocytes** and the production of thymic hormones (thymosin, thymopoietin).
s2405 Under the influence of adverse factors (infection, stress, antibiotics, glucocorticoids, cytostatics) **accidental (reversible) involution** may occur — at an early age it leads to secondary immunodeficiency.
s2406 The spleen
s2407 It lies in the left hypochondrium of the abdominal cavity at the level of ribs IX–XI; its long axis runs along rib X from back to front and downward.
s2408 It is not fully formed at birth: the trabeculae and capsule are weak, the shape is round and lobulated.
s2409 Mass: newborn — **11 g**, 1 year — **26 g**, 12 years — **94 g**, adults — **150 g** (0.25–0.3% of the body mass — constant).
s2410 Functions: immune (formation of T and B lymphocytes and antibodies), destruction of aged erythrocytes and platelets, blood depot; hematopoiesis in the fetal period.

s2418 Lymph nodes are oval formations located in groups where large lymphatic vessels converge; they are the site of tissue fluid drainage, **a barrier** against infection and the multiplication of lymphocytes.
