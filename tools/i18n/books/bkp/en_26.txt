s5173 8.8. Radiological examination and cardiometry
s5174 **Chest X-ray** (in frontal and lateral projections, and if necessary in oblique projections with esophageal contrast) gives information on the size, shape (configuration) and position of the heart, the state of the great vessels and **the pulmonary blood flow** (increased or reduced lung markings).
s5175 **The normal cardiac shadow** in the frontal projection:
s5176 **right contour** — 2 arcs: above, the superior vena cava (and the ascending aorta), below, the right atrium;
s5177 **left contour** — 4 arcs: the aortic arch, the pulmonary artery, the left atrial appendage, the left ventricle.
s5178 In the newborn the cardiac shadow is relatively large, spherical and horizontally positioned, with a wide vascular bundle (thymus); with age it becomes elongated and oblique.

s5185 **Cardiometry** — measuring the dimensions of the heart on an X-ray. The most widely used parameter is **the cardiothoracic index (CTI)**:
s5186 **CTI = (CE + ED) / AB**, where CE is the distance from the midline to the outermost point of the right cardiac contour, ED — from the midline to the outermost point of the left contour, AB — the inner transverse diameter of the chest at the level of the diaphragmatic dome.
s5187 **Normal:** **≤0.55** in young children, **≤0.5** in older children. An increased index means **cardiomegaly**.

s5190 Pathological configurations:
s5191 **mitral** — the “cardiac waist” is flattened (the arcs of the pulmonary artery and the left atrial appendage bulge): mitral stenosis and insufficiency;
s5192 **aortic** — the “cardiac waist” is deepened, the left ventricle enlarged, the apex displaced downward and to the left (“sitting duck”): aortic defects, arterial hypertension;
s5193 **spherical or triangular (“trapezoid”)** — exudative pericarditis;
s5194 **“wooden shoe” (“Dutch clog”)** — **tetralogy of Fallot** (raised apex, deep “cardiac waist”, reduced lung markings);

s5202 8.9. ECG and echocardiography — basic concepts
s5203 **Electrocardiography (ECG)** — recording of the electrophysiological processes in the heart; it gives information on the rhythm, HR, conduction, hypertrophy of the heart chambers and the state of the myocardium. 12 standard leads: 3 standard (I, II, III), 3 augmented (aVR, aVL, aVF) and 6 chest (V1–V6) leads.
s5204 **P wave** — atrial excitation;
s5205 **PQ interval** — conduction of the impulse from the atria to the ventricles;
s5206 **QRS complex** — ventricular excitation (depolarization);
s5207 **ST segment and T wave** — ventricular repolarization;
s5208 **QT interval** — electrical systole of the ventricles.
s5209 HR = 60 / RR (in seconds). The age-related features of the ECG in children (right axis deviation, short waves and intervals, negative T waves) are given in detail in topic 9.
s5210 **Echocardiography** — ultrasound examination of the structure and function of the heart (2.5–7.5 MHz probe); **the main method in pediatric cardiology**. Its possibilities:
s5211 the size of the heart chambers, wall thickness, **contractility** (ejection fraction, normally ≥55–60%);
s5212 the structure and movement of the valves, **regurgitation** and **stenosis** (Doppler echocardiography);
s5213 **congenital defects** (septal defects, patent ductus arteriosus, tetralogy of Fallot, etc.) — the direction and volume of the shunt;
s5214 pulmonary artery pressure;
s5215 the state of the pericardium and **fluid in the pericardial cavity**;
s5216 intracardiac formations (thrombus, tumor, vegetations — infective endocarditis, false chords).
s5217 For early detection of congenital heart defects in newborns, **pulse oximetry screening** is carried out: SpO2 is measured on the right hand and a foot at 24–48 hours of life. If **SpO~2~ <95%** or the difference between the hand and the foot is **>3%**, the baby is referred for echocardiography.

s5219 Suspicion of a congenital heart defect in a newborn

s5221 Physical detection of cardiomegaly
s5222 Signs of cardiomegaly: “cardiac hump”, a diffuse and displaced apex beat, a cardiac impulse, widening of the borders of relative dullness on percussion. Confirmation — an increased **CTI** on X-ray and **echocardiography**.
s5223 Skill 1. Palpation of the apex beat
s5224 Wash and warm the hands; the child lies on the back or sits, the doctor on the right.
s5225 Place the right palm on the sternum and the fingers along the left intercostal spaces toward the anterior axillary line.
s5226 Where the pulsation is felt, find the outermost and lowest point with the tips of the 2nd–3rd fingers.

s5228 Check the cardiac area for a “cat's purr” with the palm and determine whether it coincides with systole or diastole (comparing with the carotid pulse).
s5229 Skill 2. Determining the borders of relative cardiac dullness
s5230 The child stands or lies, the doctor on the right.
s5231 Find the upper border of liver dullness along the right midclavicular line.
s5232 One intercostal space higher, place the finger parallel to the sternum and determine the right border from outside inward.

s5234 Determine the left border in the intercostal space of the apex beat from outside inward.

s5236 Skill 3. Examining the pulse in the peripheral arteries
s5237 Check the synchrony and equality of the pulse in the radial arteries of both arms simultaneously.
s5238 Examine the pulse symmetrically in the carotid, brachial, femoral, popliteal, posterior tibial and dorsalis pedis arteries.

s5241 Skill 4. Calculating the cardiothoracic index
s5242 On a frontal X-ray draw the midline (along the spinous processes).
s5243 Measure the distance from the midline to the outermost point of the right contour (CE) and to the outermost point of the left contour (ED).
s5244 Measure the inner transverse diameter of the chest at the level of the diaphragmatic dome (AB).
s5245 Calculate CTI = (CE + ED) / AB.
s5246 Assessment: ≤0.55 at an early age, ≤0.5 in older children — normal; if increased — cardiomegaly.

s5405 1) What is the bulge on the chest called and what does it indicate? 2) Assess the apex beat and percussion findings. 3) Which defect is the systolic thrill typical of? 4) Which investigations are needed?
