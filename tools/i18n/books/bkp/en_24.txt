s4958 The fetus lives **under conditions of hypoxia**, but is adapted to it: the respiratory surface of the placenta is large, the blood flow is rapid, fetal blood contains **much hemoglobin and many erythrocytes**, **HbF** has a greater affinity for oxygen than adult hemoglobin, and the tissues need little oxygen.
s4959 In the neonatal period, with hypoxia, acidosis, cold exposure, pneumonia or RDS, pulmonary vascular resistance may rise again and **the fetal communications may reopen** — the **“persistent fetal circulation” syndrome** (pulmonary hypertension, right-to-left shunt, cyanosis). s4960 With age the heart **“becomes smaller and descends”**: **the upper border rib II → 2nd intercostal space → rib III**; **the right border from the parasternal line → to the edge of the sternum**; **the left border from 2 cm outside the midclavicular line → to the midclavicular line**. The numbers: **“2 – 1 – 0”** cm outside (0–2, 2–7, 7+ years).
s4961 In the newborn, **weak pulses in the femoral arteries** are a sign of **coarctation of the aorta**; when the ductus arteriosus closes (in the 1st–2nd weeks of life) the child's condition may deteriorate sharply (“duct-dependent” defects). s4962 8.1. Development of the heart and the fetal circulation
s4963 The heart forms from the mesoderm as a tube in **the 3rd week** of intrauterine development and begins to contract **from day 22**. The division of the heart into atria and ventricles and the formation of the valves take place in **weeks 2–8** — this is **the critical period** for the formation of **congenital heart defects** (viruses, especially rubella, drugs, alcohol, ionizing radiation, maternal diabetes mellitus).
s4964 Features of the fetal circulation:
s4965 there is **a placental circulation**: gas exchange takes place not in the lungs but in the placenta;
s4966 **the pulmonary circulation hardly works** — only **≈10%** of the cardiac output passes through the lungs;
s4967 blood passes from both ventricles into the systemic circulation through **three shunts**: **the ductus venosus (Arantius)**, **the foramen ovale**, **the ductus arteriosus (Botallo)**;
s4968 the fetal organs are supplied with **mixed blood**; the most oxygenated blood goes to **the liver, brain and arms** (branches close to the superior vena cava), and the lower part of the body receives less oxygenated blood;
s4969 the pressure in the pulmonary artery and the aorta is almost the same and low.
s4970 **The path of the blood:** oxygen-rich blood comes from the placenta to the fetus via **the umbilical vein** → half to the liver (portal vein), the rest via **the ductus venosus** to the inferior vena cava → right atrium. Most of the blood from the inferior vena cava passes, with the help of the Eustachian valve, through **the foramen ovale** to the left atrium → left ventricle → aorta → head and arms. Blood from the superior vena cava goes to the right atrium → right ventricle → pulmonary artery → via **the ductus arteriosus** to the descending aorta (pulmonary vascular resistance is high). From the aorta blood returns to the placenta via **the two umbilical arteries**.

s4975 8.2. Neonatal circulation: the transitional period
s4976 After birth the circulatory system is fundamentally reorganized:
s4977 **the placental circulation stops**; the umbilical vessels close;
s4978 with **the first breath** the lungs expand, pulmonary vascular resistance falls sharply and pulmonary blood flow increases **≈5 times** — **the pulmonary circulation starts working**;
s4979 venous return to the left atrium increases, and the pressure in the left atrium exceeds that in the right atrium — **the valve of the foramen ovale closes**;
s4980 under the influence of increased blood oxygen and decreased prostaglandins, the smooth muscle of **the ductus arteriosus** contracts and it closes;
s4981 the systemic and pulmonary circulations begin to work **in series**; the load on the left ventricle increases.
