s1480 3.3. Development of the sense organs
s1481 At birth all the sense organs work, but their reactions are generalized and undifferentiated.
s1482 **Vision.** The newborn's cornea is thick, the lens round and the eyeball short — **all newborns are farsighted (hypermetropic)**. In the first 2–3 weeks there is photophobia and no tears. At 2 weeks tears begin to be secreted and brief monocular fixation appears; at 3 weeks the baby looks binocularly at a stationary object; **at 2 months** he follows a bright toy; **at 3 months** binocular vision forms and he distinguishes red and yellow; at 6 months he distinguishes bright colors; at 3 years color discrimination is developed; visual acuity is low until 4–5 years. Transient strabismus and nystagmus are normal in the first months.
s1483 Figure 3.2
s1484 Structure of the eye (diagram): 1 — pupil; 2 — cornea; 3 — lens; 4 — iris; 5 — ciliary body; 6 — sclera; 7 — choroid; 8 — vitreous body; 9 — retina; 10 — fovea; 11 — blind spot; 12 — optic nerve (Navruzova, Geppe)
s1485 **Hearing.** The auditory analyzer is anatomically and functionally formed at birth — the newborn responds to sound by startling or crying. The external auditory canal is narrow and short, the eardrum thick and horizontal, and **the Eustachian tube short and wide** — a tendency to otitis. From 7–8 weeks the baby turns the head toward a sound, and at 3–4 months recognizes the mother's voice. **Audiological screening** is performed on all newborns in the maternity hospital.
s1486 Figure 3.3
s1487 Structure of the ear (diagram): 1 — auricle; 2 — external auditory canal; 3 — eardrum; 4 — malleus; 5 — incus; 6 — stapes; 7 — Eustachian tube; 8 — vestibular apparatus; 9 — cochlea; 10 — auditory nerve (Navruzova, Geppe)
s1488 **Taste** works from birth: the baby responds positively (sucks) to sweet taste and negatively to bitter, sour and salty tastes; taste is fully differentiated by 4–5 months.
s1489 **Smell:** the baby distinguishes strong odors from the 1st month, pleasant and unpleasant ones at 2–4 months, and faint odors at 7–8 months.
s1490 **Skin sensation:** tactile sensation appears before the other senses. The newborn responds to a touch on the skin with a generalized movement; at 7–8 months he shows precisely the place of the stimulus; he distinguishes pain points at 7–8 years. In the newborn the sensitivity to cold is higher than to heat.
s1491 **The vestibular apparatus** works at birth: when the newborn is rotated, nystagmus appears in the direction opposite to the rotation; rocking calms him.
s1492 3.4. Methods of examining the nervous system
s1493 **History:** complaints (headache, dizziness, impaired memory and mood, convulsions, sleep disturbance), pregnancy, delivery and the neonatal period, development of static and mental functions. The parents of a child older than 5–6 years are questioned in the child's absence.
s1494 **Inspection:** the child's response to the surroundings (indifference, drowsiness, excitability), facial expression, mimicry, behavior, consciousness, position, shape and size of the head, fontanelles and sutures, stigmas of dysembryogenesis, coordination of movements (finger-nose and heel-knee tests), gait, speech.
s1495 **The cranial nerves** (12 pairs) are examined by methods appropriate to the child's age: response to odors (I), response to light and following an object (II), eye movements, pupillary reaction, ptosis, strabismus (III, IV, VI), sucking and chewing (V), symmetry of the face at rest and when crying (VII), response to sound and the rotation reflex (VIII), sucking and swallowing (IX, X), turning the head (XI), movements of the tongue (XII).
s1496 **Muscle tone:** the newborn has physiological flexor hypertonia. When the child is pulled to sitting by the hands, the arms extend slightly at the elbows and the head follows the trunk. In older children the tone is assessed by the resistance to passive movements. Reduced tone is **hypotonia**, increased tone **hypertonia**, and alternating tone **dystonia**.
s1497 **Reflexes:** in the newborn and infant, the innate unconditioned reflexes (section 3.5); tendon reflexes (easily elicited and with a wide zone until 2 years), skin (abdominal) reflexes (weak in children).
s1498 **The autonomic nervous system — dermographism:** a line is drawn on the skin with a blunt object. **Red** dermographism — predominance of parasympathetic tone; **white** — predominance of sympathetic tone; **raised (urticarial)** — excessive vagal tone and increased vascular permeability; **mixed** — autonomic dystonia.
s1499 **Additional methods: lumbar puncture** and analysis of the cerebrospinal fluid (between LIII and LIV), **neurosonography** (ultrasound of the brain through the anterior fontanelle — in newborns and infants), electroencephalography, rheoencephalography, computed and magnetic resonance tomography, Doppler ultrasound of the cerebral vessels, electromyography.

s1596 3.6. Semiotics of nervous system damage
s1597 Main complaints and objective signs
s1598 Headache, dizziness, vomiting, convulsions, disturbed sleep and behavior, reduced intellect and memory, movement disorders. On inspection: indifference to the surroundings or excitability, a forced position (the “pointer dog” position — meningitis, opisthotonus), the shape and size of the head (hydrocephalus, microcephaly, “tower skull”), convulsions, muscle development and tone, impaired coordination, stigmas of dysembryogenesis (there should be no more than 5).
s1599 **Focal signs:** a difference in the width of the palpebral fissures, ptosis, strabismus (pairs III, IV, VI), drooping of the corner of the mouth and flattening of the nasolabial fold on one side (pair VII — facial nerve paresis), bulbar disorders (pairs IX–X); paresis and paralysis, tremor, hyperkinesis; ataxia, nystagmus; sensory disturbances.

s1606 Main syndromes
s1607 **Syndrome of reduced excitability (hypoexcitability)** — reduced motor and mental activity, a prolonged latent period of the reflexes, hyporeflexia, muscle hypotonia. It occurs in perinatal CNS damage, hereditary diseases (Down syndrome, phenylketonuria), metabolic disorders (hypoglycemia, acidosis) and severe somatic diseases.
s1608 **Hyperexcitability syndrome** — motor restlessness, emotional instability, sleep disturbance, enhanced innate reflexes, tremor, a tendency to convulsions, often muscle hypertonia.
s1609 **Hydrocephalic and hypertensive-hydrocephalic syndrome** — an increase in the volume of cerebrospinal fluid and dilation of the cerebral ventricles: rapid enlargement of the head, separation of the sutures, enlargement and bulging of the anterior fontanelle, dilated veins on the scalp, thinning of the skin of the temples, disproportion between the cranial and facial parts. When intracranial pressure rises, these are joined by a high-pitched, painful cry, vomiting, **Graefe's sign (“setting sun”)** — a strip of sclera visible above the pupil, convergent strabismus, horizontal nystagmus, and a “cracked pot” sound on percussion of the skull.
s1610 **Convulsive syndrome** — paroxysmal involuntary muscle contractions: **clonic** (short, successive contractions), **tonic** (prolonged contraction), **tonic-clonic**; generalized and partial (local). Causes:
s1611 in newborns: brain malformations, perinatal CNS damage (hypoxia, birth injury, hemorrhage), intrauterine infections, metabolic disorders (hypoglycemia, hypocalcemia, hypomagnesemia, hyperbilirubinemia), withdrawal syndrome;
s1612 in infancy: neuroinfections, hyperthermia, spasmophilia;
s1613 in older children: epilepsy, brain tumors, hemorrhage.
s1614 **Febrile seizures** are convulsions, most often generalized tonic-clonic, that occur in children aged 6 months to 5 years against the background of fever (usually above 38 °C) without a neuroinfection. **Breath-holding spells** — cessation of breathing at the peak of a deep inspiration during intense crying or emotional stress, with cyanosis, brief loss of consciousness and sometimes convulsions.
s1615 **Movement disorder syndrome** — hypotonia, hypertonia, dystonia; **cerebral palsy (CP)** — hypotonia and hyporeflexia at birth, dystonia from the 2nd month, then muscle hypertonia (the arms internally rotated and extended, the fingers clenched in a fist, the legs crossed).
s1616 **Coma syndrome** (see topic 1, section 1.8) — grade I: eyes closed, uncoordinated defensive movements, pupillary reflexes preserved; grade II: no defensive reaction to pain and no pupillary reflexes, breathing and cardiac activity preserved; grade III: breathing impaired, pupils dilated and fixed, BP fallen.
s1617 **Syndrome of autonomic-visceral disorders** — mottled and pale skin, acrocyanosis, cold hands and feet, profuse sweating, prolonged subfebrile temperature without infection, emotional instability.
s1618 Meningeal syndrome
s1619 **The meningeal syndrome** is irritation of the meninges; it occurs in all forms of acute meningitis. It consists of three groups of signs:
s1620 **General cerebral signs:** fever, severe headache, vomiting that brings no relief, hyperesthesia (heightened sensitivity of the skin, to light and to sound), impaired consciousness, convulsions.
s1621 **Meningeal signs** (signs of the meninges).
s1622 **Changes in the cerebrospinal fluid** (increased pressure, cytosis, protein).
