s2511 Criteria for describing lymph nodes
s2512 For each palpated group the following are recorded:
s2513 **Location** (group) and **symmetry**.
s2514 **Number: single** — up to 3 in a group; **multiple** — more than 3.
s2515 **Size** — in mm or cm, or by comparison. Normally up to **0.3–0.5 cm** (up to 0.5–1 cm according to Kildiyarova).
s2516 **Consistency:** normally **soft-elastic**; in pathology dense, hard (“pebble”) or softened (fluctuation — suppuration).
s2517 **Mobility:** normally mobile; in pathology poorly mobile, immobile.
s2518 **Relation to the surrounding tissues and to each other:** normally not adherent to each other or to the skin; in pathology they form **a “packet”** (conglomerate).
s2519 **Pain:** normally painless.
s2520 **Overlying skin:** normally unchanged; in inflammation hyperemia, warmth, edema, fistula.

s2538 Examination of the spleen
s2539 **Palpation** — deep sliding palpation according to N.D. Strazhesko:
s2540 The child lies on the back with the legs slightly bent at the knees and the arms along the body; the doctor sits on the child's right side.
s2541 The doctor places **the left hand** on the left half of the child's chest in the area of ribs VII–X and squeezes it slightly (restricting respiratory movement).
s2542 The fingers of **the right hand**, slightly bent, are placed perpendicular to the left costal arch, below it — **starting from below** (from the level of the umbilicus, so as not to miss an enlarged spleen).
s2543 During the child's expiration a skin fold is formed with the fingers and they are pressed deep into the abdominal cavity; **on deep inspiration** the descending pole of the spleen strikes the fingers.
s2544 If there is no result, the fingers are moved upward and the maneuver is repeated. If the spleen is not detected in the supine position, it is palpated with the child lying **on the right side** (right leg extended, left leg bent at the knee and hip joints) — the abdominal muscles relax and the spleen moves forward.
s2545 **In a healthy child the spleen is not palpable** (its anterior edge falls 3–4 cm short of the costal arch). If it is palpable, it is **enlarged 1.5–2 times**. When it is detected, the following are assessed: how far it projects below the costal arch (cm), the edge (sharp/blunt, notches), the consistency (soft, dense, hard), the surface, mobility and tenderness.
s2546 **Percussion** — **quiet (soft)** percussion (the spleen borders the intestine and stomach, which give a tympanic sound):
s2547 The child lies **on the right side** or stands; the left arm is on the head.
s2548 **Determining the length:** the pleximeter finger is placed along rib X, parallel to it. **Anterior border** — percussion is performed from the costal arch backward along rib X; a mark is made where the dull sound appears (along the edge facing the resonant sound). **Posterior border** — percussion is performed from the posterior axillary line forward along rib X. The distance between the two points is the **length** of the spleen.
s2549 **Determining the width:** a line perpendicular to rib X is drawn from the middle of the length; with the pleximeter parallel to rib X, percussion is performed toward the center of the spleen **from above** (two intercostal spaces higher) and **from below** (from the costal arch). The borders are marked along the edge on the side of the resonant sound.

s2582 4.8. Semiotics of lymphatic system involvement
s2583 Lymphadenopathy
s2584 **Lymphadenopathy** — enlargement of the lymph nodes (inflammatory and non-inflammatory). **Lymphadenitis** — inflammation of a node: enlarged, **painful**, the overlying skin red, warm and swollen; **fluctuation** when it suppurates. **Micropolyadenia** — small (3–5 mm), dense, mobile, painless nodes in many groups (chronic intoxication, lymphatic diathesis).
s2585 **Local (regional) lymphadenopathy** — a process in the area drained by the node: an infected wound, furuncle, folliculitis, pyoderma, dental caries, stomatitis.
s2586 **Cervical nodes:** tonsillitis (angina), chronic tonsillitis, scarlet fever, diphtheria, adenoiditis; tuberculosis (a dense painless “packet” adherent to the skin, **caseous breakdown and a fistula**, followed by irregular **“stellate” scars**); the initial stage of lymphogranulomatosis (dense-elastic conglomerates fused together — **“a sack of potatoes”**, painless); lymphosarcoma (very hard, painless, enlarges rapidly).
s2587 **Occipital and mastoid: rubella** (before the rash!), infections of the hairy part of the head, pediculosis.
s2588 **Preauricular:** mumps, conjunctivitis.
s2589 **Axillary and cubital: “cat scratch” disease** (bartonellosis — a hand injury), infections of the arm and hand, BCG vaccination complication (left axilla).
s2590 Enlargement of **the supraclavicular** nodes is always a serious sign (lymphoma, mediastinal and abdominal tumors).
s2591 **Generalized lymphadenopathy** — enlargement of 2 or more non-adjacent groups.

s2594 Acute infections
s2595 Infectious mononucleosis (all groups, especially cervical — the configuration of the neck changes, “packet”; the spleen is enlarged, atypical mononuclear cells); rubella (occipital, mastoid); measles (cervical, occipital); adenovirus and parainfluenza (cervical, occipital — slight); yersiniosis, pseudotuberculosis

s2600 Autoimmune and other

s2602 **“Alarming” signs of lymphadenopathy** (a biopsy or an oncohematologist consultation is needed): a node **>2 cm** that does not shrink within 4–6 weeks; **supraclavicular** location; **hard, immobile**, fused conglomerate; **general signs** — prolonged fever, night sweats, weight loss (B symptoms), hepatosplenomegaly, cytopenia or blasts in the blood.
s2603 Splenomegaly
s2604 **Splenomegaly** — persistent enlargement of the spleen. **Primary** — diseases of the spleen itself (cyst, tumor, congenital anomalies) — rare. **Secondary** — most often:
s2605 **infections** — infectious mononucleosis, sepsis, bacterial endocarditis, malaria, leishmaniasis, tuberculosis, brucellosis, viral hepatitis, congenital infections (CMV, toxoplasmosis, rubella);
s2606 **hemolytic anemias** (“work” hypertrophy) — microspherocytosis, thalassemia, hemolytic disease of the newborn;
s2607 **liver diseases and portal hypertension** — liver cirrhosis, chronic hepatitis, portal vein thrombosis (after umbilical vein catheterization);
s2608 **tumor and infiltrative processes** — leukemias, lymphomas, histiocytosis, storage diseases (Gaucher, Niemann–Pick);
s2609 **autoimmune** — SLE, the systemic form of juvenile arthritis.
s2610 **Hypersplenism** — enlargement of the spleen together with excessive destruction of blood cells: **splenomegaly + anemia + leukopenia (neutropenia) + thrombocytopenia + compensatory hyperplasia of the bone marrow**. It is seen in all diseases with portal hypertension.
s2611 Lymphatic-hypoplastic diathesis
s2612 **Lymphatic-hypoplastic diathesis** — a constitutional anomaly with **generalized hyperplasia** of the lymphoid tissue, reduced function of the endocrine system (adrenal glands) and reduced adaptability. Signs may be present from the first months (especially with thymomegaly), but are most often pronounced at **2–5 years**.
s2613 Signs:
s2614 **thymomegaly** (widening of the vascular bundle area on percussion, enlarged thymic shadow on X-ray);
s2615 generalized **lymphadenopathy**, hyperplasia of the **adenoids** and tonsils;
s2616 **hepatosplenomegaly**;
s2617 appearance: **pastose**, pale, sluggish, low muscle tone, well-developed fat layer but reduced turgor, long limbs (disproportionate build);
s2618 in the blood — moderate **leukocytosis and lymphocytosis**, neutropenia; arterial hypotension;
s2619 frequent ARVI, reduced adaptation to stress (infection, surgery, anesthesia, vaccination) — **risk of sudden death** due to adrenal insufficiency;
s2620 an enlarged thymus at an early age may cause **respiratory disturbances** (dyspnea, stridor, apnea) and compression of the esophagus.
s2621 Before anesthesia, surgery or a severe infection in a child with thymomegaly, the pediatrician and anesthesiologist are warned — because of the risk of adrenal insufficiency a glucocorticoid should be kept ready.
s2622 4.9. Clinical section
s2623 A child with a rash — diagnostic approach
s2624 When examining a child with a rash, the answers to three questions are sought:
s2625 **What is the element?** (macule, papule, vesicle, hemorrhagic) — examined by diascopy.
s2626 **In what order and where did it appear?** (in stages — measles; simultaneously — rubella, scarlet fever; polymorphic — chickenpox; dense in the folds — scarlet fever; symmetric on the legs — Henoch–Schönlein).
s2627 **What is the general condition and what are the accompanying signs?** (fever, lymph nodes, mucous membranes, meningeal signs).

s2632 Description of a hemorrhagic rash: in **thrombocytopenia** (Werlhof) — petechiae and ecchymoses are **asymmetric, “leopard skin”**, bleeding from the mucous membranes; in **vasculitis** (Henoch–Schönlein) — a **symmetric, palpable** papular-hemorrhagic rash, often with joint and abdominal pain; platelets are normal; in **meningococcemia** — stellate, necrotic, against a background of fever and intoxication.
s2633 Assessment of dehydration by skin signs
s2634 According to WHO (IMCI), **the return of the skin pinch** is an important sign of dehydration: the abdominal skin halfway between the umbilicus and the side of the abdomen is pinched in a vertical direction with the thumb and index finger and released. If it goes back **slowly (less than 2 seconds)** — a sign of **some** dehydration; **very slowly (≥2 seconds)** — of **severe** dehydration (assessed together with other signs: condition, eyes, thirst).
s2635 A child with lymphadenopathy — algorithm
s2636 Determine whether it is local or generalized (palpate all groups!).
s2637 If local — carefully examine the drainage area (tonsils, teeth, skin, scratches).
s2638 Features of the node: painful, warm, red — **inflammation**; dense, painless, adherent — **tuberculosis, tumor**.
s2639 Examine the spleen and liver.
s2640 Complete blood count — the first test; then targeted investigations.
s2641 Skill 1. Inspection of the skin and its appendages
s2642 A warm room, daylight; wash and warm the hands; talk to the child, obtain the mother's consent.
s2643 Undress the child step by step.
s2644 Inspect the skin from top to bottom: head, face, neck, trunk, limbs, folds, palms and soles, between the fingers, perineum.
s2645 Assess the color, moisture, cleanliness, venous network and integrity.
s2646 If there is a rash — describe it by all criteria; examine it by diascopy.
s2647 Inspect the hair and nails.
s2648 Record the result in the case history.
s2649 Skill 2. Palpation of the skin and special tests
s2650 Determine moisture by stroking symmetric areas with the palm.
s2651 Determine temperature with the back of the hand.
s2652 Elasticity — pick up a skin fold on the front of the chest or the back of the hand and assess how quickly it smooths out.
s2653 **Tourniquet sign:** place a sphygmomanometer cuff on the upper arm, raise the pressure to midway between systolic and diastolic (or to a level at which venous outflow stops but the pulse is preserved), hold for 3–5 minutes; count the petechiae in the antecubital area (within a circle 2.5 cm in diameter); **>5 — positive**.
s2654 **Pinch sign:** pick up a fold in the subclavian area and shift it; assess for hemorrhage.
s2655 **Dermographism:** draw lines on the skin of the chest with a spatula; record the time of appearance, color, width and time of disappearance.
s2656 Skill 3. Assessment of the subcutaneous fat and turgor
s2657 Pick up a skin-fat fold on the abdomen (at the level of the umbilicus), chest, below the shoulder blade, the back of the upper arm, the inner thigh and the face; measure the thickness (with a caliper or ruler).
s2658 Compare the result with the age norm; assess the distribution (even/uneven).
s2659 Consistency — by palpation (soft, finely granular, foci of hardening).
s2660 Turgor — squeeze all the soft tissues on the inner surface of the thigh and assess the resistance.
s2661 Detect edema: press on the sternum, the anterior surface of the shin, the ankle and the lumbar region for 5–10 seconds.
s2662 Conclusion: “The subcutaneous fat is sufficiently (insufficiently / excessively) developed, evenly distributed, soft-elastic in consistency, turgor preserved, no edema.”
s2663 Skill 4. Palpation of the peripheral lymph nodes
s2664 The child sits or stands, the doctor in front of or behind the child.
s2665 Palpate symmetrically with the fingertips of both hands, with light circular movements, pressing the nodes against the bony base.
s2666 Order: occipital → preauricular → mastoid → submandibular → submental → anterior cervical and tonsillar → posterior cervical → supraclavicular → infraclavicular → axillary → thoracic → cubital → inguinal → popliteal.
s2667 For each group: number, size, consistency, mobility, adhesion, tenderness, state of the overlying skin.
s2668 Conclusion: normal / micropolyadenia / regional or generalized lymphadenopathy / lymphadenitis.
s2669 Skill 5. Palpation and percussion of the spleen
s2670 The child lies on the back with the legs slightly bent; the doctor is on the right side.
s2671 The left hand fixes the lower left part of the chest; the fingers of the right hand, starting from the level of the umbilicus toward the left costal arch, are pressed deeper during expiration, and the pole of the spleen is sought on deep inspiration.
