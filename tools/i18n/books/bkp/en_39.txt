s7996 13.2. Functional investigations
s7997 **Contraindications to tube methods:** exacerbation of peptic ulcer disease, gastrointestinal bleeding, acute cholecystitis, chronic diseases in the stage of decompensation, diverticula, esophageal anomalies, swallowing disorders, an attack of bronchial asthma, seizure syndrome.
s7998 Examination of gastric secretion
s7999 **Fractional gastric intubation** (secretory, acid-forming, enzymatic and motor functions) — now hardly used.
s8000 **Intragastric pH-metry** — the juice is not aspirated; a probe with pH electrodes measures while in contact with the mucosa. In a 5-sensor probe the sensors are located in the duodenum, antrum, body of the stomach, cardia and esophagus; the measurement lasts 30 minutes before stimulation and 1 hour after stimulation (histamine, pentagastrin). The method allows **gastroesophageal and duodenogastric refluxes** to be recorded and the dose of antacid/antisecretory drugs to be chosen. **24-hour pH monitoring** is the “gold standard” for the diagnosis of GERD.
s8001 Fractional duodenal intubation
s8002 It gives the most complete information on the biliary system; on an empty stomach, the tube is passed into the duodenum and bile is collected. As a choleretic stimulus, **33% (or 25%) magnesium sulfate** or 20% glucose solution is given at **0.3–0.5 ml/kg** (warm).

s8023 13.3. Radiological investigations
s8024 Ultrasound examination
s8025 Radiological examination of the abdomen **begins with ultrasound** — safe, noninvasive, informative, with no contraindications. The size and structure of the organs are assessed. **Ultrasound signs:** in ascites, free fluid (if little — in the lateral canals and small pelvis) and the intestine floating upward; in acute inflammation the ultrasound conductivity of the liver increases; tumors have clear borders; gallbladder anomalies, bile thickening, **stones**; in portal hypertension **the diameter of the portal and splenic veins increases**; thickening of the gastric wall (gastritis); in acute pancreatitis the gland is enlarged with reduced echogenicity, in chronic pancreatitis it is small with indistinct contours; abscess, hematoma, cysts; in pyloric stenosis the pyloric canal is elongated and the muscle layer thickened; in intussusception **the “target” sign**.
s8026 **Ultrasound with a cholekinetic breakfast** — the motor function of the gallbladder: 2 egg yolks or 20 g sorbitol in 100 ml water are given, and the gallbladder volume is measured every 10 minutes for 1 hour. Normally **by 40–50 minutes the gallbladder contracts evenly by 50–60%**; **>60%** — hypermotor, **<50%** and a slow rhythm — hypomotor dyskinesia.
