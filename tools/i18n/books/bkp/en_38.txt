s7574 Other percussion signs
s7575 **Mendel's sign** — pain on tapping with half-bent fingers over the projection of the greater and lesser curvature of the stomach — peptic ulcer disease, erosive gastritis;
s7576 **Razdolsky's sign** — pain on percussion over the gallbladder projection.
s7577 12.7. Auscultation and auscultofriction
s7578 **Auscultation of the abdomen:** normally **faint, periodic sounds of intestinal peristalsis** are heard over the abdominal wall. **Increased peristalsis** — enteritis (loud gurgling over a wide area), the part proximal to a stenosis; **absence of peristalsis (“dead silence”)** — intestinal paresis, peritonitis.
s7579 **Auscultofriction** — a method of determining the lower border of the stomach: the stethoscope is placed on the epigastric area (over the stomach projection), and at the same time light “stroke-like” rubbing movements are made with one finger along the abdominal skin **from below upward** (toward the stomach) to the left of the linea alba, radially to the stethoscope. As soon as the finger crosses the border of the stomach the sound becomes sharply **louder** — this is the lower border of the stomach. If light percussion taps are used instead of rubbing — **auscultatory percussion** (auscultopercussion). Normally the lower border of the stomach is **2–4 cm above** the umbilicus.
s7580 12.8. Examination of the rectum and anal area
s7581 **Inspection** — the anus and the surrounding skin: fissures, scratch marks (enterobiasis), maceration, **prolapse of the rectal mucosa**, gaping anus, polyps and hemorrhoidal nodes, fistulas, scars, infiltrates.
s7582 **Digital examination** — the child in the knee-elbow position (in a severe condition on the left side, legs drawn up to the abdomen), after defecation; with a gloved little finger (in small children) or index finger lubricated with petroleum jelly. **Determined:** sphincter tone, smoothness of the mucosa, state of the pararectal tissue, pain, tumor, polyp, intussusceptum; after withdrawal of the finger, traces of **blood, mucus, pus** on the glove. This examination is important for excluding **surgical pathology (intussusception, appendicitis)** in abdominal pain.
s7583 12.9. Clinical section
s7584 “Acute abdomen” — alarming signs
s7585 Signs in abdominal pain that suggest **acute surgical pathology** (appendicitis, intussusception, intestinal obstruction, perforation): refusal of food, change in behavior (intense crying or lying motionless, forced position); waking from pain; vomiting against a background of pain; **no passage of stool or gas**; pallor, cold sweat, **tachycardia out of proportion to the temperature**; **tension of the abdominal muscles**, a positive Shchetkin–Blumberg sign. Such a child is **not given** analgesics, and urgent surgical examination is arranged.
s7586 Intussusception
s7587 Most often in children **aged 4–12 months** (when complementary foods are introduced); sudden **colicky abdominal pain** (the child cries sharply, draws up the legs, then calms down), vomiting, **a sausage-shaped mass** palpable in the abdomen, later blood-stained mucus like **“raspberry jelly”** from the anus. On digital rectal examination — blood on the glove. Diagnosis — ultrasound; treatment — air reduction or surgery.
s7588 Hepatomegaly — is the organ enlarged?
s7589 Projection of the liver edge below the costal arch is not always enlargement: **hepatoptosis** (the diaphragm descends in pulmonary emphysema, asthma, right-sided pleurisy, pneumothorax) — the Kurlov dimensions are normal. In true hepatomegaly the dimensions are also increased: hepatitis, heart failure (congestion), storage diseases, hemolytic anemias, leukemias, tumors.
