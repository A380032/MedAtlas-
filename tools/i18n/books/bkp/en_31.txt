s6266 **Tetralogy of Fallot** — the most common **“blue”** defect; it consists of four components:
s6267 **pulmonary stenosis** (narrowing of the right ventricular outflow tract);
s6268 **a large VSD**;
s6269 **right ventricular hypertrophy**;
s6270 **dextroposition of the aorta** (the aorta “overrides” the ventricular septum).
s6271 Hemodynamics: reduced pulmonary blood flow, mixed (venous) blood enters the systemic circulation — **central cyanosis**.
s6272 Diagnostic criteria:
s6273 **marked cyanosis** (usually increasing from 3–6 months), **hypercyanotic spells**;
s6274 forced position — **squatting** (or lying with the legs drawn up to the abdomen) — systemic resistance rises and pulmonary blood flow increases;
s6275 **“drumstick fingers”** and **“watch-glass” nails**;
s6276 **weakening of S2** over the pulmonary artery;
s6277 **a harsh systolic murmur** over the pulmonary artery (2nd–3rd intercostal space on the left) (stenosis);
s6278 **polycythemia** in the blood (Hb up to 250 g/l, erythrocytes up to 8×10^12^/l), raised hematocrit;
s6279 ECG — **right ventricular hypertrophy**, right axis deviation;
s6280 X-ray — a heart shaped like **a “wooden shoe” (“Dutch clog”)** (raised apex, deep “cardiac waist”), **reduced lung markings**.
s6281 Figure 10.3
s6282 X-ray in tetralogy of Fallot: a “wooden shoe”-shaped heart, reduced lung markings (Kildiyarova, Figure 9.25)
s6283 **The hypercyanotic spell** is the most dangerous complication of tetralogy of Fallot; most often **at 2–5 years** (from 6 months), provoked by exertion, crying, feeding, defecation, minor surgical procedures, excitement. Mechanism — **spasm** of the right ventricular outflow tract → almost all the venous blood enters the aorta → CNS hypoxia. Signs: restlessness, **marked cyanosis** and dyspnea (deep rapid breathing), tachycardia, **weakening or disappearance** of the murmur (pulmonary blood flow sharply reduced), a fall in BP; in a severe spell — fainting, seizures, coma.
s6284 **First aid in a hypercyanotic spell:** calm the child, put the child in **the knee-chest position** (pressing the knees to the chest — peripheral resistance increases), **humidified oxygen**, on the doctor's order **morphine** (or promedol) IM or IV, **sodium bicarbonate** (correction of acidosis), **β-blockers** (propranolol); infusion. Cardiac glycosides **must not be given** (they increase the spasm).
s6285 **Coarctation of the aorta** — narrowing in the aortic isthmus (at the transition from the arch to the descending part). There are **two regimes** in the hemodynamics: **arterial hypertension** in the upper part of the body and **hypotension** in the lower part.
s6286 **Diagnostic criteria:** in the newborn poor weight gain, early heart failure (worsening sharply when the ductus arteriosus closes); at an older age — headache, nosebleeds, pain and coldness in the legs; **an “athletic” shoulder girdle, thin legs**; **a BP gradient between the arms and legs** (high in the arms, low in the legs); **weak or absent femoral pulses**; pulsation of the intercostal arteries; a systolic murmur between the shoulder blades and in the 2nd intercostal space on the left; ECG — **left ventricular hypertrophy**; X-ray — a dilated ascending aorta, an enlarged left ventricle, **rib notching** from 8–10 years; echocardiography — the narrowing and turbulent flow.
s6287 Transposition of the great arteries
s6288 The aorta arises from the right ventricle and the pulmonary artery from the left ventricle — the systemic and pulmonary circulations work **in parallel**; life is possible only thanks to mixing of blood through a patent foramen ovale, PDA or VSD. **Marked generalized cyanosis from birth** (not decreasing with oxygen), dyspnea, rapidly developing heart failure; on X-ray an **“egg on its side”**-shaped heart with a narrow vascular bundle. Urgent surgical treatment (or balloon atrial septostomy) is required.

s6300 **Types:** by rate of development — **acute** and **chronic**; by the chamber mainly affected — **left ventricular** and **right ventricular** failure.
s6301 Left ventricular failure
s6302 The left atrium and ventricle are affected — **congestion in the pulmonary circulation**: the respiratory surface of the lungs and oxygen diffusion decrease, and hypoxia and acidosis increase.
s6303 nonspecific signs: restlessness, irritability, disturbed sleep, loss of appetite, nausea, vomiting, sometimes abdominal pain;
s6304 **dyspnea** — the earliest, and in infants **the main** sign; it decreases with oxygen and with the head raised; flaring of the nasal alae, participation of the accessory muscles; **cough** (a dry night cough is an early sign), orthopnea in a severe condition;
s6305 **tachycardia** — progressive, **out of proportion to the temperature, persisting in sleep**, resistant to treatment other than cardiac glycosides;
s6306 peripheral or central cyanosis;
s6307 **congestive rales** in the lungs — **fine-bubbling non-consonant moist rales**, symmetric, **in the posterior lower parts**, not disappearing after coughing; the percussion sound is shortened in these areas;
s6308 the cardiac borders enlarged to the left, muffled heart sounds, **“gallop” rhythm**, accentuation or splitting of S2 over the pulmonary artery;
s6309 in a severe condition — **pulmonary edema** (frothy, pink sputum, moist rales over the whole lung);
s6310 X-ray — increased lung markings (venous congestion), reduced transparency in the area of the lung roots.
s6311 Right ventricular failure
s6312 The right atrium and ventricle are affected — **congestion in the systemic circulation**.
s6313 rapid fatigue, lethargy, weakness, reduced appetite, sweating;
s6314 dyspnea, tachycardia, cyanosis;
s6315 **liver enlargement** — the earliest and most constant sign (first the left lobe, then the right); the liver is soft, elastic, **tender**; **Plesch's sign** (distension of the neck veins increases on pressure over the liver);
s6316 **distension of the neck veins** (increases in the horizontal position);
s6317 **edema** — first in the legs (in a lying child the sacrum and lower back, in infants the face and scrotum), then generalized — **anasarca**, **ascites**, hydrothorax, hydropericardium; diuresis decreases (**oliguria**).

s6349 10.5. Acute vascular insufficiency syndrome
s6350 Three clinical forms of acute vascular insufficiency: **fainting (syncope), collapse, shock**.
s6351 **Fainting** — a brief loss of consciousness due to acute cerebral hypoxia:
s6352 **prodromal period:** general weakness, darkening before the eyes, ringing in the ears, nausea, sweating — if the child is laid down at this time, fainting can be prevented;
s6353 **sudden brief loss of consciousness** (seconds, minutes), marked pallor, weakening of breathing and circulation;
s6354 dilated pupils, BP sharply reduced, muffled heart sounds, weak pulse; tonic-clonic seizures may occur.
s6355 **Collapse** — an acute fall in vascular tone:
s6356 the condition suddenly worsens, marked weakness, apathy; **consciousness is preserved**, but the child is indifferent;
s6357 pale skin, acrocyanosis, cold clammy sweat; breathing shallow and rapid;
s6358 **the pulse small, rapid, barely detectable**; **BP sharply reduced** (systolic and diastolic); the veins collapsed; the heart sounds loud at first, then muffled.
s6359 The main sign distinguishing fainting from collapse — **in fainting consciousness is lost, in collapse it is preserved**.
s6360 **Shock** — an acutely developing life-threatening condition caused by an extremely strong stimulus (injury, infection — septic shock, allergen — anaphylactic shock, blood loss — hypovolemic shock, heart — cardiogenic shock): a severe disturbance of organ perfusion; it has **compensated** (BP normal, tachycardia, cold limbs, capillary refill >2 s, oliguria) and **decompensated** (hypotension, impaired consciousness) stages; its severity is assessed by BP and the character of the pulse.
s6361 **First aid in fainting:** lay the child horizontally, **raise the legs by 30–40 cm**, loosen the collar and belt, fresh air, splash cold water on the face, hold ammonia (smelling salts) to the nose. If the child does not regain consciousness or fainting recurs — exclude a cardiogenic cause (ECG, echocardiography, Holter).
