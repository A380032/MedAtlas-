s8540 At an early age, the width, hypotonia and tortuosity of the urinary tract and the mobility of the kidney lead to **disturbed urodynamics** and the addition of **a microbial-inflammatory process (pyelonephritis)**.
s8541 Diuresis: **“600 + 100 for each year”**. A 7-year-old child: 600 + 100 × 6 = **1200 ml**. Number of voidings: newborn **20–25**, 1 year **15**, 3 years **≈10**, school age **6–7**.
s8542 Geppe's textbook gives the norm for leukocytes in the Nechiporenko test as **up to 2000** (not divided by sex); Kildiyarova gives **up to 4000** for girls. In practice **leukocytes <2000, erythrocytes <1000 per 1 ml** are often taken as the norm.
s8543 Source of hematuria: **“Erythrocyte cast + protein + dysmorphic erythrocyte = glomerulus”**. Leukocyturia + bacteriuria + fever + positive Pasternatsky = **pyelonephritis**; leukocyturia + dysuria, without fever = **cystitis**.
s8544 **Critical period 1 — the neonatal period (1st month):** **physiological depression** of the immune system; passive protection by maternal IgG; low phagocytosis, complement and interferon — susceptibility to pyogenic and gram-negative flora and viruses, a tendency of infection to **generalize** (sepsis). The first crossover of the leukocyte differential on day 4–5. **Critical period 2 — 3–6 months:** breakdown of maternal antibodies; the primary immune response (IgM) predominates, **immunological memory is not formed** — whooping cough and measles run an atypical course, reinfection is possible; local immunity is insufficient; allergic reactions and food allergy appear. **Critical period 3 — the 2nd year:** active exploration of the world, increasing contacts; local immunity remains insufficient; the response to the polysaccharide antigens of pneumococcus and Haemophilus is weak; most primary immunodeficiencies become manifest; diseases of the ENT organs and airways recur. **Critical period 4 — 4–6 years:** the second crossover of the leukocyte differential; IgG and IgM reach adult levels, secretory IgA is still low; parasitic invasions, atopic and immune complex diseases, late immunodeficiencies. **Critical period 5 — puberty (12–16 years in boys, 11–15 years in girls):** involution of the thymus, the mass of the lymphoid organs decreases; the influence of sex hormones — autoimmune and lymphoproliferative diseases increase.
s8545 In an infant with **unexplained fever (>38 °C)**, vomiting, poor appetite, failure to gain weight and restlessness, **urinalysis and urine culture** are mandatory (urinary tract infection is the most common bacterial cause of “occult” fever at an early age). After the first febrile pyelonephritis **renal ultrasound** is performed, and if it recurs — **voiding cystourethrography** (vesicoureteral reflux).

s8547 At birth the morphological and functional development of the kidneys is **incomplete**. Defects of the urinary organs make up **≈1/3** of all congenital defects.
s8548 Figure 14.1
s8549 Anatomical and physiological features of the urinary system in young children (Navruzova, Geppe)
s8550 The kidneys
s8551 **Mass and shape:** at an early age the kidneys are relatively **large**: in the newborn the kidney mass to body mass ratio is **1:100**, in adults **1:200**. Up to 1 year the kidney is **round**, then bean-shaped.
s8552 **Position:** because of their relatively large size and the shortness of the lumbar vertebrae, up to 1 year the kidneys lie **lower**: the upper pole at ThXI–XII, the lower pole **below L~IV~** (below the iliac crest); this feature disappears **by 7 years**. At an early age the kidneys lie almost **parallel**.
s8553 **Mobility:** the perirenal fat and fasciae are poorly developed — the kidneys are **mobile**; the fixation mechanisms form **at 5–8 years**.
s8554 **Structure:** in the 1st year **the lobulated structure** persists (it disappears at 2–5 years); the medulla is thicker than the cortex (1:4; 1:2 in adults). In a term newborn **the number of nephrons is the same as in adults (≈2 million)**, but the glomeruli are small, the nephrons are insufficiently differentiated until 2 years, and the loop of Henle is short. Morphological maturation of the cortex is completed **at 3–5 years**, and full maturation of the kidney **at school age**.
