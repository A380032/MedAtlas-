s8561 Functional features of the kidneys
s8562 **Low glomerular filtration:** the visceral layer of the glomerular capsule has **cuboidal epithelium**; the filtering surface is **5 times smaller** than in adults; renal blood flow is low (**5%** of the cardiac output in the newborn, 25% in adults). Filtration approaches adult levels **by 1 year**.
s8563 **Tubular reabsorption and secretion** develop gradually: amino acids, phosphates and bicarbonates are excreted in large amounts; glucose reabsorption is good; sodium is actively reabsorbed — **predisposition to edema**; water and NaCl loads cannot be excreted quickly; excretion of K, Ca and Mg is reduced.
s8564 **Concentrating function is low** (little ADH, a short loop of Henle, an immature distal tubule) — **low specific gravity** of the newborn's urine; adult concentrating ability develops **at 9–12 months**.
s8565 **Regulation of acid-base balance** is immature (low secretion of hydrogen and ammonium ions) — **acidosis** develops quickly in diseases at an early age.
s8566 The optimal homeostatic function of the kidney is reached **at 10–11 years**. Therefore **edema and dehydration** develop easily in children; the weak excretion of the kidneys must be taken into account when giving drugs (antibiotics) and solutions.
s8567 The urinary tract
s8568 **The renal pelvises** are relatively wide and up to 5 years often lie **within the kidney**; **the ureters** are relatively long, wide, hypotonic and tortuous, leaving the pelvis at a right angle; the intravesical part of the ureter is **short** (it lengthens at 10–12 years), and the muscle fibers at its orifice are weak — predisposition to **vesicoureteral reflux**.
s8569 **The bladder** in infancy lies **high** (it is palpable above the pubis) and descends into the small pelvis with age; its mucosa is rich in blood vessels.

s8592 14.2. Clinical examination of the urinary system
s8593 **Main complaints:** abdominal and **lumbar pain** (small children cannot localize it); **dysuric disturbances** (frequent or infrequent, painful voiding, incontinence, enuresis); **edema of the face and legs** (the parents notice rapid weight gain); **raised BP**, headache, dizziness; **change in the appearance of the urine** (turbidity, sediment, change of color — “meat washings”); unexplained **fever** or prolonged subfebrile temperature; visual and hearing disturbances; dyspepsia, thirst, itching of the skin; weakness, weight loss.
s8594 **History:** onset of the complaints, results of previous urine tests and treatment; **family history** (glomerulonephritis, pyelonephritis, urinary tract defects, urolithiasis, deafness, renal failure); lagging physical development at an early age; recurrent episodes of fever without signs of infection.

s8682 **Three-glass (two-glass) test** — determining **the source** of hematuria and leukocyturia: in the morning, without washing the external genitals, the urine is voided successively into three containers. Changes only in **the 1st portion** — the external genitals or urethra; in **the 3rd portion** — the bladder (neck); **the same in all three portions** — above the bladder (kidney, ureter or bladder).
s8683 **Bacteriological examination of the urine** — the midstream portion collected into a sterile container is cultured; the pathogen, its amount and antibiotic sensitivity are determined. **Diagnostically significant bacteriuria:** in free voiding **≥100,000 (10^5^) CFU/ml**; through a catheter **≥10,000 (10^4^)/ml**; on suprapubic puncture — **any** amount; in children under 1 year in free voiding, E. coli **≥50,000/ml**. Proteus, Klebsiella and Pseudomonas aeruginosa are significant even in smaller amounts.
s8684 **Zimnitsky test** — assesses the **excretory and concentrating** function of the kidneys and the **circadian rhythm**. During the day the urine is collected into a separate container **every 3 hours** (8 portions; at an early age — at free intervals), **the volume and specific gravity** of each portion are measured, and the fluid intake is taken into account.
s8685 **Excretory function:** normally **70–80%** of the fluid taken is excreted.
s8686 **Concentrating function:** during the day the specific gravity varies within **1008–1025**; in children over 3 years the maximal specific gravity in at least one portion is **1018–1020**; a difference between the maximal and minimal specific gravity of **≥10 units** (optimally 15) — the ability to concentrate and dilute is preserved.
s8687 **Circadian rhythm: daytime diuresis (9 a.m.–9 p.m.) is 2/3–3/4 of the daily diuresis**; predominance of night diuresis — **nocturia** (chronic renal failure, pyelonephritis).
s8688 **Hyposthenuria** — specific gravity constantly **<1010–1012**; **isosthenuria** — monotonous specific gravity around **1008–1010** (the specific gravity of primary urine) — a severe disturbance of the concentrating function; **hypersthenuria** — >1030 (glucosuria, proteinuria, dehydration).
s8689 **Blood creatinine and urea** — easily determined indicators of renal function; an increase indicates impaired filtration function.
s8690 **Rehberg test** — assesses **the glomerular filtration rate (GFR)** by **the endogenous creatinine clearance** and **tubular reabsorption** (creatinine is freely filtered in the glomeruli and is almost neither reabsorbed nor secreted in the tubules). The urine is collected over 24 hours (or 2 hours), and venous blood is taken after the last voiding.
s8691 Clearance: **C = (U × V / P) × (1.73 / S)**, where U and P are the creatinine in the urine and plasma, V is the minute diuresis, and S is the child's body surface area.
s8692 **Tubular reabsorption** = [(clearance – minute diuresis) / clearance] × 100% — normally **97–99%**.
s8693 In practice GFR is calculated by **the Schwartz formula**: **GFR = k × height (cm) / creatinine (µmol/l × 0.0113)**; k — 0.33 in preterm babies; 0.45 in term newborns and up to 2 years; 0.55 at 2–14 years; 0.70 in boys over 14 years.
