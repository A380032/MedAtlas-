s6647 A 2.5-year-old child. Since birth the lips have turned blue when crying, and from 6 months the cyanosis has become constant. The child tires quickly when walking and rests by squatting. This morning, while crying, the child suddenly became restless, turned markedly blue, breathed rapidly, then became lethargic. On examination: generalized cyanosis, “drumstick fingers”, “watch-glass” nails. S2 weakened over the pulmonary artery, a harsh systolic murmur in the 2nd–3rd intercostal space on the left (weakened during the spell). CBC: Hb 210 g/l, erythrocytes 7.2×10^12^/l.
s6648 1) Presumptive diagnosis and today's condition? 2) Name the four components of the defect. 3) What is the meaning of squatting? 4) What X-ray changes are expected? 5) First aid in the spell?
s6649 1) Tetralogy of Fallot; today — a hypercyanotic spell. 2) Pulmonary stenosis, a large VSD, right ventricular hypertrophy, dextroposition of the aorta. 3) Squatting compresses the systemic arteries and increases peripheral resistance — the right-to-left shunt decreases, more blood goes to the lungs, and cyanosis decreases. 4) A “wooden shoe”-shaped heart, reduced lung markings. 5) Calming, the knee-chest position, humidified oxygen, on the doctor's order morphine, sodium bicarbonate, propranolol; cardiac glycosides must not be given; urgent hospitalization.
s6650 A 9-year-old child had tonsillitis 3 weeks ago. For 5 days: fever 38 °C, pain and swelling in the knee and ankle joints (in turn), weakness, dyspnea. On examination: pale skin, HR 124/min. The cardiac border is enlarged 1 cm to the left. At the apex S1 is weakened and there is a blowing systolic murmur radiating to the left axilla. ECG: PQ 0.20 s. ESR 45 mm/h, CRP strongly positive, ASO 800 IU/ml.
s6651 1) Which disease? Justify the diagnosis with the criteria. 2) Which cardiological syndromes are present? 3) What complication is there a risk of in the future?
s6652 1) Acute rheumatic fever: major criteria — carditis, migratory polyarthritis; minor criteria — fever, raised ESR and CRP, prolonged PQ; evidence of a streptococcal infection — a history of tonsillitis and a high ASO titer. 2) Endocardial involvement (signs of mitral valve insufficiency — a systolic murmur at the apex radiating to the axilla, weakened S1) and myocardial involvement (tachycardia, enlarged border, prolonged PQ) — rheumatic carditis. 3) Formation of an acquired heart defect (mitral insufficiency) in 8–12 months, recurrences; therefore long-term bicillin prophylaxis is needed.
