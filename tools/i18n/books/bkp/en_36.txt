s7326 In an infant, **regurgitation 1–2 times a day** (within 1 hour after feeding, without distress, with good weight gain) is physiological; the causes are a weak cardiac sphincter, the horizontal position of the stomach and swallowing air. s7328 12.1. Development of the digestive system
s7329 The digestive organs form at very early stages of embryogenesis: **on day 7–8** the **primitive gut** begins to form from the endoderm, and **on day 12** it divides into an intraembryonic part (the future digestive tube) and an extraembryonic part (the yolk sac).
s7330 **Foregut** — pharynx, esophagus, stomach, part of the duodenum, the pancreatic and hepatic buds;
s7331 **midgut** — duodenum, jejunum and ileum, the right half of the colon;
s7332 **hindgut** — the remaining parts of the colon.
s7333 In **weeks 4–10** of pregnancy the primitive gut grows rapidly, leaves the abdominal cavity through the umbilical hernia, **rotates 270°** and returns. Disturbance of these processes results in **intestinal rotation anomalies, embryonic hernias (omphalocele), atresias, stenoses, Meckel's diverticulum**. **From week 16–20** the fetus swallows amniotic fluid (up to 500 ml per day) — the digestive organs “train”; **meconium** forms.
s7334 12.2. Anatomical and physiological features of the digestive organs
s7335 General features:
s7336 up to 1–1.5 years, especially in the first half-year, the digestive system is adapted to **lactotrophic (milk) nutrition**;
s7337 along the whole digestive tube the mucosa is **thin, delicate, drier and easily injured**; the submucosa is **rich in blood vessels** and consists of loose connective tissue; **muscle and elastic fibers are poorly** developed;
s7338 the sucking and swallowing reflexes are well developed;
s7339 the **secretory activity** of the glands is **low**: little digestive juice, low enzyme activity;
s7340 conditioned reflex activity is easily disturbed by external influences (overheating, chilling, pain, acute illness, separation from the mother) — predisposition to **functional dyspepsia**.
s7341 The oral cavity is relatively **small** and filled by the tongue; the tongue is short, wide and thick; the lips and cheeks are thick with well-developed muscles; in the cheeks there are dense **Bichat's fat pads** (rich in solid fatty acids) — the cheeks are not drawn in during sucking; the gums have **ridge-like thickenings** that help sucking, and the lips transverse folds.
s7342 The mucosa is **delicate, dry** and rich in blood vessels — it is easily injured and infected (**candidiasis** — “thrush”, aphthous stomatitis).
s7343 **The salivary glands** are insufficiently developed in the newborn: saliva is scanty and thick, with a neutral or weakly acidic reaction and little amylase. **From 3–4 months** salivation increases sharply and the child cannot swallow it all — **physiological drooling** (3–6 months). Salivary amylase reaches adult levels at 1 year.
s7344 **The act of sucking** consists of three phases: **aspiration** (the lips enclose the nipple and negative pressure is created in the mouth), **compression** (milk is extracted) and **swallowing**. Because the entrance to the larynx lies at the posterior border of the hard palate, the child **can breathe and swallow at the same time**. Absence of the swallowing reflex is a sign of extreme prematurity.
s7345 The esophagus and stomach
s7346 **The esophagus** is funnel-shaped, relatively short (10–12 cm in the newborn) and narrow; its mucosa is thin, with few glands, rich in blood vessels; the muscle layer is weak; the physiological narrowings are poorly expressed.
s7347 **The lower esophageal (cardiac) sphincter is poorly** developed, while the muscles of the pyloric part are well developed — predisposition to **regurgitation** and **aerophagia**.
s7348 **The stomach** in infancy lies **horizontally** and varies in shape (round, pear-shaped); once the child begins to walk (9–12 months) it takes a **vertical** position; the fundus and muscle layer are poorly developed.

s7362 In the gastric mucosa, the pepsin-producing **chief cells** and the hydrochloric acid-secreting **parietal cells** are few and functionally immature; the cells producing protective mucus
s7363 are few. The acidity of the gastric juice is low (pH ≈5–6 in the newborn, ≈3–4 at 1 year); it reaches adult levels **at 10–12 years**. s7364 The intestines
s7365 The intestines are relatively **long**: in the newborn **6–8 times** the body length (4–5 times in adults); the small intestine is ≈1 m per 1 kg of body mass. The mesentery is long and mobile and the intestinal loops are poorly fixed — predisposition to **intussusception and volvulus**.
s7366 **The duodenum** is ring-shaped, at the level of LI, mobile; it becomes fixed by fatty tissue by 7 years.
s7367 The mucosa is thin, rich in blood vessels, with many villi — **a large absorptive surface**, but **high permeability** (weak barrier function): toxins, allergens and microbes easily pass into the blood — predisposition to toxicosis and allergy.
s7368 **The ileocecal valve (Bauhin's valve)** is relatively weak — cecal flora may pass into the ileum.
s7369 **The appendix** is conical with a wide entrance — at an early age **appendicitis is rare**, but it runs a rapid destructive course and peritonitis develops quickly (the omentum is short).
s7370 **The colon** is not fully mature; formation of the cecum is completed at the end of the 1st year; the sigmoid colon is relatively long and mobile — predisposition to **constipation**.
s7371 **The rectum** in the newborn is cylindrical, without an ampulla or folds, relatively long and with little fatty tissue — predisposition to **prolapse** of the mucosa (straining, whooping cough); it takes up its final position at 2 years.
s7372 **Digestion:** in infancy **membrane (parietal) digestion** predominates over cavity digestion; in the newborn **pinocytosis** (direct absorption of proteins — breast milk immunoglobulins) is preserved. **Lactase** activity is high in the 1st year and then decreases.
s7373 **Motility** is active: food passes through the intestine in **4–18 hours** (≈24 hours in adults) — the bowel empties frequently.
s7374 The pancreas and liver
s7375 **The pancreas** is small in the newborn (≈3 g; ≈50 g at 15 years) and insufficiently differentiated; its exocrine activity is low in the 1st year (little amylase and lipase), increases rapidly after complementary foods are introduced and reaches adult levels **at 5–6 years**. The juice contains trypsin, chymotrypsin, carboxypeptidases, elastase (proteins), amylase and lipase.
s7376 **The liver** is **large** relative to body mass: **≈4%** in the newborn (2% in adults), occupying a large part of the abdominal cavity; the left lobe is relatively large (it becomes smaller at 1.5 years); lobulation becomes distinct at the end of the 1st year; its structure approaches that of adults **at 8 years**. It is rich in blood vessels and poor in connective tissue — it **enlarges quickly** in infection and intoxication.
s7377 The **detoxifying** and **conjugating** (glucuronyltransferase) functions of the liver are immature — **physiological jaundice**, sensitivity to drugs. Little bile is secreted and there are few bile acids (poor emulsification and absorption of fats — more fat in the stool); taurine conjugates predominate in bile (antibacterial effect), and there is little cholesterol and salts — **gallstones are rare**.
s7378 **The gallbladder** in the newborn is cylindrical or pear-shaped and lies deep in the liver; at 10–12 years it resembles that of adults.
s7379 Intestinal microflora and stool
s7380 The fetal intestine is **sterile**. In the first hours after birth the intestine is **colonized** by microbes (the first “aseptic” phase — 10–20 hours; the second — multiplication; the third — stabilization).
s7381 In a **breastfed** child **bifidobacteria** predominate from the end of the 1st month (90–99%) — the bifidus factor in breast milk (lactulose, oligosaccharides) supports them; they create an acidic environment, protect against pathogenic flora and synthesize vitamins. **With artificial feeding** there are more E. coli, enterococci, lactobacilli and opportunistic flora.
s7382 **Meconium** — the first stool: a dark green, sticky, odorless mass passed in the first **1–3 days** of life (swallowed amniotic fluid, shed epithelium, bile, mucus). **On days 3–5** — **transitional stool** (green-yellow, mucous, frequent). From day 5 — permanent stool.

s7435 History
