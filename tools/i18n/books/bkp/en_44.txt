s8794 Urinary syndrome
s8795 **Urinary syndrome** — pathological changes in the urine: proteinuria, hematuria, leukocyturia, cylindruria, bacteriuria, crystalluria.
s8796 **Proteinuria** — excretion of **>150–200 mg** of protein per day (or more than 0.033 g/l). Degree: **mild** — <1 g/day; **moderate** — 1–2.5 g/day (glomerulonephritis); **marked (nephrotic)** — >2.5–3 g/day or **>50 mg/kg/day**. **Renal (glomerular, tubular)** and **extrarenal** (prerenal — Bence Jones protein; postrenal — inflammation of the urinary tract); **functional** — fever, physical strain, chilling, **orthostatic**. **Selective** (mainly albumin — milder damage) and **nonselective** (large-molecule globulins — deep damage).
s8797 **Hematuria** — **>2–3 erythrocytes** per field of view in the morning urine; **microhematuria** (the color does not change) and **macrohematuria** (>25–30, red urine or “meat washings”). **Glomerular** (dysmorphic erythrocytes, erythrocyte casts, with proteinuria — glomerulonephritis, IgA nephropathy, hereditary nephritis) and **nonglomerular** (isomorphic erythrocytes — urolithiasis, cystitis, tumor, trauma). With pain — renal colic; with dysuria — cystitis.
s8798 **Leukocyturia** — **>5–6 (>10 in girls)** per field of view in the urinalysis, >2000(–4000)/ml by Nechiporenko, >2 million/day by Addis–Kakovsky; **pyuria** — leukocytes cover the whole field of view. **Neutrophilic** type (on the urocytogram) — microbial inflammation (**pyelonephritis, cystitis**, urethritis); **mononuclear/lymphocytic** — glomerulonephritis, interstitial nephritis, SLE.
s8799 **Cylindruria** — **always a sign of renal pathology** (except for single hyaline casts): hyaline — physical strain, fever, nephrotic syndrome; granular and waxy — severe degenerative damage to the tubules; **erythrocyte** — renal hematuria (glomerulonephritis); leukocyte — renal leukocyturia (pyelonephritis).
s8800 **Bacteriuria** — the main sign of urinary infection (significant cut-offs above).
s8801 **Crystalluria:** urates — urate diathesis (neuro-arthritic diathesis), high meat intake; oxalates — oxalate nephropathy; triple phosphates — urinary tract infection.
s8802 **Glucosuria** (diabetes mellitus, **renal glucosuria** — normal blood sugar), **ketonuria** (acetonemic vomiting, diabetes mellitus).
s8803 Edema syndrome
s8804 Mechanisms of renal edema: **reduced oncotic pressure** of the plasma (hypoalbuminemia); increased capillary permeability; activation of **the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system** (retention of Na and water); **reduced glomerular filtration**. Renal edema appears **in the morning on the face and eyelids** and descends during the day; it is soft and warm, the skin pale (cardiac edema — in the evening on the legs, with cyanosis, firm).
s8805 Pain syndrome
s8806 **Dull, constant lumbar pain** — stretching of the renal capsule (glomerulonephritis, pyelonephritis, hydronephrosis);
s8807 **sharp, paroxysmal pain in the lower back radiating down along the ureter — to the groin and genitals** — **renal colic** (urolithiasis, a blood clot);
s8808 **suprapubic pain** — cystitis, bladder stone, urinary retention;
s8809 pain in the lower back and one side of the abdomen during voiding — **vesicoureteral reflux**.
s8810 Main clinical syndromes
s8811 **Nephritic (acute nephritic) syndrome** — **hematuria** (from micro- to macro-, “meat washings”), **arterial hypertension**, moderate **proteinuria** (1–2 g/day), moderate **edema** (face, shins) or latent edema, **oliguria**, cylindruria. Typical of **acute poststreptococcal glomerulonephritis** (**1–3 weeks** after tonsillitis or pyoderma), Henoch–Schönlein nephritis, SLE.
s8812 **Nephrotic syndrome** — **massive edema** (up to anasarca), **marked proteinuria (>50 mg/kg/day or >3 g/day)**, **hypoproteinemia and hypoalbuminemia** (<25 g/l), dysproteinemia (increased α~2~-globulins), **hyperlipidemia** (hypercholesterolemia). **Complete** (all signs) and **incomplete**; **pure** (without hematuria and hypertension — most often “minimal change” — in children aged 2–6 years, steroid-sensitive) and **mixed** (with hematuria and/or hypertension). **Primary** (congenital, idiopathic, glomerulonephritides) and **secondary** (SLE, vasculitides, amyloidosis, diabetes mellitus, hepatitis B and C, HIV, heavy metals).

s8834 **Syndrome of urinary tract involvement (infection)** — pyelonephritis, cystitis: pain, dysuria, a positive Pasternatsky sign, **intoxication and fever** (in pyelonephritis); laboratory — **neutrophilic leukocyturia, bacteriuria**, microhematuria; instrumental — dilatation and deformation of the pelvicalyceal system, signs of obstruction. At an early age pyelonephritis often presents with **“unexplained” fever**, vomiting and failure to gain weight.
s8835 **Tubular dysfunction (tubulopathy) syndrome** — polyuria, hypo- and isosthenuria; **proximal** — renal glucosuria, **phosphate diabetes** (vitamin D-resistant rickets: severe rickets-like deformities, low blood P, normal Ca, phosphaturia), **de Toni–Debré–Fanconi syndrome** (glucosuria + aminoaciduria + phosphaturia + rickets-like changes); **distal** — **nephrogenic diabetes insipidus** (polyuria, polydipsia, hypernatremia), **renal tubular acidosis** (acidosis in the blood, alkaline urine — calcinosis, stones), pseudohypoaldosteronism.
s8836 **Arterial hypertension syndrome** — **renoparenchymal** (glomerulonephritis, pyelonephritis, defects, reflux nephropathy) and **renovascular** (renal artery stenosis, thrombosis, aortoarteritis). The most common cause of secondary hypertension in children is **kidney disease**.
s8837 **Acute kidney injury (acute renal failure)** — a sharp, **reversible** decline in renal function: **oliguria/anuria, hyperazotemia (raised creatinine and urea), hyperkalemia, metabolic acidosis, hyperhydration**. Causes: **prerenal** (shock, dehydration, blood loss), **renal** (hemolytic uremic syndrome — the most common cause at an early age, acute glomerulonephritis, nephrotoxins — aminoglycosides, sepsis), **postrenal** (obstruction). Phases of the course: initial, oligoanuric, polyuric, recovery.
s8838 **Chronic renal failure (chronic kidney disease)** — **irreversible** disturbance of homeostasis as a result of a progressive decrease in the number of functioning nephrons. At the onset — **polyuria, nocturia, isosthenuria**, a moderate rise in creatinine; then intoxication, **anemia**, arterial hypertension, acidosis, **renal osteodystrophy**, growth retardation, hyperkalemia; **the terminal (uremic) stage** — GFR <15–20 ml/min/1.73 m^2^, itching of the skin, a grayish-yellow skin color, **ammonia odor**, nausea, vomiting, hemorrhagic syndrome, pericarditis (the “death knell” rub), seizures, uremic coma.

s8840 **The immune system** — the set of organs, tissues and cells that protects the body from **genetically foreign** substances (antigens) entering from outside or formed in the body.

s8847 Innate (nonspecific) defense
s8848 **Natural barriers:** skin, mucous membranes, ciliated epithelium, the acidic environment of the stomach, the blood-brain barrier.
s8849 **Humoral factors: lysozyme** (saliva, tears, breast milk; high in the newborn), **properdin** (low at birth, increases at 1–3 weeks), **interferons** (low production capacity in the newborn, maximal in puberty), **the complement system** (≈**50%** of adult levels at birth, reaching adult levels by the end of the 1st month).
s8850 **Phagocytosis** (neutrophils, macrophages): in the newborn the engulfing activity is adequate, but **the final stage (digestion) is imperfect** — it reaches adult levels at 6–12 months; phagocytosis of pneumococcus, Klebsiella and Haemophilus is incomplete — these infections are severe and generalized.
s8851 Adaptive (specific) immunity
s8852 **Cell-mediated immunity** — **T lymphocytes** (mature in the thymus): **CD4^+^ T helpers** (Th1, Th2), **CD8^+^ cytotoxic T lymphocytes (killers)**, T regulators (suppressors). A T lymphocyte recognizes an antigen together with HLA molecules after it has been processed by **an antigen-presenting cell** (macrophage); it acts against intracellular viruses, tumor cells and intracellular pathogens.
s8853 **Humoral immunity** — **B lymphocytes** (mature in the bone marrow) become **plasma cells** and produce **immunoglobulins (antibodies)**; they recognize the antigen in its native form; they act against extracellular pathogens.
s8854 **Memory cells** — store information about the antigen and ensure a rapid and strong response on re-encounter (the basis of vaccination).
