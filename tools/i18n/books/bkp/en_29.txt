s5777 9.7. Clinical section
s5778 A child with a murmur — diagnostic approach
s5779 **Determine the phase of the murmur:** diastolic or continuous — **organic**; systolic — continue.
s5780 **Assess its features:** soft, short, non-radiating, position-dependent — most likely functional; harsh, long, radiating, with a thrill — organic.
s5781 **Additional signs:** changes in the heart sounds, cardiomegaly, cyanosis, signs of failure, changes in the peripheral pulses, an arm–leg BP difference — organic.
s5782 **Exclude extracardiac causes**: anemia (CBC), fever, thyrotoxicosis.
s5783 **Investigations:** ECG, **echocardiography** (the main one), X-ray if necessary.
s5784 In an infant, **any murmur**, and also a murmur accompanied by **dyspnea, cyanosis, poor weight gain or sweating**, requires a cardiology consultation and echocardiography. In large defects (e.g., a large VSD) there may be **no** murmur in the first weeks of life, because pulmonary vascular resistance is still high.
s5785 A child who has fainted
s5786 Types of fainting (syncope): **vasovagal** (the most common — prolonged standing, pain, fear, a stuffy room; preceded by nausea, sweating, pallor), **orthostatic** (on standing up abruptly), **sinocarotid**, **post-tussive**, **cardiogenic** (aortic stenosis, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, tetralogy of Fallot, arrhythmias — complete AV block, long QT, paroxysmal tachycardia). **Dangerous signs of cardiogenic syncope:** fainting during physical exertion, preceding palpitations, fainting while lying down, sudden death at a young age in the family — ECG, Holter and echocardiography are needed.
s5787 Skill 1. Auscultation of the heart
s5788 A warm, quiet room; warm the stethoscope; the child lies on the back, the doctor on the right.
s5789 Assess the rhythm and HR (if necessary, palpate the pulse simultaneously to detect a pulse deficit).
s5790 Listen in order: apex → 2nd space right → 2nd space left → base of the xiphoid process → Botkin–Erb point.
s5791 At each point identify S1 and S2 (S1 coincides with the carotid pulse) and assess their loudness and ratio, splitting and extra sounds.
s5792 If there is a murmur — describe it by all criteria.
s5793 Repeat auscultation lying on the left side, standing, and after exertion.
s5794 Listen over the great vessels (carotid and subclavian arteries, neck veins).
s5795 Skill 2. Examining the pulse
s5796 Palpate both radial arteries simultaneously with the 2nd–4th fingers and assess synchrony and equality.
s5797 Count the rate in one arm for 1 minute.
s5798 Assess the rhythm; in arrhythmia repeat while the breath is held.
s5799 Determine the tension, filling, size and shape.
s5800 Skill 3. Measuring arterial pressure
s5801 Choose an age-appropriate cuff; the child has been sitting quietly for 5 minutes.
s5802 Place the cuff 2 cm above the antecubital fossa; the arm at heart level.
s5803 Find the brachial artery and place the stethoscope.
s5804 Inflate to 30–40 mm above the point at which the radial pulse disappears; deflate at 2–3 mm per second.
s5805 Record the systolic and diastolic BP; repeat 2–3 times and take the average.
s5806 Measure in the other arm and in the legs.
s5807 Assess by the formula and centile tables.
s5808 Skill 4. Performing the Martinet (or Shalkov) functional test
s5809 Measure the pulse (over 10 seconds) and BP at rest.
s5810 Ask the child to do 20 deep squats in 30 seconds (or the age-appropriate Shalkov load).
s5811 Measure the pulse and BP immediately after the exercise, then repeat every minute for 3–5 minutes.
s5812 Calculate the percentage increase in pulse, the change in BP and the recovery time.
s5813 Assess the reaction as favorable or unfavorable and write the conclusion.
s5814 Skill 5. Analyzing an ECG
s5815 Check the voltage and paper speed.
s5816 Determine the rhythm source (is there a P before each QRS).
s5817 Calculate the HR from the RR interval.
s5818 Determine the electrical axis (from the QRS in standard leads I and III).
s5819 Measure the duration of P, PQ, QRS and QT; assess ST and T.
s5820 Compare the results with the age norm and write the conclusion (e.g., “Sinus rhythm, HR 92/min, respiratory arrhythmia, normal electrical axis — age norm”).
