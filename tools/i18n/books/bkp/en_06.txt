s1025 Signs of functional immaturity:
s1026 **nervous system:** lethargy, a weak cry or squeal, weak or absent sucking and swallowing reflexes, muscle hypotonia; the unconditioned reflexes appear from 1–2 months of age; psychomotor development lags by 1–3 months;

s1040 2.9. Features of examining the newborn
s1041 **Conditions:** ward temperature **22–26 °C**, **30 minutes after** a feed, in daylight, on a heated changing table. The condition is assessed at 1 and 5 minutes by **the Apgar score** (topic 1). The history establishes the obstetric history and risk factors.
s1042 **Inspection:** proportions, position (flexed in a term baby; on the back with the limbs extended in a preterm baby), spontaneous movements, eye movements, facial expressions and cry are assessed. Pathological positions: **the “frog position”** (limbs extended, thighs lying flat to the sides — hypotonia), **opisthotonus** (arching of the body supported on the head and heels).
s1043 **Skin:** a healthy newborn is pink; slight cyanosis of the hands, feet and around the mouth is normal if the rest of the body is pink.
s1044 **Head:** size, shape, fontanelles and sutures. **Caput succedaneum** — doughy, with indistinct borders, lies over several bones and disappears in a few days. **Cephalohematoma** (hemorrhage under the periosteum) — firmer, fluctuant, located **within the border of a single bone** (most often the parietal bone).
s1045 **Neck:** a firm mass in the sternocleidomastoid muscle (hemorrhage — a cause of torticollis); **the clavicle** is palpated (fracture — in breech delivery, with obstetric forceps).
s1046 **Chest:** shape, accessory nipples, respiratory movements, nasal flaring, retraction of the intercostal spaces and sternum.
s1047 **Abdomen:** distension (intestinal obstruction, ascites) or retraction (diaphragmatic hernia); **the umbilical stump falls off on days 4–10**; the state of the umbilical wound (hyperemia, discharge, palpable umbilical vessels).
s1048 **Spine:** a spinal hernia (meningocele, myelomeningocele) is sought.
s1049 **Limbs:** malformations, fractures, symmetry, number of digits; the state of **the hip joint** — **the Ortolani test**: with the newborn on the back, the hip and knee joints are flexed at a right angle and the thighs are abducted; a **“click”** felt as the femoral head slips into place is a sign of congenital dislocation (dysplasia) of the hip. Limited abduction of the thighs also raises suspicion of this.
s1050 **Stigmas of dysembryogenesis:** up to 5–7 stigmas are normal in a newborn.
s1051 **Palpation and percussion:** size, bulging or depression and pulsation of the fontanelles; liver size; pulsation of the femoral and brachial arteries (generally weak pulsation — left ventricular failure; absent femoral pulse — coarctation of the aorta). Because the chest is small, percussion is of limited value.
s1052 **Auscultation:** breathing is mainly weakened vesicular; HR 100–160. A murmur heard immediately after birth is not always a sign of a congenital heart defect — it often disappears during the neonatal period.

s1072 2.10. Clinical part
s1073 Assessing physical development — a mirror of health
s1074 Regular assessment of physical development makes it possible to detect many diseases before their other signs appear.
s1075 **Arrest of weight gain** in infancy may be the first sign of insufficient breast milk (hypogalactia), urinary tract infection, celiac disease, a congenital heart defect or cystic fibrosis.
s1076 **Slowing of growth in height** is an early sign of hypothyroidism, growth hormone deficiency, chronic kidney disease or Crohn's disease.
s1077 **Rapid enlargement of the head circumference** (more than 2 cm per month after 3 months) — hydrocephalus; neurosonography is needed.
s1078 **The dynamics matter, not a single measurement:** if a child has always grown along the 25th centile, this is his individual norm; if he was previously on the 75th centile and has “dropped” to the 25th, this is a sign of disease.
s1079 The newborn: the border between normal and pathology
s1080 Distinguishing physiological jaundice from pathological jaundice is one of the most important tasks in practice. Jaundice **appearing on the first day of life** is never physiological — it is most often a sign of hemolytic disease (Rh or ABO incompatibility) and requires immediate measurement of bilirubin. Jaundice lasting longer than **2 weeks** in a term newborn and longer than **3 weeks** in a preterm newborn also requires investigation (hypothyroidism, biliary atresia, hepatitis).
s1081 Similarly, **if the weight falls by more than 10%** or is not regained in 2 weeks, insufficient breast milk, incorrect attachment to the breast or infection is sought.
