s2158 Palpation
s2159 Palpation is carried out from top to bottom, carefully over affected areas.
s2160 **Moisture** is determined by stroking symmetric areas of the body (palms, soles, axillae, groins) with the palm. Normally the skin is slightly shiny and moderately moist.
s2161 **Temperature** is determined by touching symmetric areas with the back of the hand; a local rise in temperature is characteristic of inflammation.
s2162 **Elasticity** — in an area where subcutaneous fat is poorly developed (front of the chest, back of the hand, elbow crease), a fold of **skin only** is picked up with the thumb and index finger. If it smooths out immediately after release, elasticity is preserved; if it smooths out slowly, it is **reduced**.

s2165 Special tests
s2166 **The state (fragility) of the vessel wall** is assessed by three signs:
s2167 **The tourniquet sign (Konchalovsky–Rumpel–Leede).** A tourniquet (or a sphygmomanometer cuff) is applied to the middle third of the upper arm so that the venous outflow stops but the radial pulse is preserved. **After 3–5 minutes** the petechiae on the elbow crease and the inner surface of the forearm are counted. **More than 5 petechiae** is pathological (impaired vascular permeability or platelet deficiency).
s2168 **The pinch sign.** A fold of skin on the front or side of the chest is picked up with the fingers of both hands (2–3 mm apart) and shifted in opposite directions; if a hemorrhage appears in the area of the fold, the sign is positive.
s2169 **The hammer sign.** The sternum is tapped with a percussion hammer without causing pain; if hemorrhages appear on the skin, the sign is positive.
s2170 **Dermographism** is an assessment of the tone of the skin vessels. Several lines are drawn on the skin of the chest or abdomen with a fingernail or the tip of a spatula:
s2171 normally **after 5–20 seconds** a **white line** appears (white dermographism — sympathetic effect), which is replaced **after 1–10 minutes** by a **red line** (parasympathetic effect);
s2172 a rapidly appearing, broad, long-lasting **red** dermographism indicates parasympathetic predominance (vagotonia); a **white**, long-lasting one — sympathicotonia; a raised one — **urticarial** dermographism (allergic predisposition).

s2174 Eliciting dermographism (Navruzova, Geppe)
s2175 Figure 4.20
s2176 Red dermographism (Navruzova, Geppe)

s2203 Changes in skin color
s2204 **Pallor.** Diffuse pallor of the skin, visible mucous membranes and conjunctivae is a sign of **anemia**. Pallor also occurs in vascular spasm: aortic defects, hypertensive crisis, collapse, “white” fever, shock. Specific tints: **waxy (alabaster)** — chlorosis and kidney diseases; **earthy** — malignant tumors; **“café-au-lait”** — infective endocarditis.
s2205 **Hyperemia (redness)** — dilation of the peripheral vessels (fever, overheating, vasodilators, inflammation, burns, excitement, physical exertion) or erythrocytosis (polycythemia — a dark red-bluish tint). In carbon monoxide poisoning the skin is **bright red (cherry red)**. In Itsenko–Cushing syndrome redness of the cheeks gives a **“matron face”**.

s2217 **Jaundice (icterus)** is the accumulation of bilirubin in the tissues. First **the sclerae, the soft palate and the area under the tongue** turn yellow. s2289 **Fever + a hemorrhagic rash that does not disappear on pressure** is regarded as meningococcal infection until another diagnosis is proven. s2290 Main skin diseases in children (in brief)
s2291 **Pyodermas of the newborn:** vesiculopustulosis, pseudofurunculosis (Figner), epidemic pemphigus of the newborn, exfoliative dermatitis (Ritter) — blisters form easily because of the weak connection between the epidermis and dermis. **Omphalitis** — redness, infiltration and discharge of the umbilical ring.
s2292 **Streptoderma** — impetigo (purulent vesicles, yellow “honey” crusts), erysipelas.
s2293 **Viral:** herpes (grouped vesicles), molluscum contagiosum (pearly papules with a depressed center).
s2294 **Fungal:** trichophytosis, microsporia, candidiasis; **parasitic:** scabies (paired papulovesicles, itching at night, between the fingers), pediculosis.
s2295 **Allergic:** seborrheic dermatitis, **atopic dermatitis** (cheeks, flexor surfaces, itching), strophulus, urticaria, **Quincke's edema** (lips, eyelids, tongue, larynx — risk of respiratory obstruction).
s2296 **Hereditary:** epidermolysis bullosa (blisters from trivial injury), ichthyosis, acrodermatitis enteropathica (impaired zinc metabolism).
