s3887 6.7. Auscultation of the lungs
s3888 **Main idea:** auscultation is performed **in the order** of comparative percussion, at symmetric points, listening to **2–3 breaths** at each point; it is essential to know the type of breathing typical of the child's age.
s3889 Technique and rules:
s3890 Complete quiet; the clothes are removed (rustling of clothes interferes).
s3891 The child stands or sits; a seriously ill patient lies. In a crying child — during the deep inspiration between cries (crying also helps).
s3892 In small children the diaphragm of the stethoscope **should not be larger than 2 cm in diameter**; it is placed in the intercostal space.
s3893 First listen during breathing through the nose, then during deep breathing through the mouth (adventitious sounds become louder). To induce a deep breath in an infant: close the nose for a few seconds, tickle, press lightly on the trachea.
s3894 Listening points: the apices, the anterior surface, the axillae, suprascapular, interscapular, subscapular and paravertebral areas.
s3895 **Areas requiring special attention in children:** the axillae (in segmental pneumonia bronchial breathing appears early); paravertebral and suprascapular (segments 2, 6, 10 — foci of pneumonia); interscapular (lung root); subscapular (crepitation is heard early); the cardiac area (left lingula).

s4001 Foreign body aspiration
s4002 Choking and a severe coughing fit that begin **suddenly during play or eating** in a previously healthy child are considered foreign body aspiration until proven otherwise. It most often enters the **right** bronchus. Signs: unilateral weakening of breathing, localized whistling rales, later atelectasis or recurrent pneumonia. Diagnosis and treatment — **bronchoscopy**. Emergency care for a child who cannot cough or speak: under 1 year — 5 back blows between the shoulder blades + 5 chest thrusts; in older children — **the Heimlich maneuver**.
s4003 Clinical detection of pneumonia
s4004 In small children the first signs of pneumonia are often not physical but **general and respiratory**: fever lasting more than 3 days, **tachypnea**, flaring of the nasal alae, perioral cyanosis, chest indrawing, “grunting” expiration, intoxication. Physical changes (shortened sound, fine-bubbling consonant rales, crepitation) may not be detected if the focus is small — confirmation is by **X-ray**.
s4005 Skill 1. Counting the respiratory rate and assessing dyspnea
s4006 Observe the child unnoticed at rest (if possible, asleep).
s4007 Place a hand on the child's chest or abdomen (or observe visually).
s4008 Count the respiratory movements for 1 minute (up to 3 months — always a full minute).
s4009 Assess the rhythm, depth and type of breathing.
s4010 Look for signs of dyspnea: flaring of the nasal alae, accessory muscles, retraction of the intercostal spaces and the suprasternal area, stridor, prolonged expiration.
s4011 Compare the result with the age norm, calculate the ratio to the pulse, and record it.
s4012 Skill 2. Determining vocal fremitus
s4013 The child stands or sits; the doctor's hands are warm.
s4014 Place the palms on symmetric areas: supra- and infraclavicular, lateral areas, suprascapular, interscapular, subscapular.
s4015 Ask the child to say words with the letter “R” (in an infant — during crying).
s4016 Compare the two sides at each pair of points; if necessary, swap the hands and repeat.
s4017 Conclusion: equal / increased / weakened (in which area).
s4018 Skill 3. Comparative percussion
s4019 The child stands straight and symmetrically; for anterior percussion the doctor stands on the right.
s4020 Place the pleximeter finger firmly in the intercostal space parallel to the ribs.
s4021 With a quiet stroke, percuss the supraclavicular, clavicular and infraclavicular areas, then the lateral areas.
s4022 From behind: the suprascapular, interscapular (finger parallel to the spine) and subscapular areas.
s4023 Compare the sound at each pair of symmetric points.
s4024 Conclusion: clear pulmonary sound over the whole lung / site and type of change.
s4025 Skill 4. Topographic percussion
s4026 Height of the apices (from 5–7 years): from the front — relative to the clavicle, from behind — relative to CVII; Krönig's field.
s4027 Lower borders: on the right starting from the parasternal line, finger parallel to the ribs, from top to bottom, from the clear sound toward the dull sound; along each topographic line.
s4028 On the left — starting from the anterior axillary line.
s4029 Mark the border along the edge of the finger facing the clear sound; count the ribs or vertebrae.
s4030 Determine the excursion of the lower edge on the midaxillary line.
s4031 Compare the result with the age norm.
s4032 Skill 5. Auscultation of the lungs
s4033 A quiet, warm room; the child undressed; a warm stethoscope.
