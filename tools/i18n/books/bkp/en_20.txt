s3890 6.7. Auscultation of the lungs
s3891 **Main idea:** auscultation is performed **in the order** of comparative percussion, at symmetric points, listening to **2–3 breaths** at each point; it is essential to know the type of breathing typical of the child's age.
s3892 Technique and rules:
s3893 Complete quiet; the clothes are removed (rustling of clothes interferes).
s3894 The child stands or sits; a seriously ill patient lies. In a crying child — during the deep inspiration between cries (crying also helps).
s3895 In small children the diaphragm of the stethoscope **should not be larger than 2 cm in diameter**; it is placed in the intercostal space.
s3896 First listen during breathing through the nose, then during deep breathing through the mouth (adventitious sounds become louder). To induce a deep breath in an infant: close the nose for a few seconds, tickle, press lightly on the trachea.
s3897 Listening points: the apices, the anterior surface, the axillae, suprascapular, interscapular, subscapular and paravertebral areas.
s3898 **Areas requiring special attention in children:** the axillae (in segmental pneumonia bronchial breathing appears early); paravertebral and suprascapular (segments 2, 6, 10 — foci of pneumonia); interscapular (lung root); subscapular (crepitation is heard early); the cardiac area (left lingula).

s4004 Foreign body aspiration
s4005 Choking and a severe coughing fit that begin **suddenly during play or eating** in a previously healthy child are considered foreign body aspiration until proven otherwise. It most often enters the **right** bronchus. Signs: unilateral weakening of breathing, localized whistling rales, later atelectasis or recurrent pneumonia. Diagnosis and treatment — **bronchoscopy**. Emergency care for a child who cannot cough or speak: under 1 year — 5 back blows between the shoulder blades + 5 chest thrusts; in older children — **the Heimlich maneuver**.
s4006 Clinical detection of pneumonia
s4007 In small children the first signs of pneumonia are often not physical but **general and respiratory**: fever lasting more than 3 days, **tachypnea**, flaring of the nasal alae, perioral cyanosis, chest indrawing, “grunting” expiration, intoxication. Physical changes (shortened sound, fine-bubbling consonant rales, crepitation) may not be detected if the focus is small — confirmation is by **X-ray**.
s4008 Skill 1. Counting the respiratory rate and assessing dyspnea
s4009 Observe the child unnoticed at rest (if possible, asleep).
s4010 Place a hand on the child's chest or abdomen (or observe visually).
s4011 Count the respiratory movements for 1 minute (up to 3 months — always a full minute).
s4012 Assess the rhythm, depth and type of breathing.
s4013 Look for signs of dyspnea: flaring of the nasal alae, accessory muscles, retraction of the intercostal spaces and the suprasternal area, stridor, prolonged expiration.

s4018 Ask the child to say words with the letter “R” (in an infant — during crying).
s4019 Compare the two sides at each pair of points; if necessary, swap the hands and repeat.
s4020 Conclusion: equal / increased / weakened (in which area).
s4021 Skill 3. Comparative percussion
s4022 The child stands straight and symmetrically; for anterior percussion the doctor stands on the right.
s4023 Place the pleximeter finger firmly in the intercostal space parallel to the ribs.
