s4536 Syndrome of upper airway involvement
s4537 In rhinitis, nasopharyngitis, pharyngitis, laryngitis and tracheitis: runny nose (serous-watery at first, then thick), pain on swallowing, a scratchy throat; **dry cough**; hyperemia and granularity of the posterior pharyngeal wall; fever, intoxication. Palpation is unchanged; percussion gives a clear pulmonary sound; breathing is **harsh**, and the rales are **transmitted** in character — **they disappear after coughing**.
s4538 **Adenoid facies** (enlargement of the nasopharyngeal tonsil): a puffy face, the mouth always open, no nasal breathing, snoring and apnea in sleep, malocclusion, a high “gothic” palate, nasal speech, hearing loss (otitis), frequent colds, reduced attention and memory from prolonged hypoxia.
s4539 Croup syndrome
s4540 **Croup** — narrowing (stenosis) of the laryngeal lumen. At an early age (6 months – 3 years) it is most often **false croup** — **stenosing laryngotracheitis** in influenza, parainfluenza and adenovirus infection (edema of the loose tissue below the vocal cords). **The croup triad: barking cough + hoarse voice + inspiratory dyspnea (stridor)**; in addition: restlessness, tachycardia, participation of the accessory muscles, retraction of the suprasternal area and intercostal spaces.

s4588 Syndrome of respiratory disorders in newborns (respiratory distress)
s4589 Pneumopathies: atelectasis of the newborn, **hyaline membrane disease**, edematous-hemorrhagic syndrome, meconium aspiration, transient tachypnea of the newborn; mainly **in preterm babies** (surfactant deficiency). Signs: restlessness or lethargy, cyanosis, **frothy discharge on the lips**, retraction of the compliant parts of the chest (sternum, intercostal spaces), **“seesaw” breathing** (thoracoabdominal asynchrony), respiratory arrhythmia and **apnea**, **“grunting” expiration**, weakened breathing, scattered fine-bubbling rales and crepitation, tachycardia, muffled heart sounds, muscle hypotonia, seizures. Severity is assessed with the **Silverman** (preterm) and **Downes** (term) scales (see topic 2).
s4590 Escape of fluid from the capillaries into the interstitium and the alveoli: **cardiac** (left ventricular failure, mitral stenosis) and **noncardiac** (drowning, toxic gases, severe pneumonia, sepsis, rapid and large-volume infusion, nephrotic and nephritic syndromes) causes. Signs: restlessness, fear of death, orthopnea, **cough with frothy, sometimes pink sputum**, rapidly increasing dyspnea and cyanosis, cold sweat, a rapid weak pulse; on percussion a shortened sound in the lower posterior parts; on auscultation **many bilateral fine-bubbling non-consonant** rales, later medium- and coarse-bubbling (“boiling samovar” breathing).
s4591 7.9. Respiratory failure (RF) syndrome
s4592 **Main idea:** RF is a condition in which the normal gas composition of the blood is not maintained, or is maintained only **through strain** of the external respiratory apparatus. The cut-offs in children: **PaO~2~ <80 mm Hg**, **PaCO~2~ >40–45 mm Hg**. RF accompanies any respiratory disease and diseases of other organs (CNS, heart, muscles).
s4593 Types (by pathogenesis):
s4594 **Ventilatory (hypercapnic)** — lung ventilation is insufficient: PaO2 low, **PaCO~2~ high**; the clinical picture is related to **hypercapnia**: headache, sweating, tachycardia, arterial hypertension, drowsiness, tremor, later cerebral edema and coma.
s4595 **Parenchymal (diffusion, hypoxemic)** — the ventilation-perfusion ratio or alveolar-capillary diffusion is disturbed: PaO2 low, PaCO2 normal or low; the clinical picture is related to **hypoxemia**: cyanosis, restlessness → CNS depression, tachycardia, arrhythmia, muffled heart sounds, arterial hypotension.
s4596 Causes of acute RF:
s4597 **Obstructive (mechanical)** — upper airway obstruction (croup, epiglottitis, foreign body, tongue falling back in unconsciousness), bronchospasm (asthma), viscous secretions (cystic fibrosis), exudate in the bronchi (bronchitis, bronchiolitis, aspiration).
s4598 **Restrictive** — reduced compliance of the lungs and chest wall: pneumonia, pleurisy, pneumothorax, atelectasis, pulmonary edema, meteorism and a high diaphragm, rib injury.
s4599 **Neuromuscular** — depression of the respiratory center (CNS injury, neuroinfection, drug poisoning), paralysis of the respiratory muscles (poliomyelitis, myasthenia, botulism, muscular dystrophies).
s4600 **Diffusion disorder** — pulmonary edema, interstitial pneumonia, fibrosing alveolitis.

s4663 Bronchial asthma is chronic allergic inflammation and hyperreactivity of the bronchi with recurrent attacks of reversible obstruction. s4665 Skill 1. Performing and assessing peak flowmetry
s4666 Set the peak flow meter pointer to zero; show the child the technique.
s4667 The child stands and holds the peak flow meter horizontally (the fingers should not touch the scale).
s4668 The child takes a deep breath, seals the lips tightly around the mouthpiece and breathes out **sharply and quickly**.
s4669 Record the result; repeat 3 times and note the highest.
s4670 Measure in the morning and evening; calculate the daily variability: (evening – morning) / mean × 100%.
s4671 Compare the result with the norm for height and the personal best (the “traffic light” zones).
s4672 Skill 2. Assessing a spirogram
s4673 Determine the child's data (age, sex, height) and find the predicted values.
s4674 Compare the actual values of VC, FEV1, Tiffeneau index and MVV with the predicted values (in %).
s4675 Determine the type of ventilatory disorder: restrictive (VC ↓, Tiffeneau normal), obstructive (FEV1, Tiffeneau ↓), mixed.
s4676 If there is a bronchodilator test — calculate the increase in FEV1 (≥12% — reversible obstruction).
s4677 Write the conclusion.

s4687 Skill 4. Performing pulse oximetry
s4688 Place the pulse oximeter sensor on the child's finger (in a newborn — on the hand or foot).
