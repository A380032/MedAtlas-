s4535 **Adenoid facies** (enlargement of the nasopharyngeal tonsil): a puffy face, the mouth always open, no nasal breathing, snoring and apnea in sleep, malocclusion, a high “gothic” palate, nasal speech, hearing loss (otitis), frequent colds, reduced attention and memory from prolonged hypoxia.
s4536 Croup syndrome
s4537 **Croup** — narrowing (stenosis) of the laryngeal lumen. At an early age (6 months – 3 years) it is most often **false croup** — **stenosing laryngotracheitis** in influenza, parainfluenza and adenovirus infection (edema of the loose tissue below the vocal cords). **The croup triad: barking cough + hoarse voice + inspiratory dyspnea (stridor)**; in addition: restlessness, tachycardia, participation of the accessory muscles, retraction of the suprasternal area and intercostal spaces.

s4582 Syndrome of a cavity in the lung
s4583 An (emptied) abscess, tuberculous cavern, bronchiectasis, suppurated cyst, bullae in destructive (staphylococcal) pneumonia. X-ray — **a ring-shaped shadow**, possibly with air and a fluid level inside. When an abscess ruptures into a bronchus — “a mouthful” of purulent, fetid sputum.
s4584 Syndrome of respiratory disorders in newborns (respiratory distress)
s4585 Pneumopathies: atelectasis of the newborn, **hyaline membrane disease**, edematous-hemorrhagic syndrome, meconium aspiration, transient tachypnea of the newborn; mainly **in preterm babies** (surfactant deficiency). Signs: restlessness or lethargy, cyanosis, **frothy discharge on the lips**, retraction of the compliant parts of the chest (sternum, intercostal spaces), **“seesaw” breathing** (thoracoabdominal asynchrony), respiratory arrhythmia and **apnea**, **“grunting” expiration**, weakened breathing, scattered fine-bubbling rales and crepitation, tachycardia, muffled heart sounds, muscle hypotonia, seizures. Severity is assessed with the **Silverman** (preterm) and **Downes** (term) scales (see topic 2).
s4586 Escape of fluid from the capillaries into the interstitium and the alveoli: **cardiac** (left ventricular failure, mitral stenosis) and **noncardiac** (drowning, toxic gases, severe pneumonia, sepsis, rapid and large-volume infusion, nephrotic and nephritic syndromes) causes. Signs: restlessness, fear of death, orthopnea, **cough with frothy, sometimes pink sputum**, rapidly increasing dyspnea and cyanosis, cold sweat, a rapid weak pulse; on percussion a shortened sound in the lower posterior parts; on auscultation **many bilateral fine-bubbling non-consonant** rales, later medium- and coarse-bubbling (“boiling samovar” breathing).
s4587 7.9. Respiratory failure (RF) syndrome
s4588 **Main idea:** RF is a condition in which the normal gas composition of the blood is not maintained, or is maintained only **through strain** of the external respiratory apparatus. The cut-offs in children: **PaO~2~ <80 mm Hg**, **PaCO~2~ >40–45 mm Hg**. RF accompanies any respiratory disease and diseases of other organs (CNS, heart, muscles).
s4589 Types (by pathogenesis):
s4590 **Ventilatory (hypercapnic)** — lung ventilation is insufficient: PaO~2~ low, **PaCO~2~ high**; the clinical picture is related to **hypercapnia**: headache, sweating, tachycardia, arterial hypertension, drowsiness, tremor, later cerebral edema and coma.
s4591 **Parenchymal (diffusion, hypoxemic)** — the ventilation-perfusion ratio or alveolar-capillary diffusion is disturbed: PaO~2~ low, PaCO~2~ normal or low; the clinical picture is related to **hypoxemia**: cyanosis, restlessness → CNS depression, tachycardia, arrhythmia, muffled heart sounds, arterial hypotension.
s4592 Causes of acute RF:
s4593 **Obstructive (mechanical)** — upper airway obstruction (croup, epiglottitis, foreign body, tongue falling back in unconsciousness), bronchospasm (asthma), viscous secretions (cystic fibrosis), exudate in the bronchi (bronchitis, bronchiolitis, aspiration).
s4594 **Restrictive** — reduced compliance of the lungs and chest wall: pneumonia, pleurisy, pneumothorax, atelectasis, pulmonary edema, meteorism and a high diaphragm, rib injury.
s4595 **Neuromuscular** — depression of the respiratory center (CNS injury, neuroinfection, drug poisoning), paralysis of the respiratory muscles (poliomyelitis, myasthenia, botulism, muscular dystrophies).
s4596 **Diffusion disorder** — pulmonary edema, interstitial pneumonia, fibrosing alveolitis.

s4658 A child in an attack of bronchial asthma
s4659 Bronchial asthma is chronic allergic inflammation and hyperreactivity of the bronchi with recurrent attacks of reversible obstruction. Signs of an attack: expiratory dyspnea, whistling breathing heard at a distance, orthopnea, dry cough, box-like sound, dry whistling rales. s4660 In an asthma attack, **a “silent chest”** (breath sounds and rales are no longer heard), inability to speak, cyanosis, lethargy and SpO~2~ <90% are signs of **a life-threatening** attack: urgent oxygen, salbutamol inhalation (via a spacer or nebulizer), systemic glucocorticoids, admission to the intensive care unit.
s4661 Skill 1. Performing and assessing peak flowmetry
s4662 Set the peak flow meter pointer to zero; show the child the technique.
s4663 The child stands and holds the peak flow meter horizontally (the fingers should not touch the scale).
s4664 The child takes a deep breath, seals the lips tightly around the mouthpiece and breathes out **sharply and quickly**.
s4665 Record the result; repeat 3 times and note the highest.
s4666 Measure in the morning and evening; calculate the daily variability: (evening – morning) / mean × 100%.
s4667 Compare the result with the norm for height and the personal best (the “traffic light” zones).
s4668 Skill 2. Assessing a spirogram

s4671 Determine the type of ventilatory disorder: restrictive (VC ↓, Tiffeneau normal), obstructive (FEV~1~, Tiffeneau ↓), mixed.
s4672 If there is a bronchodilator test — calculate the increase in FEV~1~ (≥12% — reversible obstruction).
s4673 Write the conclusion.
