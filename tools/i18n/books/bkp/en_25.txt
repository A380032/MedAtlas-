s5022 **Growth rate:** heart volume increases fastest **in the first 2 years of life, at 5–9 years and in puberty**. Up to 2 years mainly **the atria** grow, at 2–10 years the whole heart, and after 10 years mainly **the ventricles**.
s5023 Blood vessels:
s5024 the arteries are relatively **wide**, with thin walls and poorly developed muscle and elastic fibers; their structure becomes similar to that of adults **by 12 years**;
s5025 the ratio of the arterial and venous lumens in the child is **1:1** (1:2 in adults); the veins later grow faster and at 15–16 years are 2 times wider than the arteries;
s5026 in the newborn the pulmonary artery is wider than the aorta (21 and 16 mm); they become equal at 12 years, and in puberty the aorta is wider;
s5027 **the capillaries are wide, short and tortuous**, with higher permeability than in adults — predisposition to **edema** in infections, **exsicosis** in toxicosis, and vascular insufficiency;
s5028 there are **many anastomoses** between the coronary arteries — myocardial infarction is very rare.
s5029 **Innervation:** at an early age the **sympathetic** influence predominates — physiological **tachycardia**. Myelination of the vagus nerve is completed **at 3–4 years**; after that the heart rate (HR) decreases and **respiratory arrhythmia** appears (HR increases on inspiration and slows on expiration); it is maximal **at 4–12 years**.
s5030 **physiological tachycardia** — small heart volume, high oxygen demand, sympathicotonia;
s5031 HR is **labile** — it changes quickly with movement, crying, fever and excitement;
s5032 stroke and minute volume increase with age, but the minute volume relative to body mass is **greater** in the child;
s5033 **arterial pressure is low** — the arteries are wide, the pumping force of the heart small and the peripheral resistance low; it rises gradually with age;
s5034 the circulation rate is high (a full circulation ≈12 s in the newborn, 22 s in adults).

s5038 **Variants of the adolescent heart** (puberty): **the “drop” (small) heart** — with rapid growth in height the heart seems to hang on the vessels; rapid fatigue, dizziness, low BP, a functional systolic murmur, fainting; **the “hypertrophic” heart** — more often in boys, the left ventricle enlarged, BP slightly raised; **“mitral” configuration** — only on X-ray (the left contour is flattened).
s5039 8.4. History taking
s5040 Complaints:
s5041 **weakness, rapid fatigue** on physical exertion; in the newborn — **tiring during feeding**, stopping feeding to rest, sweating, poor weight gain;
s5042 **dyspnea** — on exertion or at rest;
s5043 **cyanosis** (lips, nails, generalized) — on exertion or constant; **hypercyanotic spells**;
s5044 **edema** (legs, lower back, face);
s5045 **palpitations**, a feeling of “skipped beats”;
s5046 **pain in the heart area** (location, character, duration, radiation, relation to exertion or excitement, relief with medication);
s5047 **fainting**, dizziness, headache;
s5048 pain in the calf muscles on exertion (intermittent claudication) — insufficient arterial circulation in the legs;
s5049 frequent bronchopulmonary diseases (in defects with increased pulmonary blood flow).
s5050 **History of the present illness:** when the complaints appeared (since birth — congenital defect; 1–3 weeks after tonsillitis or ARVI — rheumatic fever, myocarditis), relation to exertion, the course.
s5051 **Life history:** the mother's pregnancy (infections, drugs, alcohol and diabetes in the 1st trimester, occupational hazards); in the family — **congenital heart defects**, cardiomyopathies, rheumatic fever, cases of **early (before 50 years) sudden death**, arterial hypertension; the child's past illnesses (frequent tonsillitis, scarlet fever), previous ECG and echocardiography results.
s5052 8.5. Inspection
s5053 General inspection
s5054 **Position:** in heart failure the child lies with the head raised, often on the right side; in a severe condition — **orthopnea** (sitting with the legs hanging down); in exudative pericarditis — sits leaning forward or in the **knee-elbow** position; in **tetralogy of Fallot** — **squats** after exertion (the “squatting” position); in acute vascular insufficiency — horizontal, head low.
s5055 **Skin color: pallor** (vascular insufficiency, aortic defects, “white”-type defects, rheumatic fever); **acrocyanosis** (right ventricular failure, slowed peripheral circulation); **generalized (central) cyanosis** — “blue” congenital defects (right-to-left shunt), pulmonary hypertension; **differential cyanosis** — only in the legs or only in the arms (defects of the great vessels); crimson cheeks — mitral stenosis.
s5056 **“Drumstick fingers”** and **“watch-glass” nails** — chronic hypoxia (“blue” defects, infective endocarditis).
s5057 **Edema** — right ventricular failure: first on the feet and ankles, increasing in the evening; then the shins, thighs, lower back and abdomen; in a lying child — the sacrum and lower back; in infants — the face and scrotum.
s5058 **Physical development:** hypotrophy, short stature (congenital defects); **disproportion between the upper and lower body** (an “athletic” shoulder girdle, thin legs) — **coarctation of the aorta**.
s5059 **Precapillary pulse (Quincke)** — a change in the color of the nail bed in time with the pulse when the tip of the nail is lightly pressed — **aortic valve insufficiency**.

s5062 Inspection of the cardiac area
s5063 **The apex beat** — the rhythmic impact of the apex of the heart on the chest wall; it is visible in thin children.
s5064 **The cardiac impulse** — diffuse pulsation in the cardiac area (at the left sternal edge and in the epigastrium); only in pathology (right ventricular hypertrophy, cardiomegaly) or on physical exertion, in thin children.
s5065 **“Cardiac hump”** — bulging of the chest in the cardiac area; a sign of **cardiomegaly** that developed at an early age (the chest is soft): congenital defects, early carditis.
s5066 Smoothing of the intercostal spaces — pericarditis with a large effusion.
s5067 Epigastric pulsation, pulsation in the 2nd intercostal space to the right and left of the sternum.
s5068 Inspection of the blood vessels
s5069 **Strong pulsation of the carotid arteries (“carotid dance”)** and nodding of the head in time with the pulse (**de Musset's sign**) — aortic valve insufficiency.
s5070 **Distension and pulsation of the neck veins** — right ventricular failure, tricuspid valve insufficiency, exudative pericarditis, compression of the superior vena cava (**“Stokes' collar”** — edema of the face and neck).
s5071 Subcutaneous venous collaterals (occlusion of the venae cavae), pulsation of the intercostal arteries (coarctation of the aorta).
s5072 Signs of impaired arterial circulation in the limbs: coldness, pallor, trophic changes.
s5073 8.6. Palpation
s5074 The apex beat
s5075 **Technique:** the child lies on the back or sits; the doctor stands on the right. The base of the right palm is placed on the sternum and the fingers on the cardiac area toward the anterior axillary line along the intercostal spaces (in girls the breast is moved upward). Once the pulsation is found, its exact location, area, height and force are determined with the tips of the 2nd–3rd fingers. If it cannot be found in the supine position, the child is turned on the left side or leans forward.

s5115 8.7. Percussion of the heart
s5116 **Percussion determines:** the size of the heart (the borders of relative and absolute dullness), its configuration, position, and the width of the vascular bundle.
s5117 Rules:
s5118 percussion should be **quiet** or **very quiet**;
s5119 the pleximeter finger is placed **parallel** to the border sought;
s5120 the pleximeter step is small (not more than the width of a finger);
s5121 the stroke is directed **strictly from front to back**;
s5122 the border is marked along **the outer edge of the finger (facing the clear sound)**;
s5123 in children over 4 years the technique is as in adults; at an early age, direct percussion is done **with one finger only**, and in indirect percussion the pleximeter finger is placed **with the 1st phalanx only**; in infants the most reliable method of determining the left border is **orthopercussion** (strictly in the sagittal direction, with the pleximeter finger placed on its lateral edge).
s5124 Order of determining the borders of relative dullness:
s5125 **Right border.** First, percussing from top to bottom along the right midclavicular line, find **the upper border of liver dullness** (usually rib V–VI). Then move up one intercostal space (the 4th intercostal space), place the finger parallel to the sternum and percuss **from outside inward** — from the clear sound until dullness appears. The right border is formed by **the right atrium**.
s5126 **Upper border.** The finger is placed along the left parasternal line in the 1st intercostal space parallel to the ribs, and percussion is performed **from top to bottom**; the border is marked along the upper edge of the finger. The upper border is formed by **the pulmonary artery cone and the left atrial appendage**.
s5127 **Left border.** First find the apex beat; in the same intercostal space place the finger **1–1.5 cm outside** the beat (or from the anterior axillary line), perpendicular to the ribs (parallel to the midclavicular line), and percuss **from outside toward the heart**. The left border is formed by **the left ventricle**.
