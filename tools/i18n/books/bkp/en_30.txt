s6106 The most important early signs of myocarditis are **tachycardia out of proportion to the temperature**, **muffled heart sounds (especially S1)** and **a gallop rhythm**. An ECG must be taken in weakness, dyspnea and tachycardia that appear 1–3 weeks after tonsillitis or a viral infection.

s6108 10.1. Carditis syndrome
s6109 **Carditis** — inflammatory involvement of the heart; the three layers of the heart — **endocardium, myocardium and pericardium** — may be affected separately or together. Simultaneous involvement of all layers is **pancarditis**.
s6110 **Causes:** rheumatic (acute rheumatic fever — after group A β-hemolytic streptococcal infection), nonspecific (viral — Coxsackie B, ECHO, adenoviruses, influenza; bacterial — diphtheria), infective endocarditis, autoimmune diseases (SLE, juvenile arthritis), toxic and allergic damage.
s6111 Syndrome of endocardial involvement
s6112 It is seen in infective or rheumatic **endocarditis** and **acquired heart defects**. As a result of inflammation the valves become deformed: **insufficiency** (incomplete closure of the cusps, backward flow of blood — regurgitation) or **stenosis** (narrowing of the orifice) develops. The clinical picture mainly reflects involvement of **the mitral** and **aortic** valves.

s6126 **Distinguishing endocarditis from an established acquired defect** is important: endocarditis is an ongoing process, the murmur is less long-lasting and regresses with treatment, the heart chambers are unchanged, it lasts up to 6–8 weeks; **an acquired defect** forms **8–12 months** after the onset of endocarditis — the murmur is long-lasting and widely radiating, and dilatation and hypertrophy of the heart chambers develop.
s6127 Signs of **infective endocarditis**: prolonged fever, chills, sweating, weakness, weight loss, pallor (“café-au-lait” color), a new or changed murmur, splenomegaly, petechiae, **Osler nodes** (painful nodules on the fingertips), **Janeway lesions**, **“drumstick fingers”**, emboli; the pathogen in blood culture, **vegetations** on the valves on echocardiography. Risk group — congenital and acquired defects, prosthetic valves, central venous catheters.
s6128 Syndrome of myocardial involvement
s6129 In **myocarditis** of various etiologies (rheumatic, viral — especially enteroviral, diphtheria), dystrophic and toxic damage, and cardiomyopathies.
s6130 **Complaints:** pain in the heart area (at an early age — restlessness), palpitations, “skipped beats”, dyspnea, weakness, rapid fatigue; in infants — refusal to feed, sweating, vomiting.
s6131 Physical signs:
s6132 **pulse changes** — **tachycardia out of proportion to the temperature**, less often bradycardia, arrhythmia;
s6133 the apex beat **weakened, diffuse**, displaced outward;
s6134 the cardiac borders **enlarged** (cardiomegaly);
s6135 **muffled heart sounds**, especially S1 at the apex; splitting, **“gallop” rhythm**;
s6136 **an organic-functional (muscular) systolic murmur** at the apex — relative mitral insufficiency;
s6137 reduced BP;
s6138 signs of heart failure (dyspnea, liver enlargement, edema).
s6139 **Instrumental signs:** ECG — reduced voltage of the waves, QRS deformation, rhythm and conduction disorders (sinus tachycardia, extrasystole, AV blocks, bundle branch block), **ST depression of more than 1 mm**, deformation and inversion of the T wave; X-ray — **CTI >0.5** (>0.55 at an early age); echocardiography — left ventricular dilatation, **reduced ejection fraction**; blood — raised **CK-MB and troponins**.
s6140 Syndrome of pericardial involvement
s6141 In pericarditis of various etiologies (viral, bacterial, tuberculous, rheumatic, uremic, SLE), accumulation of fluid (blood, transudate) in the pericardial cavity, and systemic diseases.
s6142 Dry (fibrinous) pericarditis:
s6143 **severe stabbing** or sharp pain behind the sternum (often in its middle part), **increased by movement, coughing and deep breathing**, decreased on leaning forward; it radiates to the left shoulder and neck;
s6144 **pericardial friction rub** — “creaking”, “scraping”, at the base of the heart or in the area of absolute dullness, **does not radiate anywhere**, variable, increases on leaning forward and on pressure with the stethoscope, **persists when the breath is held**;
s6145 ECG — **ST elevation** in all leads (concordant), then T inversion.
s6146 Exudative pericarditis:
s6147 dyspnea, a feeling of heaviness; **forced position** — sitting leaning forward or the knee-elbow position;
s6148 smoothing of the intercostal spaces, distension of the neck veins;
s6149 the apex beat **weakened or not detected**; the apex beat lies **inside** the percussion border;
s6150 cardiac dullness **enlarged in all directions**, **the difference between relative and absolute dullness disappears**;
s6151 **markedly muffled heart sounds**; the friction rub disappears as the fluid increases;
s6152 the pulse rapid, small, **paradoxical**; BP reduced, venous pressure raised;
s6153 X-ray — the cardiac shadow **spherical or triangular**, its arcs smoothed; ECG — **a sharp decrease in the voltage of all waves**; echocardiography — **fluid** in the pericardial cavity (the main diagnostic method).
s6154 **Cardiac tamponade** — fluid accumulates rapidly and compresses the heart: marked dyspnea, cyanosis, tachycardia, paradoxical pulse, a fall in BP, distended neck veins, muffled heart sounds (**Beck's triad**: hypotension + raised venous pressure + muffled heart sounds) — **urgent pericardiocentesis** is required.
s6155 **Constrictive (adhesive) pericarditis** — the pericardial layers thicken and fuse: the heart cannot expand in diastole — ascites, liver enlargement, edema, distended neck veins; **systolic retraction at the site of the apex beat**.

s6173 10.2. Dystonia syndrome
s6174 **Dystonia syndrome** — a disturbance of the regulation of vascular tone; it may occur with a rise or a fall in arterial pressure.
s6175 Arterial hypertension syndrome
s6176 **Definition:** systolic and/or diastolic BP **above the 95th centile** for sex, age and height (measured at least 3 times, on different days).
s6177 **Clinical picture:** morning **headache** (often a feeling of pressure in the occiput), dizziness, tinnitus, rapid fatigue, “flies” before the eyes; it often has no symptoms and is found by chance. Physical — the apex beat increased, **S2 accentuated over the aorta**; ECG — first increased left ventricular potentials, then **left ventricular hypertrophy**; fundus — narrowing of the arteries, dilatation of the veins.
s6178 Types:
s6179 **primary (essential)** — in adolescents, with obesity, low activity, high salt intake, stress and hereditary predisposition;
s6180 **secondary (symptomatic)** — **renal** (glomerulonephritis, pyelonephritis, polycystic disease, hydronephrosis, renal artery stenosis — the most common cause in children), **cardiovascular** (coarctation of the aorta, aortic valve insufficiency), **endocrine** (pheochromocytoma, Itsenko–Cushing syndrome, adrenogenital syndrome, hyperaldosteronism, thyrotoxicosis), **neurogenic** (brain tumors, injury, encephalitis), drugs (glucocorticoids, sympathomimetics).
s6181 **Hypertensive crisis** — a sudden sharp rise in BP: the general condition suddenly worsens, severe headache, visual disturbance (flies, a veil), nausea, vomiting, trembling, pallor or flushing of the face, a feeling of fear; seizures may occur.
s6182 In a young child (especially under 10 years) severe arterial hypertension is almost always **secondary** — first of all, kidney diseases and coarctation of the aorta must be excluded (urinalysis, renal ultrasound and Doppler, arm–leg BP difference).
s6183 Arterial hypotension syndrome
s6184 **Definition:** BP **below the 5th–10th centile**. **Signs:** weakness, rapid fatigue, sensitivity to cold, dizziness and headache (especially in the morning, on exertion, in a stuffy room), a tendency to faint, pallor, cold and moist palms and soles, tachycardia; on auscultation sometimes a “gallop” rhythm; on ECG ST depression, flattened T.
s6185 **Causes:** constitutional (predominance of parasympathetic tone — vagotonia), cardiac (aortic hypoplasia, aortic stenosis), endocrine (hypoglycemia, **adrenal insufficiency** — Addison disease, hypothyroidism, pituitary insufficiency), blood loss, dehydration, intoxication, anaphylaxis, drugs.
s6186 Autonomic dystonia syndrome (autonomic dysfunction)
s6187 A disturbance of the balance between the sympathetic and parasympathetic divisions of the autonomic nervous system; most often in adolescence (prepubertal and pubertal periods), more often in girls. Cardiovascular disturbances (palpitations, cardialgia, labile BP, fainting) are often leading.
