s9896 A 1.5-month-old child. Birth weight 3300 g, now 4300 g. Breastfed strictly every 3.5 hours with a 6-hour night break; only one breast is offered at each feed. In recent days the child is restless, wakes often between feeds, and urinates 5–6 times a day. The mother says milk “is not staying” in her breasts. She is worried about returning to work and sleeps little.
s9897 1) Assess the weight gain. 2) What is happening and what are its causes? 3) Doctor's recommendations? 4) Should formula be prescribed immediately?
s9898 1) Weight has increased by 1000 g in 1.5 months (normally ≈600 g in the 1st month and ≈800 g in the 2nd month — ≈1000–1100 g is expected) — borderline, slightly low. 2) Secondary hypogalactia or a lactation crisis (around week 6); causes: a strict regimen and a long night break (prolactin is produced mostly at night), feeding from only one breast, maternal stress and lack of sleep (the oxytocin reflex weakens). 3) Breastfeed on demand, often, with night feeds and from both breasts at each feed; check breastfeeding technique; adequate rest, sleep, emotional calm and full nutrition for the mother, a warm drink and lactogenic teas before feeding; support from family members; monitor weight and number of urinations after 3–5 days. 4) No — the degree of hypogalactia has not been established; only if all measures fail and grade III–IV hypogalactia is confirmed (control weighing) is supplementary feeding with an adapted formula prescribed, given by spoon or cup.
