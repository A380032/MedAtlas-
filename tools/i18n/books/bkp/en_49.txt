s9889 A 1.5-month-old child. Birth weight 3300 g, now 4300 g. Breastfed strictly every 3.5 hours with a 6-hour night break; only one breast is offered at each feed. In recent days the child is restless, wakes often between feeds, and urinates 5–6 times a day. The mother says milk “is not staying” in her breasts. She is worried about returning to work and sleeps little.
s9890 1) Assess the weight gain. 2) What is happening and what are its causes? 3) Doctor's recommendations? 4) Should formula be prescribed immediately?
s9891 1) Weight has increased by 1000 g in 1.5 months (normally ≈600 g in the 1st month and ≈800 g in the 2nd month — ≈1000–1100 g is expected) — borderline, slightly low. 2) Secondary hypogalactia or a lactation crisis (around week 6); causes: a strict regimen and a long night break (prolactin is produced mostly at night), feeding from only one breast, maternal stress and lack of sleep (the oxytocin reflex weakens). 3) Breastfeed on demand, often, with night feeds and from both breasts at each feed; check breastfeeding technique; adequate rest, sleep, emotional calm and full nutrition for the mother, a warm drink and lactogenic teas before feeding; support from family members; monitor weight and number of urinations after 3–5 days. 4) No — the degree of hypogalactia has not been established; only if all measures fail and grade III–IV hypogalactia is confirmed (control weighing) is supplementary feeding with an adapted formula prescribed, given by spoon or cup.
s9892 A 6-month-old child weighing 7500 g, healthy, exclusively breastfed. Psychomotor development is age-appropriate: sits with support, is interested in food. Stools 1 time a day, sometimes 1 time in 2 days, rather hard. The mother asks for advice on complementary foods.
s9893 1) Is it time to introduce complementary foods? What are the signs of readiness? 2) What should the first complementary food be and why? 3) Rules of introduction? 4) Draw up a plan for the next 2–3 months.
s9894 1) Yes, according to WHO, 6 months is the time to introduce complementary foods; signs of readiness: sits with support, holds the head, is interested in food, has doubled the birth weight. 2) Because of the tendency to constipation and adequate weight — a single-component vegetable puree (zucchini, cauliflower, broccoli), then vegetable oil is added. 3) Only to a healthy child, in the first half of the day, before breastfeeding, starting with ½–1 teaspoon and increasing to 150 g over 5–7 days; one new food at a time; from a spoon; without salt or sugar; monitor tolerance (rash, stools). 4) 6 months — vegetable puree (single, then mixed); 6.5 months — gluten-free porridge (buckwheat, rice, corn) + butter; 6.5–7 months — meat puree (5–30 g, then 50 g); 7 months — fruit puree, egg yolk (¼), cottage cheese; 8 months — adapted kefir, bread, rusks; 8–9 months — fish puree (1–2 times a week). Breastfeeding continues.
s9895 A 4-month-old child is artificially fed (the mother stopped breastfeeding at 2 months, saying her milk had “gone”). Weight 6400 g. The child has been fed whole cow's milk diluted with water, 7 times a day. Over the last 2 weeks there has been frequent regurgitation, irregular stools, and redness with itchy rashes on the face and neck. Blood: Hb 98 g/l.
s9896 1) Feeding errors? 2) Causes of the rash and anemia? 3) Calculate the child's daily and single-feed volumes. 4) Recommendations?
s9897 1) Whole cow's milk given to a child under 1 year; no adapted formula used; no measures taken to maintain and restore lactation. 2) Cow's milk proteins (casein, β-lactoglobulin) are highly allergenic — rashes typical of atopic dermatitis (cow's milk protein allergy); cow's milk is low in iron, which is poorly absorbed, and diapedetic bleeding from the intestinal mucosa occurs — iron deficiency anemia (Hb 98 g/l). 3) Volumetric method: at 4 months (on the 2–4-month boundary) 1/6 × 6400 ≈ 1070 ml, but it should not exceed 1000 ml — ≈1000 ml/day; with 7 feeds the single-feed volume is ≈140–145 ml (if switched to 6 feeds — ≈165 ml). 4) Stop cow's milk and switch to a therapeutic formula based on extensively hydrolyzed protein; allergist consultation; iron preparations; once formula tolerance improves, at 4–6 months (cautiously, given the allergy) a hypoallergenic single-component complementary food; advise the mother on the possibilities of relactation.
