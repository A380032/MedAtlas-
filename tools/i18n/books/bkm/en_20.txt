s3757 8. Metabolic syndrome
s3758 **Metabolic syndrome** is a cluster of interrelated factors that develops on the basis of insulin resistance: **abdominal obesity** (waist circumference: men > 94 cm, women > 80 cm), **arterial hypertension** (≥ 130/85 mm Hg), **hyperglycemia** (fasting ≥ 5.6 mmol/l), **hypertriglyceridemia** (≥ 1.7 mmol/l), **decreased HDL cholesterol** (men < 1.0, women < 1.3 mmol/l). The diagnosis is made if three of them are present.
s3759 **Biochemical basis:** visceral adipose tissue releases many free fatty acids and inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-6), and adiponectin decreases → insulin signaling in muscle and liver is impaired (IRS-1 phosphorylation) → compensatory hyperinsulinemia. In the liver VLDL synthesis increases (hypertriglyceridemia, small dense LDL) and HDL decreases. Hyperinsulinemia increases renal Na+ reabsorption and sympathetic activity (hypertension). Complications: type 2 diabetes mellitus, atherosclerosis, coronary heart disease, nonalcoholic fatty liver disease, gout, polycystic ovary syndrome.

s3769 Experiment 2: determination of glucose and ketone bodies in urine (test strips)

s3869 A healthy male volunteer, under a physician's supervision, underwent therapeutic fasting and drank only water. Blood was drawn on day 3 and in week 3. Day 3: glucose 3.9 mmol/l, ketone bodies 2.5 mmol/l, a lot of urea in the urine. Week 3: glucose 3.6 mmol/l, ketone bodies 6.5 mmol/l, urinary urea decreased, ammonium salts increased.
s3870 Why is there a lot of urea in the urine on day 3 and little in week 3? What are the main sources of glucose? Why did ammonium excretion increase?
s3871 On day 3 liver glycogen is depleted and glucose comes mainly from gluconeogenesis — muscle proteins are broken down, and alanine and glutamine go to the liver; their nitrogen is converted to urea (a lot of urea). In week 3 the brain obtains a large part of its energy from ketone bodies, the need for glucose decreases, and protein breakdown slows down (proteins are spared) — urea decreases. Sources of glucose: amino acids, glycerol (lipolysis), lactate. To compensate for ketoacidosis, glutaminase is activated in the kidney: NH3 excretes H+ as NH~4~^+^, and the carbon skeleton of glutamine is used in renal gluconeogenesis.
s3872 A 16-year-old girl has been drinking a lot of water and urinating a lot for a month and has lost weight; today she has abdominal pain, vomiting, deep noisy breathing (Kussmaul) and an acetone smell on the breath. Blood glucose 24 mmol/l, pH 7.15, ketone bodies 9 mmol/l; the urine contains a lot of glucose and ketone bodies.
s3873 What is the diagnosis? How are carbohydrate, lipid and protein metabolism disturbed? Why has the patient lost weight?
s3874 Type 1 diabetes mellitus, diabetic ketoacidosis. Without insulin, glucose does not enter muscle and adipose tissue, and glycogenolysis and gluconeogenesis are enhanced in the liver (hyperglycemia, glucosuria, osmotic diuresis — polyuria, polydipsia, dehydration). Lipolysis is enhanced and free fatty acids undergo β-oxidation in the liver; because oxaloacetate is used for gluconeogenesis, acetyl-CoA is converted to ketone bodies — ketoacidosis (Kussmaul breathing — compensation by exhaling CO2). Protein synthesis decreases and breakdown increases (negative nitrogen balance). The weight loss is due to the breakdown of adipose and muscle tissue and the loss of glucose in the urine (loss of energy).

s3914 1. Structure of nucleic acids
s3915 **Nucleic acids** are polymers formed by the joining of mononucleotides through 3’,5’-**phosphodiester bonds**. The 5’-end of the chain carries a phosphate and the 3’-end a free OH group; the sequence is written in the 5’→3’ direction.

s3936 **Chargaff's rules:** in DNA A = T and G = C; the sum of purines equals the sum of pyrimidines (A + G = T + C); the (G + C)/(A + T) ratio is species-specific.
s3937 **Watson–Crick model (1953, B-form):** two **antiparallel** polynucleotide chains form a right-handed double helix twisted around a common axis; the bases are inside and the sugar-phosphate backbone is outside. The bases pair **complementarily**: A=T (2 hydrogen bonds), G≡C (3 hydrogen bonds). One turn of the helix is ≈ 10 base pairs (3.4 nm), the diameter is 2 nm; the major and minor grooves are important for the binding of proteins to DNA. The helix is stabilized by hydrogen bonds and by the **stacking interactions** of the bases.
s3938 **Tertiary structure and chromatin:** DNA winds 1.7 times around a histone octamer (H2A, H2B, H3, H4 — 2 of each) with ≈ 146 base pairs to form a **nucleosome**; the linker histone H1 assembles nucleosomes into a 30 nm fibril; then come loops and metaphase chromosomes. **Euchromatin** is loosely packed and actively transcribed; **heterochromatin** is dense and inactive.
s3939 **Denaturation and hybridization:** heating (or alkali) breaks the hydrogen bonds and separates the double helix — the **melting temperature (T~m~)** is higher when there are many G≡C pairs; on denaturation the absorbance at 260 nm increases (**hyperchromic effect**). On cooling, complementary strands reassociate (renaturation). The joining of complementary strands from different sources is **hybridization** (Southern blot, FISH, DNA microarrays, PCR-based methods).
s3940 **Genetic system of mitochondria:** mtDNA is circular, 16,569 base pairs, 37 genes (13 respiratory chain proteins, 22 tRNAs, 2 rRNAs); it is inherited only **through the mother**; repair is weak and mutations are frequent — mitochondrial diseases (MELAS, Leber optic atrophy), aging.
