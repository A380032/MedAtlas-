s7741 6. Biochemistry of sensory systems
s7742 **Vision:** in the rods **rhodopsin** = opsin + **11-cis-retinal** (vitamin A). A quantum of light isomerizes 11-cis-retinal into **all-trans-retinal** → metarhodopsin II → the G protein **transducin** → **cGMP phosphodiesterase** is activated → cGMP decreases → cGMP-gated Na+/Ca2+ channels close → **hyperpolarization** → glutamate release decreases → signal. One photon activates hundreds of transducin molecules (amplification). The visual cycle: all-trans-retinal → retinol → converted back to 11-cis-retinal in the pigment epithelium. **Vitamin A deficiency** — night blindness (hemeralopia), xerophthalmia. Cones contain three kinds of iodopsin (color vision); the genes are on the X chromosome — color blindness.
s7743 **Hearing:** sound waves bend the stereocilia of the hair cells → mechanosensitive channels open and K+ enters (the endolymph is rich in K+ — 150 mmol/l, produced by the stria vascularis) → depolarization → glutamate is released. Aminoglycosides and furosemide are ototoxic.
s7744 **Smell:** ≈ 400 kinds of odorant receptors (G protein-coupled; the largest gene family) → Golf → adenylate cyclase → cAMP → cation channels open.
s7745 **Taste** — topic 24: sweet, umami, bitter (G protein — gustducin), salty and sour (ion channels).
s7746 **Pain (nociception):** TRPV1 (capsaicin, heat), TRPA1, acid-sensing channels; mediators — prostaglandin E2 (increases sensitivity — the basis of NSAID action), bradykinin, substance P, glutamate; endogenous analgesia — enkephalins, endorphins.

s7776 Experiment 1: isolation of lipids from brain tissue and qualitative reactions for them
s7777 **Principle of the method:** lipids dissolve in organic solvents; cholesterol and phospholipids give characteristic reactions.
s7778 Grind 1 g of brain tissue with 10 ml of chloroform–methanol mixture (2:1) and filter (extract).
s7779 **Cholesterol — the Salkowski reaction:** carefully add 1 ml of concentrated H2SO4 to 1 ml of the (chloroform) extract — the chloroform layer turns red and the acid layer gives a yellow-green fluorescence. **The Liebermann–Burchard reaction:** with acetic anhydride and H2SO4 — a blue-green color (the basis of cholesterol determination in serum).
s7780 **Phosphate (phospholipids):** evaporate the extract, heat the residue with HNO3 and add ammonium molybdate — a yellow precipitate (phosphomolybdate).
s7781 **The acrolein test** (glycerol): heat the residue with KHSO4 — a pungent odor (glycerol of the glycerophospholipids). **Results and analysis:** the brain extract contains a lot of cholesterol and phospholipids (the Salkowski and molybdate reactions are clearly positive); this confirms the high lipid content of the brain and the composition of its membranes (myelin).

s7883 A 19-year-old man with type 1 diabetes mellitus went to training after injecting insulin without eating. 1 hour later he developed sweating, tremor and confusion, then seizures and loss of consciousness. Blood glucose 1.9 mmol/l.
s7884 What condition is this? Why is the brain especially sensitive to hypoglycemia? How should help be provided?
s7885 Hypoglycemic coma. The brain obtains its energy almost exclusively from the aerobic oxidation of glucose; its glycogen store is very small, it cannot use fatty acids, and the use of ketone bodies develops only as an adaptation to prolonged starvation (here insulin has also stopped ketogenesis). When glucose falls, ATP formation decreases, Na+,K+-ATPase stops working and the neurons depolarize — confusion, seizures (a lot of glutamate is released), coma; if prolonged, irreversible damage. Sweating and tremor are a contrainsular adrenergic reaction. Help: if conscious, a sweet drink; if unconscious, 40% glucose intravenously or glucagon intramuscularly (hepatic glycogenolysis); then a carbohydrate meal and analysis of the cause.
s7886 A 65-year-old man has a resting hand tremor, slowness of movement, muscle rigidity and short steps when walking. Parkinson disease was diagnosed and levodopa + carbidopa was prescribed.
s7887 What is the biochemical basis of the disease? Why is levodopa given rather than dopamine itself? Why is carbidopa added?
s7888 The dopaminergic neurons of the substantia nigra die (because of α-synuclein aggregates, mitochondrial dysfunction and oxidative stress), and dopamine decreases in the striatum; the balance with acetylcholine is disturbed, and movement disorders appear. Dopamine does not cross the blood–brain barrier, whereas its precursor L-DOPA crosses through an amino acid transporter and is converted to dopamine in the brain by DOPA decarboxylase (B6). Carbidopa is a peripheral DOPA decarboxylase inhibitor (it does not cross the BBB): it stops the conversion of L-DOPA into dopamine in the blood, ensuring that more of it reaches the brain, and reduces the peripheral adverse effects (nausea, arrhythmia). Protein-rich food competes with L-DOPA for absorption and for crossing the BBB.
s7889 A farmer working in the field was brought in after spraying pesticide with blurred vision, sharply constricted pupils, profuse salivation, shortness of breath, diarrhea and muscle twitching. Blood cholinesterase activity is sharply decreased.
s7890 What is the diagnosis? What is the biochemical mechanism of the symptoms? What are the principles of treatment?
s7891 Acute poisoning with an organophosphorus compound. The poison phosphorylates the serine in the active site of acetylcholinesterase and inhibits it irreversibly; acetylcholine accumulates in the synapses. Through M-cholinergic receptors — miosis, blurred vision, increased salivary and bronchial secretion, bronchospasm, bradycardia, diarrhea; through N-receptors — muscle fibrillation, then paralysis (respiratory muscles); in the CNS — agitation, seizures, coma. A decrease in blood cholinesterase activity confirms the diagnosis. Treatment: washing the poison off the skin, high-dose atropine (stops the muscarinic signs), oxime reactivators (pralidoxime — detaches the phosphate group from the enzyme, effective if given early), benzodiazepines (seizures), artificial ventilation.
