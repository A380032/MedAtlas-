s1129 A person rescued from a fire was brought in unconscious. The skin is pink-red, blood lactate is sharply increased, and the oxygen content of venous blood is close to that of arterial blood. Cyanide and CO were released from the burning synthetic materials.
s1130 Why is there a lot of oxygen in the venous blood? What is the cause of the lactic acidosis? Which antidotes are used?
s1131 Cyanide and CO block cytochrome oxidase (complex IV) — electrons do not pass to oxygen and the tissues cannot use it (tissue, or histotoxic, hypoxia), so the venous blood remains rich in oxygen. Once oxidative phosphorylation stops, ATP is formed only by anaerobic glycolysis — pyruvate is reduced to lactate and lactic acidosis develops. CO also forms carboxyhemoglobin with hemoglobin (red skin). Treatment is given by the set emergency protocol: oxygen (including hyperbaric oxygenation) and the antidotes used against cyanide (hydroxocobalamin, nitrite and thiosulfate preparations).
s1132 A gym member bought a “fat burner” online — 2,4-dinitrophenol — and took it. A few days later his body temperature rose to 41 °C, with profuse sweating, tachycardia and shortness of breath.
s1133 How does this substance act? Why did hyperthermia develop?
s1134 2,4-dinitrophenol is an uncoupler of oxidative phosphorylation: it carries protons into the matrix bypassing ATP synthase, and the proton gradient is lost. The respiratory chain works continuously (O2 consumption rises sharply), but little ATP is formed and the energy is released as heat. The body cannot dissipate this heat — uncontrollable hyperthermia, a life-threatening condition. The substance is not a medicine and its use is dangerous.
s1135 A 14-year-old adolescent has exercise intolerance, muscle weakness and recurrent stroke-like attacks. Blood lactate is increased at rest. His mother also has a mild myopathy; his father is healthy.
s1136 Which group of diseases is suspected? Why is lactate increased? What does the pattern of inheritance indicate?
s1137 A mitochondrial disease (MELAS syndrome is suspected). Because of a defect of respiratory chain proteins, NADH oxidation and ATP formation are impaired; the NADH/NAD+ ratio rises and pyruvate is reduced to lactate — lactic acidosis. The tissues with the greatest energy needs (brain, muscles) are affected. Transmission of the disease through the mother points to a mutation of mitochondrial DNA, because mitochondria pass to the zygote only from the egg cell.

s1179 1. The concept of the common pathways of catabolism
s1180 The specific catabolic pathways of carbohydrates, fats and amino acids (glycolysis, β-oxidation, amino acid breakdown) lead to the common metabolites — **pyruvate** and **acetyl-CoA**. Their further oxidation proceeds through reactions common to all substances:
s1181 **Oxidative decarboxylation of pyruvate** — pyruvate → acetyl-CoA;
s1182 **The citric acid cycle (Krebs cycle, tricarboxylic acid cycle)** — oxidation of the acetyl residue to CO2;
s1183 **The respiratory chain and oxidative phosphorylation** — transfer of hydrogen atoms to oxygen and ATP synthesis. These processes take place in the **mitochondrial matrix** (the Krebs cycle enzymes, except succinate dehydrogenase, which is in the inner membrane) and provide the main part of the body’s energy.
s1184 2. Oxidative decarboxylation of pyruvate
s1185 Pyruvate formed in the cytosol in glycolysis enters the mitochondria through a special carrier and is converted to acetyl-CoA by the **pyruvate dehydrogenase complex (PDC)**:
s1186 Pyruvate + NAD+ + CoA-SH → Acetyl-CoA + NADH + H+ + CO2
s1187 The reaction is **irreversible**; therefore glucose cannot be synthesized from acetyl-CoA (the reason why glucose is not formed from fatty acids).

s1263 6. Functions of the Krebs cycle. Anaplerotic reactions
s1264 **Catabolic (energy) function** — oxidizing the acetyl residue to CO2 and supplying NADH and FADH2 to the respiratory chain.
s1265 **Anabolic functions** — the cycle metabolites are raw material for synthesis:
s1266 oxaloacetate → phosphoenolpyruvate → **glucose** (gluconeogenesis);
s1267 oxaloacetate → **aspartate**, α-ketoglutarate → **glutamate** (transamination);
s1268 citrate moves to the cytosol and gives acetyl-CoA → **synthesis of fatty acids and cholesterol**;
s1269 succinyl-CoA + glycine → **heme synthesis**. For this reason the cycle is considered an **amphibolic** pathway.
s1270 **Anaplerotic (“filling-up”) reactions** replenish the metabolites withdrawn for synthesis:
s1271 **Pyruvate carboxylase** (biotin, ATP): pyruvate + CO2 → oxaloacetate — the most important; acetyl-CoA activates it allosterically (when acetyl-CoA increases, its “partner” oxaloacetate increases too);
s1272 formation of α-ketoglutarate from glutamate (glutamate dehydrogenase, transamination);
s1273 formation of succinyl-CoA from odd-chain fatty acids, valine, isoleucine, methionine and threonine (propionyl-CoA → methylmalonyl-CoA → succinyl-CoA; requires B12).
s1274 In starvation and diabetes mellitus oxaloacetate is used up in gluconeogenesis; the acetyl-CoA formed in the liver from the β-oxidation of fatty acids cannot fully enter the Krebs cycle and is directed to the synthesis of **ketone bodies** (topic 9).
s1275 7. Hypoenergetic states
s1276 A **hypoenergetic state** is a fall in ATP formation in the cell below its needs. Causes:
s1277 **Alimentary** — starvation, lack of nutrients and vitamins (B1, B2, PP, B5);
s1278 **Hypoxic** — lack of oxygen (respiratory and circulatory failure, anemia, CO poisoning);
s1279 **Histotoxic** — inhibition of respiratory chain enzymes (cyanides), the action of uncouplers;
s1280 **Mitochondrial diseases** — congenital defects of respiratory chain and Krebs cycle enzymes. Consequences: ion pumps fail (cell swelling), synthetic processes weaken, anaerobic glycolysis increases and lactic acidosis develops; the most sensitive organs are the brain and the heart.
s1281 8. Laboratory work
s1282 Experiment 1: determination of citric acid in urine (Deniges reaction)
s1283 Pour 2 ml of urine, 0.5 ml of 10% H2SO4 and 0.5 ml of 5% KMnO4 solution into a test tube and heat to boiling.
s1284 After decolorization add 1 ml of mercuric sulfate reagent (Deniges reagent). **Result and analysis:** citric acid is oxidized to acetone dicarboxylic acid, which gives a white precipitate with mercuric sulfate. Normally 0.2–1.2 g of citrate is excreted in the urine per day; it binds calcium and prevents the formation of kidney stones (hypocitraturia is a risk factor for stone formation).
s1285 Experiment 2: determination of pyruvate in urine (sodium nitroprusside test)
s1286 Add to a test tube 2 ml of urine, 0.5 ml of 10% NaOH and 3–5 drops of freshly prepared 5% sodium nitroprusside solution.
s1287 Observe the color that appears, then add 1 ml of concentrated acetic acid. **Result and analysis:** if pyruvate is present, a deep orange-red color forms in the alkaline medium and turns violet when acetic acid is added. An increase of pyruvate in the urine (normal 10–25 mg per day) is characteristic of B1 hypovitaminosis, diabetes mellitus, hypoxia and PDC deficiency.
s1288 Experiment 3: inhibition of succinate dehydrogenase in muscle tissue by malonate
s1289 Put equal amounts of washed minced muscle into three test tubes.
s1290 Add 2 ml of succinate to tube 1, 2 ml of succinate + 1 ml of malonate to tube 2, and 2 ml of water to tube 3 (control); add 2 drops of methylene blue and a layer of petroleum jelly oil to all of them.
s1291 Compare the time of decolorization at 37 °C.
s1292 **Result and analysis:** in the tube with malonate decolorization slows down — malonate is similar in structure to succinate and competes with it for the active site. Increasing the amount of substrate (succinate) reduces the inhibition.
