s6230 In the parietal cell, **carbonic anhydrase** forms H~2~CO~3~ from CO~2~ and H~2~O; H^+^ is pumped into the lumen by the **H^+^,K^+^-ATPase** (proton pump) in the apical membrane (against a ≈ 106-fold concentration gradient), and HCO~3~^−^ passes into the blood across the basolateral membrane in exchange for Cl^-^ (the postprandial **“alkaline tide”** — the urine becomes alkaline). Cl^-^ and K^+^ leave into the lumen through apical channels. **Stimulators:** acetylcholine (vagus nerve, M~3~), gastrin, **histamine** (from ECL cells, H~2~ receptor — cAMP); **inhibitors:** somatostatin, prostaglandin E~2~, secretin, GIP, pH < 2 (feedback).
s6231 **Gastric and duodenal ulcer** — disturbance of the balance between aggression (HCl, pepsin, Helicobacter pylori, NSAIDs) and protection (mucus, HCO~3~^−^, prostaglandins, blood flow). The H. pylori enzyme **urease** breaks down urea into NH~3~ and CO~2~, creating an alkaline “cloud” around the bacterium; NH~3~ damages the epithelium. Diagnosis — the ^13^C-urea breath test; treatment — proton pump inhibitors + antibiotics. NSAIDs inhibit cyclooxygenase and reduce protective prostaglandins. **Zollinger–Ellison syndrome** — a gastrin-producing tumor (gastrinoma): multiple recurrent ulcers.

s6261 5. Bile
s6262 0.6–1 l is formed in the liver per day; in the gallbladder water is reabsorbed and bile is concentrated 5–10 times.
s6263 **Composition: bile acids** (bile salts — ≈ 67% of the dry residue), **phospholipids** (lecithin), **cholesterol**, **bilirubin diglucuronide** (conjugated bilirubin — the color of bile), proteins, electrolytes (HCO~3~^−^), metabolites of drugs and toxins.
s6264 **Bile acids** are synthesized in the liver **from cholesterol** (key enzyme — **7α-hydroxylase**, a cytochrome P450; topic 10): **primary** — cholic and chenodeoxycholic acids; with glycine or taurine they **are conjugated** (glycocholic and taurocholic acids — highly soluble, amphipathic). In the intestine, bacteria deconjugate them and 7-dehydroxylate them into **secondary** bile acids (deoxycholic, lithocholic).
s6265 **Functions:** **emulsification** of fats, enhancement of lipase activity, formation of **micelles** for the absorption of lipids and fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K), keeping cholesterol in solution, stimulation of intestinal peristalsis, bacteriostatic action, excretion of cholesterol and bilirubin.
s6266 **Enterohepatic circulation:** ≈ 95% of bile acids are reabsorbed in the **ileum** (active transport together with Na^+^) and return to the liver via the portal vein; they circulate 6–10 times a day; only ≈ 0.5 g/day is lost with the feces — this is the main route of cholesterol elimination from the body. Sequestrants (colestyramine) bind bile acids and lower blood cholesterol.
s6267 **Cholesterol gallstones** — an excess of cholesterol in bile relative to bile acids and phospholipids (lithogenic bile): obesity, estrogens, pregnancy, rapid weight loss, ileal resection (bile acids are lost). **Ursodeoxycholic acid** is used to dissolve small stones. **Pigment stones** — calcium bilirubinate (hemolysis, infection).
s6268 6. Intestinal juice and membrane digestion
s6269 Intestinal juice (2–3 l/day, pH 7.5–8.0) consists of the secretion of the Brunner and Lieberkühn glands and shed epithelial cells. Digestion takes place mainly as **membrane (parietal) digestion** — by enzymes located in the brush border of the enterocyte microvilli:
s6270 **disaccharidases:** sucrase-isomaltase, **lactase** (β-galactosidase), maltase-glucoamylase, trehalase;
s6271 **peptidases:** aminopeptidases, dipeptidases, tripeptidases;
s6272 **enteropeptidase (enterokinase)** — activates trypsinogen;
s6273 alkaline phosphatase, nucleotidases.

s6292 **Malabsorption syndrome** — weight loss, diarrhea, steatorrhea, anemia, osteoporosis, hypoproteinemic edema, vitamin deficiencies.
s6293 **Lactose intolerance** — lactase deficiency (in adults a genetically programmed decline — widespread in many populations; secondary — in intestinal diseases): after drinking milk, lactose passes into the colon, where bacteria ferment it to gases (H~2~, CO~2~) and short-chain acids — **osmotic diarrhea**, bloating. Diagnosis — the hydrogen breath test, the lactose tolerance test (blood glucose does not rise).
s6294 **Celiac disease (gluten enteropathy)** — an autoimmune response to **gliadin** peptides of wheat, barley and rye (rich in proline and glutamine, resistant to pepsin and trypsin): **tissue transglutaminase** deamidates gliadin, and T cells are activated through HLA-DQ2/DQ8; atrophy of the intestinal villi — the absorption of all substances is impaired (iron deficiency anemia, osteoporosis). Diagnosis — IgA antibodies against tissue transglutaminase, biopsy; treatment — a **lifelong gluten-free diet**.
s6295 **Exocrine pancreatic insufficiency** (chronic pancreatitis, **cystic fibrosis** — a CFTR defect, thick secretions block the ducts): steatorrhea, decreased fecal **elastase-1**; treatment — enzyme preparations (pancreatin).
s6296 **Bile acid deficiency** (obstructive jaundice, ileal resection) — the absorption of fats and vitamins A, D, E, K is impaired (vitamin K deficiency — bleeding).
s6297 **Crohn disease and ulcerative colitis** are chronic inflammatory bowel diseases: genetic predisposition (NOD2 — a receptor that recognizes bacterial muramyl dipeptide), disturbed microbiota (dysbiosis), disturbed epithelial barrier and immune response (excess TNF-α, IL-12/23); protein loss, anemia, malabsorption (in Crohn disease, the ileum — B~12~, bile acids). Activity marker — fecal **calprotectin**. Treatment includes monoclonal antibodies against TNF-α (infliximab) and 5-aminosalicylates.
s6298 **Familial adenomatous polyposis** — mutation of the APC gene: hundreds of polyps in the colon, with a ≈ 100% chance of malignant transformation (topic 19). **Functions of the colon:** absorption of water and electrolytes; the **microbiota** (10^14^ cells) — fermentation of undigested polysaccharides (fiber) to form **short-chain fatty acids** (acetate, propionate, **butyrate** — the main energy source of colonocytes, anti-inflammatory), synthesis of vitamin K~2~ and B vitamins, transformation of bile acids and bilirubin (urobilinogen, stercobilin), **putrefaction** of amino acids (indole, skatole, phenol, cresol, putrescine, cadaverine — topic 26), a competitive barrier to pathogens.
s6299 8. Mechanisms of action of drugs in diarrhea and constipation
s6300 **Secretory diarrhea** — cholera toxin ADP-ribosylates the G~s~ protein and permanently activates adenylate cyclase → cAMP ↑ → a lot of Cl^-^ and water is secreted through CFTR (up to 10–20 l per day).
s6301 **Oral rehydration solution** (glucose + NaCl) — based on the fact that the absorption of Na^+^ and water together with glucose through SGLT1 is preserved.
s6302 **Osmotic diarrhea** — unabsorbed substances (lactose, sorbitol, magnesium salts).
s6303 **Antidiarrheal drugs:** loperamide (a μ-opioid receptor agonist — peristalsis ↓, secretion ↓), adsorbents (diosmectite, activated charcoal), racecadotril (an enkephalinase inhibitor — secretion ↓), probiotics.
s6304 **Laxatives:** osmotic (lactulose — bacteria ferment it to form acids; macrogol; magnesium sulfate), bulk-forming (fiber, psyllium), stimulant (bisacodyl, sennosides — enhance epithelial secretion and peristalsis), softeners (liquid paraffin).
s6305 Experiment 1: determination of the components of saliva
s6306 **Mucin:** add 3–4 drops of acetic acid to 2 ml of saliva — a white stringy precipitate (mucin); filter the precipitate and perform the biuret reaction (the protein part).
s6307 **Amylase:** add 1 ml of diluted (1:10) saliva to 1 ml of 1% starch solution and keep at 37 °C for 10 minutes; reaction with iodine — no color or reddish (starch has been broken down); the Trommer reaction is positive (maltose).
s6308 **Thiocyanates (rhodanides):** add 2–3 drops of HCl and 2 drops of 5% FeCl~3~ to 1 ml of saliva — a red color (Fe(SCN)3). **Results and analysis:** the saliva of smokers contains a lot of thiocyanates (a product of the detoxification of cyanides in tobacco smoke). Preserved amylase activity indicates the digestive function of saliva.
s6309 Experiment 2: qualitative reactions for the components of bile
s6310 **Bile acids — the Pettenkofer reaction:** place 2 drops of bile and 1 drop of 20% sucrose solution in a dry test tube and carefully add 1 ml of concentrated sulfuric acid down the wall — a red-violet ring at the interface (condensation of the hydroxymethylfurfural formed from sucrose with bile acids).
s6311 **Bile pigments — the Gmelin reaction:** pour 1 ml of concentrated nitric acid into a test tube and layer 1 ml of bile over it down the wall — colored rings at the interface (green, blue, violet, red, yellow) — products of successive oxidation of bilirubin (biliverdin and others).
s6312 **Reduction of surface tension by bile (Hay's test):** sprinkle sulfur powder on the surface of two test tubes containing water and diluted bile — in the bile the powder sinks (the surface tension is reduced). **Results and analysis:** the reactions confirm the presence of bile acids and pigments in bile; a positive Gmelin reaction in urine indicates conjugated bilirubinuria (obstructive and hepatocellular jaundice).
s6313 Experiment 3: interpretation of serum amylase and lipase results (case-based)
s6314 Compare the results of serum α-amylase (normal < 100 U/l; pancreatic isoenzyme < 53 U/l) and lipase (< 60 U/l) with the normal values.
s6315 Take into account the result of urinary diastase (amylase). **Results and analysis:** in acute pancreatitis amylase rises within 2–12 hours and returns to normal in 3–5 days; urinary amylase persists longer; **lipase** is more specific and stays high longer (7–14 days) — an increase of **3 times** the upper limit of normal is the diagnostic criterion. Amylase also rises in mumps, intestinal obstruction and macroamylasemia.
s6316 Skill: biochemical diagnosis of acute pancreatitis
s6317 Determine lipase and amylase activity (≥ 3-fold increase).
s6318 Assess severity: glucose ↑, calcium ↓ (calcium soaps in fat necrosis), LDH ↑, leukocytosis, CRP > 150 mg/l (at 48 hours), urea ↑.
s6319 Determine the cause: gallstones (bilirubin, alkaline phosphatase, ALT ↑), alcohol, hypertriglyceridemia (> 11 mmol/l), hypercalcemia.
s6320 Explain the mechanism: premature activation of trypsinogen inside the gland → activation of the other proenzymes → self-digestion of the gland (autolysis), elastase — vascular damage, phospholipase A~2~ — destruction of membranes.
