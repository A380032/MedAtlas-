s7066 **Progesterone** — progesterone receptor: secretory phase of the endometrium, maintenance of pregnancy (reduces myometrial excitability), development of the alveoli of the mammary glands, thermogenic effect (rise in basal temperature).
s7067 **Pharmacological use:** oral contraceptives (estrogen + progestin — suppress gonadotropin secretion by feedback), tamoxifen (an estrogen antagonist in the breast), aromatase inhibitors (letrozole), 5α-reductase inhibitors (finasteride — in prostatic adenoma), antiandrogens, anabolic steroids (abuse — liver, heart, infertility).
s7068 8. Chorionic gonadotropin
s7069 **Human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG)** is a glycoprotein produced by the **syncytiotrophoblast**; its α subunit is identical to that of LH, FSH and TSH, while the **β subunit** is specific (that is why tests detect β-hCG). Its function is to act like LH and maintain the **corpus luteum** and progesterone secretion during the first 8–10 weeks of pregnancy (after that the placenta produces progesterone itself). It is detectable in the blood 8–10 days after fertilization, doubles every 48–72 hours and peaks at 8–10 weeks. At high concentrations it stimulates TSH receptors (signs of hyperthyroidism in hyperemesis of pregnancy).
s7070 **Diagnostic importance:** early detection of pregnancy; ectopic (extrauterine) pregnancy — hCG rises slowly; threatened miscarriage; trophoblastic diseases (**hydatidiform mole**, **choriocarcinoma** — very high hCG; a tumor marker); embryonal tumors of the testis; prenatal screening (elevated in Down syndrome in the 2nd trimester).
s7071 Experiment 1: determination of chorionic gonadotropin in urine with an immunochromatographic test
s7072 **Principle of the method:** the test strip contains monoclonal antibodies against β-hCG labeled with gold particles. Urine moves along the membrane by capillary action: if hCG is present, it forms a complex with the labeled antibodies and is captured by the immobilized second antibodies in the “test” zone — a colored line appears (a “sandwich”). Antiglobulins in the “control” zone capture the excess conjugate — this shows that the test has worked correctly.
s7073 Collect morning urine (the highest concentration) in a clean container.
s7074 Dip the test strip into the urine up to the indicated mark for 10–15 seconds (do not go beyond the mark).
s7075 Place it on a flat horizontal surface and read the result after 3–5 minutes (a result read after 10 minutes is unreliable). **Interpretation:** two lines — positive (hCG ≥ 10–25 IU/l); only the control line — negative; no control line — the test is invalid. The test is reliable from the first day of a missed period. False negative —
s7076 too early, dilute urine; false positive — treatment with hCG preparations, trophoblastic tumors, a recent abortion.

s7167 In the maternity ward a newborn (XX karyotype) has ambiguous external genitalia. In the 2nd week vomiting and dehydration began: Na+ 124, K+ 6.8 mmol/l, glucose 2.8 mmol/l. Blood 17-hydroxyprogesterone is sharply increased, and ACTH is high.
s7168 What is the diagnosis? Explain all the signs on the basis of the steroid synthesis scheme. What is the principle of treatment?
s7169 Congenital adrenal hyperplasia — 21-hydroxylase deficiency, salt-wasting form. Progesterone and 17α-hydroxyprogesterone are not 21-hydroxylated: cortisol (hypoglycemia) and aldosterone (Na+ loss, K+ retention, dehydration) are not synthesized. Because of the low cortisol, ACTH increases by feedback and the gland becomes hyperplastic; the accumulated 17-hydroxyprogesterone is diverted to the androgen synthesis pathway — the excess androgens virilize the external genitalia of the girl in the fetal period. Treatment: glucocorticoids (hydrocortisone — replaces cortisol and, by lowering ACTH, stops the overproduction of androgens), a mineralocorticoid (fludrocortisone), table salt; in a crisis — intravenous fluids, glucose, hydrocortisone.
s7170 A 27-year-old woman's period is 2 weeks late; she has one-sided lower abdominal pain and bloody discharge. Blood β-hCG 1200 IU/l, and 1350 IU/l 48 hours later. Ultrasound did not detect a gestational sac in the uterine cavity.
s7171 What condition is suspected? Explain the hCG dynamics. What is the physiological role of hCG in pregnancy?
s7172 Extrauterine (ectopic, probably tubal) pregnancy. In a normal intrauterine pregnancy β-hCG roughly doubles every 48–72 hours in the early weeks; here it hardly rose (≈ 12%) — the trophoblast is not developing adequately; the absence of a gestational sac in the uterus at this hCG level indicates an ectopic location. hCG secreted by the syncytiotrophoblast maintains the corpus luteum through LH receptors and ensures progesterone secretion; progesterone maintains the endometrium and stops menstruation. An ectopic pregnancy requires urgent treatment because of the risk of tubal rupture and internal bleeding.
s7173 A 40-year-old patient has extreme fatigue, weight loss, nausea, arterial hypotension and dark pigmentation of the skin and gums. Blood Na+ 128, K+ 5.9 mmol/l, morning cortisol 60 nmol/l (normal 140–690), ACTH sharply increased.
s7174 At what level is the disorder? What is the biochemical cause of the pigmentation? Explain the electrolyte changes.
s7175 Primary adrenocortical insufficiency (Addison disease, most often autoimmune). Low cortisol and high ACTH — the disorder is in the peripheral gland (feedback is preserved, and the pituitary produces a lot of ACTH). ACTH is formed from proopiomelanocortin (POMC); when POMC is cleaved, α-melanocyte-stimulating hormone also increases, and ACTH itself stimulates melanocortin-1 receptors — melanin synthesis is enhanced (pigmentation; absent in secondary insufficiency). Aldosterone is also deficient: renal Na+ reabsorption and K+ secretion decrease — hyponatremia, hyperkalemia, hypovolemia and hypotension. Treatment — lifelong replacement therapy with hydrocortisone and fludrocortisone.

s7209 1. Hormonal regulation of carbohydrate, lipid and amino acid metabolism
s7210 **Insulin** — in the **β-cells** of the islets of Langerhans of the pancreas: preproinsulin → proinsulin (A and B chains + C-peptide) → insulin + **C-peptide** (released in equal amounts; C-peptide is an indicator of endogenous insulin secretion). **Mechanism of secretion:** glucose enters through GLUT2 → glucokinase → glycolysis and oxidation → ATP/ADP ↑ → ATP-sensitive K+ channels close (sulfonylurea drugs act in the same way) → depolarization → Ca2+ channels open → exocytosis of granules. Incretins (GLP-1, GIP) enhance secretion; epinephrine (α2) and somatostatin inhibit it.
s7211 **Mechanism of insulin action:** tyrosine kinase receptor → autophosphorylation → **IRS-1/2** → **PI3K → Akt (PKB)**: translocation of GLUT4 to the membrane (muscle, adipose tissue), inhibition of glycogen synthase kinase-3 (glycogen synthesis ↑), activation of phosphodiesterase 3B (cAMP ↓ — lipolysis ↓), mTOR (protein synthesis ↑), inactivation of FOXO1 (gluconeogenesis genes ↓); **Ras-MAPK** — growth and division.

s7224 **Diabetes mellitus** (topic 15): **type 1** — autoimmune destruction of β-cells (antibodies to GAD, insulin, IA-2), absolute insulin deficiency, tendency to ketoacidosis, low C-peptide; **type 2** — insulin resistance + relative deficiency, often with obesity, C-peptide normal or high; **gestational**, MODY (mutations in glucokinase and other genes) and other types. **Diagnostic criteria:** fasting glucose ≥ 7.0 mmol/l, ≥ 11.1 at 2 hours in the GTT, random ≥ 11.1 (with symptoms), HbA1c ≥ 6.5%.
s7225 **Acute complications:** diabetic ketoacidosis (type 1), hyperosmolar hyperglycemic state (type 2; glucose > 33 mmol/l, osmolality > 320, no ketosis — the small amount of insulin stops lipolysis), lactic acidosis (metformin, hypoxia), **hypoglycemia** (overdose of insulin or a sulfonylurea; < 3.9 mmol/l — the most dangerous for the brain).
s7226 **Age-related features:** in newborns the glycogen store is small and the gluconeogenic enzymes are immature — a tendency to hypoglycemia (especially in preterm infants and infants of diabetic mothers — fetal hyperinsulinism); type 1 diabetes is more common in children; in the elderly insulin sensitivity declines and muscle mass decreases — type 2 diabetes becomes more common.

s7229 Biosynthesis of thyroid hormones (drawn after Lippincott’s Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry)
s7230 **Synthesis:** follicular cells concentrate iodide from the blood 20–40 times through the **Na^+^/I^-^ symporter (NIS)**; **thyroperoxidase** (heme-containing, requires H2O2) oxidizes iodide and iodinates the tyrosine residues within **thyroglobulin** (mono- and diiodotyrosine), then condenses them — T4 and T3 are stored in the colloid as part of thyroglobulin. Under the action of TSH the hormones are released into the blood by endocytosis of colloid and lysosomal proteolysis (mainly T4, ≈ 80–90%). The daily iodine requirement is **150 µg** (250 µg in pregnancy and lactation).
