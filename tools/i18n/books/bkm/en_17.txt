s3206 **Energy cost:** 3 ATP for one urea (**4 high-energy bonds**, because one ATP is split into AMP + PPi).
s3207 **The link with the Krebs cycle (the “Krebs bicycle”):** the **fumarate** formed in the ornithine cycle is converted in the Krebs cycle into malate and oxaloacetate; oxaloacetate is transaminated back into **aspartate** and returns to the ornithine cycle. The NADH formed in the malate dehydrogenase reaction recovers part of the energy spent on urea synthesis.
s3208 Regulation:
s3209 **N-acetylglutamate** (acetyl-CoA + glutamate; its synthesis is activated by arginine) is the obligatory allosteric activator of carbamoyl phosphate synthetase I;
s3210 with a protein-rich diet and in starvation (when amino acid catabolism increases) the synthesis of the cycle enzymes is induced (glucagon, cortisol). **Normal values of urea:** in serum **2.5–8.3 mmol/l**; in the urine **20–35 g** (333–583 mmol) per day.

s3241 5. Metabolism of one-carbon groups. Serine and glycine
s3242 **Tetrahydrofolate (THF)** is the active form of folic acid (B9); it is formed by dihydrofolate reductase (NADPH). THF carries **one-carbon groups** (methyl −CH3, methylene =CH2, methenyl =CH−, formyl −CHO, formimino). The main sources of one-carbon groups are **serine** (serine + THF → glycine + N5,N10-methylene-THF; serine hydroxymethyltransferase, PLP) and **glycine** (the glycine cleavage system).
s3243 **One-carbon groups are used for:** the synthesis of purine nucleotides (atoms C2 and C8), the synthesis of **thymidylate** (dUMP → dTMP), the regeneration of methionine (homocysteine → methionine), and the interconversion of serine and glycine.
s3244 Syntheses in which **glycine** takes part: proteins (every third amino acid of collagen), heme (succinyl-CoA + glycine), creatine, purines, glutathione, conjugation of bile acids (glycocholic acid), detoxification of benzoic acid (hippuric acid).
s3245 **Sulfonamides** competitively inhibit the synthesis of folate from para-aminobenzoic acid in bacteria (humans obtain folate from food) — a bacteriostatic effect. **Methotrexate** inhibits dihydrofolate reductase — a lack of THF stops the synthesis of thymidylate and purines; rapidly dividing tumor cells die. **Folate deficiency** — megaloblastic anemia; in pregnancy — neural tube defects in the fetus (which is why folic acid is prescribed when a pregnancy is planned).
s3246 6. Metabolism of methionine. Transmethylation
s3247 **Methionine** is an essential amino acid and the main **donor of methyl groups** in the body. With ATP it forms **S-adenosylmethionine (SAM)**. SAM gives its methyl group to various acceptors — **transmethylation** (by methyltransferases):
s3248 noradrenaline → **adrenaline**;
s3249 guanidinoacetate → **creatine**;
s3250 phosphatidylethanolamine → **phosphatidylcholine** (choline);
s3251 **methylation** of nucleotides and DNA (epigenetic regulation of gene expression);
s3252 synthesis of carnitine and melatonin; detoxification of foreign substances and hormones (catecholamines — COMT). After giving up its methyl group, SAM is converted into **S-adenosylhomocysteine** and then into **homocysteine**. The fate of homocysteine:
s3253 **remethylation** — it returns to methionine: the methyl donor is N5-methyl-THF and the coenzyme is **methylcobalamin (B~12~)** (methionine synthase);
s3254 **transsulfuration** — with serine it forms cystathionine (cystathionine β-synthase, **B~6~**) and then **cysteine**; cysteine → taurine, glutathione, sulfates (PAPS).
s3255 **Hyperhomocysteinemia** (normal blood level 5–15 µmol/l) is seen in deficiency of B12, folate or B6, in renal failure or in congenital enzyme defects (cystathionine β-synthase — **homocystinuria**: dislocation of the lens, skeletal changes, mental retardation, early thrombosis). Homocysteine damages the endothelium and is an independent risk factor for **atherosclerosis and thrombosis**. In B12 deficiency N5-methyl-THF is not used and accumulates (the “folate trap”) — the other forms of THF fall and megaloblastic anemia develops.
s3256 7. Metabolism of phenylalanine and tyrosine
s3257 **Phenylalanine** (essential) is converted into **tyrosine** mainly in the liver by **phenylalanine hydroxylase** (coenzyme — **tetrahydrobiopterin, BH~4~**). From tyrosine are formed: the **catecholamines** (dopamine, noradrenaline, adrenaline), **melanin** (tyrosinase in melanocytes) and the **thyroid hormones** (T3, T4); the final catabolism of tyrosine leads through homogentisic acid to **fumarate** and **acetoacetate**.

s3277 Experiment 1: determination of urea in serum by the urease method
s3278 **Principle of the method: urease** breaks urea down into ammonia and CO2; the ammonia forms **blue indophenol** with phenol and hypochlorite (catalyzed by sodium nitroprusside) (the Berthelot reaction). The intensity of the color is proportional to the amount of urea (546–600 nm). Analyzers use a kinetic method: the ammonia is measured by the consumption of NADH in the glutamate dehydrogenase reaction (340 nm).
s3279 Pour 1 ml of urease-containing buffer solution into each of three test tubes.
s3280 Add 10 µl of serum to tube 1, 10 µl of standard (for example, 8.33 mmol/l) to tube 2 and 10 µl of water to tube 3; keep at 37 °C for 10 minutes.
s3281 Add the phenol-nitroprusside and alkaline hypochlorite reagents and keep at 37 °C for another 10 minutes.
s3282 Measure the optical density against the control on a photometer. **Calculation:** C (mmol/l) = (Esample / Estandard) × Cstandard
s3283 **Evaluation:** the normal value is 2.5–8.3 mmol/l. It is evaluated together with creatinine (kidney function).
s3284 Experiment 2: determination of phenylpyruvate in urine (Fölling test)
s3285 Pour 1 ml of fresh urine into a test tube and add 2–3 drops of 10% iron (III) chloride solution.
s3286 Observe the change of color within 30–60 s. **Result and analysis:** if phenylpyruvate is present, a **blue-green** color appears (it fades within a few minutes) — suspicion of phenylketonuria. False positives: some drugs (salicylates — violet), bilirubin, homogentisic acid (a transient blue color). The result is confirmed by measuring phenylalanine in the blood.
s3287 Experiment 3: determination of homogentisic acid in urine (demonstration)
s3288 Pour 2 ml of urine from a patient with alkaptonuria (or a solution of homogentisic acid) into each of two test tubes.
s3289 Add 1 ml of 10% NaOH to one of them and shake it in air. **Result and analysis:** in an alkaline medium homogentisic acid is oxidized quickly and gives a **black-brown** color; in the tube without alkali the urine darkens slowly over several hours in air.

s3371 A 3-day-old boy refused to breastfeed and developed vomiting, lethargy and seizures. Blood ammonia is 450 µmol/l (normal < 50), glutamine is increased, citrulline is low, and orotic acid is sharply increased in the urine.
s3372 Which enzyme is deficient? Why is orotic acid increased? State the principles of treatment.
s3373 Ornithine carbamoyltransferase deficiency (hyperammonemia type II; X-linked, which is why it is severe in a boy). Carbamoyl phosphate is not converted into citrulline (citrulline is low), accumulates in the mitochondria, leaves for the cytosol and enters pyrimidine synthesis, increasing orotic acid. Ammonia is not detoxified — in the brain α-ketoglutarate falls, glutamine accumulates (swelling) and encephalopathy develops. Treatment: temporarily stopping protein, glucose (to reduce catabolism), sodium benzoate and phenylbutyrate (which remove nitrogen as hippuric acid and phenylacetylglutamine), arginine and, in severe cases, hemodialysis; later a protein-restricted diet and liver transplantation.

s3376 Classic phenylketonuria — phenylalanine hydroxylase deficiency. Phenylalanine is not converted into tyrosine, accumulates and is transaminated into phenylpyruvate, phenyllactate and phenylacetate (a “mousy” odor in the urine). A high phenylalanine level blocks the transport of other large neutral amino acids (tyrosine, tryptophan) into the brain, myelination is impaired and the synthesis of neurotransmitters (dopamine, serotonin) falls — if untreated, severe mental retardation and seizures; melanin is low — the skin and hair are pale.
