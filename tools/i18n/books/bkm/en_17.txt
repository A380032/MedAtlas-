s3206 **The sources of the two nitrogens of the urea molecule:** the first is **ammonia** (from the deamination of glutamate and the glutaminase reaction), the second is **aspartate** (from the transamination of oxaloacetate). In this way the nitrogen of all amino acids is delivered to the cycle through transamination.
s3207 **Energy cost:** 3 ATP for one urea (**4 high-energy bonds**, because one ATP is split into AMP + PP~i~).
s3208 **The link with the Krebs cycle (the “Krebs bicycle”):** the **fumarate** formed in the ornithine cycle is converted in the Krebs cycle into malate and oxaloacetate; oxaloacetate is transaminated back into **aspartate** and returns to the ornithine cycle. The NADH formed in the malate dehydrogenase reaction recovers part of the energy spent on urea synthesis.

s3242 5. Metabolism of one-carbon groups. Serine and glycine
s3243 **Tetrahydrofolate (THF)** is the active form of folic acid (B~9~); it is formed by dihydrofolate reductase (NADPH). THF carries **one-carbon groups** (methyl −CH~3~, methylene =CH~2~, methenyl =CH−, formyl −CHO, formimino). The main sources of one-carbon groups are **serine** (serine + THF → glycine + N5,N10-methylene-THF; serine hydroxymethyltransferase, PLP) and **glycine** (the glycine cleavage system).
s3244 **One-carbon groups are used for:** the synthesis of purine nucleotides (atoms C2 and C8), the synthesis of **thymidylate** (dUMP → dTMP), the regeneration of methionine (homocysteine → methionine), and the interconversion of serine and glycine.
s3245 Syntheses in which **glycine** takes part: proteins (every third amino acid of collagen), heme (succinyl-CoA + glycine), creatine, purines, glutathione, conjugation of bile acids (glycocholic acid), detoxification of benzoic acid (hippuric acid).
s3246 **Sulfonamides** competitively inhibit the synthesis of folate from para-aminobenzoic acid in bacteria (humans obtain folate from food) — a bacteriostatic effect. **Methotrexate** inhibits dihydrofolate reductase — a lack of THF stops the synthesis of thymidylate and purines; rapidly dividing tumor cells die. **Folate deficiency** — megaloblastic anemia; in pregnancy — neural tube defects in the fetus (which is why folic acid is prescribed when a pregnancy is planned).
s3247 6. Metabolism of methionine. Transmethylation
s3248 **Methionine** is an essential amino acid and the main **donor of methyl groups** in the body. With ATP it forms **S-adenosylmethionine (SAM)**. SAM gives its methyl group to various acceptors — **transmethylation** (by methyltransferases):
s3249 noradrenaline → **adrenaline**;
s3250 guanidinoacetate → **creatine**;
s3251 phosphatidylethanolamine → **phosphatidylcholine** (choline);
s3252 **methylation** of nucleotides and DNA (epigenetic regulation of gene expression);
s3253 synthesis of carnitine and melatonin; detoxification of foreign substances and hormones (catecholamines — COMT). After giving up its methyl group, SAM is converted into **S-adenosylhomocysteine** and then into **homocysteine**. The fate of homocysteine:
s3254 **remethylation** — it returns to methionine: the methyl donor is N5-methyl-THF and the coenzyme is **methylcobalamin (B~12~)** (methionine synthase);
s3255 **transsulfuration** — with serine it forms cystathionine (cystathionine β-synthase, **B~6~**) and then **cysteine**; cysteine → taurine, glutathione, sulfates (PAPS).
s3256 **Hyperhomocysteinemia** (normal blood level 5–15 µmol/l) is seen in deficiency of B~12~, folate or B~6~, in renal failure or in congenital enzyme defects (cystathionine β-synthase — **homocystinuria**: dislocation of the lens, skeletal changes, mental retardation, early thrombosis). Homocysteine damages the endothelium and is an independent risk factor for **atherosclerosis and thrombosis**. In B~12~ deficiency N5-methyl-THF is not used and accumulates (the “folate trap”) — the other forms of THF fall and megaloblastic anemia develops.
s3257 7. Metabolism of phenylalanine and tyrosine
s3258 **Phenylalanine** (essential) is converted into **tyrosine** mainly in the liver by **phenylalanine hydroxylase** (coenzyme — **tetrahydrobiopterin, BH~4~**). s3278 Experiment 1: determination of urea in serum by the urease method
s3279 **Principle of the method: urease** breaks urea down into ammonia and CO~2~; the ammonia forms **blue indophenol** with phenol and hypochlorite (catalyzed by sodium nitroprusside) (the Berthelot reaction). The intensity of the color is proportional to the amount of urea (546–600 nm). Analyzers use a kinetic method: the ammonia is measured by the consumption of NADH in the glutamate dehydrogenase reaction (340 nm).
s3280 Pour 1 ml of urease-containing buffer solution into each of three test tubes.
s3281 Add 10 µl of serum to tube 1, 10 µl of standard (for example, 8.33 mmol/l) to tube 2 and 10 µl of water to tube 3; keep at 37 °C for 10 minutes.
s3282 Add the phenol-nitroprusside and alkaline hypochlorite reagents and keep at 37 °C for another 10 minutes.
s3283 Measure the optical density against the control on a photometer. **Calculation:** C (mmol/l) = (Esample / Estandard) × Cstandard
s3284 **Evaluation:** the normal value is 2.5–8.3 mmol/l. It is evaluated together with creatinine (kidney function).
s3285 Experiment 2: determination of phenylpyruvate in urine (Fölling test)
s3286 Pour 1 ml of fresh urine into a test tube and add 2–3 drops of 10% iron (III) chloride solution.
s3287 Observe the change of color within 30–60 s. **Result and analysis:** if phenylpyruvate is present, a **blue-green** color appears (it fades within a few minutes) — suspicion of phenylketonuria. False positives: some drugs (salicylates — violet), bilirubin, homogentisic acid (a transient blue color). The result is confirmed by measuring phenylalanine in the blood.
s3288 Experiment 3: determination of homogentisic acid in urine (demonstration)
s3289 Pour 2 ml of urine from a patient with alkaptonuria (or a solution of homogentisic acid) into each of two test tubes.
s3290 Add 1 ml of 10% NaOH to one of them and shake it in air. **Result and analysis:** in an alkaline medium homogentisic acid is oxidized quickly and gives a **black-brown** color; in the tube without alkali the urine darkens slowly over several hours in air.

s3372 A 3-day-old boy refused to breastfeed and developed vomiting, lethargy and seizures. Blood ammonia is 450 µmol/l (normal < 50), glutamine is increased, citrulline is low, and orotic acid is sharply increased in the urine.
s3373 Which enzyme is deficient? Why is orotic acid increased? State the principles of treatment.
s3374 Ornithine carbamoyltransferase deficiency (hyperammonemia type II; X-linked, which is why it is severe in a boy). Carbamoyl phosphate is not converted into citrulline (citrulline is low), accumulates in the mitochondria, leaves for the cytosol and enters pyrimidine synthesis, increasing orotic acid. Ammonia is not detoxified — in the brain α-ketoglutarate falls, glutamine accumulates (swelling) and encephalopathy develops. Treatment: temporarily stopping protein, glucose (to reduce catabolism), sodium benzoate and phenylbutyrate (which remove nitrogen as hippuric acid and phenylacetylglutamine), arginine and, in severe cases, hemodialysis; later a protein-restricted diet and liver transplantation.
s3375 Newborn screening found a blood phenylalanine level of 1200 µmol/l (normal < 120). Tyrosine is reduced.
s3376 What is the diagnosis? What are the consequences if it is not treated? What is the essence of the diet?
s3377 Classic phenylketonuria — phenylalanine hydroxylase deficiency. Phenylalanine is not converted into tyrosine, accumulates and is transaminated into phenylpyruvate, phenyllactate and phenylacetate (a “mousy” odor in the urine). A high phenylalanine level blocks the transport of other large neutral amino acids (tyrosine, tryptophan) into the brain, myelination is impaired and the synthesis of neurotransmitters (dopamine, serotonin) falls — if untreated, severe mental retardation and seizures; melanin is low — the skin and hair are pale. Treatment: from the first weeks of life a diet low in phenylalanine (special formulas) with tyrosine supplements; blood phenylalanine is monitored regularly.
