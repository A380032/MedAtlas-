s2199 A 50-year-old patient has yellow skin and sclerae, itching, colorless (acholic), shiny and bulky stools and dark urine. Ultrasound revealed a stone in the common bile duct. The clotting time is prolonged.
s2200 Why has the fat in the feces increased? Explain the clotting disorder.
s2201 Because the stone has blocked the bile duct, bile acids do not reach the intestine: fats are not emulsified, the surface on which lipase acts is small, and mixed micelles are not formed — the products of digestion are not absorbed and leave with the feces (steatorrhea). There are no bilirubin derivatives (stercobilin) in the feces — they are acholic. Fat-soluble vitamins are also not absorbed; because of vitamin K deficiency the γ-carboxylation of clotting factors II, VII, IX and X in the liver is impaired — the clotting time is prolonged. Vitamin K is given parenterally.
s2202 An 8-year-old child has recurrent attacks of severe abdominal pain (acute pancreatitis), yellow nodules on the skin and buttocks (eruptive xanthomas) and enlargement of the liver and spleen. The fasting serum is milky and, after being left in the refrigerator, formed a creamy layer on top; triglycerides are 25 mmol/l.
s2203 What is the disease? Explain its biochemical mechanism and the principle of treatment.
s2204 Type I hyperlipoproteinemia — deficiency of lipoprotein lipase (or of its activator apo C-II). The TAG of the chylomicrons is not hydrolyzed and the chylomicrons stay in the blood for a long time (even in the fasting state) — the serum is milky and in the refrigerator a “cream” layer floats to the top. Excess triglycerides are broken down by pancreatic lipase in the capillaries of the pancreas and form toxic fatty acids — pancreatitis. Treatment: sharp restriction of long-chain fats in the diet; medium-chain TAG are allowed, because they are absorbed into the portal vein without chylomicrons.
s2205 A 5-year-old child with cystic fibrosis is underweight and thin even though the appetite is good. The stools are bulky, fatty and strong-smelling; there is a lot of neutral fat in the feces and elastase-1 is sharply reduced. He sees poorly in the evening.
s2206 What is the cause of the steatorrhea? Explain the “night blindness.” What treatment is needed?
s2207 In cystic fibrosis a defect of the CFTR Cl- channel makes the pancreatic secretion thick and it blocks the ducts; the gland tissue undergoes fibrosis — lipase and the other enzymes do not reach the intestine (exocrine insufficiency). TAG is not broken down — there is a lot of neutral fat in the feces. Fat-soluble vitamins are not absorbed: vitamin A deficiency impairs rhodopsin synthesis and vision in the dark deteriorates. Treatment: pancreatic enzyme preparations (pancreatin) with meals, fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K), a high-calorie diet.

s2245 1. Storage and mobilization of fats
s2246 **Adipose tissue** is the body’s main energy store (15–25% of body weight in healthy adults). In adipocytes TAG is formed from two sources: from the fatty acids released from chylomicrons and VLDL by lipoprotein lipase, and from glucose (glucose → glycerol-3-phosphate, excess glucose → acetyl-CoA → fatty acids).
s2247 **TAG synthesis (lipogenesis)** — in the liver and adipose tissue:
s2248 glycerol-3-phosphate + 2 acyl-CoA → phosphatidic acid → (phosphatase) → diacylglycerol + acyl-CoA → **TAG**
s2249 In adipose tissue glycerol-3-phosphate is formed only from **glucose** (reduction of dihydroxyacetone phosphate), because adipocytes have almost no glycerol kinase. For this reason the accumulation of TAG in adipose tissue depends on the availability of glucose and insulin. The liver does have glycerol kinase.
s2250 **Lipolysis (mobilization of fats)** — the hydrolysis of TAG to glycerol and fatty acids in starvation, physical work and stress:
s2251 **Adipose triglyceride lipase (ATGL)**: TAG → diacylglycerol + FA;
s2252 **Hormone-sensitive lipase (HSL)**: DAG → monoacylglycerol + FA (the key, regulated enzyme);
s2253 **Monoacylglycerol lipase**: MAG → glycerol + FA.

s2265 2. β-oxidation of fatty acids
s2266 **β-oxidation** is the oxidation of fatty acids in the mitochondrial matrix with the successive removal of two-carbon fragments (acetyl-CoA) (F. Knoop, 1904). It takes place mainly in the heart, skeletal muscle, liver and kidney.
s2267 **1. Activation of the fatty acid** (in the cytosol, on the outer mitochondrial membrane):
s2268 Fatty acid + CoA-SH + ATP → Acyl-CoA + AMP + PPi (acyl-CoA synthetase; 2 high-energy bonds are used)
s2269 **2. Transport of the acyl residue into the mitochondria — the carnitine shuttle system.** The inner membrane is impermeable to acyl-CoA:
s2270 **carnitine palmitoyltransferase I (CPT-I)** (outer membrane) transfers the acyl group to carnitine → acylcarnitine; this is the **key (regulated) step of β-oxidation**;
s2271 **carnitine-acylcarnitine translocase** carries the acylcarnitine into the matrix;
s2272 **CPT-II** in the matrix transfers the acyl group back to CoA. CPT-I is inhibited by **malonyl-CoA**, the first product of fatty acid synthesis — so synthesis and breakdown do not occur at the same time.

s2288 The oxidation of **unsaturated fatty acids** needs additional enzymes (isomerase, reductase); each double bond means one FADH2 less. From **odd-chain fatty acids** propionyl-CoA is formed at the end → methylmalonyl-CoA → (B12) succinyl-CoA → it can enter gluconeogenesis. **Very long chain** fatty acids (C22 and longer) are first shortened in the **peroxisomes** (here H2O2 is formed instead of FADH2).
s2289 **Carnitine or CPT deficiency** and **medium-chain acyl-CoA dehydrogenase (MCAD) deficiency**: during starvation or illness fatty acids are not oxidized — **hypoketotic hypoglycemia** (ketone bodies are not formed and there is not enough ATP for gluconeogenesis), muscle weakness, cardiomyopathy and liver damage; one of the causes of sudden infant death syndrome. Treatment: avoiding long fasts, frequent carbohydrate feeding, carnitine preparations.
s2290 3. Biosynthesis of fatty acids
s2291 Fatty acids are synthesized in the **cytosol**, mainly in the **liver**, **adipose tissue** and the **lactating mammary gland**; the source is **acetyl-CoA** formed from excess carbohydrates. Synthesis increases under the action of **insulin**, after a carbohydrate-rich meal.
s2292 **1. Export of acetyl-CoA from the mitochondria to the cytosol (the citrate shuttle).** Acetyl-CoA does not cross the mitochondrial membrane: it forms citrate with oxaloacetate, citrate leaves for the cytosol and is split again into acetyl-CoA and oxaloacetate by **citrate lyase** (ATP). Oxaloacetate is converted into malate and then, by **malic enzyme**, into pyruvate, and **NADPH** is formed.
s2293 **2. Carboxylation of acetyl-CoA** (the key reaction):
s2294 Acetyl-CoA + CO2 + ATP → **Malonyl-CoA** + ADP + Pi — **acetyl-CoA carboxylase** (coenzyme — **biotin**)
s2295 **3. Fatty acid synthase** — a multienzyme complex (a dimer with 7 active sites in each subunit and an acyl-carrier part — the **pantetheine group** (B5)). In each cycle 2 carbons are added from malonyl-CoA (CO2 is released); **2 NADPH** are used for the reduction. In 7 cycles **palmitic acid** (C16:0) is formed.

s2297 8 Acetyl-CoA + 7 ATP + 14 NADPH + 14 H+ → Palmitate + 8 CoA-SH + 7 ADP + 7 Pi + 14 NADP+ + 6 H2O
