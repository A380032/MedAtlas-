s5898 A 45-year-old sailor returning from a long sea voyage (for several months he ate mainly dried bread and canned food) complains of swollen, bleeding gums, loose teeth and small hemorrhages on his legs. An old wound scar has reopened.
s5899 What is the diagnosis? Explain the biochemical mechanism of the symptoms.
s5900 Scurvy (vitamin C deficiency). Ascorbic acid is the cofactor of prolyl and lysyl hydroxylases: it keeps the iron in the enzyme in the Fe2+ state. When it is deficient, proline and lysine are not hydroxylated; without hydroxyproline the triple helix is not stabilized by hydrogen bonds and is degraded in the cell, and without hydroxylysine cross-links are not formed. Collagen is scarce and fragile: capillary walls weaken (hemorrhages, bleeding gums), the periodontal ligaments break down (loose teeth), and because scar collagen is constantly renewed, old scars reopen. Treatment — ascorbic acid, fresh fruit and vegetables.
s5901 A 3-year-old child breaks bones after minor falls (6 times in two years). The sclerae are blue, and the teeth are translucent and yellowish. One of the parents also has blue sclerae and reduced hearing.
s5902 What is the disease? What is its molecular basis? Why does a single mutant allele cause the disease?
s5903 Osteogenesis imperfecta (autosomal dominant). A mutation in one of the type I collagen genes (COL1A1 or COL1A2) — most often substitution of glycine in the triple helix by a larger amino acid: such a chain cannot pack tightly into the triple helix. Because the triple helix consists of three chains, the mutant chain combines with normal chains and makes the whole molecule defective (a dominant-negative effect) — most of the molecules are abnormal and are degraded. As a result, bone, the sclera (it thins and the vascular layer shows through — blue color), dentin and the auditory ossicles are weakened.
s5904 A 2-year-old child has coarse facial features, corneal clouding, an enlarged liver and spleen, delayed mental development and spinal deformity. The urine contains a lot of dermatan sulfate and heparan sulfate.
s5905 What is the disease? What is its biochemical basis? What treatment is possible?
s5906 Mucopolysaccharidosis type I — Hurler syndrome. The lysosomal enzyme α-L-iduronidase is deficient: iduronic acid residues at the ends of dermatan sulfate and heparan sulfate chains are not removed, the GAGs accumulate in lysosomes (cells enlarge, organ function is impaired), and the excess is excreted in the urine. Accumulation in cartilage, bone (skeletal deformities), the cornea (clouding), liver, spleen and brain (intellectual disability) produces the symptoms. Treatment: enzyme replacement therapy with recombinant α-L-iduronidase (it does not reach the brain), hematopoietic stem cell transplantation at an early age; prenatal diagnosis and genetic counseling (autosomal recessive).

s5966 2. Mechanism of muscle contraction and relaxation
s5967 **Sliding filament theory** (Huxley): during contraction the length of the filaments does not change — the thin (actin) filaments slide between the thick (myosin) filaments, and the sarcomere shortens.
s5968 Figure 23.1
s5969 Biochemical cycle of skeletal muscle contraction and relaxation (drawn after Severin E.S.)
s5970 **Excitation–contraction coupling:** the action potential spreads through the T tubules → the **dihydropyridine receptor** (voltage-sensitive) opens the **ryanodine receptor** → **Ca^2+^** is released from the sarcoplasmic reticulum (10-7 → 10-5 M) → Ca2+ binds to **troponin C** → tropomyosin shifts → the myosin heads bind to actin.
s5971 **Cross-bridge cycle:** myosin–ADP–Pi binds to actin → Pi is released and the “power stroke” occurs (the filament moves ≈ 10 nm) → ADP is released → a new **ATP** binds and detaches myosin from actin → ATP hydrolysis returns the myosin head to its initial position. Without ATP, myosin remains bound to actin — **rigor mortis**.
s5972 **Relaxation: Ca^2+^-ATPase (SERCA)** returns Ca2+ to the sarcoplasmic reticulum (ATP is consumed); Ca2+ dissociates from troponin C, and tropomyosin covers the binding sites. Thus, ATP is required for both contraction and relaxation.
s5973 **Smooth muscle:** there is no troponin; Ca2+ combines with **calmodulin** and activates **myosin light chain kinase** — myosin is phosphorylated and interacts with actin. Relaxation — myosin phosphatase; **NO** → cGMP → PKG causes relaxation (vasodilation).

s6000 **Creatine phosphate shuttle:** mitochondrial creatine kinase forms creatine phosphate using ATP from oxidative phosphorylation; it diffuses through the cytosol to the myofibrils, where creatine kinase regenerates ATP — an energy carrier.
s6001 **Creatine synthesis:** 1) in the kidney: arginine + glycine → guanidinoacetate (amidinotransferase); 2) in the liver: guanidinoacetate + S-adenosylmethionine → **creatine** (methyltransferase). s6002 **Types of muscle fibers: type I (red, slow)** — rich in myoglobin and mitochondria, aerobic, fatigue-resistant (postural muscles, marathon runners); **type II (white, fast)** — rich in glycogen and glycolytic enzymes, anaerobic, fatigue quickly (sprinters).
s6003 Causes of **fatigue**: depletion of creatine phosphate and glycogen, accumulation of lactate and H+ (acidosis weakens the binding of Ca2+ to troponin and PFK-1), accumulation of Pi.
s6004 5. Biochemical changes in muscular dystrophy and denervation
s6005 **Duchenne muscular dystrophy** — X-linked recessive; a deletion or frameshift in the **dystrophin** gene (the largest gene, ≈ 2.4 million base pairs) — dystrophin is absent. The sarcolemma tears during contraction, Ca2+ enters and activates proteases, and the muscle fibers undergo necrosis and are replaced by fat and connective tissue. Signs at 3–5 years: weakness of proximal muscles, the **Gowers sign**, pseudohypertrophy of the calf muscles; respiratory and heart failure at about 20 years. **Becker dystrophy** — in-frame mutations, partially functional dystrophin, a milder course.
s6006 **Biochemical signs of dystrophies:** serum **CK (MM) is sharply increased** (10–100 times, even before clinical signs), ALT, AST, LDH, aldolase and myoglobin are increased; muscle does not retain creatine — urinary **creatinuria** increases and **creatinine** excretion decreases (muscle mass is reduced); in muscle, myofibrillar proteins decrease and stromal (collagen) proteins increase; ATP and creatine phosphate decrease.
s6007 **Denervation** (nerve injury, poliomyelitis, amyotrophic lateral sclerosis): muscle atrophy, decreased protein synthesis, enhanced proteolysis; CK is usually slightly increased; creatinuria; acetylcholine receptors spread over the entire sarcolemma.
s6008 **Other myopathies: McArdle disease** (glycogenosis type V — muscle glycogen phosphorylase is absent; pain and cramps on exertion, lactate does not rise), **carnitine and carnitine palmitoyltransferase** deficiency (myoglobinuria on prolonged exertion or fasting), mitochondrial myopathies (lactic acidosis), **statin myopathy**, **rhabdomyolysis** (trauma — “crush syndrome”, heavy exertion, alcohol, drugs): CK > 10,000 U/l, myoglobinuria (brown urine), hyperkalemia, acute kidney injury. **Myasthenia gravis** — autoantibodies against acetylcholine receptors.
s6009 6. Metabolism and secretory function of the heart muscle
s6010 **Myocardial metabolism:** the heart works continuously and consumes ≈ 6 kg of ATP per day (the ATP reserve lasts a few seconds). Mitochondria occupy ≈ 35% of the cell volume; > 95% of ATP comes from **oxidative phosphorylation**. Energy sources: **fatty acids** (60–80% at rest), glucose, **lactate** (during exercise — to pyruvate via LDH-1), ketone bodies (in fasting and diabetes). In oxygen deficiency (ischemia), β-oxidation stops and anaerobic glycolysis cannot meet the ATP demand — lactate and H+ accumulate (anginal pain), ATP is depleted and the membrane pumps fail, and Ca2+ overload and free radicals (on reperfusion) lead to cardiomyocyte necrosis.
s6011 **Regulation of myocardial contraction:** β1-adrenoceptors → cAMP → PKA: L-type Ca2+
s6012 channels and **phospholamban** are phosphorylated (inhibition of SERCA is removed — rapid relaxation), and troponin I is phosphorylated. **Cardiac glycosides** (digoxin) inhibit Na+,K+-ATPase → Na+ ↑ in the cell → Na+/Ca2+ exchange decreases → Ca2+ ↑ → contraction is enhanced.
s6013 **Endocrine function — natriuretic peptides: atrial natriuretic peptide (ANP)** is released when the atria are stretched, and **brain natriuretic peptide (BNP)** mainly when the ventricular walls are stretched. Action: guanylate cyclase receptor → cGMP; natriuresis and diuresis, vasodilation, decreased secretion of renin, aldosterone and vasopressin — lowering blood volume and pressure. **BNP and NT-proBNP** are the main laboratory markers of **heart failure** (diagnosis, severity and prognosis): NT-proBNP < 125 pg/ml practically excludes (chronic) heart failure.
