s6037 Experiment 1: determination of creatine kinase activity in blood serum (“Biotest” kit, kinetic method)
s6038 **Principle of the method (coupled reactions):** creatine phosphate + ADP → (CK) → creatine + ATP; ATP + glucose → (hexokinase) → ADP + glucose-6-phosphate; glucose-6-phosphate + NADP+ → (G6PD) → 6-phosphogluconate + NADPH. The rate of NADPH formation at 340 nm is proportional to CK activity. N-acetylcysteine activates CK, while AMP and diadenosine pentaphosphate inhibit erythrocyte myokinase.
s6039 Warm the working reagent to 37 °C; add 1 ml of reagent and 20 µl of serum to the cuvette and mix.
s6040 After 2 minutes of incubation record the absorbance at 340 nm, then measure every minute for 3 minutes.
s6041 Calculate the mean ΔE/min. **Calculation:** activity (U/l) = ΔE/min × 8095 (based on the millimolar extinction coefficient of NADPH, 6.22, and the sample dilution).
s6042 **Interpretation:** < 190 U/l in men, < 170 U/l in women. Hemolysis falsely increases the result (adenylate kinase and ATP from erythrocytes). Physiological increases occur after intramuscular injections and heavy physical exertion.
s6043 Experiment 2: determination of CK-MB by the immunoinhibition method (demonstration)
s6044 **Principle of the method:** antibodies against the M subunit completely inhibit CK-MM and half of CK-MB (its M part); the remaining activity (B subunit) is multiplied by 2.
s6045 Incubate the serum with the antibody-containing reagent.
s6046 Measure the residual activity as in Experiment 1; × 2. **Results and analysis:** CK-MB > 25 U/l and > 6% of total CK are characteristic of myocardial injury; today this method has been replaced by troponin determination.

s6130 A 58-year-old man presented with severe crushing chest pain lasting for 2 hours. On arrival high-sensitivity troponin T was 18 ng/l (normal < 14), and 74 ng/l 1 hour later; myoglobin was increased; CK-MB 28 U/l.
s6131 What is the diagnosis? Explain the dynamics of the markers. Why do myocardial cells die?
s6132 Acute myocardial infarction: troponin above the 99th percentile with a sharp rise within 1 hour (dynamics) together with clinical signs of ischemia. Myoglobin is a small molecule and is released the earliest, but it is also present in skeletal muscle; CK-MB rises at 3–6 hours; troponin becomes detectable at 1–3 hours and persists for 7–14 days (the sarcomere-bound fraction is released slowly). Coronary thrombosis cuts off oxygen: β-oxidation and oxidative phosphorylation stop, and anaerobic glycolysis cannot meet the ATP demand; lactate and H+ accumulate; Na+,K+-ATPase and Ca2+-ATPases fail, Na+ and Ca2+ increase inside the cell, phospholipases and proteases are activated, and the membranes are destroyed — necrosis, and the enzymes are released into the blood.
s6133 A 5-year-old boy tires quickly, has difficulty climbing stairs and pushes on his legs with his hands when getting up from the floor; his calf muscles are enlarged. Serum CK 15,000 U/l, ALT and AST increased.
s6134 What is the disease? Explain the biochemical changes. How can the diagnosis be confirmed?
s6135 Duchenne muscular dystrophy (X-linked recessive). Because dystrophin is absent, the cytoskeleton is not linked to the sarcolemma and the extracellular matrix; the membrane tears during contraction, Ca2+ enters and activates calpains, and the fibers undergo necrosis — CK (MM), aminotransferases, LDH and aldolase are released into the blood (the increase in ALT/AST must not be mistaken for liver disease). The enlarged calves are a pseudohypertrophy due to fat and connective tissue; the way of getting up from the floor is the Gowers sign. Confirmation: molecular analysis of the DMD gene (deletions — MLPA, sequencing), immunohistochemical demonstration of the absence of dystrophin in a muscle biopsy.
s6136 A 25-year-old man presented after a marathon with severe muscle pain and weakness; his urine is dark brown. CK 85,000 U/l, potassium 6.2 mmol/l, creatinine 280 µmol/l. The urine test strip is positive for “blood”, but there are no erythrocytes on microscopy.
s6137 What condition is this? Explain the laboratory results. Why are the kidneys damaged?
s6138 Rhabdomyolysis after heavy physical exertion. As a result of ATP depletion and Ca2+ accumulation in muscle cells, the fibers break down and CK, myoglobin and K+ are released into the blood (hyperkalemia — dangerous for the heart). The test strip detects the peroxidase activity of heme — myoglobin also gives a positive result, while erythrocytes are absent (myoglobinuria). Myoglobin is filtered in the glomeruli, precipitates in the tubules in acidic urine and causes oxidative injury through heme iron; together with dehydration and vasoconstriction this leads to acute kidney injury (creatinine increased). Treatment: abundant intravenous fluids, correction of hyperkalemia, alkalinization of the urine, dialysis if necessary.

s6175 1. Saliva
s6176 Saliva consists of the secretions of three pairs of major glands (parotid — serous; submandibular and sublingual — mixed) and many minor glands; 1–1.5 l is secreted per day; pH 6.4–7.4.
s6177 **Composition:** water (≈ 99.4%); organic substances — **mucin** (a glycoprotein that makes the food bolus slippery), **α-amylase** (ptyalin — breaks down starch into dextrins; activated by Cl-), lingual lipase (important in infants), **lysozyme** (breaks down the bacterial wall), lactoferrin, **secretory IgA**, histatins, kallikrein, growth factors (epidermal growth factor); inorganic — Na+, K+ (more than in plasma), Ca2+, phosphates, HCO~3~^−^, Cl-, **thiocyanates** (rhodanides; abundant in smokers), F-.
s6178 **Functions:** digestive (the start of carbohydrate digestion), protective (antibacterial, buffering, remineralization of enamel — saturated with Ca2+ and phosphate), moistening and swallowing of food, taste perception (dissolves taste substances), excretory (urea, drugs, heavy metals).
s6179 Reduced salivary secretion (xerostomia — Sjögren syndrome, radiation, anticholinergic drugs) increases dental caries and oral infections. Caries — **lactic acid**, formed when bacteria (Streptococcus mutans) ferment sucrose, dissolves the hydroxyapatite of enamel; fluoride forms the more resistant **fluorapatite** in enamel.
s6180 2. Taste perception. Regulation of appetite and hunger
s6181 **Biochemical basis of taste perception:** there are five basic tastes. **Sweet** (T1R2/T1R3), **umami** (T1R1/T1R3 — L-glutamate) and **bitter** (the T2R family, ≈ 25 types) are G protein-coupled receptors; the G protein **gustducin** → phospholipase Cβ2 → IP3 → Ca2+ → TRPM5 channel → depolarization. **Salty** — direct entry of Na+ through ENaC sodium channels; **sour** — H+ ions (the OTOP1 proton channel). Zinc (gustin) is required for taste perception — in Zn deficiency taste is impaired (hypogeusia).
s6182 **Regulation of appetite and hunger** — two groups of neurons in the **arcuate nucleus** of the hypothalamus:
s6183 **orexigenic** (increase appetite): **neuropeptide Y** and AgRP;
s6184 **anorexigenic** (satiety): **POMC → α-MSH** (melanocortin-4 receptor) and CART.
