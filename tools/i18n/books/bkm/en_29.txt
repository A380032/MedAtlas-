s5668 **hydroxylysine** — for glycosylation and cross-links;
s5669 **no tryptophan**, little cysteine and tyrosine — collagen is not a nutritionally complete protein.
s5670 Structure:
s5671 **primary** — (Gly–X–Y)n;
s5672 **secondary** — each α-chain is a left-handed **polyproline helix** (not an α-helix; the proline ring does not allow an α-helix);
s5673 **tertiary** — three α-chains wind around one another to form a right-handed **triple helix** (superhelix) — **tropocollagen** (length 300 nm, diameter 1.5 nm); hydrogen bonds (with the participation of the OH groups of hydroxyproline) stabilize it;
s5674 **quaternary** — tropocollagen molecules are arranged in a staggered manner (shifted by 1/4 of their length) to form **fibrils** (periodic cross-striations of 67 nm in the electron microscope); fibrils combine into fibers.

s5689 3. Collagen synthesis
s5690 Figure 22.1
s5691 Stages of collagen biosynthesis (drawn after Severin E.S.)
s5692 Intracellular stages (fibroblast):
s5693 Synthesis of the **preprocollagen** α-chains in the rough endoplasmic reticulum; the signal peptide is cleaved off.
s5694 **Hydroxylation** — **prolyl 4-hydroxylase**, prolyl 3-hydroxylase and **lysyl hydroxylase**; cofactors: **Fe^2+^, O~2~, α-ketoglutarate and ascorbic acid** (keeps the iron in the reduced state). Hydroxylation is possible only until the helix is formed.
s5695 **Glycosylation** of hydroxylysine residues (galactose, glucosyl-galactose).
s5696 The C-propeptides join the three chains by disulfide bonds — the **triple helix** forms like a “zipper” (from the C-end to the N-end) — **procollagen**.
s5697 **Secretion** via the Golgi apparatus. **Extracellular stages:**
s5698 **Procollagen N- and C-peptidases** cleave off the propeptides → **tropocollagen** (the propeptides enter the blood — **markers of collagen synthesis**: PINP, PICP).
s5699 The molecules assemble spontaneously into fibrils.
s5700 **Lysyl oxidase** (a copper-containing enzyme; cofactor — Cu2+) oxidizes the ε-amino groups of lysine and hydroxylysine to aldehydes (allysine); the aldehydes form **cross-links** with amino groups on neighboring molecules — the fibers are strengthened. With age the number of cross-links increases (skin elasticity decreases).
s5701 **Scurvy (vitamin C deficiency)** — prolyl and lysyl hydroxylases do not work; unhydroxylated chains do not form a stable triple helix and are degraded in the cell. Collagen is scarce and fragile: swollen and bleeding gums, loosening and loss of teeth, perifollicular hemorrhages, slow wound healing, reopening of old scars, bone fragility (Möller–Barlow disease in children). Treatment — ascorbic acid.
s5702 **Regulation:** collagen synthesis is enhanced by **TGF-β**, insulin, somatotropin (IGF-1) and ascorbate; **glucocorticoids** reduce it (with long-term use the skin thins, wounds heal slowly, osteoporosis develops). Factors that chronically activate fibroblasts cause **fibrosis** (liver cirrhosis, pulmonary fibrosis, keloid).
s5703 4. Breakdown and turnover of collagen
s5704 Collagen turns over slowly (half-life from weeks to years), faster in bone and during growth. **Collagenases** (matrix metalloproteinases — MMP-1, MMP-8, MMP-13; Zn2+-containing) cut the triple helix at a specific site (into 3/4 and 1/4 fragments); then gelatinases (MMP-2, MMP-9) and lysosomal cathepsins break it down. MMPs are regulated by tissue inhibitors (TIMPs).
s5705 Because **hydroxyproline** is found almost only in collagen and is not reused, its **amount in the urine** is an indicator of collagen breakdown: normal in adults 15–50 mg/day (more in children because of growth). **It increases** with enhanced bone resorption (hyperparathyroidism, Paget disease, bone metastases, osteoporosis), collagenoses (rheumatoid arthritis, systemic lupus erythematosus, scleroderma), burns, thyrotoxicosis. **It decreases** in hypothyroidism, scurvy and hypopituitarism. Modern markers of bone resorption — **C-telopeptide (CTX)**, deoxypyridinoline.

s5734 6. Elastin
s5735 **Elastin** is the protein that gives elasticity to the large vessels (aorta), lungs, skin and ligaments (ligamentum flavum); it stretches and then returns to its original state.
s5736 It contains a lot of glycine, alanine, valine and proline (hydrophobic); little hydroxyproline, no hydroxylysine and no carbohydrates.
s5737 The monomer is **tropoelastin**; outside the cell it assembles on **fibrillin** microfibrils.
s5738 **Lysyl oxidase** forms from lysine residues the characteristic cross-links that join four lysine residues — **desmosine and isodesmosine** — a three-dimensional network of randomly coiled chains (“rubber”).
s5739 It turns over very slowly (over a lifetime); it is broken down by **neutrophil elastase**; α1-antitrypsin inhibits elastase.
s5740 **α~1~-Antitrypsin deficiency** — in the lungs neutrophil elastase acts uncontrolled and breaks down the elastin in the alveolar walls — early **panacinar emphysema** (smoking oxidizes and inactivates α1-antitrypsin and sharply increases the risk); in the PiZZ variant the misfolded protein accumulates in hepatocytes and leads to liver cirrhosis. **Marfan syndrome** — mutation of the **fibrillin-1** gene: tall stature, long fingers (arachnodactyly), dislocation of the lens, aortic aneurysm and dissection.
s5741 7. Glycosaminoglycans and proteoglycans
s5742 **Glycosaminoglycans (GAGs)** are long unbranched polysaccharides made of repeating disaccharide units: a **uronic acid** (glucuronic or iduronic) + an **amino sugar** (N-acetylglucosamine or N-acetylgalactosamine); most of them are sulfated. They carry many negative charges (–COO-, –SO~3~^−^) — they strongly bind Na+ and **water** and form a gel.

s5767 **Biological functions:** supportive (resistance of cartilage to compression), protective (a barrier to the spread of bacteria — hyaluronic acid; bacterial **hyaluronidase** is a “spreading factor”), mechanical (lubrication of joints), binding (ordering of collagen fibrils — decorin), ion exchange (binding of Na+, K+, Ca2+ — bone mineralization), filtration (heparan sulfate in the glomerular basement membrane repels albumin by its negative charge), signaling (binding of growth factors).
s5768 **Synthesis:** the core protein is synthesized in the RER; in the Golgi apparatus the chains are elongated from UDP-sugars (UDP-glucuronic acid, UDP-N-acetylglucosamine) by **glycosyltransferases**; the sulfate donor is **PAPS** (phosphoadenosine phosphosulfate). Hyaluronic acid is synthesized at the plasma membrane.
s5769 **Breakdown:** in lysosomes, exoglycosidases and sulfatases degrade them step by step.
s5770 **Mucopolysaccharidoses** are hereditary defects of lysosomal enzymes: incompletely degraded GAGs accumulate in tissues and are excreted in the urine. **Hurler syndrome** (α-L-iduronidase; autosomal recessive) — coarse facial features (“gargoylism”), corneal clouding, intellectual disability, enlarged liver and spleen, skeletal deformities. **Hunter syndrome** (iduronate-2-sulfatase; X-linked) — the cornea is clear. **Sanfilippo syndrome** — mainly severe neurological disorders. Diagnosis — increased dermatan and heparan sulfate in the urine, determination of the enzyme in leukocytes; treatment — enzyme replacement therapy, stem cell transplantation.
s5771 **Use of GAGs in medicine:** heparin (anticoagulant), chondroitin sulfate and glucosamine (chondroprotectors in osteoarthritis), hyaluronic acid (intra-articular — “liquid prosthesis”, ophthalmic surgery, cosmetology), hyaluronidase (softening of scars, enhancing the spread of drugs).
s5772 8. Adhesive and antiadhesive proteins
s5773 **Fibronectin** is a dimeric glycoprotein; it binds collagen, heparin, fibrin and cell **integrins** (through the RGD — Arg-Gly-Asp sequence); cell migration (embryogenesis, wound healing), opsonin.
s5774 **Laminin** is the main glycoprotein of the basement membrane (cross-shaped); it binds type IV collagen, nidogen and cells.
s5775 **Integrins** are receptors for matrix proteins (α and β subunits); they link the matrix to the cytoskeleton and transmit signals.
s5776 **Cadherins** (Ca2+-dependent), selectins and adhesion molecules of the immunoglobulin family (ICAM, VCAM) — cell–cell adhesion; attachment of leukocytes to the endothelium.
s5777 **Antiadhesive proteins** — **tenascin**, **thrombospondin**, **SPARC (osteonectin)** — enable cells to detach from the matrix and migrate (embryogenesis, wound healing, tumor invasion).
s5778 Experiment 1: determination of hydroxyproline in urine
s5779 **Principle of the method:** urine is subjected to acid hydrolysis to convert bound hydroxyproline into the free form; it is oxidized by chloramine T to form a pyrrole, which gives a pink-red color with **Ehrlich's reagent** (p-dimethylaminobenzaldehyde) (558 nm).
s5780 Add 1 ml of concentrated HCl to 1 ml of daily urine, hydrolyze at 110 °C for 3 hours and neutralize.
s5781 Add chloramine T solution to the hydrolysate and keep for 20 minutes at room temperature.
s5782 Add Ehrlich's reagent, heat at 60 °C for 15 minutes, cool and measure at 558 nm.
s5783 Calculate by comparison with a standard hydroxyproline solution.
s5784 **Interpretation:** 15–50 mg/day in adults. An increase indicates enhanced bone resorption and collagen breakdown (hyperparathyroidism, metastases, collagenoses, burns). Collagen-rich foods (jelly, aspic) should not be eaten for 2 days before the test.
s5785 Experiment 2: determination of sialic acids in blood serum (Hess method)
s5786 **Principle of the method:** sialic (N-acetylneuraminic) acids are the terminal residues of the oligosaccharide chains of glycoproteins and GAGs; after the serum proteins are precipitated, the sialic acids in the hydrolysate give a colored compound when heated with a mixture of acetic and sulfuric acids.
s5787 Add 1 ml of 5% trichloroacetic acid to 0.1 ml of serum, boil for 5 minutes and centrifuge.
s5788 Add 5 ml of Hess reagent to 0.4 ml of supernatant and keep in a boiling water bath for 30 minutes.
s5789 Cool and measure at 540 nm against the blank; find the amount from the calibration graph. **Interpretation:** normal 2.0–2.33 mmol/l (≈ 180–200 conventional units). An increase is seen in rheumatic fever, rheumatoid arthritis, collagenoses, acute inflammation and tumors (a nonspecific indicator of inflammatory activity).
