s4198 1. The genetic code and its properties
s4199 The **genetic code** is the system that matches the sequence of mRNA nucleotides to the sequence of amino acids in a protein. The 4 kinds of nucleotides form 64 **codons** (triplets): 61 of them code for amino acids and 3 are **stop codons** (UAA, UAG, UGA). **AUG** is the start codon (methionine).

s4218 2. Components of the protein-synthesizing system
s4219 **Amino acids** (20 standard ones; selenocysteine — the UGA codon in a special context).
s4220 **mRNA** — the template.
s4221 **tRNA** — the “adapter”: a “cloverleaf” secondary structure and an L-shaped tertiary structure; the **anticodon loop** (recognizes the codon) and the **CCA at the 3’-end** (the amino acid is attached).
s4222 **Aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases** — a separate enzyme for each amino acid; they activate the amino acid and attach it to the corresponding tRNA (2 high-energy bonds are consumed). They are the **main guarantee of the fidelity** of translation — they hydrolyze a wrongly attached amino acid (proofreading).

s4225 **Energy sources:** ATP (activation), GTP (initiation, elongation, termination).

s4240 3. Stages of translation
s4241 Figure 17.1
s4242 Stages of translation (drawn after Severin E.S.)
s4243 **Activation of amino acids:** amino acid + ATP → aminoacyl-AMP + PPi; aminoacyl-AMP + tRNA → aminoacyl-tRNA + AMP.
s4244 **Initiation (in eukaryotes):** the 40S subunit, the initiator **Met-tRNA~i~^Met^**, eIF2-GTP and other factors bind together to the **5’-cap of the mRNA** (eIF4E recognizes the cap) and move along the mRNA to the first **AUG** codon (**scanning**, the Kozak sequence). GTP is hydrolyzed and the 60S subunit joins — the **80S** initiation complex is formed, with Met-tRNAi in the **P site**. In prokaryotes the initiator is **formylmethionine-tRNA**, and 16S rRNA recognizes the **Shine–Dalgarno** sequence in the mRNA.
s4245 **Elongation** — three steps repeated for each codon:
s4246 The next aminoacyl-tRNA arrives at the **A site** with eEF1α-GTP (if the anticodon matches the codon, GTP is hydrolyzed).
s4247 **Peptidyl transferase** transfers the peptide in the P site to the amino group of the amino acid in the A site — a peptide bond is formed. This activity belongs to the **28S rRNA** of the 60S subunit (a **ribozyme**; in prokaryotes, 23S rRNA).
s4248 **Translocation** — under the action of eEF2-GTP the ribosome moves one codon along the mRNA; the peptidyl-tRNA moves to the P site, and the empty tRNA moves to the E site and is released. **4 high-energy bonds** are consumed for each peptide bond (2 in activation, 2 GTP in elongation). Many ribosomes read one mRNA at the same time — a **polysome**.
s4249 **Termination:** when a stop codon enters the A site, **release factors** (eRF1, eRF3-GTP) bind instead of a tRNA; peptidyl transferase transfers the peptide to water (hydrolysis), the polypeptide is released and the ribosome dissociates into subunits.
s4250 4. Post-translational modification and protein targeting
s4251 **Folding:** a polypeptide often reaches its correct conformation spontaneously; **chaperones** (heat shock proteins — Hsp70, Hsp60/chaperonins) prevent misfolding and aggregation. Misfolded proteins are degraded by the **ubiquitin–proteasome** system. Folding disorders — **prion diseases**, Alzheimer disease (β-amyloid), Parkinson disease (α-synuclein), cystic fibrosis (CFTR ΔF508).
s4252 **Targeting:** secretory and membrane proteins are directed to the **rough endoplasmic reticulum** by an N-terminal **signal peptide** (SRP — signal recognition particle) and are then sorted in the Golgi apparatus. Lysosomal enzymes are directed to lysosomes by the **mannose-6-phosphate** tag (I-cell disease — absence of this tag). Mitochondrial and nuclear proteins have special signal sequences.

s4299 The **selective action** of antibiotics is based on the difference in structure between prokaryotic (70S) and eukaryotic (80S) ribosomes. Because mitochondria contain 70S-type ribosomes, chloramphenicol can damage the bone marrow (aplastic anemia; in newborns, the “gray baby syndrome” — due to insufficient glucuronidation).
s4300 6. Regulation of gene expression in prokaryotes
s4301 An **operon** (Jacob and Monod, 1961) is a group of structural genes under the control of one promoter and operator that is transcribed as a single polycistronic mRNA.
s4302 Figure 17.2
s4303 Regulation of the lactose operon — induction (drawn after Lippincott’s Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry)
s4304 **Lactose operon (inducible):** structural genes — lacZ (β-galactosidase), lacY (permease), lacA (transacetylase). In the absence of lactose the **repressor** binds to the operator and blocks transcription. Lactose (more precisely, allolactose — the **inducer**) binds to the repressor and releases it from the operator — the genes are expressed. **Positive control:** when glucose is low, cAMP increases, and the **cAMP–CAP** complex binds to the promoter and enhances transcription (catabolite repression — when glucose is present, the lactose operon works weakly).
s4305 **Tryptophan operon (repressible):** when tryptophan is abundant it acts as a **corepressor**, activating the repressor, and the genes for the synthetic enzymes are switched off; an additional mechanism is **attenuation**.
s4306 7. Regulation of gene expression in eukaryotes
s4307 In eukaryotes, regulation operates at many levels:
s4308 **Chromatin level** — histone **acetylation** (histone acetyltransferases) loosens chromatin and activates transcription, while deacetylation silences it; **DNA methylation** (CpG) silences the gene — **epigenetic** regulation; genomic imprinting (Prader–Willi and Angelman syndromes).
s4309 **Transcription level** — the main level: **transcription factors** bind to the promoter, **enhancers** (which work even thousands of base pairs away) and silencers. Examples: **steroid hormone receptors** — bind the hormone and then bind to hormone response elements in DNA (GRE); cAMP → PKA → **CREB**; metals → the metallothionein gene; hypoxia → HIF-1 → the erythropoietin gene.
s4310 **Gene amplification** — an increase in the number of gene copies (HER2, MYC in tumors; the dihydrofolate reductase gene in methotrexate resistance).
s4311 **Gene rearrangement** — V(D)J recombination of immunoglobulin genes.
s4312 **Post-transcriptional level** — alternative splicing, RNA editing, mRNA stability (the transferrin receptor mRNA is regulated by iron, topic 21), **microRNAs** and siRNAs (RNA interference) degrade mRNA or stop translation.
s4313 **Translation level** — phosphorylation of eIF2 (under stress, and in heme deficiency globin synthesis stops in reticulocytes); iron-dependent translation of ferritin mRNA (IRE/IRP).
s4314 **Post-translational level** — phosphorylation, proteolysis, degradation by ubiquitination. **Cell differentiation:** the genome is the same in all somatic cells, but in each cell type only a certain set of genes is expressed (globin in erythrocyte precursors, insulin in β-cells). This **selective expression** is provided by tissue-specific transcription factors and epigenetic marks; in ontogenesis the **homeotic (HOX) genes** determine the body plan. For example, the switch from fetal hemoglobin (HbF) to adult hemoglobin (HbA) is the silencing of the γ-globin gene and the activation of the β-globin gene.
s4315 **Hydroxycarbamide** (hydroxyurea) in sickle cell anemia reactivates expression of the γ-globin gene and increases the amount of HbF — HbF reduces the polymerization of HbS.
