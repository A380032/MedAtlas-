s6553 **False neurotransmitters:** aromatic amino acids (phenylalanine, tyrosine, tryptophan) increase in the blood (the liver does not break them down), while branched-chain amino acids (valine, leucine, isoleucine — used in muscle) decrease — the **Fischer ratio** (BCAA/AAA) falls from 3–3.5 to < 1. Many aromatic amino acids enter the brain and form octopamine, phenylethanolamine (false transmitters) and serotonin; dopamine and norepinephrine decrease.
s6554 **Enhanced GABAergic inhibition** (endogenous benzodiazepines).
s6555 **Intestinal toxins:** mercaptans (the liver odor — “foetor hepaticus”), phenols, short-chain fatty acids, indole, skatole — the liver does not detoxify them.
s6556 Hypoglycemia, electrolyte disturbances (hypokalemia, alkalosis — enhances the passage of NH3 into the brain), acidosis, renal failure (hepatorenal syndrome). **Principles of treatment:** temporary restriction of protein (but not complete), **lactulose** (in the colon bacteria ferment it and acidify the medium: NH3 → NH~4~^+^ — not absorbed, excreted from the intestine; laxative effect), bowel cleansing, nonabsorbable antibiotics (rifaximin — reduces the ammonia-producing bacteria), L-ornithine L-aspartate (enhances the synthesis of urea and glutamine), branched-chain amino acids, correction of hypokalemia; in severe cases liver transplantation.
s6557 Experiment 1: determination of total and direct bilirubin in blood on a biochemical analyzer (Jendrassik–Grof method)
s6558 **Principle of the method:** bilirubin forms red-violet azobilirubin with diazotized sulfanilic acid (the diazo reagent). Conjugated bilirubin reacts directly (**direct bilirubin**); unconjugated bilirubin requires an **accelerator** (caffeine benzoate) to be released from albumin — with it **total bilirubin** is determined. Indirect bilirubin = total − direct. Measurement — 546 nm (in an alkaline medium — 578 nm, blue color).
s6559 Select the “Total bilirubin” and “Direct bilirubin” tests on the analyzer; perform quality control with calibrators and control sera.
s6560 Place the serum sample in the rack (the sample must be protected from light — bilirubin breaks down in light).
s6561 The analyzer mixes 10–20 µl of sample with the appropriate reagents, measures after the set time and reports the result. **Interpretation:** total bilirubin 3.4–20.5 µmol/l; direct ≤ 3.4–5 µmol/l (≤ 25% of the total). Hemolysis and lipemia alter the result.
s6562 Experiment 2: determination of bilirubin and urobilinogen in urine
s6563 **Bilirubin — the Rosin test:** carefully layer a 1% alcoholic iodine solution over 3 ml of urine — a **green ring** at the interface (bilirubin is oxidized to biliverdin). Test strips (diazo reaction) are also used.
s6564 **Urobilinogen — the Ehrlich reaction:** add 0.5 ml of Ehrlich's reagent (p-dimethylaminobenzaldehyde + HCl) to 2 ml of urine — a red color at room temperature (a lot of urobilinogen); normally the color is faint and appears only on heating. **Results and analysis:** bilirubin in urine — conjugated hyperbilirubinemia (hepatocellular or obstructive jaundice); a lot of urobilinogen — hemolytic or hepatocellular jaundice; no urobilinogen and a lot of bilirubin — complete obstruction of the bile ducts.

s6662 A 62-year-old patient complains of gradually increasing yellowing of the skin and sclerae, severe itching and weight loss; there is no pain. The urine is dark and the stools are colorless. Total bilirubin 285 µmol/l (direct 230), alkaline phosphatase 980 U/l, GGT 650 U/l, ALT 95 U/l, no urobilinogen in the urine. PT is prolonged and normalized after vitamin K was given. CA 19-9 is sharply increased.
s6663 Determine the type of jaundice. Explain the laboratory results. Why was the PT corrected by vitamin K?
s6664 Obstructive (cholestatic) jaundice; the painless progression, weight loss and CA 19-9 point to a tumor of the head of the pancreas. Because bile flow is blocked, conjugated bilirubin returns to the blood (the direct fraction predominates), is filtered by the kidneys and darkens the urine; no bile reaches the intestine — urobilinogen and stercobilin are not formed (acholic stools, no urobilinogen in the urine). In cholestasis the synthesis of alkaline phosphatase and GGT and their release into the blood increase sharply; bile acids accumulate and cause itching. Because there are no bile acids in the intestine, fat-soluble vitamin K is not absorbed — prothrombin and other factors are not carboxylated; since the synthetic function of the liver is preserved, parenteral vitamin K corrected the PT (this does not happen with hepatocellular damage).
s6665 A 3-day-old newborn (from the second pregnancy of an Rh-negative mother) developed jaundice 10 hours after birth. Total bilirubin was 150 µmol/l on day 1 and 310 µmol/l on day 2 (almost all unconjugated), Hb 105 g/l, many reticulocytes, direct Coombs test positive.
s6666 What is the diagnosis? Why is the jaundice not physiological? What is the danger and how should help be provided?
s6667 Hemolytic disease of the newborn (Rh incompatibility): the mother's IgG antibodies against the Rh antigen cross the placenta and destroy the fetal erythrocytes (Coombs positive). The jaundice began on the first day, bilirubin is rising by > 85 µmol/l per day, and there is anemia — it is pathological. A lot of heme is broken down and unconjugated bilirubin is formed; the newborn's low UGT1A1 activity cannot conjugate it in time. The danger is bilirubin encephalopathy (> 340 µmol/l; free bilirubin exceeding the albumin capacity enters the brain). Help: intensive phototherapy, intravenous immunoglobulin, exchange transfusion (removal of antibodies and bilirubin, replacement of erythrocytes); avoidance of drugs that occupy albumin binding sites (sulfonamides, ceftriaxone). Prevention — anti-D immunoglobulin for the Rh-negative mother.
s6668 A patient with liver cirrhosis developed drowsiness, confusion, hand tremor (“flapping”) and a peculiar sweetish-musty breath odor after bleeding from esophageal varices. Blood ammonia 145 µmol/l (normal < 50), albumin 26 g/l, PT prolonged.
s6669 What condition is this? Why did the condition worsen after the bleeding? What are the biochemical mechanism and the principles of treatment?
s6670 Hepatic encephalopathy (precoma). Blood in the intestine is a large protein load: bacteria break it down and form a lot of ammonia; in cirrhosis urea synthesis in the liver is reduced, and NH3 reaches the brain through portocaval shunts. In the brain, astrocytes convert NH3 into glutamine and swell; α-ketoglutarate is consumed and ATP formation decreases; aromatic amino acids increase and false neurotransmitters are formed; GABAergic inhibition is enhanced. Mercaptans — the liver odor. Albumin and PT show the decline in the synthetic function of the liver. Treatment: stopping the bleeding, bowel cleansing, lactulose (elimination as NH~4~^+^), rifaximin, L-ornithine L-aspartate, correction of electrolytes and glucose, temporary protein restriction.

s6707 1. General principles of biotransformation
s6708 **Xenobiotics** are substances foreign to the body: drugs, industrial poisons, pesticides, food additives, carcinogens. Most of them are **lipophilic** — even when filtered in the kidneys they are reabsorbed in the tubules, and they accumulate in membranes and adipose tissue. The aim of biotransformation is to convert them into **hydrophilic** (water-soluble) compounds and excrete them in the urine or bile.
s6709 Figure 26.1
s6710 The two phases of xenobiotic detoxification (drawn after Severin E.S.)
s6711 **Phase I (functionalization)** — polar groups (–OH, –NH2, –COOH, –SH) are introduced into the molecule or unmasked: **oxidation** (mainly cytochrome P450), reduction (nitro and azo compounds), **hydrolysis** (esterases, amidases, epoxide hydrolase).
s6712 **Phase II (conjugation)** — a small endogenous molecule is attached to the polar group; the product is highly water-soluble and usually inactive.
s6713 **Phase III** — export of the conjugates from the cell by membrane transporters (P-glycoprotein, MRP). Besides the liver, detoxification also takes place in the intestine, kidneys, lungs and skin.
s6714 2. Microsomal oxidation
s6715 **Microsomes** are fragments of the smooth endoplasmic reticulum isolated from a cell homogenate. The **monooxygenase** (mixed-function oxidase) reaction:
s6716 RH + O~2~ + NADPH + H^+^ → ROH + H~2~O + NADP^+^
s6717 One oxygen atom goes to the substrate and the other to water.
s6718 **Electron transport chain:** NADPH → **NADPH–cytochrome P450 reductase** (a flavoprotein: FAD, FMN) → **cytochrome P450** (a hemoprotein). The second electron can also be donated by cytochrome b5 (via NADH–cytochrome b5 reductase).
s6719 **The cytochrome P450 cycle:** the substrate binds to Fe3+-P450 → first electron (Fe2+) → O2 binds → second electron → the O–O bond is broken, one O atom forms water → the active iron–oxo complex hydroxylates the substrate → the product is released. The name “P450”: its reduced form bound to CO gives an absorption peak at 450 nm.
s6720 **The P450 family:** 57 genes in humans; mainly the **CYP1, 2, 3** families take part in xenobiotic metabolism: **CYP3A4** (≈ 50% of drugs), CYP2D6 (≈ 25%), CYP2C9, CYP2C19, CYP1A2, CYP2E1 (ethanol, paracetamol, organic solvents). Other P450s take part in the synthesis of endogenous substances: steroid hormones (adrenal glands, gonads), bile acids (7α-hydroxylase), hydroxylation of vitamin D, eicosanoids.
s6721 **Types of reactions:** aliphatic and aromatic hydroxylation, epoxidation, N-, O- and S-dealkylation, deamination, N- and S-oxidation, dehalogenation.
s6722 **Toxic activation (lethal synthesis):** some substances become more toxic under the action of P450: benzo(a)pyrene → diol epoxide (a carcinogen, topic 19), paracetamol → NAPQI, CCl4 → trichloromethyl radical (lipid peroxidation, liver necrosis), methanol → formaldehyde → formic acid (blindness, acidosis), ethylene glycol → oxalate (kidney).
