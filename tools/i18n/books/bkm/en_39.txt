s7357 Central (hypothalamic–pituitary) diabetes insipidus — the synthesis or secretion of vasopressin is impaired because of the injury. Without vasopressin the V2 receptor → cAMP → aquaporin-2 pathway in the collecting ducts does not work and water is not reabsorbed — a large amount of low-osmolality urine is excreted; the water loss raises plasma osmolality and Na+ and stimulates the thirst center. Glucose is normal (not diabetes mellitus). The response to the synthetic vasopressin analog desmopressin (concentration of the urine) shows that the renal receptors are preserved and the defect is central; in nephrogenic diabetes insipidus there is no response. Treatment — desmopressin.

s7391 1. Mechanism of urine formation
s7392 ≈ 1.2 l of blood passes through the kidneys per minute (≈ 20–25% of the cardiac output). Each kidney has ≈ 1 million **nephrons**.
s7393 Figure 29.1
s7394 The main functions of the parts of the nephron (drawn after Lippincott’s Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry)
s7395 **1. Glomerular filtration** — under hydrostatic pressure, plasma passes through the glomerular filter (fenestrated endothelium, basement membrane — type IV collagen, negatively charged heparan sulfate, podocytes). Substances with a molecular mass < 60–70 kDa that are not negatively charged pass through the filter; albumin (66 kDa, negative charge) hardly passes. **Primary urine** is ≈ 180 l per day — its composition is close to that of protein-free plasma. The **glomerular filtration rate (GFR)** is 90–120 ml/min/1.73 m^2^; it is the main indicator of kidney function.
s7396 **2. Tubular reabsorption** — ≈ 99% of the filtrate is reabsorbed:
s7397 **proximal tubule** — glucose (**SGLT2**, SGLT1; completely), amino acids, ≈ 65% of Na+ and water, HCO~3~^−^ (≈ 80%), K+, phosphate, urate, small proteins (endocytosis — megalin, cubilin), vitamins; the energy comes from Na+,K+-ATPase;
s7398 **loop of Henle** — in the descending limb water, in the ascending limb Na+, K+ and 2Cl- via **NKCC2** (the target of furosemide); the countercurrent mechanism creates an osmotic gradient in the renal medulla (urea also contributes);
s7399 **distal tubule** — NaCl (**NCC**, the target of thiazides), Ca2+ (PTH);
s7400 **collecting ducts** — Na+ (ENaC, aldosterone), water (**aquaporin-2**, vasopressin). **Renal threshold:** when the maximum capacity of the transporters is exceeded, the substance passes into the urine — for glucose ≈ 8.9–10 mmol/l in the blood. **SGLT2 inhibitors** (dapagliflozin, empagliflozin) deliberately increase glucosuria, treating type 2 diabetes and protecting the heart and kidneys.
s7401 **3. Secretion** — active transport from the blood into the tubular lumen: H+, K+, NH3, organic anions (urate, para-aminohippurate, penicillin, diuretics — OAT transporters) and cations (creatinine partly, metformin — OCT2).
s7402 **Clearance** is the volume of plasma completely cleared of a substance in 1 minute: C = (U × V) / P (U — concentration in urine, P — concentration in plasma, V — urine flow per minute). Inulin clearance = GFR; para-aminohippurate clearance — renal plasma flow. In practice GFR is estimated from **creatinine** (the CKD-EPI formula; age and sex are taken into account) or from **cystatin C**.
s7403 2. The role of the kidneys in acid–base balance
s7404 ≈ 50–100 mmol of nonvolatile acids (sulfuric acid from sulfur-containing amino acids, phosphoric acid, organic acids) are formed in the body per day; only the kidneys can excrete them (the lungs excrete only CO2).
s7405 Figure 29.2
s7406 H+ excretion and bicarbonate regeneration in the kidney (drawn after Severin E.S.)
s7407 In the tubular cells **carbonic anhydrase** forms H2CO3; H+ is secreted into the urine (Na+/H+ exchanger NHE3 — proximal; H+-ATPase and H+,K+-ATPase — intercalated cells of the collecting ducts), and HCO~3~^−^ passes into the blood. In the urine H+ is bound in three ways:
s7408 **Reabsorption of filtered bicarbonate** — H+ + HCO3 - → H2CO3 → CO2 + H2O (brush-border carbonic anhydrase); CO2 enters the cell.
s7409 **Titratable acids** — H+ is bound mainly by the phosphate buffer: HPO4 2- + H+ → H2PO4 - (≈ 10–30 mmol per day).
s7410 **Ammoniagenesis** (the main adaptive mechanism) — in the proximal tubules **glutamine** → **glutaminase** → glutamate + NH3 → (glutamate dehydrogenase) α-ketoglutarate + NH3; NH3 binds H+ and is excreted as **NH~4~^+^** (≈ 30–50 mmol per day, up to 300 mmol in acidosis). α-Ketoglutarate is used in gluconeogenesis, and **new HCO~3~^-^** is formed. Urine pH is normally 5.0–7.0 (on average ≈ 6.0); acidic with a meat diet, alkaline with a plant and dairy diet.
s7411 **Renal tubular acidosis:** type I (distal) — H+ secretion is impaired, urine pH > 5.5, nephrocalcinosis and stones; type II (proximal) — HCO~3~^−^ reabsorption is impaired (together with Fanconi syndrome: glucosuria, aminoaciduria, phosphaturia); type IV — aldosterone deficiency or resistance, hyperkalemia.
s7412 3. Specific features of metabolism and energy supply in the kidney
s7413 The kidneys make up ≈ 0.5% of body weight but consume ≈ 10% of the body's oxygen; ≈ 70–80% of the energy is spent on the work of **Na^+^,K^+^-ATPase** (which drives secondary active transport).
s7414 **Cortex** (proximal tubules) — aerobic; energy sources: **fatty acids**, ketone bodies, lactate, glutamine, citrate; many mitochondria; little glycolysis.
s7415 **Medulla** — little oxygen, **anaerobic glycolysis** predominates (glucose is the main source) — very sensitive to ischemia (papillary necrosis).
s7416 The kidney is an organ of **gluconeogenesis** (second after the liver; in prolonged fasting and acidosis up to 40% of blood glucose); substrates — glutamine, lactate, glycerol.
s7417 Amino acid metabolism: **the first step of creatine synthesis** (guanidinoacetate), synthesis of **arginine** from citrulline, metabolism of glycine and serine.
s7418 Small proteins and peptide hormones (insulin, PTH, β2-microglobulin) are reabsorbed in the tubules and degraded in lysosomes — in kidney failure the insulin requirement decreases. **Endocrine functions of the kidney: renin** (RAAS, topic 28), **erythropoietin** (in hypoxia — HIF; anemia in kidney failure), **calcitriol** (1α-hydroxylase), prostaglandins (vasodilation), kinins (kallikrein), urodilatin.
s7419 **The role of the kidneys in calcium, phosphorus and bicarbonate metabolism:** ≈ 98% of Ca2+ is reabsorbed (in the distal part it is regulated by PTH and calcitriol); phosphate — in the proximal tubule via NaPi-IIa (PTH and FGF-23 reduce it); bicarbonate — reabsorption and formation of new bicarbonate. **In chronic kidney disease:** phosphate is retained and calcitriol decreases → hypocalcemia → secondary hyperparathyroidism → renal osteodystrophy; metabolic acidosis (excretion of NH~4~^+^ decreases); anemia (low erythropoietin); hyperkalemia; uremia — urea, creatinine and “uremic toxins” (indoxyl sulfate, p-cresyl sulfate, guanidines) accumulate.

s7474 6. Pathological components of urine
s7475 **Proteinuria** — urine protein > 150 mg/day (in a healthy person albumin < 30 mg/day).
s7476 **Glomerular** — damage to the filter (glomerulonephritis, **diabetic nephropathy**, amyloidosis): selective (albumin) or nonselective (large proteins as well); **nephrotic syndrome** — > 3.5 g/day, hypoalbuminemia, edema, hyperlipidemia (topic 20).
s7477 **Tubular** — small proteins (β2-microglobulin, α1-microglobulin, retinol-binding protein) are not reabsorbed: pyelonephritis, heavy metals, nephrotoxic drugs.
s7478 **Overflow (prerenal)** — many small proteins in the blood: **Bence Jones protein** (myeloma), myoglobin (rhabdomyolysis), hemoglobin (intravascular hemolysis).
s7479 **Postrenal** — inflammation of the urinary tract, blood.
s7480 **Functional** — physical exertion, fever, orthostatic (in adolescents).
s7481 **Microalbuminuria** (albumin 30–300 mg/day or an albumin/creatinine ratio of 3–30 mg/mmol) is the earliest sign of **diabetic nephropathy** and an indicator of cardiovascular risk; it is checked every year in diabetes mellitus and hypertension. Ordinary test strips do not detect it.
s7482 **Glucosuria** — when blood glucose exceeds the renal threshold (diabetes mellitus, steroids, stress) or when the threshold is lowered (**renal glucosuria** — SGLT2 gene mutation, pregnancy, Fanconi syndrome, SGLT2 inhibitors).
s7483 **Ketonuria** — acetoacetate and acetone: decompensated diabetes mellitus, starvation, repeated vomiting (hyperemesis of pregnancy), ketogenic diet, acetonemic vomiting in children, alcoholic ketoacidosis.
s7484 **Bilirubinuria** (conjugated only) — hepatocellular and obstructive jaundice; **urobilinogenuria** — hemolytic and hepatocellular jaundice (topic 25).
s7485 **Hematuria** (erythrocytes — glomerulonephritis, stones, tumors, infection), **hemoglobinuria** (intravascular hemolysis: G6PD deficiency, incompatible blood transfusion, burns), **myoglobinuria** (rhabdomyolysis) — the “blood” zone of the test strip is positive in all three (peroxidase activity of heme); microscopy is needed to distinguish them.
s7486 **Leukocyturia and nitrites** — urinary tract infection: gram-negative bacteria (E. coli) reduce nitrates to nitrites.
s7487 **Other pathological components:** phenylpyruvate (phenylketonuria — green color with FeCl3), homogentisic acid (alkaptonuria — the urine darkens in air), porphyrins and porphobilinogen (porphyrias), cystine, aminoaciduria (Fanconi, Hartnup disease), oxalates, orotic acid (topic 14), GAGs (mucopolysaccharidoses), 5-hydroxyindoleacetic acid (carcinoid tumor), vanillylmandelic acid (pheochromocytoma), hCG (pregnancy).
