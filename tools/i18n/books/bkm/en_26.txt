s5141 **C-reactive protein (CRP)** reacts with the C-polysaccharide of pneumococci; it activates complement and is an opsonin. Normal < 5 mg/l; in bacterial infection > 100 mg/l; **high-sensitivity CRP** (1–3 mg/l) is used to assess cardiovascular risk. **Procalcitonin** is a specific marker of bacterial sepsis. **Ferritin** is also an acute phase protein (it can mask iron deficiency, topic 21). An increase in acute phase proteins (especially fibrinogen) causes an increase in the **erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR)**.
s5142 4. The role of albumin in the distribution of body water. Edema
s5143 Water exchange in capillaries is determined by **Starling forces**: at the arterial end the hydrostatic pressure (≈ 30–35 mm Hg) is greater than the oncotic pressure (≈ 25 mm Hg) — fluid moves out into the tissue; at the venous end the hydrostatic pressure (≈ 15) is lower than the oncotic pressure — fluid returns. **Albumin** provides ≈ 80% of the oncotic pressure.
s5144 Mechanisms of edema:
s5145 **decreased oncotic pressure** (hypoalbuminemia < 25–30 g/l): nephrotic syndrome (proteinuria), liver cirrhosis (synthesis ↓), starvation and kwashiorkor (protein deficiency), protein-losing enteropathy, burns;
s5146 increased hydrostatic pressure (heart failure, venous obstruction);
s5147 increased capillary permeability (inflammation, allergy);
s5148 impaired lymph flow (lymphedema).
s5149 5. Dysproteinemias
s5150 **Hypoproteinemia** (< 60 g/l): malnutrition, liver diseases, nephrotic syndrome, blood loss, burns.
s5151 **Hyperproteinemia** (> 85 g/l): relative — dehydration; absolute — paraproteinemia (multiple myeloma), chronic infections (γ-globulins ↑).

s5167 6. Hematopoiesis and erythrocyte maturation. Heme synthesis
s5168 **Hematopoiesis** begins in the bone marrow from a pluripotent stem cell and is controlled by growth factors: **erythropoietin** (produced in the kidney in hypoxia via HIF-1), thrombopoietin (liver), G-CSF, GM-CSF and interleukins. Erythropoiesis requires iron, **B~12~ and folate** (DNA synthesis), B6 (heme synthesis), copper and protein.
s5169 Erythroid precursors → reticulocyte (anucleate, remnants of ribosomes and mitochondria, 1–2 days) → erythrocyte (lives ≈ 120 days). In erythroid cells the synthesis of heme and globin is coordinated: heme activates globin synthesis (it blocks the eIF2 kinase).
s5170 Figure 20.2
s5171 Stages and regulation of heme synthesis (drawn after Severin E.S.)
s5172 Heme is synthesized mainly in the **bone marrow** (≈ 85%) and the **liver** (for cytochromes). The first and the last three reactions take place in the mitochondria, and the intermediate reactions in the cytosol. **The key enzyme is δ-ALA synthase** (coenzyme — pyridoxal phosphate); heme inhibits it by feedback (it decreases its synthesis and its entry into the mitochondria). Hepatic ALA synthase-1 is induced by barbiturates, alcohol and some drugs (heme is needed for P450 synthesis).
s5173 **Porphyrias** are hereditary or acquired defects of the enzymes of heme synthesis; intermediate products accumulate.
s5174 **Acute intermittent porphyria** — PBG deaminase deficiency (autosomal dominant); severe abdominal pain, neurological and psychiatric disturbances; a lot of **δ-ALA and porphobilinogen** in the urine (the urine darkens in light); attacks are provoked by barbiturates, sulfonamides, alcohol and fasting. Treatment — hemin (inhibits ALA synthase), glucose.
s5175 **Porphyria cutanea tarda** — uroporphyrinogen decarboxylase (the most common): skin photosensitivity, blisters; provoked by alcohol, hepatitis C, iron overload.
s5176 **Erythropoietic protoporphyria**, congenital erythropoietic porphyria (Günther disease) — photosensitivity.
s5177 **Lead poisoning** — δ-ALA dehydratase and ferrochelatase are inhibited: δ-ALA increases in the urine and protoporphyrin (zinc protoporphyrin) in erythrocytes; microcytic anemia, basophilic stippling of erythrocytes.
s5178 7. Features of erythrocyte metabolism
s5179 The mature erythrocyte has no nucleus, mitochondria or ribosomes — protein synthesis, the Krebs cycle and oxidative phosphorylation do not take place.
s5180 **Glucose** (GLUT1, insulin-independent) is the only energy source; ≈ 90% goes through **anaerobic glycolysis** → lactate (ATP for Na+,K+-ATPase and the membrane).
s5181 **The 2,3-bisphosphoglycerate (2,3-BPG) shunt** (Rapoport–Luebering) decreases the affinity of hemoglobin for oxygen (facilitates the delivery of oxygen to tissues); it increases at high altitude and in anemia.
s5182 **Pentose phosphate pathway** (≈ 10%) — **NADPH** → glutathione reductase → reduced **glutathione** → detoxification of H2O2 and lipid peroxides (glutathione peroxidase, Se). **In G6PD deficiency**, oxidizing drugs (primaquine, sulfonamides) and legumes (favism) cause a hemolytic crisis (topic 7).
s5183 **Methemoglobin reductase** (NADH–cytochrome b5 reductase) reduces methemoglobin (Fe3+) back to hemoglobin (Fe2+).
s5184 Antioxidant enzymes: superoxide dismutase, catalase, glutathione peroxidase.
s5185 **Pyruvate kinase deficiency** — ATP decreases and erythrocytes undergo hemolysis (2,3-BPG increases).
s5186 8. Hemoglobin and its derivatives
s5187 **Hemoglobin** is a hemoprotein of 4 subunits (HbA: α2β2), each of which contains one heme (Fe2+ + protoporphyrin IX); it binds 4 molecules of O2. **Cooperativity** — the dissociation curve is S-shaped (hyperbolic for myoglobin). **Bohr effect** — an increase in H+ and CO2 decreases the affinity for O2 (facilitating O2 delivery in tissues); 2,3-BPG has the same effect.

s5235 10. The lymphoreticular system. Metabolism of phagocytes
s5236 **The lymphoreticular (immune) system:** central organs — bone marrow and thymus; peripheral — lymph nodes, spleen and mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue. Functions: recognition and elimination of foreign antigens, immune memory, surveillance of tumor cells. **Lymph** is formed from tissue fluid; its composition is close to that of plasma but it contains little protein (20–40 g/l; intestinal lymph contains many chylomicrons — it is “milky”) and many lymphocytes. Age-related features: in children the thymus and lymphoid tissue are active; in old age the thymus involutes and the immune response weakens.
s5237 **Biochemical tools of the immune system:** immunoglobulins (IgG — the most abundant, crosses the placenta; IgM — primary response, a pentamer; IgA — mucous membranes, milk; IgE — allergy), the complement system, cytokines, interferons.
s5238 **Metabolism of phagocytes (neutrophils, macrophages):** they obtain energy mainly from **glycolysis** (there is little oxygen at the site of inflammation); the pentose phosphate pathway is active. During phagocytosis there is a **“respiratory (oxidative) burst”**: O2 consumption rises sharply —
s5239 **NADPH oxidase** (in the membrane): 2O2 + NADPH → 2O2 •- (superoxide) + NADP+ + H+;
s5240 **superoxide dismutase**: 2O2 •- + 2H+ → H2O2 + O2;
s5241 **myeloperoxidase** (neutrophil granules): H2O2 + Cl- → HOCl (hypochlorite) — a powerful bactericide;
s5242 NO synthase (in macrophages) — NO and peroxynitrite.
s5243 **Chronic granulomatous disease** is a hereditary defect of NADPH oxidase (most often X-linked): phagocytes can engulf bacteria but cannot kill them — severe recurrent infections with catalase-positive microorganisms (staphylococci, Aspergillus) and granulomas. Diagnosis — the nitroblue tetrazolium test (negative) or the dihydrorhodamine test.
s5244 11. Laboratory work
s5245 Experiment 1: determination of total protein in blood serum by the biuret method
s5246 **Principle of the method:** in an alkaline medium peptide bonds form a violet complex with Cu2+ ions; the color intensity is proportional to the amount of protein (540 nm).
s5247 Add 1 ml of biuret reagent and 20 µl of serum to a test tube, 20 µl of standard (70 g/l) to a second tube and 20 µl of water to a third.
s5248 Keep for 30 minutes at room temperature.
s5249 Measure at 540 nm against the blank. **Calculation:** C (g/l) = (Esample / Estandard) × 70. **Normal:** 65–85 g/l.
s5250 Experiment 2: determination of albumin in blood plasma on a biochemical analyzer (with bromocresol green)
s5251 **Principle of the method:** at pH 4.2 albumin binds the dye bromocresol green (BCG) and changes its color from yellow to green-blue (628 nm).
s5252 Select the “Albumin (BCG)” test on the analyzer; measure the calibrator and the control serum (the control values must be within the permitted range).
s5253 Place the tube with the patient's serum in the rack and enter its identifier.
s5254 The analyzer automatically mixes 5 µl of sample with 1 ml of reagent, measures after 1 minute and reports the result. **Interpretation:** normal 35–50 g/l. **Albumin/globulin ratio** = albumin / (total protein − albumin), normal 1.2–2.0; it decreases in inflammation, cirrhosis and myeloma.
s5255 Experiment 3: fractionation of serum proteins by electrophoresis (demonstration)
s5256 Apply serum to a cellulose acetate strip and run electrophoresis in veronal buffer (pH 8.6) at 200 V for 20 minutes.
s5257 Stain (amido black or Ponceau S) and read on a densitometer. **Results and analysis:** at pH 8.6 the proteins are negatively charged and move toward the anode; albumin is the fastest. Compare the proportions of the fractions with the normal values and determine the type of dysproteinemia.
s5258 Skill: interpreting a proteinogram
s5259 Assess the amounts of total protein and albumin.
s5260 Compare the proportion of each fraction with the normal values.
s5261 Look for characteristic features: α2 ↑ (acute inflammation, nephrotic syndrome), β–γ bridge (cirrhosis), narrow M spike (myeloma), γ ↓ (immunodeficiency), α1 ↓ (α1-antitrypsin deficiency).
s5262 Draw a conclusion by comparing with the clinical picture.
s5263 Skill: distinguishing hemoglobin derivatives
s5264 Assess the color of the blood: cherry-red (HbCO), chocolate-brown (MetHb), dark red (deoxy-Hb).
