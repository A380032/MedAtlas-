s5462 **Amplification** — this small amount of thrombin activates platelets and activates factors V, VIII (releasing it from vWF) and XI.
s5463 **Propagation** — on the surface of activated platelets **tenase** (IXa + VIIIa) generates a lot of Xa, and **prothrombinase** (Xa + Va + Ca2+ + phospholipid) provides the thrombin “burst”. **Fibrin formation:** thrombin cleaves fibrinopeptides A and B from fibrinogen → fibrin monomers polymerize spontaneously (soluble fibrin) → **XIIIa** (activated by thrombin) forms isopeptide bonds between glutamine and lysine residues, creating a stable, insoluble fibrin mesh. Actomyosin inside the platelets (thrombosthenin) contracts the clot (**retraction**).
s5464 The classical “extrinsic” (TF, VII) and “intrinsic” (XII, XI, IX, VIII) pathways have been retained to explain laboratory tests: **prothrombin time (PT/INR)** — the extrinsic and common pathways (VII, X, V, II, I); **activated partial thromboplastin time (aPTT)** — the intrinsic and common pathways.
s5465 7. Anticoagulant and fibrinolytic systems
s5466 Physiological anticoagulants:
s5467 **antithrombin III** — a serpin; it inhibits thrombin, Xa, IXa and XIa; **heparin** (and heparan sulfate on the endothelium) increases its activity ≈ 1000 times;
s5468 **the protein C – protein S system** — thrombin binds to **thrombomodulin** on the endothelium and activates protein C; activated protein C (cofactor — protein S) proteolyzes Va and VIIIa. **Factor V Leiden** (Arg506Gln) is a factor V resistant to activated protein C, the most common hereditary thrombophilia;
s5469 **tissue factor pathway inhibitor (TFPI)** — blocks the TF–VIIa–Xa complex;
s5470 α2-macroglobulin, heparin cofactor II.
s5471 **Fibrinolysis: plasminogen** (synthesized in the liver) binds to fibrin and is converted into **plasmin** by **tissue plasminogen activator (t-PA)** (endothelium) or **urokinase**; plasmin breaks down fibrin into **fibrin degradation products**, including **D-dimer**. **D-dimer** is a marker of the breakdown of stabilized (cross-linked by XIIIa) fibrin; it increases in deep vein thrombosis, pulmonary embolism and DIC.
s5472 **Antifibrinolytic system: α~2~-antiplasmin** (inhibits plasmin), **plasminogen activator inhibitor-1 (PAI-1)**, thrombin-activatable fibrinolysis inhibitor. Drug — **tranexamic acid** (a lysine analog that blocks the binding of plasminogen to fibrin).
s5473 8. Disorders of hemostasis and antithrombotic drugs
s5474 **Hemophilia A** (factor VIII) and **B** (factor IX) — X-linked recessive; bleeding into joints (hemarthrosis) and muscles; aPTT prolonged, PT normal.
s5475 **Von Willebrand disease** is the most common hereditary bleeding disorder (autosomal dominant): mucosal bleeding; bleeding time and aPTT prolonged.
s5476 **Vitamin K deficiency** — hemorrhagic disease of the newborn (no intestinal microflora, little vitamin K in breast milk — vitamin K is given in the maternity ward), obstructive jaundice (impaired fat absorption), prolonged antibiotic use, warfarin overdose; PT prolonged.
s5477 **Thrombocytopenias and thrombocytopathies** — hereditary (Glanzmann thrombasthenia — GPIIb/IIIa; Bernard–Soulier syndrome — GPIb) and acquired (aspirin, uremia, immune thrombocytopenia).
s5478 **DIC** (disseminated intravascular coagulation) — sepsis, obstetric complications, tumors: first hypercoagulation, then consumption of factors and platelets and bleeding; fibrinogen ↓, platelets ↓, PT and aPTT prolonged, D-dimer ↑↑.
s5479 **Thrombophilias** — factor V Leiden, prothrombin G20210A, deficiency of antithrombin III, protein C and S, antiphospholipid syndrome, hyperhomocysteinemia.

s5497 Experiment 1: determination of iron in blood serum by the ferrozine method
s5498 **Principle of the method:** in an acidic medium iron is released from transferrin, and a reducing agent (ascorbic acid) converts Fe3+ to Fe2+; Fe2+ forms a violet complex with ferrozine (562 nm).
s5499 Add 1 ml of working reagent to the tubes, then 200 µl of serum, standard (17.9 µmol/l) or water.
s5500 Keep for 10 minutes at room temperature and measure at 562 nm. **Calculation:** C (µmol/l) = (Esample / Estandard) × 17.9.
s5501 **Interpretation:** 11–30 µmol/l in men, 9–27 µmol/l in women. A hemolyzed sample is unsuitable (iron is released from erythrocytes). The diurnal variation of iron is large (higher in the morning) — therefore ferritin and transferrin saturation are also determined for diagnosis.
s5502 Experiment 2: determination of coagulogram parameters (prothrombin time and aPTT, demonstration)
s5503 **Principle of the method:** the time to clot formation after adding thromboplastin (tissue factor + phospholipid) and CaCl2 to citrated plasma (PT); with a kaolin–phospholipid activator and CaCl2 — aPTT.
s5504 Collect blood with 3.2% sodium citrate (9:1) and separate the plasma by centrifugation.
s5505 At 37 °C add 100 µl of thromboplastin–calcium reagent to 50 µl of plasma and record the clotting time on a coagulometer.
s5506 For aPTT add 50 µl of activator to 50 µl of plasma, incubate for 3 minutes, then add 50 µl of CaCl2 and record the time.

s5615 A 32-year-old woman complains of weakness, fatigue, hair loss and brittle nails; she craves chalk. Her menstrual periods are heavy and prolonged. Hb 92 g/l, MCV 68 fl, serum iron 5 µmol/l, TIBC 88 µmol/l, transferrin saturation 6%, ferritin 6 µg/l.
s5616 What kind of anemia is this? Explain the parameters biochemically. What are the principles of treatment?
s5617 Iron deficiency anemia (chronic blood loss — menorrhagia). Storage iron is exhausted (ferritin very low), transport iron is low (serum iron and transferrin saturation low); in iron deficiency the liver increases transferrin synthesis — TIBC is high. There is not enough iron for heme synthesis — the erythrocytes are small and contain little hemoglobin (microcytosis, hypochromia). Deficiency of iron-containing enzymes (in the epithelium) causes changes in the skin, hair and nails and pica. Treatment: identify and eliminate the cause of the menorrhagia; Fe2+ preparations with ascorbic acid, taken separately from tea and dairy products; continue for 2–3 months after Hb normalizes to replenish the stores (under ferritin control).
s5618 A 68-year-old man takes warfarin for atrial fibrillation. A week after he started taking an antibiotic for pneumonia, his gums began to bleed and his urine turned red. INR 7.5 (target 2–3).
s5619 What is the mechanism of action of warfarin? Why did the INR rise? How should help be provided?
s5620 Warfarin inhibits vitamin K epoxide reductase and decreases the reduced form of vitamin K; factors II, VII, IX, X and proteins C and S are not carboxylated in the liver — they cannot bind Ca2+ and phospholipids, and clotting slows down. Antibiotics eliminate the intestinal microflora and reduce the source of vitamin K2; some of them (metronidazole, fluoroquinolones, macrolides) inhibit CYP2C9 in the liver and slow the breakdown of warfarin — its effect increases. Help: stop warfarin and give vitamin K (phytomenadione); in serious bleeding, prothrombin complex concentrate or fresh frozen plasma (ready-made factors).

s5658 1. The extracellular matrix
s5659 **Connective tissue** consists of cells (fibroblasts, chondrocytes, osteoblasts, macrophages, mast cells) and a large amount of **extracellular matrix**. Composition of the matrix:
s5660 **fibers** — collagen, elastin, reticulin;
