s5469 **the protein C – protein S system** — thrombin binds to **thrombomodulin** on the endothelium and activates protein C; activated protein C (cofactor — protein S) proteolyzes Va and VIIIa. **Factor V Leiden** (Arg506Gln) is a factor V resistant to activated protein C, the most common hereditary thrombophilia;
s5470 **tissue factor pathway inhibitor (TFPI)** — blocks the TF–VIIa–Xa complex;
s5471 α2-macroglobulin, heparin cofactor II.
s5472 **Fibrinolysis: plasminogen** (synthesized in the liver) binds to fibrin and is converted into **plasmin** by **tissue plasminogen activator (t-PA)** (endothelium) or **urokinase**; plasmin breaks down fibrin into **fibrin degradation products**, including **D-dimer**. **D-dimer** is a marker of the breakdown of stabilized (cross-linked by XIIIa) fibrin; it increases in deep vein thrombosis, pulmonary embolism and DIC.
s5473 **Antifibrinolytic system: α~2~-antiplasmin** (inhibits plasmin), **plasminogen activator inhibitor-1 (PAI-1)**, thrombin-activatable fibrinolysis inhibitor. Drug — **tranexamic acid** (a lysine analog that blocks the binding of plasminogen to fibrin).
s5474 8. Disorders of hemostasis and antithrombotic drugs
s5475 **Hemophilia A** (factor VIII) and **B** (factor IX) — X-linked recessive; bleeding into joints (hemarthrosis) and muscles; aPTT prolonged, PT normal.
s5476 **Von Willebrand disease** is the most common hereditary bleeding disorder (autosomal dominant): mucosal bleeding; bleeding time and aPTT prolonged.
s5477 **Vitamin K deficiency** — hemorrhagic disease of the newborn (no intestinal microflora, little vitamin K in breast milk — vitamin K is given in the maternity ward), obstructive jaundice (impaired fat absorption), prolonged antibiotic use, warfarin overdose; PT prolonged.
s5478 **Thrombocytopenias and thrombocytopathies** — hereditary (Glanzmann thrombasthenia — GPIIb/IIIa; Bernard–Soulier syndrome — GPIb) and acquired (aspirin, uremia, immune thrombocytopenia).
s5479 **DIC** (disseminated intravascular coagulation) — sepsis, obstetric complications, tumors: first hypercoagulation, then consumption of factors and platelets and bleeding; fibrinogen ↓, platelets ↓, PT and aPTT prolonged, D-dimer ↑↑.
s5480 **Thrombophilias** — factor V Leiden, prothrombin G20210A, deficiency of antithrombin III, protein C and S, antiphospholipid syndrome, hyperhomocysteinemia.

s5498 Experiment 1: determination of iron in blood serum by the ferrozine method
s5499 **Principle of the method:** in an acidic medium iron is released from transferrin, and a reducing agent (ascorbic acid) converts Fe3+ to Fe2+; Fe2+ forms a violet complex with ferrozine (562 nm).
s5500 Add 1 ml of working reagent to the tubes, then 200 µl of serum, standard (17.9 µmol/l) or water.
s5501 Keep for 10 minutes at room temperature and measure at 562 nm. **Calculation:** C (µmol/l) = (Esample / Estandard) × 17.9.
s5502 **Interpretation:** 11–30 µmol/l in men, 9–27 µmol/l in women. A hemolyzed sample is unsuitable (iron is released from erythrocytes). The diurnal variation of iron is large (higher in the morning) — therefore ferritin and transferrin saturation are also determined for diagnosis.
s5503 Experiment 2: determination of coagulogram parameters (prothrombin time and aPTT, demonstration)
s5504 **Principle of the method:** the time to clot formation after adding thromboplastin (tissue factor + phospholipid) and CaCl2 to citrated plasma (PT); with a kaolin–phospholipid activator and CaCl2 — aPTT.
s5505 Collect blood with 3.2% sodium citrate (9:1) and separate the plasma by centrifugation.
s5506 At 37 °C add 100 µl of thromboplastin–calcium reagent to 50 µl of plasma and record the clotting time on a coagulometer.
s5507 For aPTT add 50 µl of activator to 50 µl of plasma, incubate for 3 minutes, then add 50 µl of CaCl2 and record the time.

s5616 A 32-year-old woman complains of weakness, fatigue, hair loss and brittle nails; she craves chalk. Her menstrual periods are heavy and prolonged. Hb 92 g/l, MCV 68 fl, serum iron 5 µmol/l, TIBC 88 µmol/l, transferrin saturation 6%, ferritin 6 µg/l.
s5617 What kind of anemia is this? Explain the parameters biochemically. What are the principles of treatment?
s5618 Iron deficiency anemia (chronic blood loss — menorrhagia). Storage iron is exhausted (ferritin very low), transport iron is low (serum iron and transferrin saturation low); in iron deficiency the liver increases transferrin synthesis — TIBC is high. There is not enough iron for heme synthesis — the erythrocytes are small and contain little hemoglobin (microcytosis, hypochromia). Deficiency of iron-containing enzymes (in the epithelium) causes changes in the skin, hair and nails and pica. Treatment: identify and eliminate the cause of the menorrhagia; Fe2+ preparations with ascorbic acid, taken separately from tea and dairy products; continue for 2–3 months after Hb normalizes to replenish the stores (under ferritin control).
s5619 A 68-year-old man takes warfarin for atrial fibrillation. A week after he started taking an antibiotic for pneumonia, his gums began to bleed and his urine turned red. INR 7.5 (target 2–3).
s5620 What is the mechanism of action of warfarin? Why did the INR rise? How should help be provided?
s5621 Warfarin inhibits vitamin K epoxide reductase and decreases the reduced form of vitamin K; factors II, VII, IX, X and proteins C and S are not carboxylated in the liver — they cannot bind Ca2+ and phospholipids, and clotting slows down. Antibiotics eliminate the intestinal microflora and reduce the source of vitamin K2; some of them (metronidazole, fluoroquinolones, macrolides) inhibit CYP2C9 in the liver and slow the breakdown of warfarin — its effect increases. Help: stop warfarin and give vitamin K (phytomenadione); in serious bleeding, prothrombin complex concentrate or fresh frozen plasma (ready-made factors).

s5659 1. The extracellular matrix
s5660 **Connective tissue** consists of cells (fibroblasts, chondrocytes, osteoblasts, macrophages, mast cells) and a large amount of **extracellular matrix**. Composition of the matrix:
s5661 **fibers** — collagen, elastin, reticulin;
s5662 **ground (amorphous) substance** — proteoglycans, glycosaminoglycans, water;
s5663 **adhesive glycoproteins** — fibronectin, laminin, nidogen, tenascin. **Functions:** supportive-mechanical (tendon, bone, cartilage), protective (barrier, phagocytosis), trophic (exchange of substances), water–salt balance, reparative (wound healing), signaling (storage of growth factors, cell migration and differentiation).
s5664 2. Collagen: composition and structure
s5665 **Collagen** is the most abundant protein in the body (≈ 25–33% of total protein).
s5666 Specific features of the amino acid composition:
s5667 every third amino acid is **glycine** (≈ 33%): the (Gly–X–Y)n repeat; because glycine is the smallest, the three chains pack tightly along the axis of the helix;
s5668 there is a lot of **proline** and **hydroxyproline** (≈ 21%; proline is most often in position X and hydroxyproline in position Y);
s5669 **hydroxylysine** — for glycosylation and cross-links;
s5670 **no tryptophan**, little cysteine and tyrosine — collagen is not a nutritionally complete protein.
s5671 Structure:
s5672 **primary** — (Gly–X–Y)n;
s5673 **secondary** — each α-chain is a left-handed **polyproline helix** (not an α-helix; the proline ring does not allow an α-helix);
s5674 **tertiary** — three α-chains wind around one another to form a right-handed **triple helix** (superhelix) — **tropocollagen** (length 300 nm, diameter 1.5 nm); hydrogen bonds (with the participation of the OH groups of hydroxyproline) stabilize it;
s5675 **quaternary** — tropocollagen molecules are arranged in a staggered manner (shifted by 1/4 of their length) to form **fibrils** (periodic cross-striations of 67 nm in the electron microscope); fibrils combine into fibers.
