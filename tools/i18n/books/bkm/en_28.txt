s5485 **Amplification** — this small amount of thrombin activates platelets and activates factors V, VIII (releasing it from vWF) and XI.
s5486 **Propagation** — on the surface of activated platelets **tenase** (IXa + VIIIa) generates a lot of Xa, and **prothrombinase** (Xa + Va + Ca2+ + phospholipid) provides the thrombin “burst”. **Fibrin formation:** thrombin cleaves fibrinopeptides A and B from fibrinogen → fibrin monomers polymerize spontaneously (soluble fibrin) → **XIIIa** (activated by thrombin) forms isopeptide bonds between glutamine and lysine residues, creating a stable, insoluble fibrin mesh. Actomyosin inside the platelets (thrombosthenin) contracts the clot (**retraction**).
s5487 The classical “extrinsic” (TF, VII) and “intrinsic” (XII, XI, IX, VIII) pathways have been retained to explain laboratory tests: **prothrombin time (PT/INR)** — the extrinsic and common pathways (VII, X, V, II, I); **activated partial thromboplastin time (aPTT)** — the intrinsic and common pathways.
s5488 7. Anticoagulant and fibrinolytic systems
s5489 Physiological anticoagulants:
s5490 **antithrombin III** — a serpin; it inhibits thrombin, Xa, IXa and XIa; **heparin** (and heparan sulfate on the endothelium) increases its activity ≈ 1000 times;
s5491 **the protein C – protein S system** — thrombin binds to **thrombomodulin** on the endothelium and activates protein C; activated protein C (cofactor — protein S) proteolyzes Va and VIIIa. **Factor V Leiden** (Arg506Gln) is a factor V resistant to activated protein C, the most common hereditary thrombophilia;
s5492 **tissue factor pathway inhibitor (TFPI)** — blocks the TF–VIIa–Xa complex;
s5493 α2-macroglobulin, heparin cofactor II.
s5494 **Fibrinolysis: plasminogen** (synthesized in the liver) binds to fibrin and is converted into **plasmin** by **tissue plasminogen activator (t-PA)** (endothelium) or **urokinase**; plasmin breaks down fibrin into **fibrin degradation products**, including **D-dimer**. **D-dimer** is a marker of the breakdown of stabilized (cross-linked by XIIIa) fibrin; it increases in deep vein thrombosis, pulmonary embolism and DIC.
s5495 **Antifibrinolytic system: α~2~-antiplasmin** (inhibits plasmin), **plasminogen activator inhibitor-1 (PAI-1)**, thrombin-activatable fibrinolysis inhibitor. Drug — **tranexamic acid** (a lysine analog that blocks the binding of plasminogen to fibrin).
s5496 8. Disorders of hemostasis and antithrombotic drugs
s5497 **Hemophilia A** (factor VIII) and **B** (factor IX) — X-linked recessive; bleeding into joints (hemarthrosis) and muscles; aPTT prolonged, PT normal.
s5498 **Von Willebrand disease** is the most common hereditary bleeding disorder (autosomal dominant): mucosal bleeding; bleeding time and aPTT prolonged.
s5499 **Vitamin K deficiency** — hemorrhagic disease of the newborn (no intestinal microflora, little vitamin K in breast milk — vitamin K is given in the maternity ward), obstructive jaundice (impaired fat absorption), prolonged antibiotic use, warfarin overdose; PT prolonged.
s5500 **Thrombocytopenias and thrombocytopathies** — hereditary (Glanzmann thrombasthenia — GPIIb/IIIa; Bernard–Soulier syndrome — GPIb) and acquired (aspirin, uremia, immune thrombocytopenia).
s5501 **DIC** (disseminated intravascular coagulation) — sepsis, obstetric complications, tumors: first hypercoagulation, then consumption of factors and platelets and bleeding; fibrinogen ↓, platelets ↓, PT and aPTT prolonged, D-dimer ↑↑.
s5502 **Thrombophilias** — factor V Leiden, prothrombin G20210A, deficiency of antithrombin III, protein C and S, antiphospholipid syndrome, hyperhomocysteinemia.

s5520 Experiment 1: determination of iron in blood serum by the ferrozine method
s5521 **Principle of the method:** in an acidic medium iron is released from transferrin, and a reducing agent (ascorbic acid) converts Fe3+ to Fe2+

s5523 Add 1 ml of working reagent to the tubes, then 200 µl of serum, standard (17.9 µmol/l) or water.

s5639 A 32-year-old woman complains of weakness, fatigue, hair loss and brittle nails; she craves chalk. Her menstrual periods are heavy and prolonged. Hb 92 g/l, MCV 68 fl, serum iron 5 µmol/l, TIBC 88 µmol/l, transferrin saturation 6%, ferritin 6 µg/l.
s5640 What kind of anemia is this? Explain the parameters biochemically. What are the principles of treatment?
s5641 Iron deficiency anemia (chronic blood loss — menorrhagia). Storage iron is exhausted (ferritin very low), transport iron is low (serum iron and transferrin saturation low); in iron deficiency the liver increases transferrin synthesis — TIBC is high. There is not enough iron for heme synthesis — the erythrocytes are small and contain little hemoglobin (microcytosis, hypochromia). Deficiency of iron-containing enzymes (in the epithelium) causes changes in the skin, hair and nails and pica. Treatment: identify and eliminate the cause of the menorrhagia; Fe2+ preparations with ascorbic acid, taken separately from tea and dairy products; continue for 2–3 months after Hb normalizes to replenish the stores (under ferritin control).
s5642 A 68-year-old man takes warfarin for atrial fibrillation. A week after he started taking an antibiotic for pneumonia, his gums began to bleed and his urine turned red. INR 7.5 (target 2–3).
s5643 What is the mechanism of action of warfarin? Why did the INR rise? How should help be provided?
s5644 Warfarin inhibits vitamin K epoxide reductase and decreases the reduced form of vitamin K; factors II, VII, IX, X and proteins C and S are not carboxylated in the liver — they cannot bind Ca2+ and phospholipids, and clotting slows down. Antibiotics eliminate the intestinal microflora and reduce the source of vitamin K2; some of them (metronidazole, fluoroquinolones, macrolides) inhibit CYP2C9 in the liver and slow the breakdown of warfarin — its effect increases. Help: stop warfarin and give vitamin K (phytomenadione); in serious bleeding, prothrombin complex concentrate or fresh frozen plasma (ready-made factors).
s5645 A 5-year-old boy has prolonged bleeding after minor injuries and bleeding into the knee joint. His mother's brother has the same disease. Platelets 280 × 10^9^/l, PT 13 s, aPTT 78 s, factor VIII activity 2%.
s5646 What is the diagnosis? Explain the laboratory results on the basis of the hemostasis model. What is the mode of inheritance?
s5647 Hemophilia A (severe form).
