s6928 A 28-year-old woman with tuberculosis who is taking rifampicin became pregnant even though she was taking an oral contraceptive. In addition, she takes warfarin, and her INR has fallen to 1.3 (target 2–3).
s6929 How do you explain these situations? What is the mechanism? What measures should be taken?
s6930 Rifampicin is a potent P450 inducer: through the nuclear receptor PXR it enhances the synthesis of CYP3A4, CYP2C9 and other enzymes, as well as UGT and P-glycoprotein. As a result, the estrogens and progestins of the contraceptive (CYP3A4) are broken down rapidly and their blood concentration falls below the effective level — contraception fails. Warfarin (its S-enantiomer via CYP2C9) is metabolized rapidly — the anticoagulant effect decreases (INR fell, risk of thrombosis). Measures: barrier methods of contraception during treatment and for 4 weeks after it ends; increasing the warfarin dose under INR control (and reducing the dose again after rifampicin is stopped, as the induction wears off over 2 weeks — otherwise there is a risk of bleeding).
s6931 A 50-year-old man has been drinking large amounts of alcohol for 20 years. He was brought in confused after heavy drinking on an empty stomach in the morning. Blood glucose 2.1 mmol/l, lactate 5.8 mmol/l, β-hydroxybutyrate 4.5 mmol/l, uric acid 560 µmol/l, triacylglycerols 4.8 mmol/l, GGT 420 U/l, MCV 104 fl.
s6932 Explain all the biochemical changes in relation to alcohol metabolism.
s6933 When ethanol is oxidized by alcohol dehydrogenase and aldehyde dehydrogenase, a lot of NADH is formed and the NADH/NAD+ ratio rises. As a result, pyruvate is reduced to lactate (lactic acidosis) and oxaloacetate to malate — substrates for gluconeogenesis are lacking; when the glycogen store is exhausted on an empty stomach, hypoglycemia develops (confusion). Lactate competes with urate secretion in the kidney — hyperuricemia. β-Oxidation slows because of the excess NADH, glycerol-3-phosphate increases and TAG synthesis is enhanced — hypertriglyceridemia and fatty liver. Acetyl-CoA formed from acetate is converted to ketone bodies when oxaloacetate is lacking, and NADH reduces acetoacetate to β-hydroxybutyrate — alcoholic ketoacidosis. The increase in GGT is due to the induction of CYP2E1 and microsomal enzymes and to liver damage; macrocytosis is due to the direct effect of alcohol on the bone marrow and folate deficiency. Treatment: thiamine before giving glucose (to prevent Wernicke encephalopathy).

s7051 5. Interrelation of the endocrine and nervous systems. Ontogenesis
s7052 The hypothalamus is the center that links the nervous and endocrine systems: its neurons produce neurohormones (**neurosecretion**). The adrenal medulla is a modified sympathetic ganglion (acetylcholine from preganglionic fibers triggers epinephrine secretion). Hormones affect brain function (thyroid hormones — brain development, cortisol — mood and memory, sex hormones — behavior); in stress the hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal system and the sympathoadrenal system are activated together. Circadian rhythms (cortisol high in the morning, melatonin at night) are controlled by the suprachiasmatic nucleus.
s7053 **Changes in ontogenesis:** in the fetal period thyroid hormones are crucial for brain and skeletal development (congenital hypothyroidism — cretinism; newborn screening — TSH); in childhood somatotropin and IGF-1 ensure growth; at puberty gonadoliberin pulsatility begins and sex hormones increase; in old age somatotropin (somatopause), sex hormones (menopause, andropause) and DHEA decrease, and insulin sensitivity declines.
s7054 6. Synthesis and catabolism of steroid hormones
s7055 All steroid hormones are synthesized **from cholesterol** (obtained mainly through LDL). The key (rate-limiting) step is the transport of cholesterol to the inner mitochondrial membrane by the **StAR protein** and its conversion to **pregnenolone** by **CYP11A1** (the side-chain cleavage enzyme); this step is stimulated through cAMP by ACTH (adrenal gland) and LH (gonads). The enzymes of synthesis belong mainly to the cytochrome P450 family.

s7060 **Congenital adrenal hyperplasia** — most often **21-hydroxylase** deficiency (autosomal recessive): cortisol and aldosterone are not synthesized; by feedback ACTH increases, the gland becomes hyperplastic and the accumulated 17α-hydroxyprogesterone is converted into androgens — virilization of the external genitalia in girls, salt-wasting crisis (hyponatremia, hyperkalemia). Newborn screening — blood 17-hydroxyprogesterone. Treatment — replacement with glucocorticoids and mineralocorticoids.
s7061 **Transport:** in the blood most steroids are bound to proteins: cortisol — to **transcortin** (≈ 90%) and albumin; testosterone and estradiol — to **sex hormone-binding globulin (SHBG)** and albumin; only the free fraction (1–3%) is active.
s7062 **Catabolism (in the liver):** reduction of ring A, hydroxylation, then conjugation with **glucuronic acid** or **sulfate** — water-soluble metabolites are excreted in the urine: **17-hydroxycorticosteroids** from cortisol, **17-ketosteroids** from androgens and part of cortisol, **pregnanediol** from progesterone, and estriol glucuronides from estrogens. The daily urinary free cortisol is a screening test for Cushing syndrome. In liver cirrhosis the inactivation of estrogens decreases (topic 26).
s7063 7. Mechanism of action of sex hormones
s7064 **Androgens** (testosterone, dihydrotestosterone) bind to the androgen receptor (nuclear): formation of the male genitalia in the fetal period, secondary sexual characteristics at puberty, spermatogenesis (together with FSH), **anabolic effect** (muscle protein synthesis, erythropoiesis, bone mass), libido. **Androgen receptor defect** — complete androgen insensitivity syndrome (genotype XY, female phenotype).
s7065 **Estrogens** (estradiol, estrone, estriol) — estrogen receptors (ERα, ERβ): development of the female genitalia and mammary glands, endometrial proliferation (the 1st phase of the menstrual cycle), reduction of bone resorption (osteoporosis after menopause), raising HDL and lowering LDL (atheroprotective), increasing the hepatic synthesis of transport proteins (SHBG, transcortin, thyroxine-binding globulin) and clotting factors (risk of thrombosis with oral contraceptives).
