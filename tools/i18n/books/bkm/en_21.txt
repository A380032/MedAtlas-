s3967 3. DNA replication
s3968 **Replication** is the copying of DNA; it takes place in the **S phase** of the cell cycle.
s3969 Principles:
s3970 **semiconservative** — each new molecule contains one old and one new strand (the Meselson–Stahl experiment);
s3971 **template-directed and complementary**;
s3972 **antiparallel** — the new strand is elongated only in the 5’→3’ direction;
s3973 **discontinuous (semidiscontinuous)** — the leading strand is synthesized continuously, the lagging strand as **Okazaki fragments**;
s3974 **requires a primer** — DNA polymerases cannot start synthesis; they only add nucleotides to an existing 3’-OH;
s3975 in eukaryotes there are many **origins of replication (ori)** — replicons.

s3987 **Fidelity:** the **3’→5’ exonuclease (proofreading)** activity of DNA polymerases immediately removes a wrongly inserted nucleotide; together with mismatch repair, the error rate is ≈ 10^−9^–10^−10^.
s3988 **Relation to the cell cycle:** G~1~ → S → G~2~ → M; the transitions are controlled by **cyclins** and **cyclin-dependent kinases (CDKs)**; at the checkpoints the **p53** and **Rb** proteins arrest the cycle when DNA is damaged (topics 18–19). Replication licensing is granted only once per cycle.
s3989 4. Post-replicative modifications: methylation, telomeres
s3990 **DNA methylation:** DNA methyltransferases (donor — S-adenosylmethionine) convert cytosine in **CpG** dinucleotides to 5-methylcytosine. Methylation of a promoter **silences** the gene — epigenetic regulation (X-chromosome inactivation, genomic imprinting; silencing of tumor suppressor genes by methylation in tumors).
s3991 **Telomeres and telomerase:** the primer site at the 3’-end of linear DNA is not filled in, so the chromosome shortens with every division. The ends carry **telomeres** — (TTAGGG)n repeats. **Telomerase** is a **reverse transcriptase** with its own **RNA template**; it lengthens telomeres. It is active in germ cells, stem cells and **≈ 85–90% of tumor cells** (immortality), while its activity in somatic cells is low — telomere shortening is associated with **aging** (the Hayflick limit).
s3992 5. Transcription
s3993 **Transcription** is the synthesis of RNA on a DNA template. One DNA strand (antisense, noncoding) serves as the template; the RNA is complementary to it and similar to the coding strand (with U instead of T); synthesis proceeds in the 5’→3’ direction, **no primer is required**, and the substrates are NTPs.

s4006 Stages:
s4007 **Initiation** — the general transcription factors (TFIID binds to the TATA box) and RNA polymerase II bind to the **promoter** and open the DNA over ≈ 14 base pairs; TFIIH phosphorylates the CTD domain of the polymerase.

s4014 **5’-cap** — 7-methylguanosine attached by a 5’-5’-triphosphate bond; it protects the mRNA from exonucleases and is required for ribosome binding;

s4042 8. Use of recombinant DNA and PCR in medicine
s4043 **Recombinant DNA technology: restriction enzymes** (bacterial endonucleases that cut DNA at specific palindromic sequences) are used to cut out a gene, which is joined into a **vector** (plasmid, virus) with **DNA ligase** and introduced into bacteria, yeast or animal cells. This is how the following are obtained: human **insulin**, growth hormone, erythropoietin, interferons, clotting factors (VIII), hepatitis B vaccine, monoclonal antibodies.
s4044 **Polymerase chain reaction (PCR):** in vitro amplification of a specific DNA segment. Each cycle: **denaturation** (94–95 °C) → **primer binding (annealing)** (50–65 °C) → **elongation** (72 °C, thermostable **Taq polymerase**). In 30–35 cycles the number of copies increases ≈ 2^n^ times (billions). The result is detected by agarose gel electrophoresis or in real time (fluorescent probes).
s4045 **PCR-based diagnosis:** infections (SARS-CoV-2, HIV, hepatitis B and C viral load, mycobacteria of tuberculosis), hereditary diseases (mutations in cystic fibrosis and phenylketonuria), tumors (BCR-ABL), personal identification and paternity testing (forensic medicine), prenatal diagnosis. **Reverse transcription PCR (RT-PCR)** detects RNA viruses.
s4046 Experiment 1: isolation and hydrolysis of nucleoproteins from yeast
s4047 **Principle of the method:** nucleoproteins dissolve in weak alkali and precipitate when acid is added; on acid hydrolysis they break down into protein, purine bases, pentose and phosphoric acid.
s4048 Grind 5 g of yeast with 0.4% NaOH solution, stir for 20 minutes and centrifuge.
s4049 Add acetic acid to the supernatant — the nucleoproteins precipitate as white flakes.
s4050 Hydrolyze the precipitate by boiling with 10% sulfuric acid for 1 hour, cool and filter.
s4051 Experiment 2: qualitative reactions for the components of the hydrolysate
s4052 **Protein part** — the biuret reaction: a violet color when NaOH and CuSO~4~ are added to the hydrolysate.
s4053 **Purine bases** — the silver test: when ammoniacal AgNO~3~ is added, the silver salts of the purine bases form a light brown precipitate.
s4054 **Pentose** — the orcinol (Bial) reaction: a **green** color on heating with HCl and FeCl~3~ (ribose); the **diphenylamine** reaction — a **blue** color (deoxyribose, DNA).
s4055 **Phosphoric acid** — the molybdate reagent: on heating with ammonium molybdate and HNO~3~, a **yellow** crystalline precipitate (ammonium phosphomolybdate). **Results and analysis:** positive results of all the reactions confirm that nucleoproteins contain protein, nitrogenous bases, pentose and phosphate. Yeast contains mainly RNA, so the orcinol reaction is clearly positive.
s4056 Experiment 3: interpretation of a PCR electropherogram (case-based)
s4057 Identify the lanes on the gel image: marker (lengths of DNA fragments), positive control, negative control, patient samples.
s4058 Compare the band in the patient sample with the positive control (the length must be the same).
s4059 There must be no band in the negative control (if there is one — contamination, the result is invalid). **Results and analysis:** a band of the expected length in the patient lane is a positive result (pathogen DNA or a mutant allele is present).
s4060 Skill: writing the complementary strand and the mRNA from a DNA sequence
s4061 Mark the direction of the given strand (5’→3’).
s4062 Write the complementary strand antiparallel: A↔T, G↔C (3’→5’).
s4063 Write the mRNA from the template strand: U instead of T; the mRNA is identical to the coding strand (with U).
s4064 Example: coding 5’-ATG GCT TAA-3’ → template 3’-TAC CGA ATT-5’ → mRNA 5’-AUG GCU UAA-3’.

s4157 A family ate mushrooms gathered in the forest. After 10–12 hours vomiting, diarrhea and abdominal pain began; on day 3 jaundice appeared, blood ALT was 3500 U/l and the prothrombin index fell sharply.
s4158 Which toxin is responsible? What is its molecular mechanism of action? Why is the liver specifically affected?
s4159 The toxin of the death cap (Amanita phalloides) — α-amanitin. It inhibits eukaryotic RNA polymerase II: mRNA synthesis stops, proteins (including clotting factors) are not synthesized, and the cell dies. The toxin is absorbed from the intestine and reaches the liver first via the portal blood, actively enters hepatocytes through OATP transporters and acts again and again through the enterohepatic circulation — liver necrosis. The fall in the prothrombin index shows that protein synthesis in the liver is impaired.
s4160 A patient was diagnosed with tuberculosis and treatment with rifampicin was started. After a few weeks his urine and tears turned orange-red. The patient asked whether the drug also stops RNA synthesis in human cells.
s4161 What is the mechanism of action of rifampicin? Why does it have almost no effect on human cells?
s4162 Rifampicin binds to the β subunit of the bacterial DNA-dependent RNA polymerase and blocks the initiation of transcription (the start of chain elongation). Eukaryotic RNA polymerases differ in structure and are not sensitive to rifampicin — hence its selective action. The orange color is the drug's own color (harmless). In addition, rifampicin induces cytochrome P450 in the liver — interactions with other drugs must be taken into account.
s4163 A nasopharyngeal swab was taken from a patient and tested for a viral infection by real-time RT-PCR. Result: Ct = 18 (positive control Ct = 25, no signal in the negative control).
s4164 How do you interpret the result? Why is the “RT” step needed to detect an RNA virus? What does the Ct value mean?
s4165 The result is positive: the sample contains viral RNA and the controls worked correctly. Taq polymerase cannot amplify the genome of RNA viruses — first, complementary DNA (cDNA) is synthesized from the RNA by reverse transcriptase and then amplified by PCR. Ct is the number of the cycle at which the fluorescent signal crosses the threshold; the smaller the Ct, the more starting copies there are (Ct 18 — a high viral load, the patient is likely to be infectious).
