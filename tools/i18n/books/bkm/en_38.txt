s7225 **Endemic goiter** is enlargement of the thyroid gland in iodine-deficient regions (mountainous areas, including some regions of Central Asia). **Pathogenesis:** when iodine is lacking, T4 synthesis decreases → TSH increases by feedback → the follicular cells undergo hypertrophy and hyperplasia (the gland enlarges); the gland adapts by producing more T3 (which requires less iodine); if this continues for a long time, hypothyroidism develops. s7227 The body contains ≈ 1 kg of calcium (99% in bones as hydroxyapatite) and ≈ 0.6 kg of phosphorus (85% in bones). s7245 **Disorders: primary hyperparathyroidism** (adenoma) — hypercalcemia, hypophosphatemia, kidney stones, osteoporosis, peptic ulcer (“bones, stones, abdominal groans, psychic moans”); **hypoparathyroidism** (after surgery) — hypocalcemia, hyperphosphatemia, tetany (Chvostek and Trousseau signs); **chronic kidney disease** — low calcitriol, phosphate retention — secondary hyperparathyroidism, renal osteodystrophy.
s7246 **Rickets in children** is impaired mineralization of growing bone. **Causes:** vitamin D deficiency (little exposure to the sun, especially in those born in winter, dark skin, prolonged exclusive breastfeeding without prophylaxis, malnutrition, prematurity), malabsorption, liver and kidney diseases (impaired hydroxylation), hereditary forms (defects of 1α-hydroxylase or the vitamin D receptor — **vitamin D-dependent rickets**; **X-linked hypophosphatemic rickets** — FGF-23 excess, vitamin D-resistant). **Pathogenesis:** absorption of Ca2+ and phosphate decreases → hypocalcemia → secondary hyperparathyroidism → phosphaturia and hypophosphatemia, bone resorption; the osteoid is not mineralized. **Biochemical signs:** blood phosphate ↓, calcium N or ↓, **alkaline phosphatase ↑↑** (osteoblast activity), PTH ↑, 25(OH)D ↓ (< 30 nmol/l). **Clinical signs:** sweating, restlessness, softening of the skull (craniotabes), the “rachitic rosary”, bowing of the legs, delayed tooth eruption, muscle hypotonia. **Prevention and treatment** — vitamin D (prophylactic dose 400–500 IU/day), sunlight, calcium-rich food. The same condition in adults is **osteomalacia**.
s7247 4. Regulation of water–salt metabolism
s7248 Water makes up ≈ 60% of body weight in adults (≈ 75–80% in newborns); intracellular (≈ 2/3) and extracellular (interstitial and plasma) fluids. The main cation outside the cells is Na+, and inside — K+.
s7249 **Vasopressin (antidiuretic hormone)** is synthesized in the hypothalamus and released from the posterior pituitary when plasma **osmolality rises** (osmoreceptors; even 1–2% is enough) and when blood volume falls sharply. In the renal collecting ducts: V2 receptor → cAMP → **aquaporin-2** moves to the apical membrane → water is reabsorbed (concentrated urine). V1 — vasoconstriction. **Diabetes insipidus** — vasopressin deficiency (central) or renal insensitivity to it (nephrogenic — V2 or AQP2 mutations, lithium): 5–20 l of dilute urine per day (specific gravity < 1.005), thirst, hypernatremia. The syndrome of inappropriate vasopressin secretion — hyponatremia.
s7250 **The renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system:** when blood pressure and Na+ fall (and via sympathetic β1), the juxtaglomerular cells of the kidney release **renin** → angiotensinogen (liver) → **angiotensin I** → (**angiotensin-converting enzyme**, mainly in the pulmonary endothelium) → **angiotensin II**: powerful vasoconstriction, aldosterone secretion, thirst and vasopressin, proximal Na+ reabsorption. **Aldosterone** (a nuclear receptor) — synthesis of ENaC and Na+,K+-ATPase in the distal tubules and collecting ducts: Na+ and water are retained, K+ and H+ are excreted. ACE inhibitors (enalapril), angiotensin receptor blockers (losartan), aldosterone antagonists (spironolactone) — in hypertension and heart failure.
s7251 **Natriuretic peptides** (ANP, BNP) oppose the RAAS: natriuresis, vasodilation (topic 23). **Age-related features:** in newborns the concentrating ability of the kidney is low and the relative water content and turnover are high — very prone to dehydration (with diarrhea, vomiting); in the elderly thirst and concentrating ability decline.
s7252 5. Locally acting hormones
s7253 **Eicosanoids** (prostaglandins, thromboxanes, leukotrienes), histamine, serotonin, kinins, NO and gastrointestinal hormones (gastrin, secretin, cholecystokinin, GIP, GLP-1, somatostatin — topic 24) act at the site of formation or at a short distance and are rapidly inactivated. **Prostaglandin E~2~** — fever (hypothalamus), pain, gastric protection, maintenance of renal blood flow; NSAIDs reduce it and can cause gastric ulcer and kidney damage.
s7254 Experiment 1: determination of serum insulin by enzyme immunoassay (based on a video)
s7255 **Principle of the method (“sandwich” ELISA):** the wells of the plate are coated with monoclonal antibodies against one epitope of insulin. Insulin in the sample binds to them; a second, **peroxidase-labeled** antibody against another epitope is added. After washing, the substrate (tetramethylbenzidine + H2O2) gives a blue color, which turns yellow when the stop acid is added (450 nm). The color intensity is **directly proportional** to the amount of insulin.
s7256 Pipette 25 µl of the calibrators (0–300 µIU/ml), the control and the patient's serum (taken fasting) into the wells.
s7257 Add 100 µl of conjugate (peroxidase–anti-insulin) and incubate with shaking for 60 minutes at room temperature.
s7258 Wash the wells 5 times with wash buffer.
s7259 Add 100 µl of substrate and keep in the dark for 15 minutes; add 50 µl of stop solution.
s7260 Measure the absorbance at 450 nm; find the concentration from the calibration curve. **Interpretation:** fasting insulin 2–25 µIU/ml (≈ 14–174 pmol/l). **HOMA-IR** = [glucose (mmol/l) × insulin (µIU/ml)] / 22.5; > 2.5–2.7 — insulin resistance. Insulin ↓ (C-peptide ↓) — type 1 diabetes; ↑ — insulin resistance (type 2 diabetes, metabolic syndrome, polycystic ovary syndrome), insulinoma (together with hypoglycemia). In patients treated with insulin preparations, endogenous secretion must be assessed by **C-peptide**.

s7349 A 35-year-old woman living in a mountainous region was found to have an enlarged thyroid gland in the neck; weakness, cold intolerance and dry skin. TSH 9.8 mIU/l, free T4 9 pmol/l, antibodies to TPO negative, urinary iodine 35 µg/l (normal > 100). She does not use iodized salt.
s7350 What is the diagnosis? Explain the mechanism of development of goiter and hypothyroidism. What are the measures for prevention and treatment?
s7351 Endemic (iodine deficiency) goiter with hypothyroidism. When iodine is lacking, thyroperoxidase cannot iodinate thyroglobulin sufficiently — T4 synthesis decreases; by negative feedback the pituitary secretes a lot of TSH; TSH stimulates the growth and division of the follicular cells — the gland enlarges (goiter). As an adaptation the gland synthesizes more T3, but if the iodine deficiency persists, hypothyroidism develops: basal metabolism and thermogenesis decrease (cold intolerance, weakness), and GAGs accumulate in the skin. The negative antibodies exclude autoimmune thyroiditis. Prevention — iodized table salt; treatment — potassium iodide, levothyroxine if necessary; iodine intake is especially important when planning a pregnancy.
s7352 A 10-month-old child (born in winter, exclusively breastfed, no vitamin D prophylaxis) sweats, is restless, has a bald patch on the back of the head, a “rosary” on the ribs and bowed legs, and has no teeth yet. s7353 What is the diagnosis? Explain the mechanism of the biochemical changes. What are the treatment and prevention?
s7354 Vitamin D-deficiency rickets. Because of little sunlight (winter) and insufficient vitamin D in the diet (it is low in breast milk), not enough D3 was obtained from the skin and food — 25(OH)D and calcitriol are low. Intestinal absorption of Ca2+ and phosphate decreases — hypocalcemia stimulates the parathyroid glands (secondary hyperparathyroidism): PTH mobilizes calcium from bone (calcium is kept close to normal) and enhances renal phosphate excretion — hypophosphatemia. Because the calcium × phosphate product is low, the osteoid is not mineralized; the osteoblasts are activated compensatorily and release a lot of alkaline phosphatase; the bones soften and become deformed. Treatment — therapeutic doses of vitamin D, calcium, sunbathing; prevention — vitamin D for all infants in the first year of life (400–500 IU/day).
