s4869 **Anabolic processes are intense:** synthesis of nucleotides, DNA, protein and fatty acids (fatty acid synthase ↑); the pentose phosphate pathway is active (NADPH, ribose-5-phosphate).
s4870 **Shift of the isoenzyme spectrum to the embryonic type** (“dedifferentiation”): LDH-5 ↑, synthesis of embryonic proteins (α-fetoprotein, carcinoembryonic antigen).
s4871 **Membrane changes:** loss of contact inhibition, adhesion molecules (E-cadherin) ↓, changes in the composition of glycoproteins and gangliosides.
s4872 **Mutant IDH1/2** (gliomas, leukemias) produces the “oncometabolite” 2-hydroxyglutarate, which disrupts the demethylation of DNA and histones.
s4873 **Cachexia:** the tumor “draws in” glucose and amino acids; cytokines (TNF-α, IL-6) enhance lipolysis and muscle proteolysis; lactate is used for gluconeogenesis in the liver (the Cori cycle — a waste of energy).
s4874 6. Invasion and metastasis
s4875 **Detachment** — decrease in E-cadherin, epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT).
s4876 **Degradation of the basement membrane and the extracellular matrix** — **matrix metalloproteinases** (MMP-2, MMP-9 — type IV collagenases), plasminogen activator (uPA), cathepsins.
s4877 **Migration** — integrins, cytoskeletal rearrangement.
s4878 **Entry into blood and lymphatic vessels (intravasation)**, survival in the blood (a “coat” of platelets).
s4879 **Extravasation** and settling in a new site, **angiogenesis** (VEGF) — a metastatic focus.

s4912 8. Biochemical principles of tumor treatment
s4913 **Cytotoxic chemotherapy:** antimetabolites (methotrexate, 5-fluorouracil, 6-mercaptopurine — topic 14), alkylating agents and platinum drugs (DNA cross-links), topoisomerase inhibitors (doxorubicin, etoposide), mitotic poisons (vincristine, paclitaxel — microtubules).
s4914 **Targeted therapy: imatinib** — an inhibitor of the BCR-ABL tyrosine kinase (chronic myeloid leukemia); **trastuzumab** — a monoclonal antibody against HER2; gefitinib, osimertinib — EGFR inhibitors; vemurafenib — a BRAF V600E inhibitor; PARP inhibitors (in BRCA mutation); bevacizumab — against VEGF (an angiogenesis inhibitor).
s4915 **Hormonal therapy:** tamoxifen (an estrogen receptor modulator), aromatase inhibitors, androgen blockade.
s4916 **Immunotherapy:** immune checkpoint inhibitors (antibodies against PD-1/PD-L1, CTLA-4), CAR-T cells (topic 18).
s4917 **Enzymes:** L-asparaginase — leukemia cells cannot synthesize asparagine (asparagine synthetase is low), and asparagine in the blood is broken down. **Multidrug resistance:** in the membrane of tumor cells **P-glycoprotein** (the product of the MDR1 gene, an ABC transporter) increases and, using the energy of ATP, pumps drugs of various structures (doxorubicin, vincristine, paclitaxel) out of the cell. Other mechanisms: amplification of the target enzyme (dihydrofolate reductase with methotrexate), mutation of the target (imatinib-resistant BCR-ABL), increased glutathione S-transferase activity, enhanced repair, disruption of apoptotic pathways.
s4918 Experiment 1: determination of α-fetoprotein in blood serum by enzyme immunoassay (demonstration)

s4920 Pipette 50 µl of the standards and serum samples into the wells.
s4921 Add the conjugate, incubate at 37 °C for 60 minutes and wash 5 times.

s4924 Experiment 2: assessment of lactate dehydrogenase isoenzymes in tumor tissue and blood serum (case-based)

s5016 A 58-year-old man has smoked a pack of cigarettes a day for 35 years. Prolonged cough, hemoptysis, weight loss. Bronchoscopy shows a tumor, biopsy — squamous cell carcinoma; a G→T transversion at codon 248 of the TP53 gene was found in the tumor DNA.
s5017 What is the molecular mechanism of action of the carcinogen? Why is the TP53 mutation in particular important? Which stages of carcinogenesis can be identified?
s5018 Benzo(a)pyrene from tobacco smoke is converted in lung cells by CYP1A1 and epoxide hydrolase into the ultimate carcinogen — a diol epoxide — which forms an adduct with DNA guanine; during replication a G→T transversion occurs. TP53 is a tumor suppressor: it arrests cells with damaged DNA or sends them into apoptosis; its inactivation allows damaged cells to proliferate and new mutations to accumulate (genome instability). Initiation — DNA adducts and mutation; promotion — the chronic irritant and inflammatory effect of smoke; progression — additional mutations (KRAS and others), invasion. The long duration (decades) is consistent with the multistage theory.
s5019 A 45-year-old patient has a leukocyte count of 120 × 10^9^/l and an enlarged spleen. Cytogenetic testing revealed the Philadelphia chromosome. Treatment with imatinib was started; after 3 years the effect decreased.
s5020 What is the molecular basis of the disease? How does imatinib act? What could be the cause of resistance?
s5021 Chronic myeloid leukemia. As a result of the t(9;22) translocation, the BCR and ABL genes fuse to form the constitutively active BCR-ABL tyrosine kinase; it activates signaling pathways (RAS-MAPK, PI3K/Akt, STAT), leading to uncontrolled proliferation of myeloid cells and evasion of apoptosis. Imatinib occupies the ATP-binding site of the ABL kinase and selectively inhibits it (targeted therapy). Resistance is most often due to point mutations in the kinase domain (for example, T315I), so the drug cannot bind; also BCR-ABL amplification or an increase in P-glycoprotein. In such cases next-generation inhibitors (dasatinib, nilotinib, ponatinib) are used.
s5022 A 62-year-old patient was operated on for colorectal cancer. Before surgery CEA was 42 ng/ml, 1 month after surgery 3 ng/ml (normal < 5). After 8 months CEA rose to 28 ng/ml.
s5023 How do you interpret the CEA dynamics? Why is CEA unsuitable for screening? Which genes are involved in the multistage development of this tumor?
s5024 The fall of CEA to normal after surgery indicates that the tumor was completely removed; the renewed rise after 8 months is a sign of recurrence or metastasis (most often to the liver) — investigation (CT, PET-CT) is needed. CEA is not specific: it is also raised in smokers and in liver cirrhosis, inflammatory bowel disease and pancreatitis, and it is often normal at an early stage — so it is unsuitable for screening and is used mainly for monitoring treatment. Colorectal cancer develops according to the Vogelstein model: inactivation of APC (start of the adenoma) → activation of KRAS → loss of SMAD4 → inactivation of TP53 (carcinoma).
