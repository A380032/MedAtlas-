s2301 4. Ketone bodies
s2302 **Ketone bodies** are acetoacetate, β-hydroxybutyrate and acetone. They are synthesized from acetyl-CoA only in the **liver mitochondria** (ketogenesis), are carried by the blood to other tissues and are oxidized there as an energy source.
s2303 Figure 9.2
s2304 Synthesis of ketone bodies in the liver and their use in peripheral tissues (drawn after Severin E.S.)
s2305 **Synthesis:** 2 acetyl-CoA → acetoacetyl-CoA (thiolase) → **HMG-CoA** (HMG-CoA synthase — the key enzyme) → acetoacetate + acetyl-CoA (HMG-CoA lyase). Acetoacetate is reduced to β-hydroxybutyrate at the expense of NADH or spontaneously decarboxylated to acetone.
s2306 **Use:** the heart, skeletal muscle, kidney and, in prolonged starvation, the **brain**. β-Hydroxybutyrate is oxidized to acetoacetate; acetoacetate is converted by **succinyl-CoA-acetoacetate-CoA transferase** into acetoacetyl-CoA and then into 2 acetyl-CoA. **The liver has no such transferase** — it does not use ketone bodies, it only “exports” them.
s2307 **Ketogenesis increases** when a lot of acetyl-CoA is formed and oxaloacetate is lacking: starvation, a carbohydrate-free (ketogenic) diet, prolonged physical work, **diabetes mellitus** (insulin deficiency — lipolysis and β-oxidation increase sharply and oxaloacetate is used for gluconeogenesis).
s2308 Normally blood ketone bodies are 0.1–0.5 mmol/l and they are not detected in urine by routine methods. **Ketonemia** and **ketonuria** are seen in starvation and diabetes; because ketone bodies are strong acids, when a lot of them accumulate **ketoacidosis** develops (the pH falls); acetone leaves with the breath and gives a “rotten apple” odor from the mouth.
s2309 **Diabetic ketoacidosis** is a life-threatening complication of type 1 diabetes mellitus: hyperglycemia (> 13–15 mmol/l), ketone bodies > 3 mmol/l, pH < 7.3, reduced bicarbonate; Kussmaul breathing, dehydration, vomiting, abdominal pain, coma. Treatment — insulin (it stops lipolysis and ketogenesis), fluids and potassium.
s2310 5. Metabolism of phospholipids and glycolipids
s2311 **Synthesis of glycerophospholipids** (in the endoplasmic reticulum) — on the basis of phosphatidic acid or diacylglycerol:
s2312 **phosphatidylcholine** and **phosphatidylethanolamine** — through the CDP-choline (CDP-ethanolamine) pathway; phosphatidylcholine is also formed from phosphatidylethanolamine by three methylations at the expense of S-adenosylmethionine;
s2313 **phosphatidylinositol** — CDP-diacylglycerol + inositol;
s2314 **lipotropic factors** (choline, methionine, vitamin B~12~, folate, serine) are needed for the synthesis of phosphatidylcholine and for the export of TAG from the liver in VLDL; when they are lacking, **fatty degeneration of the liver** develops. **Sphingolipids** are synthesized on the basis of **sphingosine**, formed from serine and palmitoyl-CoA: sphingosine + fatty acid → ceramide; ceramide + phosphocholine → **sphingomyelin**; ceramide + carbohydrates → **cerebrosides, gangliosides**.

s2316 Experiment 1: determination of ketone bodies in urine (Legal test)
s2317 Pour into a test tube 1 ml of urine, 0.5 ml of 10% NaOH and 0.5 ml of freshly prepared 10% sodium nitroprusside solution.
s2318 Observe the red color that appears.
s2319 Add 1 ml of concentrated acetic acid. **Result and analysis:** if acetone and acetoacetate are present, a red color forms in the alkaline medium; when acetic acid is added it turns **cherry-red (violet)** — a positive result. Creatinine also gives a red color in alkali, but after acetic acid it turns yellow (which excludes a false positive). β-Hydroxybutyrate is not detected by this test.
s2320 Experiment 2: determination of acetoacetate in urine (Gerhardt test)
s2321 Pour 2 ml of urine into a test tube and add 10% iron (III) chloride solution dropwise (the phosphates precipitate; filter off the precipitate).
s2322 Add a few more drops of FeCl~3~ to the filtrate. **Result and analysis:** if acetoacetate is present, a **red-brown (wine) color** appears. On heating, acetoacetate is converted into acetone and the color disappears (whereas the color given by salicylates persists on heating).

s2416 A 17-year-old adolescent has complained for 3 days of thirst, passing a lot of urine, vomiting and abdominal pain. The breathing is deep and noisy and there is an odor of acetone from the mouth. Blood glucose is 22 mmol/l, pH 7.15, bicarbonate 8 mmol/l; glucose and ketone bodies (+++) were found in the urine.
s2417 What is the diagnosis? Explain the mechanism of the rise in ketone bodies.
s2418 Ketoacidotic decompensation of type 1 diabetes mellitus. When insulin is lacking, glucagon and the counter-insular hormones predominate: hormone-sensitive lipase in adipose tissue is activated and lipolysis increases sharply, so a lot of fatty acids reach the liver; because malonyl-CoA is low, CPT-I is not inhibited, β-oxidation increases and a lot of acetyl-CoA is formed. Since oxaloacetate is used for gluconeogenesis, acetyl-CoA cannot enter the Krebs cycle and is converted into ketone bodies. They are acids — metabolic acidosis (compensated by Kussmaul breathing), and acetone leaves with the breath. Hyperglycemia causes osmotic diuresis and dehydration. Treatment: insulin, fluids, potassium.
s2419 A 2-year-old child with influenza, after not eating for 18 hours, developed lethargy, vomiting and seizures. Blood glucose is 1.9 mmol/l, ketone bodies were not detected, and ammonia and liver enzymes are increased. Medium-chain acylcarnitines are increased in the blood.
s2420 Which enzyme deficiency is suspected? Why does the hypoglycemia occur without ketosis?
s2421 Medium-chain acyl-CoA dehydrogenase (MCAD) deficiency. In starvation energy is obtained mainly from the oxidation of fatty acids; because the enzyme is lacking, β-oxidation stops — acetyl-CoA and ketone bodies are not formed (hypoketosis), and there is not enough ATP or acetyl-CoA (an activator of pyruvate carboxylase) for gluconeogenesis — hypoglycemia. Medium-chain acyl-CoAs bind to carnitine and accumulate in the blood (they are detected by tandem mass spectrometry). Treatment: avoiding long fasts, giving glucose during illness.
s2422 A 30-year-old woman on a diet with almost all carbohydrates excluded (a “keto diet”) has an odor of acetone from the mouth and ketone bodies (++) in the urine. Blood glucose is 4.2 mmol/l and the pH is normal.
s2423 Is this condition dangerous? Explain its mechanism and how it differs from diabetic ketoacidosis.
s2424 Physiological (dietary) ketosis. When carbohydrates are low, insulin is low and glucagon relatively high: lipolysis and β-oxidation increase, the liver forms a lot of ketone bodies and they serve as an energy source for muscle and brain. But insulin is not absent altogether — it keeps lipolysis in check, so ketone bodies stay at a moderate level (0.5–3 mmol/l), the buffer systems neutralize them, the pH is normal and glucose is normal. In diabetic ketoacidosis, by contrast, there is almost no insulin: ketones exceed 3 mmol/l and acidosis and hyperglycemia develop.

s2460 1. Sterols and steroids. The significance of cholesterol
s2461 **Steroids** are substances based on the cyclopentanoperhydrophenanthrene ring. **Sterols** are steroid alcohols with a hydroxyl group at position 3; the main sterol of animals is **cholesterol** (C~27~H~45~OH). The body contains ≈ 140 g of cholesterol; ≈ 90% of it is in the cells (in membranes) and the rest in the blood.
s2462 The biological functions of cholesterol:
s2463 **structural** — a component of membranes, regulating their fluidity and permeability; abundant in the myelin sheath;
s2464 a basis for the synthesis of **bile acids** (liver);
s2465 synthesis of the **steroid hormones**: cortisol, aldosterone (adrenal cortex), testosterone, estrogens, progesterone (gonads, placenta);
s2466 synthesis of **vitamin D~3~** — in the skin from 7-dehydrocholesterol under ultraviolet light. **Sources:** food (0.3–0.5 g per day: egg yolk, meat, liver, butter) and endogenous synthesis (≈ 0.8–1 g per day; ≈ 50% in the liver, the rest in the intestine, skin and adrenal cortex). Plant foods contain no cholesterol (phytosterols reduce the absorption of cholesterol in the intestine).
s2467 **Age-related features:** in childhood a lot of cholesterol is needed for myelination and growth; breast milk is rich in cholesterol. Blood total cholesterol rises with age: in children 2.5–4.5 mmol/l, in adults the desirable value is < 5.2 mmol/l; in women, after the menopause, LDL rises as estrogens fall.
