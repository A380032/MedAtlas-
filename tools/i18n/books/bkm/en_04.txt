s0649 2. Functions and general properties of membranes
s0650 Functions:
s0651 **Barrier** — separates the internal environment of the cell and organelles, compartmentalization.
s0652 **Transport** — selective passage of substances.
s0653 **Receptor (signaling)** — reception of hormones, mediators and other signals.
s0654 **Enzymatic** — membrane-bound enzymes (respiratory chain, ATP synthase, adenylate cyclase).
s0655 **Energy conversion** — formation of an electrochemical gradient in the mitochondria and ATP synthesis.
s0656 **Intercellular contact** — gap junctions, adhesion molecules, antigenicity (blood groups, histocompatibility antigens).
s0657 **Electrogenic** — generation of the membrane potential and conduction of excitation. **General properties:**
s0658 **Transverse asymmetry** — the composition of the outer and inner leaflets differs: the outer leaflet is rich in phosphatidylcholine, sphingomyelin and glycolipids, the inner in phosphatidylserine and phosphatidylethanolamine. The movement of lipids from one leaflet to the other (“flip-flop”) is very slow and is carried out by special enzymes (flippases). Carbohydrate parts are only on the outer surface (glycocalyx).
s0659 **Fluidity** — lateral movement of lipids and proteins; fluidity increases when unsaturated fatty acids increase and when the temperature rises, while cholesterol and saturated fatty acids reduce it.
s0660 **Selective permeability** — small hydrophobic molecules (O2, CO2, steroids) pass freely; ions and polar molecules pass only through special proteins.
s0661 In apoptosis phosphatidylserine moves to the outer leaflet of the membrane — this is an “eat me” signal for macrophages. When platelets are activated, phosphatidylserine also moves outward and forms a surface for the clotting factor complexes.

s0687 4. Membrane receptors and signal transduction
s0688 Signaling molecules (**ligands**, first messengers) are hormones, neurotransmitters, cytokines and growth factors. Hydrophilic ligands (peptide hormones, catecholamines) do not cross the membrane and act through **membrane receptors**; hydrophobic ligands (steroid and thyroid hormones, calcitriol) cross the membrane, bind to **intracellular (nuclear) receptors** and change gene transcription.

s0707 The G protein system
s0708 **G proteins** are GTP-binding heterotrimeric proteins (α, β, γ subunits). At rest the α subunit is bound to GDP. When the receptor is activated, GDP is exchanged for GTP, α-GTP dissociates and acts on the effector enzyme; the α subunit’s own GTPase activity hydrolyzes GTP to GDP and terminates the signal.
s0709 **Gs** — activates adenylate cyclase (cAMP ↑);
s0710 **Gi** — inhibits adenylate cyclase (cAMP ↓);
s0711 **Gq** — activates phospholipase C. **The adenylate cyclase system:** adenylate cyclase forms **cAMP** from ATP. cAMP binds to the regulatory subunits of **protein kinase A** and releases the catalytic subunits; these phosphorylate target proteins (phosphorylase kinase, hormone-sensitive lipase and others). cAMP is broken down to AMP by **phosphodiesterase** (caffeine and theophylline inhibit phosphodiesterase and prolong the action of cAMP).
s0712 **The inositol phosphate system:** phospholipase C cleaves membrane phosphatidylinositol-4,5-bisphosphate (PIP2) into two second messengers: **inositol-1,4,5-trisphosphate (IP~3~)** — releases Ca2+ from the endoplasmic
s0713 reticulum; **diacylglycerol (DAG)** — together with Ca2+ activates **protein kinase C**. Many effects of Ca2+ are carried out through the protein **calmodulin**.

s0738 5. Membrane damage
s0739 Membrane damage is a key link in the pathogenesis of many diseases:
s0740 **Lipid peroxidation (LPO)** — free radicals (O2
s0741 −, •OH) oxidize polyunsaturated fatty acids; membrane permeability increases, enzymes and receptors fail. Antioxidant defense: vitamins E and C, glutathione, superoxide dismutase, catalase, glutathione peroxidase.
s0742 **Activation of phospholipases** (ischemia, excess Ca2+) — phospholipids are broken down.
s0743 **Detergent action** — excess bile acids and fatty acids.
s0744 **Mechanical and osmotic damage**, the membrane attack complex of the complement system, bacterial toxins.
s0745 **Congenital defects** — a defect of membrane skeleton proteins (spectrin) — hereditary spherocytosis; ion channel diseases (channelopathies) — cystic fibrosis (the CFTR Cl- channel).
s0746 6. Laboratory work
s0747 Experiment 1: determination of the osmotic resistance of erythrocytes
s0748 Pour 1 ml each of NaCl solutions from 0.7% to 0.2% (in steps of 0.02–0.05%) into test tubes.
s0749 Add 1 drop of blood to each tube, mix and leave for 1 hour (or centrifuge).
s0750 The tube with the highest concentration in which the solution has turned pink and there is a sediment — **minimal resistance** (onset of hemolysis).
s0751 The tube in which there is no sediment at all and the solution is clear red (“lacquered blood”) — **maximal resistance** (complete hemolysis). **Evaluation:** normally minimal resistance is 0.46–0.48% and maximal 0.32–0.34% NaCl. In hereditary spherocytosis resistance decreases (hemolysis begins at a higher concentration), in thalassemia and iron deficiency anemia it increases (thin, flat erythrocytes).
s0752 **Analysis:** in a hypotonic medium water enters the erythrocyte by osmosis, the cell swells, its membrane ruptures and hemoglobin is released. Resistance depends on the membrane composition, the state of the cytoskeleton and the cell shape (surface/volume ratio).
s0753 Experiment 2: effect of a detergent and temperature on membrane permeability (demonstration)
s0754 Pour 2 ml of an erythrocyte suspension in 0.9% NaCl into three test tubes.
s0755 Add 3 drops of 1% bile salt (or saponin) to tube 1 and 3 drops of ethanol to tube 2; heat tube 3 at 60 °C for 10 minutes.
s0756 Compare with the control tube. **Result and analysis:** under the action of a detergent and heating, membrane lipids and proteins are disrupted — hemolysis (the solution is clear red). This shows the consequence of loss of membrane integrity.

s0866 A patient who came from an endemic area has watery “rice-water” diarrhea of up to 15–20 liters a day, severe dehydration and a fall in blood pressure. Vibrio cholerae was detected in the stool.
s0867 Explain the biochemical mechanism of the diarrhea. Why is glucose added to the rehydration solution?
s0868 Cholera toxin ADP-ribosylates the α subunit of the Gs protein in enterocytes; its GTPase activity is lost and adenylate cyclase is permanently activated. cAMP rises and, through protein kinase A, phosphorylates the CFTR Cl- channel — Cl-, followed by Na+ and water, moves into the intestinal lumen. The glucose in the oral rehydration solution provides, through SGLT1 (Na+-glucose symport), the absorption of Na+ and, with it, of water by osmosis; this carrier is not impaired by cAMP.
s0869 A patient with chronic heart failure takes digoxin. After losing potassium because of diarrhea, he developed nausea, yellowish spots in his vision and heart rhythm disturbances.
s0870 What is the mechanism of action of digoxin? Why does hypokalemia increase the toxicity?
s0871 Digoxin inhibits Na+,K+-ATPase in cardiomyocytes — Na+ increases inside the cell, the Na+/Ca2+ exchanger removes less Ca2+, Ca2+ accumulates in the cell and contraction becomes stronger (a positive inotropic effect). Digoxin competes with K+ for the same site on the ATPase: when K+ falls, digoxin binds to the enzyme more and its effect (toxicity) increases — arrhythmias develop.
s0872 An 8-year-old child has recurrent jaundice, anemia and an enlarged spleen. The blood smear shows small spherical erythrocytes, and osmotic resistance is reduced: hemolysis begins at 0.60% NaCl. His father has the same disease.
s0873 What is the diagnosis? What is its biochemical basis?
s0874 Hereditary spherocytosis (Minkowski–Chauffard disease). Because of a defect of membrane skeleton proteins (spectrin, ankyrin), the lipid bilayer is poorly bound to the cytoskeleton, pieces of membrane are lost, the erythrocyte becomes spherical and its surface/volume ratio decreases. Such cells tolerate swelling poorly (reduced osmotic resistance) and are trapped and destroyed in the spleen — hemolytic anemia, raised bilirubin (jaundice), splenomegaly.
