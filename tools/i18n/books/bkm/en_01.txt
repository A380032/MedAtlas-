s0018 Remember
s0019 All catabolic pathways converge on **acetyl-CoA** and are completed through the **Krebs cycle + respiratory chain**. Therefore the main part of the body’s energy (≈ 90% of ATP) is formed in the mitochondria by oxidative phosphorylation.
s0020 Memory aid
s0021 Essential amino acids: **“Very Lazy Ivan Takes Me Late For Tea”** — Valine, Leucine, Isoleucine, Threonine, Methionine, Lysine, Phenylalanine, Tryptophan.
s0022 Caution
s0023 **Hypervitaminosis** occurs mainly with the fat-soluble vitamins (A and D), because they accumulate in tissues: vitamin A hypervitaminosis — headache, liver damage, a teratogenic effect in pregnancy; vitamin D hypervitaminosis — hypercalcemia, calcinosis of the kidneys and vessels. An excess of water-soluble vitamins is excreted in the urine (except B12, which accumulates in the liver for years).
s0024 1. The concept of metabolism
s0025 **Metabolism** is the sum of the chemical reactions that take place in a living organism and provide its exchange of matter and energy with the external environment. Metabolism consists of two interrelated directions:
s0026 **Catabolism (dissimilation)** — the breakdown of complex substances into simpler ones; mainly oxidation reactions, energy is released (exergonic), part of it is stored in the form of ATP; NADH and NADPH are formed.
s0027 **Anabolism (assimilation)** — the synthesis of complex substances (proteins, nucleic acids, polysaccharides, lipids) from simple ones; energy is consumed (endergonic), and NADPH is used mainly as the reductant. Catabolic and anabolic pathways are often not the same: they proceed through different enzymes, are located in different compartments of the cell (for example, fatty acid oxidation — in the mitochondria, synthesis — in the cytosol) and are regulated separately. **Amphibolic pathways** (for example, the Krebs cycle) serve both catabolism and anabolism.

s0030 Stages of metabolism
s0031 **Digestion** — the breakdown of food polymers into monomers in the gastrointestinal tract by enzymes (proteins → amino acids, polysaccharides → monosaccharides, fats → fatty acids, glycerol, monoacylglycerols).
s0032 **Absorption** — the passage of monomers through the intestinal wall into the blood and lymph.
s0033 **Intermediary (intracellular) metabolism** — the conversion of monomers in cells: their breakdown (to obtain energy) and the synthesis of the body’s own substances.
s0034 **Excretion of end products** — CO2 (lungs), H2O, urea, uric acid, creatinine, ammonium salts (kidneys), bilirubin derivatives (intestine).

s0053 A **metabolic pathway** is a chain of transformations of a substance through successive enzymatic reactions; its starting substance is the **substrate**, the intermediates are **metabolites**, and the end product is the **product**. Metabolic pathways may be linear (glycolysis), cyclic (the Krebs cycle, the ornithine cycle) or branched. Each pathway usually has one **key (rate-limiting) enzyme**: it catalyzes the slowest reaction, controls an irreversible reaction and determines the overall rate of the pathway.
s0054 Methods of studying metabolism
s0055 **At the level of the whole organism**: balance experiments (nitrogen balance), calorimetry, respiratory quotient; determination of metabolites in blood and urine.
s0056 **At the organ level**: perfusion of an isolated organ, angiostomy (arteriovenous difference).
s0057 **At the tissue and cell level**: tissue slices, homogenates, cell cultures, isolation of organelles by differential centrifugation.
s0058 **Molecular methods**: the **labeled atoms** method (isotopes 14C, 3H, 15N, 32P) — tracing the fate of metabolites; enzyme inhibitors; genetic methods (knockout organisms, mutants); chromatography, mass spectrometry (metabolomics).
s0059 **Clinical methods**: inborn errors of metabolism (the metabolite before the blocked enzyme accumulates) — “experiments of nature.”
s0060 3. Nutritional biochemistry
s0061 **Nutrition** is a component of metabolism that meets the body’s need for energy and plastic material. The main components of food: proteins, fats, carbohydrates (macronutrients), vitamins and minerals (micronutrients), water and dietary fiber.

s0082 Essential components of food
s0083 **Essential (indispensable)** substances are not synthesized in the body, or not in sufficient amounts, and must come with food:
s0084 **8 essential amino acids**: valine, leucine, isoleucine, threonine, methionine, lysine, phenylalanine, tryptophan; partly essential in children — **arginine and histidine**.
s0085 **Essential (polyunsaturated) fatty acids**: linoleic (ω-6) and α-linolenic (ω-3); arachidonic acid is formed from linoleic acid.
s0086 **Vitamins** (vitamin D and niacin are partly synthesized).
s0087 **Minerals**: macroelements (Na, K, Ca, Mg, P, Cl) and trace elements (Fe, Zn, Cu, I, Se, F, Co, Mn, Mo).
s0088 **The biological value of proteins** is the degree to which a dietary protein is converted into the body’s proteins; it depends on the content of essential amino acids and on digestibility. Egg and milk proteins have the highest biological value; plant proteins are often deficient in lysine (wheat), methionine (legumes) or tryptophan (maize).

s0110 4. Vitamins and their coenzyme function
s0111 **Vitamins** are low-molecular-weight organic substances that are not synthesized in the body, or not in sufficient amounts, and are needed in small quantities (mg, µg); they are neither an energy source nor plastic material, but most of them are part of **coenzymes**. They are divided into **water-soluble** (B group, C) and **fat-soluble** (A, D, E, K) vitamins.

s0172 **Causes of hypovitaminosis and avitaminosis:** insufficient intake with food (exogenous); impaired absorption (gastrointestinal diseases, absence of Castle’s intrinsic factor — B12), increased requirement (pregnancy, growth, illness), suppression of the intestinal microflora by antibiotics (K, B group), the action of **antivitamins** (endogenous).
s0173 **Antivitamins** are substances similar in structure to a vitamin that block its coenzyme function: methotrexate (a folate antagonist — antitumor), dicoumarol and warfarin (vitamin K antagonists — anticoagulants), isoniazid (a B6 antagonist), avidin (binds biotin).
s0174 Thiamine deficiency is common in chronic alcoholism: pyruvate dehydrogenase activity falls, pyruvate and lactate accumulate in the blood, and an energy deficit develops in the brain (Wernicke encephalopathy). Such patients must be given thiamine before glucose — otherwise glucose oxidation uses up the thiamine reserve completely and worsens the condition.
s0175 5. Laboratory work
s0176 Experiment 1: qualitative reaction for vitamin B1 (thiochrome reaction)
s0177 Add to a test tube 1 drop of 5% thiamine solution, 5 drops of 10% NaOH and 1–2 drops of 5% potassium ferricyanide (K3[Fe(CN)6]) solution.
s0178 Add 5–10 drops of isobutyl (or butyl) alcohol, shake and wait for the layers to separate.
s0179 Observe the alcohol layer under ultraviolet light. **Result and analysis:** in an alkaline medium thiamine is oxidized to form **thiochrome** — the alcohol layer **fluoresces** blue under ultraviolet light. This reaction is the basis of the quantitative determination of thiamine.
s0180 Experiment 2: reduction reaction for vitamin B2
s0181 Add to a test tube 10 drops of riboflavin solution (yellow) and 5 drops of concentrated HCl.
s0182 Drop in a small piece of metallic zinc (Zn). **Result and analysis:** with the release of hydrogen riboflavin is reduced: the yellow color turns first pink and then colorless (leucoflavin). Under the action of atmospheric oxygen the color returns. This shows the property of FMN and FAD to accept and donate hydrogen (oxidation-reduction).
s0183 Experiment 3: determination of vitamin C with 2,6-dichlorophenolindophenol (Tillmans reaction)
s0184 Pour into a test tube 2 ml of ascorbic acid solution (or fruit juice) and 1 ml of 2% HCl.
s0185 From a burette titrate dropwise with blue 2,6-dichlorophenolindophenol solution until a pink color persists for 30 s. **Result and analysis:** ascorbic acid is a strong reductant — it reduces the blue dye to the colorless leuco form. Once all the ascorbic acid has been oxidized, the excess dye gives a pink color in the acidic medium. The amount of vitamin C is calculated from the volume of dye used.
s0186 Experiment 4: calculating the energy value of a diet
s0187 Determine from the table the amount (g) of protein, fat and carbohydrate in the student’s daily food.
s0188 Calculate the energy: E = protein × 4 + fat × 9 + carbohydrate × 4 (kcal).
s0189 Find the share (%) of each nutrient in the energy and compare it with the norm (protein 12–15%, fat 25–30%, carbohydrate 55–60%).
