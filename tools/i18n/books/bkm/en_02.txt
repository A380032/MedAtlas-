s0303 Case 1
s0304 A 48-year-old man has abused alcohol for 15 years and is poorly nourished. In the emergency department he was found to have confusion, impaired eye movements and an unsteady gait. Blood pyruvate and lactate levels are increased.
s0305 Which vitamin deficiency is suspected? Why do pyruvate and lactate accumulate? What should be taken into account in treatment?
s0306 Vitamin B1 (thiamine) deficiency — Wernicke encephalopathy. Thiamine diphosphate is the coenzyme of the pyruvate dehydrogenase complex and α-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase. In its deficiency pyruvate is not converted to acetyl-CoA, accumulates and is reduced to lactate (lactic acidosis); the Krebs cycle slows down and an ATP deficit develops in the brain. In treatment thiamine is given parenterally before glucose.
s0307 Case 2
s0308 People living in areas where they eat only maize were observed to have dermatitis on sun-exposed areas of skin, diarrhea and memory loss.
s0309 What is the disease? What is its biochemical cause?
s0310 Pellagra — niacin (vitamin PP) deficiency (“3 Ds”: dermatitis, diarrhea, dementia). In maize niacin is in a bound (non-absorbable) form and tryptophan is low — so the synthesis of niacin from tryptophan is also insufficient. Niacin is part of NAD+ and NADP+; when they are deficient many dehydrogenase reactions are impaired, and rapidly renewing tissues (skin, intestinal epithelium) and the nervous system are damaged.
s0311 Case 3
s0312 A 9-month-old child is fed only cow’s milk and semolina porridge and is rarely taken outdoors. Examination shows a flattened occiput, a “rosary” on the ribs and “bracelets” on the wrists. Blood alkaline phosphatase is increased and phosphate is decreased.
s0313 What is the diagnosis? Explain the biochemical mechanism.
s0314 Rickets — vitamin D deficiency. Lack of sunlight reduces the synthesis of cholecalciferol in the skin, and the diet is also poor in vitamin D. The active form calcitriol (hydroxylated in the liver and kidney) provides the absorption of Ca2+ and phosphate in the intestine; in its deficiency bone mineralization is impaired and osteoid tissue increases (osteoblast activity — alkaline phosphatase rises). Treatment: vitamin D, sunbathing, proper feeding.

s0355 1. The concept of enzymes. Structure
s0356 **Enzymes** are biological catalysts formed in living cells that accelerate chemical reactions. Almost all enzymes are **proteins** (except RNA molecules with catalytic activity — **ribozymes**). The science that studies enzymes is **enzymology**.
s0357 **Simple enzymes** consist only of amino acids (pepsin, trypsin, ribonuclease).
s0358 **Complex enzymes (holoenzyme)** = a protein part (**apoenzyme**) + a nonprotein part (**cofactor**). The cofactor may be a metal ion (Zn2+, Mg2+, Fe2+, Cu2+, Mn2+) or an organic substance — a **coenzyme**. A coenzyme firmly (covalently) bound to the apoenzyme is called a **prosthetic group** (for example, FAD in succinate dehydrogenase). The **active site** is the part of the enzyme molecule that binds the substrate and provides its chemical transformation. It is formed from amino acid residues located in different parts of the polypeptide chain when the tertiary structure forms, and consists of two parts:
s0359 **the binding (contact) part** — recognizes and binds the substrate (determines specificity);
s0360 **the catalytic part** — carries out the chemical transformation (often serine, histidine, cysteine, aspartate and glutamate residues).
s0361 The **allosteric site** is a part located outside the active site that binds regulatory molecules (**effectors**). When an effector binds, the conformation of the enzyme and of its active site changes: an **activator** increases activity, an **inhibitor** decreases it. Allosteric enzymes usually consist of several subunits (quaternary structure).
s0362 **Isoenzymes** are forms of an enzyme that catalyze the same reaction but differ in structure, physicochemical properties and tissue distribution. For example, **lactate dehydrogenase (LDH)** is a tetramer built of H and M subunits, with 5 isoenzymes: LDH1 (H4) — heart muscle, LDH5 (M4) — liver and skeletal muscle. **Creatine kinase**: MM — skeletal muscle, MB — heart, BB — brain.

Each enzyme has a 4-number code (EC 1.1.1.27 — LDH). The working name: substrate name + type of reaction + “-ase” (lactate dehydrogenase).

s0390 3. Mechanism of enzyme action
s0391 For any chemical reaction to start, molecules must overcome the **activation energy** barrier. Enzymes carry the reaction along another route with a lower activation energy and increase its rate 10^8^–10^12^ times. The equilibrium of the reaction and the overall energy change (ΔG) do not change.
s0392 Stages of an enzyme reaction:
s0393 E + S ⇄ ES → EP → E + P
s0394 Binding of the substrate to the active site — formation of the **enzyme–substrate complex (ES)**.
s0395 Strain (deformation) of bonds in the substrate, formation of the transition state.
s0396 Release of the product; the enzyme remains unchanged. Two models of how the substrate fits the active site: E. Fischer’s **“lock and key”** model (rigid fit) and D. Koshland’s **“induced fit”** model — when the substrate binds, the conformation of the active site changes and “adapts” to the substrate (the modern view).
s0397 Chemical mechanisms of catalysis: acid-base catalysis, covalent catalysis (the substrate temporarily forms a covalent bond with serine or cysteine in the active site), metal ion catalysis, approximation and correct orientation of substrates.
s0398 4. Properties of enzymes
s0399 **Specificity** is the selective action of an enzyme on a particular substrate or type of reaction:
s0400 **absolute** — on only one substrate (urease — urea, arginase — arginine);
s0401 **relative (group)** — on a group of substrates with the same bond (pepsin, lipase, phosphatases);
s0402 **stereospecificity** — on only one stereoisomer (L-amino acid oxidase, D-glucose enzymes). **Thermolability.** For every 10 °C rise in temperature the reaction rate doubles, but above 40–50 °C the enzyme protein denatures and its activity falls. The **temperature optimum** of human enzymes is 37–40 °C. At low temperature enzyme activity decreases, but the enzymes do not denature (reversible inactivation) — the cold storage of organs is based on this.
s0403 **pH dependence.** Each enzyme has a **pH optimum**; it depends on the ionization of groups in the active site and of the substrate.

s0414 5. Kinetics of enzyme reactions
s0415 **Dependence on enzyme concentration.** When the substrate is in excess, the reaction rate is directly proportional to the amount of enzyme. The determination of enzyme activity is based on this.
s0416 **Dependence on substrate concentration.** When the substrate is low, the rate increases in direct proportion to it; as the substrate increases, all enzyme molecules become occupied and the rate approaches its maximum (Vmax) — a **hyperbolic curve**. This dependence is expressed by the **Michaelis–Menten equation**:
s0417 v = Vmax · [S] / (Km + [S])
s0418 The **Michaelis constant (K~m~)** is the substrate concentration (mol/l) at which the reaction rate equals half the maximum rate. Km shows the **affinity** of the enzyme for the substrate: the smaller the Km, the higher the affinity. For example, the Km of hexokinase for glucose is ≈ 0.1 mmol/l (high affinity, it works even when glucose is low), while that of glucokinase is ≈ 10 mmol/l (it works only when glucose is high — in the liver after a meal).
s0419 To find Km and Vmax precisely, the double-reciprocal plot — the **Lineweaver–Burk plot** (1/v against 1/[S]) — is used: the straight line crosses the y-axis at 1/Vmax and the x-axis at −1/Km.

s0422 **Units of enzyme activity:** the international unit (IU, U) — the amount of enzyme that converts 1 µmol of substrate per minute; the **katal** (SI) — the amount of enzyme that converts 1 mol of substrate per second (1 U = 16.67 nkat). **Specific activity** is the activity per 1 mg of protein.
s0423 6. Enzyme inhibitors
s0424 **Inhibitors** are substances that reduce enzyme activity.
s0425 **Irreversible inhibition** — the inhibitor binds covalently to a functional group of the active site: organophosphorus compounds (sarin, chlorophos) with the serine of acetylcholinesterase, aspirin acetylates cyclooxygenase, heavy metals (Hg2+, Pb2+) with SH groups, cyanides with the iron of cytochrome oxidase.
s0426 **Reversible inhibition:**
s0427 **Competitive** — the inhibitor is similar in structure to the substrate and competes with the substrate for the active site. Its effect can be abolished by increasing the substrate concentration. **K~m~ increases, V~max~ does not change.** Examples: malonate — succinate dehydrogenase; sulfonamides — folate synthesis from para-aminobenzoic acid in bacteria; methotrexate — dihydrofolate reductase; statins — HMG-CoA reductase.
s0428 **Noncompetitive** — the inhibitor binds outside the active site and changes the enzyme’s conformation; the effect does not disappear even when the substrate is increased. **V~max~ decreases, K~m~ does not change.**
s0429 **Allosteric inhibition** — binding of an effector to the allosteric site (see below).
s0430 Many drugs are enzyme inhibitors: ACE inhibitors (captopril, enalapril — angiotensin-converting enzyme), statins (HMG-CoA reductase), allopurinol (xanthine oxidase — in gout), proton pump inhibitors (H+,K+-ATPase), NSAIDs (cyclooxygenase), neostigmine (acetylcholinesterase — in myasthenia). In methanol poisoning ethanol is given: ethanol **competes** with methanol for alcohol dehydrogenase and slows its conversion into toxic formaldehyde.
s0431 7. Regulation of enzyme activity
s0432 **Change in the amount of enzyme** — induction or repression of synthesis (under the action of hormones and substrates; for example, cortisol induces the enzymes of gluconeogenesis, insulin induces glucokinase) and the rate of degradation.
