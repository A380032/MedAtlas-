s1927 When a 6-month-old child was started on fruit juice and sweetened porridge as complementary food, pallor, sweating, vomiting, seizures and liver enlargement were observed after meals. While breastfed, the child had no complaints.
s1928 Which disease is suspected? Explain the mechanism of the hypoglycemia.
s1929 Hereditary fructose intolerance — aldolase B deficiency. Fructose (juice, sucrose) is converted in the liver by fructokinase into fructose-1-phosphate, but it is not broken down and accumulates. Phosphate and ATP are consumed in this process (hypophosphatemia, ATP deficit — liver damage). Fructose-1-phosphate inhibits glycogen phosphorylase and aldolase — glycogenolysis and gluconeogenesis stop and severe hypoglycemia develops. Breast milk contains no fructose. Treatment — complete exclusion of fructose, sucrose and sorbitol from the diet.
s1930 A 45-year-old man drank a lot of alcohol for 2 days without eating. He was brought in unconscious: blood glucose 2.1 mmol/l, lactate increased.
s1931 Explain the mechanism of alcoholic hypoglycemia.
s1932 In starvation the liver glycogen is exhausted within 24 hours and blood glucose is maintained by gluconeogenesis. In the liver ethanol is oxidized by alcohol dehydrogenase and aldehyde dehydrogenase and generates a lot of NADH; the NADH/NAD+ ratio rises sharply. As a result pyruvate is reduced to lactate and oxaloacetate to malate — the main substrates for gluconeogenesis (pyruvate, oxaloacetate) are lacking and gluconeogenesis stops. Hypoglycemia and lactic acidosis develop. Help: intravenous glucose (thiamine first).

s1995 Functions in the body:
s1996 **energy** — the oxidation of 1 g of fat gives 9 kcal; TAG is the main energy store (10–15 kg in adipose tissue — enough for 40 days of starvation);
s1997 **structural** — phospholipids, glycolipids and cholesterol form the basis of membranes;
s1998 **regulatory** — steroid hormones, eicosanoids, second messengers (DAG, IP3);
s1999 **protective and heat-insulating** — the subcutaneous fat layer, the fat around internal organs;
s2000 a source of fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K) and necessary for their absorption;
s2001 **bile acids** — digestion and absorption of fats; **surfactant** (dipalmitoylphosphatidylcholine) — keeps the alveoli from collapsing. **Essential fatty acids** are linoleic (ω-6) and α-linolenic (ω-3): humans have no enzymes that introduce a double bond further than carbon 9 from the carboxyl group. Sources: vegetable oils (sunflower, corn, linseed), fish oil (eicosapentaenoic and docosahexaenoic acids — ω-3). Their deficiency causes dermatitis, growth retardation and impaired wound healing.
s2002 2. Digestion of fats
s2003 The daily fat requirement of adults is 80–100 g; ≈ 90% of it is TAG.
s2004 **In the oral cavity and the stomach** fats are hardly digested in adults: gastric lipase has low activity (its pH optimum is 4–5, while gastric juice is strongly acidic) and the fat is not emulsified. **In newborns**, however, the lingual and gastric lipases play an important role: the gastric medium is less acidic, the fat of milk is already emulsified, and breast milk also contains its own lipase.
s2005 **In the small intestine** the main digestion of fats takes place:
s2006 **Emulsification** — the breaking up of large fat droplets into small ones; this is helped by the salts of **bile acids** (surface-active substances), peristalsis and the CO2 bubbles formed by bicarbonate. Emulsification increases many times over the surface on which lipase acts.
s2007 **Hydrolysis** — by the enzymes of the pancreas: – **pancreatic lipase** (its activating protein is **colipase**) cleaves the ester bonds of TAG at
s2008 positions 1 and 3: TAG → 2-monoacylglycerol + 2 fatty acids; – **phospholipase A~2~** (activated by trypsin) removes the fatty acid at position 2 from a
s2009 phospholipid → lysophospholipid; – **cholesterol esterase** breaks down cholesterol esters into cholesterol and a fatty acid.

s2012 Bile acids
s2013 **Bile acids** are synthesized in the liver from cholesterol (the key enzyme is 7α-hydroxylase). The **primary** bile acids are cholic and chenodeoxycholic acid; they are **conjugated** with glycine or taurine (glycocholic and taurocholic acids) and enter the intestine with the bile as sodium and potassium salts. In the intestine, under the action of bacteria, the **secondary** bile acids — deoxycholic and lithocholic acid — are formed.
s2014 The functions of bile acids: they emulsify fats; activate lipase; form **mixed micelles** with the products of digestion and provide for their absorption; are necessary for the absorption of fat-soluble vitamins; keep cholesterol dissolved in the bile.
s2015 **The enterohepatic circulation:** ≈ 95% of the bile acids are reabsorbed in the terminal ileum, return to the liver through the portal vein and are secreted into the bile again (6–10 times a day). Only ≈ 0.5 g/day is lost in the feces — this is the main route by which cholesterol is removed from the body.
s2016 3. Absorption of the products of digestion and their resynthesis
s2017 Monoacylglycerols, long-chain fatty acids, cholesterol and lysophospholipids form **mixed micelles** with bile acids and are delivered to the surface of the enterocytes; the lipid components cross the membrane by diffusion (and through special carriers), while the bile acids remain in the intestinal lumen. **Short- and medium-chain** fatty acids (C4–C12) are absorbed without micelles and pass directly into the blood of the portal vein (they are abundant in milk fat; in malabsorption, food with medium-chain TAG is prescribed).
s2018 **Resynthesis in the enterocytes** (in the endoplasmic reticulum):
s2019 the fatty acids are activated: fatty acid + CoA + ATP → acyl-CoA (acyl-CoA synthetase);
s2020 **the monoacylglycerol pathway**: 2-monoacylglycerol + 2 acyl-CoA → TAG;
s2021 phospholipids are formed from lysophospholipids, and cholesterol esters from cholesterol (the ACAT enzyme). **The significance of resynthesis:** dietary fats are converted into fats characteristic of the human body (the fatty acid composition changes); the products of digestion are “carried away” from the intestinal cell, which allows absorption to continue; the TAG becomes part of the transport form — the chylomicrons.
s2022 The resynthesized lipids are assembled together with the protein **apo B-48** into **chylomicrons**, which leave by exocytosis into the **lymph** (the intestinal lymph capillaries — the “lacteals”) and enter the blood through the thoracic duct (this is why the serum is cloudy after a fatty meal — **alimentary lipemia**).
s2023 **Steatorrhea** is an increase of fat in the feces (> 7 g per day). Causes: **lack of bile** (obstructive jaundice, obstruction of the bile ducts, liver diseases) — emulsification and micelle formation are impaired; **lipase deficiency** (chronic pancreatitis, cystic fibrosis, a pancreatic tumor); **impaired absorption** (celiac disease, Crohn disease, resection of the ileum). The feces are shiny, sticky, large in amount and have a sharp odor. Consequences: deficiency of fat-soluble vitamins (night blindness, osteomalacia, bleeding), a shortage of essential fatty acids, weight loss.
s2024 4. Blood lipoproteins
s2025 Lipids are insoluble in water, so they are transported in the blood in **lipoproteins** — lipid-protein complexes. The core of a lipoprotein particle contains hydrophobic lipids (TAG, cholesterol esters), and its surface amphiphilic substances — phospholipids, free cholesterol and **apoproteins**.
s2026 **Functions of the apoproteins:** forming the structure of the particle (apo B-48, B-100), activating enzymes (apo C-II — lipoprotein lipase, apo A-I — LCAT), binding to receptors (apo B-100 and apo E — LDL receptors).

s2059 Lipoprotein lipase
s2060 **Lipoprotein lipase (LPL)** is an enzyme located on the surface of the capillary endothelium (bound to heparan sulfate); it hydrolyzes the TAG of chylomicrons and VLDL. The fatty acids formed enter the tissues: in **muscle** they are oxidized (energy), in **adipose tissue** they are converted back into TAG and stored. LPL is activated by **apo C-II**; its synthesis in adipose tissue is increased by **insulin** (after a meal fat goes into store), and in muscle by starvation and physical activity. Heparin releases LPL from the endothelium into the blood (the **“clearing factor”** — lipemic serum becomes clear).
s2061 The **remnant chylomicrons**, emptied of TAG, are taken up by the liver (through apo E); VLDL are converted into IDL and LDL. LDL enter the tissues by endocytosis through **LDL receptors** (topic 10).
s2062 **Congenital deficiency of LPL or apo C-II** (type I hyperlipoproteinemia) — chylomicrons rise sharply in the blood: the serum is “milky”, triglycerides > 10–20 mmol/l, with attacks of acute pancreatitis, xanthomas on the skin and enlargement of the liver and spleen. Treatment — a diet with sharply restricted fat (medium-chain TAG are allowed, because they do not form chylomicrons).

s2078 Experiment 1: the emulsifying action of bile acids
s2079 Pour 2 ml of water into each of three test tubes.
s2080 Add 2 drops of vegetable oil to tube 1, 2 drops of oil + 5 drops of bile (or 1% sodium cholate) to tube 2, and 2 drops of oil + 5 drops of 1% sodium carbonate to tube 3.
s2081 Shake all of them vigorously and leave for 5–10 minutes. **Result and analysis:** in tube 1 the oil quickly rises to the top (the emulsion is unstable). In the tube with bile a stable, milky emulsion forms — bile acids lower the surface tension of the droplets. In the tube with sodium carbonate the emulsion is also more stable — soap is formed with the free fatty acids of the oil.
s2082 Experiment 2: determination of pancreatic lipase activity and the effect of bile
s2083 Pour 10 ml of milk (or a fat emulsion) and 2 drops of phenolphthalein into each of two flasks; neutralize with 0.1 N NaOH to a pale pink color.
s2084 Add 1 ml of pancreatin solution to flask 1 and 1 ml of pancreatin + 1 ml of bile to flask 2; keep at 37 °C.
s2085 Every 10 minutes take 1 ml from the flasks and titrate with 0.05 N NaOH to a pink color (for 30–40 minutes). **Result and analysis:** with time the amount of NaOH used for titration increases — lipase breaks down TAG and forms fatty acids. In the flask with bile this happens faster: the bile acids emulsify the fat, increase the surface on which lipase acts and activate the lipase.
