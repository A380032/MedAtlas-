s3643 A 50-year-old man ate a lot of meat and drank beer at a party, and at night woke up with severe pain at the base of his big toe: the joint is swollen and red, and cannot be touched. Uric acid in blood is 610 µmol/l. Needle-shaped crystals were found in the joint fluid.
s3644 What is the diagnosis? Explain the causes of the hyperuricemia and of the attack. State the principles of treatment.
s3645 Acute gouty arthritis. Purine-rich food (meat) and beer (purines + alcohol) increased uric acid formation; in the metabolism of alcohol lactate accumulates and reduces urate secretion in the kidney. When uric acid exceeds the saturation limit, monosodium urate crystals precipitate in cooler peripheral joints; neutrophils engulf them and release inflammatory mediators. During an attack — colchicine, NSAIDs or glucocorticoids; afterwards, long-term — allopurinol (a xanthine oxidase inhibitor), diet, restriction of alcohol, plenty of fluid.
s3646 In a patient with lymphoma, 2 days after chemotherapy was started the volume of urine fell sharply. Uric acid in blood is 950 µmol/l, potassium and phosphate are increased, calcium is decreased, creatinine is increased.
s3647 What condition is this? Explain its mechanism and the measures for preventing it.
s3648 Tumor lysis syndrome. Under the action of chemotherapy many tumor cells break down and the purines of their nucleic acids are converted into uric acid; intracellular K+ and phosphate enter the blood, and phosphate binds calcium and causes hypocalcemia. Uric acid crystallizes in the renal tubules and causes acute renal failure. Prevention: allopurinol or rasburicase before chemotherapy (it breaks uric acid down to the much more soluble allantoin), plenty of fluid (hydration), monitoring of electrolytes.
s3649 A 1-year-old child was found to have megaloblastic anemia and growth retardation. Treatment with folate and B12 gave no effect. Orotic acid in the urine is sharply increased, ammonia in blood is normal.
s3650 What disease is this? Why does the anemia not respond to folate and B12? What is the treatment?
s3651 Hereditary orotic aciduria — deficiency of UMP synthase. Orotic acid is not converted into UMP and is excreted in large amounts in the urine; pyrimidine nucleotides (UTP, CTP, dTTP) are lacking, and DNA synthesis is disturbed in rapidly dividing cells (megaloblastic anemia). The disease is caused not by a deficiency of folate or B12 but by an enzyme defect, and for that reason vitamins have no effect. The normal ammonia level rules out a disturbance of the ornithine cycle. Treatment — uridine (a source of UMP and, through UTP, a feedback inhibitor of carbamoyl phosphate synthetase II).

s3681 1. The general scheme of catabolism
s3682 The catabolism of carbohydrates, lipids and proteins proceeds in three stages (topic 5): 1) breakdown of polymers into monomers; 2) conversion of the monomers into a few common intermediate products — **pyruvate, acetyl-CoA**, metabolites of the Krebs cycle; 3) the Krebs cycle and oxidative phosphorylation. **Acetyl-CoA** and the **Krebs cycle** are the “crossroads” of all metabolic pathways.
s3683 Figure 15.1
s3684 The main points of convergence of carbohydrate, lipid and amino acid metabolism (drawn after Severin E.S.)
s3685 The main points of convergence:
s3686 **glucose-6-phosphate** — glycolysis, synthesis and breakdown of glycogen, the pentose phosphate pathway;
s3687 **dihydroxyacetone phosphate (DHAP)** — the link between glycolysis and the synthesis of triacylglycerols (glycerol-3-phosphate);
s3688 **pyruvate** — glycolysis, gluconeogenesis, alanine metabolism (ALT), formation of acetyl-CoA and oxaloacetate;
s3689 **acetyl-CoA** — synthesis of fatty acids, ketone bodies and cholesterol, and the Krebs cycle;
s3690 **oxaloacetate, α-ketoglutarate** — transamination with aspartate and glutamate, gluconeogenesis. **An important rule: the pyruvate dehydrogenase reaction is irreversible**, and therefore **glucose is not synthesized** from acetyl-CoA (and hence from even-chain **fatty acids**): the 2 carbons of acetyl-CoA leave the Krebs cycle as 2 CO2, and the net amount of oxaloacetate does not increase. The propionyl-CoA part of odd-chain fatty acids (through succinyl-CoA) and the **glycerol** part of triacylglycerols are converted into glucose.

s3706 3. Synthesis of glucose from amino acids and glycerol
s3707 In starvation and physical exertion **gluconeogenesis** (topic 7) maintains glucose in the blood. The main substrates:
s3708 **amino acids** — muscle proteins break down and reach the liver and the kidney in the form of **alanine** (the glucose-alanine cycle) and **glutamine**; alanine → pyruvate → glucose;
s3709 **glycerol** — from lipolysis in adipose tissue; in the liver glycerol kinase → glycerol-3-phosphate → DHAP → glucose;
s3710 **lactate** — from muscle and erythrocytes (the Cori cycle). Thus, although fatty acids are not converted into glucose, their oxidation supplies the liver with **ATP and NADH** for gluconeogenesis.
s3711 4. Biosynthesis of amino acids from carbohydrates and fats
s3712 The body can synthesize **nonessential amino acids** from the intermediate products of carbohydrates:
s3713 pyruvate + transamination → **alanine**;
s3714 oxaloacetate → **aspartate** → asparagine;
s3715 α-ketoglutarate → **glutamate** → glutamine, proline, arginine;
s3716 3-phosphoglycerate → **serine** → glycine, cysteine (with the sulfur of methionine). The nitrogen is taken from other amino acids or from NH3 (glutamate dehydrogenase). **Essential amino acids** (valine, leucine, isoleucine, threonine, methionine, phenylalanine, tryptophan, lysine; in children also arginine and histidine) are not formed from carbohydrates and fats — they come only from food.
s3717 **Synthesis of fats from carbohydrates:** excess glucose → pyruvate → acetyl-CoA (mitochondria) → into the cytosol in the form of citrate → synthesis of fatty acids; NADPH from the pentose phosphate pathway and from malic enzyme; glycerol-3-phosphate from DHAP. For this reason food rich in carbohydrates leads to obesity.

s3732 **Insulin** is the only hypoglycemic, **anabolic** hormone: through GLUT4 it increases the entry of glucose into muscle and adipose tissue; through phosphoprotein phosphatases it activates glycogen synthase, pyruvate dehydrogenase and acetyl-CoA carboxylase; it induces the synthesis of glucokinase, PFK-1 and pyruvate kinase.

s3734 6. Metabolism in the fed and the fasting state
s3735 **The absorptive period (2–4 hours after a meal):** the insulin/glucagon index is high. The liver converts glucose into glycogen and fatty acids (which it releases in VLDL); muscle stores glucose as glycogen and synthesizes protein; adipose tissue takes the triacylglycerols of chylomicrons and VLDL (lipoprotein lipase) into its stores.
s3736 **The postabsorptive period and starvation:** insulin decreases, and glucagon, adrenaline and cortisol increase.
