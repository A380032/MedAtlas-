s4428 An unvaccinated 6-year-old child has fever, sore throat, a gray, hard-to-remove membrane on the tonsils and swelling of the neck. A week later a heart rhythm disturbance developed (myocarditis).
s4429 What is the disease? What is the molecular mechanism of action of the toxin? Why is the heart affected?
s4430 Diphtheria. The toxin of Corynebacterium diphtheriae enters the cell by endocytosis, and its A fragment transfers an ADP-ribose residue from NAD^+^ to the elongation factor eEF2 (to the diphthamide residue) — eEF2 is inactivated, translocation and protein synthesis stop, and the cell dies. Treatment — antitoxic serum (neutralizes the toxin before it enters the cells) and antibiotics; prevention — vaccine (toxoid).
s4431 A patient has pneumonia; the physician wants to choose one of the antibiotics that act on the 70S ribosome. The patient is a pregnant woman (second trimester).
s4432 Which antibiotics that block protein synthesis are not used in pregnancy, and why? Which group is relatively safe?
s4433 Tetracyclines (30S — block the binding of aminoacyl-tRNA) form chelates with calcium and accumulate in fetal bones and teeth (tooth discoloration, slowed bone growth). s4434 A patient has been diagnosed with sickle cell anemia. In the coding strand of the β-globin gene, codon 6 is GTG instead of GAG.

s4528 3. DNA repair
s4529 Figure 18.1
s4530 Types of DNA damage, repair systems and their defects (drawn after Lippincott’s Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry)
s4531 **Direct repair:** O^6^-methylguanine-DNA methyltransferase transfers the methyl group to itself (a “suicide” enzyme); in bacteria **photolyase** splits thymine dimers under the action of light (absent in humans).
s4532 **Base excision repair (BER):** a damaged base (uracil, 8-oxoguanine, an alkylated base) is recognized by a specific **DNA glycosylase**, which cleaves the N-glycosidic bond; the resulting apurinic/apyrimidinic (AP) site is cut by **AP endonuclease**; DNA polymerase β fills the gap and DNA ligase seals it. This is also why DNA contains thymine instead of uracil: uracil formed by deamination of cytosine is recognized as “foreign”.
s4533 **Nucleotide excision repair (NER):** bulky lesions (thymine dimers, benzo(a)pyrene adducts) — a complex of XP proteins cuts the damaged strand on both sides and removes a fragment of ≈ 24–32 nucleotides; DNA polymerase δ/ε and ligase fill it in.
s4534 **Xeroderma pigmentosum** is a hereditary defect of the NER enzymes (XP-A … XP-G) (autosomal recessive). Thymine dimers formed under sunlight are not repaired: the skin is very sensitive, there are freckles, and multiple **skin cancers** (basal cell carcinoma, melanoma) appear at an early age (before 10 years). Prevention — complete protection from the sun.
s4535 **Mismatch repair (MMR):** after replication, mispaired bases and small loops are recognized by **MSH2/MSH6**, and **MLH1/PMS2** nick the new strand; the new strand is removed by an exonuclease and resynthesized. Its defect causes **microsatellite instability** and **Lynch syndrome** (hereditary nonpolyposis colorectal cancer, endometrial cancer).
s4536 Repair of double-strand breaks:
s4537 **homologous recombination** — uses the sister chromatid as a template (in the S and G~2~ phases) and is accurate; the **BRCA1 and BRCA2** proteins take part — their defect sharply increases the risk of breast and ovarian cancer; the ATM kinase senses the break (ataxia-telangiectasia);
s4538 **nonhomologous end joining (NHEJ)** — Ku proteins and DNA-PK; fast, but nucleotides are sometimes lost (error-prone). **SOS repair (translesion synthesis):** special low-fidelity DNA polymerases “bypass” the damaged site — the probability of mutations is high.
s4539 4. Genetic variability. The human genome. Protein polymorphism
s4540 **Mechanisms of genetic variability:** mutations, recombination in meiosis (crossing-over), independent assortment of chromosomes, transposons.
s4541 **The human genome:** ≈ 3.1 × 10^9^ base pairs; the protein-coding part is ≈ 1.5%; the rest consists of introns, regulatory elements, repetitive sequences (Alu, LINE, microsatellites) and pseudogenes. The genomes of two people are ≈ 99.9% identical; the differences consist mainly of **single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs)** and copy number variations.
s4542 **Polymorphism** means gene variants that occur in a population with a frequency of ≥ 1%. **Protein polymorphism** means several variants (allelic forms) of one protein:
s4543 **AB0 blood groups** — glycosyltransferase variants;
s4544 the **HLA system** — very high polymorphism (transplantation, susceptibility to autoimmune diseases);
s4545 **hemoglobin variants** — HbS, HbC, HbE (more than 300);
s4546 **glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase** variants (topic 7);
s4547 **cytochrome P450** polymorphism (CYP2D6, CYP2C19) — “fast” or “slow” drug metabolizers (**pharmacogenetics**);
s4548 **N-acetyltransferase** — slow and fast acetylators of isoniazid;
s4549 **pseudocholinesterase** — with the atypical variant suxamethonium acts for a long time (apnea);
s4550 **α~1~-antitrypsin** variants (PiZZ — emphysema, liver cirrhosis).
s4551 5. Hereditary diseases and DNA technologies
s4552 **Monogenic diseases:** autosomal dominant (familial hypercholesterolemia, Marfan syndrome, Huntington disease), autosomal recessive (phenylketonuria, cystic fibrosis, sickle cell anemia, glycogenoses), X-linked (hemophilia A and B, Duchenne muscular dystrophy, G6PD deficiency, Lesch–Nyhan syndrome). **Mitochondrial** — through the mother. **Multifactorial** — type 2 diabetes, atherosclerosis, hypertension.

s4554 **PCR** and real-time PCR — detection of mutations and infections (topic 16);

s4556 **Southern blot** (DNA), **Northern blot** (RNA), **Western blot** (protein);
s4557 **sequencing** — the Sanger method, next-generation sequencing (whole exome/genome);
s4558 **FISH** — detection of chromosomal changes with fluorescent probes;
s4559 **DNA microarrays** — expression of thousands of genes or SNPs;
s4560 **genetic fingerprinting** (microsatellites) — forensic medicine, paternity testing;
s4561 **prenatal diagnosis** — chorionic villi, amniocytes, cell-free fetal DNA in maternal blood.

s4591 **Caspases** are proteases with cysteine in the active site that cleave after aspartate; they are synthesized as procaspases. **Initiator** (8, 9) and **effector** (3, 6, 7) caspases are distinguished. Effector caspases cleave cytoskeletal proteins, nuclear lamins and DNA repair enzymes (PARP) and activate **caspase-activated DNase**.

s4596 7. Basics of cloning and gene therapy
s4597 **Cloning: gene (molecular) cloning** — inserting a gene into a vector and propagating it in bacteria or cell culture (production of recombinant proteins); **cell clones** — genetically identical cells derived from a single cell (monoclonal antibodies — hybridomas); **reproductive cloning** — somatic cell nuclear transfer (“Dolly” the sheep); **therapeutic cloning** — obtaining stem cells matched to the patient. The clinical importance of clones: monoclonal gammopathies (myeloma) and the **clonal** origin of tumors.
