s4444 A patient has pneumonia; the physician wants to choose one of the antibiotics that act on the 70S ribosome. The patient is a pregnant woman (second trimester).

s4447 A patient has been diagnosed with sickle cell anemia. In the coding strand of the β-globin gene, codon 6 is GTG instead of GAG.

s4541 3. DNA repair
s4542 Figure 18.1
s4543 Types of DNA damage, repair systems and their defects (drawn after Lippincott’s Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry)
s4544 **Direct repair:** O6-methylguanine-DNA methyltransferase transfers the methyl group to itself (a “suicide” enzyme); in bacteria **photolyase** splits thymine dimers under the action of light (absent in humans).
s4545 **Base excision repair (BER):** a damaged base (uracil, 8-oxoguanine, an alkylated base) is recognized by a specific **DNA glycosylase**, which cleaves the N-glycosidic bond; the resulting apurinic/apyrimidinic (AP) site is cut by **AP endonuclease**; DNA polymerase β fills the gap and DNA ligase seals it. This is also why DNA contains thymine instead of uracil: uracil formed by deamination of cytosine is recognized as “foreign”.
s4546 **Nucleotide excision repair (NER):** bulky lesions (thymine dimers, benzo(a)pyrene adducts) — a complex of XP proteins cuts the damaged strand on both sides and removes a fragment of ≈ 24–32 nucleotides; DNA polymerase δ/ε and ligase fill it in.
s4547 **Xeroderma pigmentosum** is a hereditary defect of the NER enzymes (XP-A … XP-G) (autosomal recessive). Thymine dimers formed under sunlight are not repaired: the skin is very sensitive, there are freckles, and multiple **skin cancers** (basal cell carcinoma, melanoma) appear at an early age (before 10 years). Prevention — complete protection from the sun.
s4548 **Mismatch repair (MMR):** after replication, mispaired bases and small loops are recognized by **MSH2/MSH6**, and **MLH1/PMS2** nick the new strand; the new strand is removed by an exonuclease and resynthesized. Its defect causes **microsatellite instability** and **Lynch syndrome** (hereditary nonpolyposis colorectal cancer, endometrial cancer).
s4549 Repair of double-strand breaks:
s4550 **homologous recombination** — uses the sister chromatid as a template (in the S and G2 phases) and is accurate; the **BRCA1 and BRCA2** proteins take part — their defect sharply increases the risk of breast and ovarian cancer; the ATM kinase senses the break (ataxia-telangiectasia);
s4551 **nonhomologous end joining (NHEJ)** — Ku proteins and DNA-PK; fast, but nucleotides are sometimes lost (error-prone). **SOS repair (translesion synthesis):** special low-fidelity DNA polymerases “bypass” the damaged site — the probability of mutations is high.
s4552 4. Genetic variability. The human genome. Protein polymorphism
s4553 **Mechanisms of genetic variability:** mutations, recombination in meiosis (crossing-over), independent assortment of chromosomes, transposons.
s4554 **The human genome:** ≈ 3.1 × 109 base pairs; the protein-coding part is ≈ 1.5%; the rest consists of introns, regulatory elements, repetitive sequences (Alu, LINE, microsatellites) and pseudogenes. The genomes of two people are ≈ 99.9% identical; the differences consist mainly of **single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs)** and copy number variations.
s4555 **Polymorphism** means gene variants that occur in a population with a frequency of ≥ 1%. **Protein polymorphism** means several variants (allelic forms) of one protein:
s4556 **AB0 blood groups** — glycosyltransferase variants;
s4557 the **HLA system** — very high polymorphism (transplantation, susceptibility to autoimmune diseases);
s4558 **hemoglobin variants** — HbS, HbC, HbE (more than 300);
s4559 **glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase** variants (topic 7);
s4560 **cytochrome P450** polymorphism (CYP2D6, CYP2C19) — “fast” or “slow” drug metabolizers (**pharmacogenetics**);
s4561 **N-acetyltransferase** — slow and fast acetylators of isoniazid;
s4562 **pseudocholinesterase** — with the atypical variant suxamethonium acts for a long time (apnea);
s4563 **α~1~-antitrypsin** variants (PiZZ — emphysema, liver cirrhosis).
s4564 5. Hereditary diseases and DNA technologies

s4604 **Caspases** are proteases with cysteine in the active site that cleave after aspartate; they are synthesized as procaspases. **Initiator** (8, 9) and **effector** (3, 6, 7) caspases are distinguished. Effector caspases cleave cytoskeletal proteins, nuclear lamins and DNA repair enzymes (PARP) and activate **caspase-activated DNase**.
s4605 The **Bcl-2 family** — antiapoptotic (Bcl-2, Bcl-xL) and proapoptotic (Bax, Bak, Bid, Bad) proteins regulate the permeability of the outer mitochondrial membrane;
s4606 **p53** (“guardian of the genome”) accumulates when DNA is damaged, arrests the cell cycle in G1 (p21) and activates repair; if the damage cannot be repaired, it triggers apoptosis through Bax, PUMA and NOXA;

s4609 7. Basics of cloning and gene therapy
s4610 **Cloning: gene (molecular) cloning** — inserting a gene into a vector and propagating it in bacteria or cell culture (production of recombinant proteins); **cell clones** — genetically identical cells derived from a single cell (monoclonal antibodies — hybridomas); **reproductive cloning** — somatic cell nuclear transfer (“Dolly” the sheep); **therapeutic cloning** — obtaining stem cells matched to the patient. The clinical importance of clones: monoclonal gammopathies (myeloma) and the **clonal** origin of tumors.
