s7313 it connects with the sympathetic and parasympathetic fibers, “relays” and modulates their influence;
s7314 its mediators are diverse: acetylcholine, noradrenaline, serotonin, ATP (purinergic), VIP, NO, substance P.
s7315 Autonomic ganglia and the axon reflex
s7316 In addition to transmitting impulses, autonomic ganglia also function as **peripheral nerve centers**: convergence, multiplication, summation and rhythm transformation of impulses take place in them; **peripheral reflexes** can close through the ganglia.
s7317 The **axon reflex** is a reflex carried out through the branching of a sensory neuron's axon, without the participation of the CNS or even a ganglion: when the skin is stimulated, the impulse passes from one branch to another and dilates nearby vessels (reddening of the skin — dermographism, the “red flare” in inflammation).

s7348 4. Effects of the sympathetic and parasympathetic divisions on organs
s7349 Most organs have **dual innervation**; the divisions usually have **opposite (antagonistic)** effects, but their effects are **complementary (functional synergism)** and together finely adjust the organ's activity. Sometimes both divisions act in the same direction (salivary glands — topic 18).

s7410 Centers regulating autonomic functions:
s7411 **The spinal cord** — centers of micturition, defecation, erection, sweating and vascular reflexes (segmental level).
s7412 **The medulla oblongata and pons** — the centers of breathing, circulation, the heart, salivation, swallowing, vomiting and coughing.
s7413 **The midbrain** — the pupillary reflex, accommodation.
s7414 **The hypothalamus** — the **highest autonomic center**: its anterior part — parasympathetic effects, its posterior part — sympathetic effects; thermoregulation, hunger, thirst; it integrates autonomic, endocrine and behavioral reactions.
s7415 **The limbic system, cerebellum, basal ganglia and cerebral cortex** — autonomic reactions associated with emotions and behavior (palpitations with excitement, pallor with fear), conditioned autonomic reflexes.
s7416 6. The adrenal medulla
s7417 The adrenal medulla is embryologically and functionally a **modified sympathetic ganglion**: its chromaffin cells resemble postganglionic neurons, but instead of an axon they release hormones directly into the blood. They are innervated by **preganglionic sympathetic fibers** (acetylcholine, N~N~ receptors).
s7418 Hormones secreted: **adrenaline (≈ 80%)** and **noradrenaline (≈ 20%)**. Their synthesis: tyrosine → DOPA → dopamine → noradrenaline → adrenaline (the enzyme phenylethanolamine-N-methyltransferase — activated by cortisol; the blood flowing from the adrenal cortex helps this). Inactivation — by the enzymes MAO and COMT; the metabolite (vanillylmandelic acid) is excreted in the urine.

s7435 The **sympathoadrenal system** is the single functional union of the sympathetic nervous system and the adrenal medulla (W. Cannon). s7437 Exercise 1: examining dermographism
s7438 Draw a line on the skin of the subject's chest or forearm with a blunt spatula using moderate pressure.
s7439 After 10–20 s observe the color, width and persistence of the line that appears. **Interpretation: white dermographism** (a white line that disappears quickly) — predominance of sympathetic tone (constriction of skin vessels); **red dermographism** (a wide red line persisting for a long time — more than 1–3 minutes) — predominance of parasympathetic tone; **raised (urticarial)** dermographism — increased vascular permeability. Normally — a narrow pink line that disappears in 1–2 minutes. The axon reflex takes part in the mechanism of the red line.
s7440 Exercise 2: the clino-orthostatic test
s7441 **Orthostatic test:** measure the pulse and pressure after 5 minutes of lying down; measure them again 1 minute after standing up. Normally the pulse increases by 10–16 /min (sympathetic reactivity).
s7442 **Clinostatic test:** the pulse is measured after lying down from the standing position. Normally the pulse decreases by 4–12 /min (parasympathetic reactivity). **Interpretation:** an increase in pulse of > 16 in the orthostatic test — increased sympathetic reactivity; a decrease of > 12 in the clinostatic test — increased parasympathetic reactivity.
s7443 Exercise 3: calculating the Kérdö autonomic index
s7444 Measure the diastolic pressure (D) and pulse (P) at rest.
s7445 Kérdö index = (1 − D / P) × 100. **Interpretation:** close to 0 — autonomic balance (eutonia); positive — predominance of sympathetic tone; negative — predominance of parasympathetic tone.
s7446 **Example:** D = 80, P = 70 → (1 − 80/70) × 100 = −14 — parasympathetic predominance.

s7539 Explain the mechanism of poisoning. Which drug is used as an antidote, and why?
s7540 Organophosphorus compounds irreversibly inhibit acetylcholinesterase — acetylcholine accumulates in the synapses without being broken down. The M-cholinergic receptors are excessively stimulated (muscarinic effects): miosis, profuse salivation and sweating, bronchoconstriction and increased mucus, bradycardia, increased intestinal motility. Stimulation of N-cholinergic receptors (nicotinic effects) — fasciculations in the skeletal muscles, then paralysis. The antidote is atropine (an M-cholinergic blocker): it eliminates the muscarinic effects; AChE reactivators (pralidoxime) are also used. In respiratory failure — artificial ventilation.
s7541 Before an exam a student's heart is beating fast, his palms are sweating, his mouth is dry, his pupils are dilated and he has no appetite.
s7542 Which system is activated? Explain each sign in terms of mediators and receptors.
s7543 Because of stress the sympathoadrenal system has been activated (an emotional reaction — via the limbic system and hypothalamus). Tachycardia — the action of noradrenaline and adrenaline on β~1~ receptors in the heart. Sweating of the palms — the sympathetic cholinergic innervation of the sweat glands (acetylcholine, M receptors). Dry mouth — under sympathetic influence little thick saliva is secreted and the parasympathetic influence decreases. Pupil dilation — α~1~ receptors in the radial muscle. Lack of appetite and inhibition of digestion — the sympathetic division inhibits gastrointestinal motility and secretion. These are elements of the “fight or flight” reaction.
