s7346 it connects with the sympathetic and parasympathetic fibers, “relays” and modulates their influence;
s7347 its mediators are diverse: acetylcholine, noradrenaline, serotonin, ATP (purinergic), VIP, NO, substance P.
s7348 Autonomic ganglia and the axon reflex
s7349 In addition to transmitting impulses, autonomic ganglia also function as **peripheral nerve centers**: convergence, multiplication, summation and rhythm transformation of impulses take place in them; **peripheral reflexes** can close through the ganglia.
s7350 The **axon reflex** is a reflex carried out through the branching of a sensory neuron's axon, without the participation of the CNS or even a ganglion: when the skin is stimulated, the impulse passes from one branch to another and dilates nearby vessels (reddening of the skin — dermographism, the “red flare” in inflammation).

s7383 Most organs have **dual innervation**; the divisions usually have **opposite (antagonistic)** effects, but their effects are **complementary (functional synergism)** and together finely adjust the organ's activity. Sometimes both divisions act in the same direction (salivary glands — topic 18).

s7450 6. The adrenal medulla
s7451 The adrenal medulla is embryologically and functionally a **modified sympathetic ganglion**: its chromaffin cells resemble postganglionic neurons, but instead of an axon they release hormones directly into the blood. They are innervated by **preganglionic sympathetic fibers** (acetylcholine, N~N~ receptors).
s7452 Hormones secreted: **adrenaline (≈ 80%)** and **noradrenaline (≈ 20%)**. Their synthesis: tyrosine → DOPA → dopamine → noradrenaline → adrenaline (the enzyme phenylethanolamine-N-methyltransferase — activated by cortisol; the blood flowing from the adrenal cortex helps this). Inactivation — by the enzymes MAO and COMT; the metabolite (vanillylmandelic acid) is excreted in the urine.

s7469 The **sympathoadrenal system** is the single functional union of the sympathetic nervous system and the adrenal medulla (W. Cannon). s7474 permeability. Normally — a narrow pink line that disappears in 1–2 minutes. The axon reflex takes part in the mechanism of the red line.
s7475 Exercise 2: the clino-orthostatic test
s7476 **Orthostatic test:** measure the pulse and pressure after 5 minutes of lying down; measure them again at the 1- minute after standing up. Normally the pulse increases by 10–16 /min (sympathetic reactivity).
s7477 **Clinostatic test:** the pulse is measured after lying down from the standing position. Normally the pulse decreases by 4– 12 /min (parasympathetic reactivity). **Interpretation:** an increase in pulse of > 16 in the orthostatic test — increased sympathetic reactivity; a decrease of > 12 in the clinostatic test — increased parasympathetic reactivity.
s7478 Exercise 3: calculating the Kérdö autonomic index
s7479 Measure the diastolic pressure (D) and pulse (P) at rest.
s7480 Kérdö index = (1 − D / P) × 100. **Interpretation:** close to 0 — autonomic balance (eutonia); positive — predominance of sympathetic tone; negative — predominance of parasympathetic tone.
s7481 **Example:** D = 80, P = 70 → (1 − 80/70) × 100 = −14 — parasympathetic predominance.

s7574 Explain the mechanism of poisoning. Which drug is used as an antidote, and why?
s7575 Organophosphorus compounds irreversibly inhibit acetylcholinesterase — acetylcholine accumulates in the synapses without being broken down. The M-cholinergic receptors are excessively stimulated (muscarinic effects): miosis, profuse salivation and sweating, bronchoconstriction and increased mucus, bradycardia, increased intestinal motility. Stimulation of N-cholinergic receptors (nicotinic effects) — fasciculations in the skeletal muscles, then paralysis. The antidote is atropine (an M-cholinergic blocker): it eliminates the muscarinic effects; AChE reactivators (pralidoxime) are also used. In respiratory failure — artificial ventilation.
s7576 Before an exam a student's heart is beating fast, his palms are sweating, his mouth is dry, his pupils are dilated and he has no appetite.
s7577 Which system is activated? Explain each sign in terms of mediators and receptors.
s7578 Because of stress the sympathoadrenal system has been activated (an emotional reaction — via the limbic system and hypothalamus). Tachycardia — the action of noradrenaline and adrenaline on β~1~ receptors in the heart. Sweating of the palms — the sympathetic cholinergic innervation of the sweat glands (acetylcholine, M- receptors). Dry mouth — under sympathetic influence little thick saliva is secreted and the parasympathetic influence decreases. Pupil dilation — α~1~ receptors in the radial muscle. Lack of appetite and inhibition of digestion — the sympathetic division inhibits gastrointestinal motility and secretion. These are elements of the “fight or flight” reaction.
