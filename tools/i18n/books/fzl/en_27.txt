s5741 A 22-year-old young man complains of bloating, rumbling in the abdomen and watery diarrhea 1–2 hours after drinking milk. He tolerates yogurt and cheese well.
s5742 What condition can be suspected? Which type of digestion is disturbed? Why can he tolerate yogurt?
s5743 Lactase deficiency (lactose intolerance). Lactase is an enzyme bound to the membrane of the microvilli, i.e. membrane digestion is disturbed. Undigested lactose, as an osmotically active substance, draws water into the intestine, and in the large intestine bacteria ferment it, forming gases (H~2~, CO~2~) and organic acids — bloating, rumbling and osmotic diarrhea. In yogurt and cheese most of the lactose has been broken down by lactic acid bacteria, so they are well tolerated. The diagnosis is confirmed by the hydrogen breath test.
s5744 A year after resection of the terminal ileum (1 m) because of Crohn's disease, a patient developed weakness, macrocytic anemia and fatty stools.
s5745 Explain these signs by linking them to the functions of the ileum.
s5746 Two important processes take place in the terminal ileum: vitamin B~12~ is absorbed as a complex with Castle's intrinsic factor, and 90–95% of the bile acids are reabsorbed (enterohepatic circulation). After resection B~12~ is not absorbed — when the stores are exhausted (months to years), DNA synthesis is disturbed and megaloblastic (macrocytic) anemia develops. Bile acids are lost and the liver cannot replace them sufficiently — micelles are not formed, fat absorption is impaired (steatorrhea) and fat-soluble vitamins are also deficient. Treatment: B~12~ injections, fat restriction.
s5747 A patient with cholera is losing 10–15 l of watery stool per day. In a setting where intravenous fluids were not available, drinking a glucose-salt solution was recommended.
s5748 What is the mechanism of diarrhea in cholera? Why is the glucose-salt solution absorbed by the intestine?
s5749 Cholera toxin permanently activates adenylate cyclase in enterocytes — cAMP increases, and Cl^-^, followed by Na^+^ and water, is secreted into the intestinal lumen through CFTR chloride channels (secretory diarrhea); the active absorption of NaCl is also inhibited. However, the glucose–sodium cotransporter (SGLT1) is not damaged by the toxin. When the glucose-salt solution is drunk, Na^+^ is actively absorbed together with glucose, and water is absorbed along the resulting osmotic gradient — the fluid loss is compensated. This is the physiological basis of oral rehydration.

s5788 1. General concept of metabolism
s5789 **Metabolism** is the exchange of substances and energy between the body and the external environment; it is the main sign of life. It consists of two interrelated processes:
s5790 **Assimilation (anabolism)** — the synthesis of complex compounds specific to the body from simple substances coming from outside; **energy is consumed** (ATP). This process ensures cell growth, renewal and the accumulation of reserves.
s5791 **Dissimilation (catabolism)** — the breakdown of complex organic substances to simple ones (CO~2~, H~2~O, urea); **energy is released** — it is stored as ATP and partly converted into heat.

s5793 Metabolism: anabolism and catabolism, formation of ATP and heat (Alavia O.T., 2018)
s5794 In childhood and during growth assimilation predominates; in adults the two processes are in balance; in old age and in diseases (fever, tumors, starvation) dissimilation predominates.
s5795 **Metabolic bases of physiological functions:** every function — muscle contraction, the nerve impulse, secretion, active transport, synthesis — is carried out with the energy of ATP. ATP is formed in the cell in three ways: **creatine phosphate** (seconds — fast but short), **anaerobic glycolysis** (minutes — lactate is formed) and **oxidative phosphorylation** (in mitochondria — the most efficient: ≈ 30–32 ATP from 1 molecule of glucose).

s5810 Proteins are not stored in reserve in the body and are constantly renewed (≈ 300–400 g of protein is renewed per day, mostly from recycled amino acids). When proteins are broken down, most of the nitrogen is excreted in the urine as **urea**.
s5811 **Nitrogen balance** is the ratio between the amount of nitrogen taken in with food and the amount excreted from the body (protein contains on average 16% nitrogen: 1 g nitrogen = 6.25 g protein).
s5812 **Nitrogen equilibrium** — intake = output (healthy adults).
s5813 **Positive nitrogen balance** — intake > output (growth, pregnancy, recovery from illness, anabolic hormones — GH, testosterone, insulin).
s5814 **Negative nitrogen balance** — intake < output (starvation, protein deficiency, severe diseases, burns, fever, glucocorticoid excess, old age). **Essential amino acids** are not synthesized in the body and must come with food: **valine, leucine, isoleucine, lysine, methionine, threonine, tryptophan, phenylalanine** (in children also **histidine**, and according to some sources arginine). Proteins that contain all the essential amino acids in adequate proportions are **complete** (meat, fish, eggs, milk); proteins lacking any of them are **incomplete** (many plant proteins; for example, maize protein is low in lysine and tryptophan).
s5815 **The protein minimum** (Rubner) is the smallest amount of protein needed to maintain nitrogen equilibrium (≈ 30–45 g/day; 0.6 g/kg). **The protein optimum** is the amount needed for full health and high working capacity: **0.8–1 g/kg** in adults (≈ 70–100 g/day), at least half of which should be animal protein; more is needed for children, pregnant and lactating women and athletes (1.2–2 g/kg).
s5816 Protein-energy malnutrition in children: **kwashiorkor** (mainly protein deficiency — edema, enlarged abdomen, fatty liver, changes in skin and hair; the edema is due to the fall in oncotic pressure from hypoalbuminemia) and **marasmus** (general calorie deficiency — severe wasting without edema).
s5817 Lipids are the energy reserve (10–15 kg in adipose tissue — energy for several weeks), a component of membranes, and a source of hormones and fat-soluble vitamins. **Essential fatty acids** are the polyunsaturated **linoleic** (ω-6) and **linolenic** (ω-3) acids; from them arachidonic acid and eicosanoids (prostaglandins, thromboxanes, leukotrienes) are formed. Sources — vegetable oils, fish oil.
s5818 **Regulation:** lipogenesis is enhanced by insulin; lipolysis is enhanced by adrenaline, noradrenaline, glucagon, GH, cortisol and thyroid hormones. Lipids are transported in the blood in **lipoproteins** (chylomicrons, VLDL, LDL, HDL).
s5819 Carbohydrates are the main fast energy source. Glucose is stored in the **liver** and **muscles as glycogen** (100–120 g in the liver, 300–400 g in the muscles). Liver glycogen is used to maintain blood glucose, while muscle glycogen is used only by the muscle itself. When glycogen stores are exhausted (on days 1–2 of starvation), **gluconeogenesis** (from amino acids, lactate and glycerol) is activated.
s5820 The blood glucose level (**3.3–5.5 mmol/l**) is regulated by insulin and the counter-insulin hormones (glucagon, adrenaline, cortisol, GH) (topic 15). The **brain** obtains almost all its energy from glucose (≈ 120 g per day); in prolonged starvation it partly uses ketone bodies.
s5821 **Fiber (dietary fiber)** — indigestible carbohydrates (cellulose, pectin): enhances intestinal peristalsis, is a substrate for the beneficial microflora, and slows the absorption of cholesterol and glucose (25–30 g per day is recommended).
s5822 3. Water and mineral metabolism
s5823 **Water** makes up ≈ 60% of body mass; it is the substance most essential for life (without water a person lives 3–7 days, without food 30–50 days). The daily requirement is **2–2.5 l** (≈ 30–35 ml/kg): 1.2–1.5 l from drinks, ≈ 0.8–1 l from food, ≈ 0.3 l of metabolic (oxidation) water. Excretion: 1–1.5 l with urine, 0.5–0.7 l through the skin (sweat and insensible evaporation), 0.3–0.4 l with breath, 0.1–0.2 l with feces. Water metabolism is regulated by **thirst** (hypothalamus), **vasopressin**, **aldosterone** and natriuretic peptide.

s5862 4. Vitamins
s5863 **Vitamins** are organic substances that are not synthesized (or are insufficiently synthesized) in the body, are needed in very small amounts and work mainly as enzyme cofactors. Their deficiency is **hypovitaminosis** (complete absence — avitaminosis), their excess **hypervitaminosis** (mainly the fat-soluble ones: A, D).
