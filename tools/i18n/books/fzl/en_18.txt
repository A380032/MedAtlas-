s3830 Chemical thermoregulation (heat production)
s3831 The main sources of heat are the **skeletal muscles** (20% at rest, up to 90% during work), the **liver** and other internal organs. In the cold, heat production increases:
s3832 **Shivering thermogenesis** — involuntary rhythmic contraction of skeletal muscles; it increases heat production 3–5 times.
s3833 **Non-shivering thermogenesis** — intensification of metabolism under the influence of the sympathetic nervous system, noradrenaline and thyroid hormones; **brown adipose tissue** (abundant in infants; in its mitochondria thermogenin — the UCP-1 protein — uncouples oxidation from phosphorylation and converts energy directly into heat).

s3847 The thermoregulatory center
s3848 The **thermoregulatory center** is in the **hypothalamus**: its anterior part (the preoptic area) is mainly the center of heat loss; the posterior part is the center of heat production and conservation. The hypothalamus has a temperature “set point”; the center receives information from peripheral (skin) and central (hypothalamus, blood) thermoreceptors and keeps body temperature at this point.

s3850 Mechanisms of thermoregulation
s3851 **Fever** — under the action of pyrogens (bacterial toxins, IL-1, IL-6, TNF-α) prostaglandin E~2~ is formed in the hypothalamus and the “set point” shifts upward; the body raises its temperature to the new level (chills, shivering). Antipyretics (paracetamol, ibuprofen) reduce prostaglandin synthesis. **Hyperthermia (heatstroke)**, in contrast, results from insufficient heat loss with an unchanged “set point” — antipyretics are ineffective and external cooling is needed.

s3877 5. The hypothalamo-pituitary system
s3878 The **hypothalamus** is part of the diencephalon; it is the highest center of autonomic, endocrine and behavioral functions. It converts nervous signals into hormonal signals. The **pituitary gland** is a small gland (0.5–0.6 g) located in the fossa of the sella turcica, the “conductor of the endocrine system”.

s4027 A 30-year-old woman complains of weight loss, sweating, heat intolerance, palpitations and hand tremor. Pulse 110 /min, arterial pressure 140/70 mm Hg.
s4028 Calculate the deviation of basal metabolism by Read's formula. Which gland's function may be disturbed?
s4029 Pulse pressure = 140 − 70 = 70. BM = 0.75 × (110 + 70 × 0.74) − 72 = 0.75 × 161.8 − 72 ≈ 49%. Basal metabolism is increased by ≈ 50% above the norm. The clinical signs (weight loss, sweating, heat intolerance, tachycardia, tremor, wide pulse pressure) are typical of thyroid hyperfunction — thyrotoxicosis. Thyroid hormones increase oxidative processes and Na^+^/K^+^-ATPase activity in almost all cells, increase heat production and raise the sensitivity of β-adrenergic receptors. The diagnosis is confirmed by blood TSH (decreased) and free T~4~, T~3~ (increased).
s4030 In summer, on a day with an air temperature of 40 °C and high humidity, a worker who had been working in the field for a long time suddenly developed headache, nausea and was close to losing consciousness. Body temperature 40.5 °C, skin dry and hot.
s4031 What is the condition? Explain its mechanism. Why does paracetamol not help in this condition?
s4032 Heatstroke (hyperthermia). When the ambient temperature is higher than body temperature, heat loss by radiation, convection and conduction is impossible; the only route is evaporation of sweat. Because the humidity was high, evaporation was ineffective; prolonged sweating led to dehydration and salt loss, and eventually sweating stopped (dry skin). Muscle work produced a lot of heat. The heat accumulated and the temperature rose. In this condition the “set point” in the hypothalamus has not changed — the thermoregulatory mechanisms are simply insufficient; therefore antipyretics, which reduce prostaglandin synthesis, are ineffective. Help: move the person to shade and a cool place, external cooling (wet sheets, ice, a fan), and restoration of fluids and salts.
s4033 An 8-year-old child is 105 cm tall (the norm for his age ≈ 127 cm), with normal body proportions and age-appropriate mental development. Blood GH and IGF-1 are low.
s4034 What is the condition? Explain the mechanism of action of GH.
s4035 Pituitary dwarfism (somatotropin deficiency). GH is formed in the adenohypophysis and stimulates the synthesis of insulin-like growth factor-1 (IGF-1, somatomedin) mainly in the liver and other tissues; IGF-1 stimulates the proliferation of chondrocytes in the growth plates of bones and ensures longitudinal growth. GH also enhances protein synthesis. In deficiency growth slows, but because thyroid hormones are normal, body proportions and mental development are preserved (the difference from cretinism). Treatment — recombinant GH until the growth plates close.
