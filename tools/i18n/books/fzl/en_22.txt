s4679 The **functional residual capacity** is important: it dilutes the air entering with each breath (500 ml) and keeps the composition of alveolar air stable throughout the respiratory cycle — gas concentrations do not fluctuate sharply. s4681 2. Pulmonary ventilation
s4682 **Minute ventilation (MV)** = tidal volume × respiratory rate = 500 ml × 14–16 = **6–8 l/min**. During heavy physical work it increases to 100–150 l/min.
s4683 **Dead space** is the volume of the airways that does not take part in gas exchange:
s4684 **Anatomical dead space** — from the nasopharynx to the terminal bronchioles: ≈ **150 ml** (≈ 2 ml/kg).
s4685 **Physiological (functional) dead space** — anatomical plus alveoli that are ventilated but not perfused; in a healthy person it is almost equal to the anatomical dead space. **Alveolar ventilation** = (TV − dead space) × respiratory rate = (500 − 150) × 14 ≈ **4.2–5 l/min**. It is alveolar ventilation that determines gas exchange.

s4727 4. Transport of oxygen in the blood
s4728 Oxygen is carried in the blood in two forms:
s4729 **Physically dissolved** — a small amount (0.3 ml per 100 ml of blood at a pO~2~ of 100 mm Hg). But it is the dissolved O~2~ that creates the partial pressure (tension) and ensures diffusion.
s4730 **Bound to hemoglobin (oxyhemoglobin)** — 98–99%. The **oxygen capacity of blood** is the maximum amount of O~2~ that 1 l of blood can bind: Hb 150 g/l × 1.34 ml ≈ **200 ml O~2~/l** (20 vol%). Arterial blood contains ≈ 190–200 ml/l of O~2~ (saturation 96–98%), venous blood ≈ 140–150 ml/l (saturation ≈ 75%).
s4731 **Arteriovenous difference** — the difference in O~2~ content between arterial and venous blood: ≈ 50 ml/l at rest. **Oxygen utilization coefficient** of the tissues = arteriovenous difference / O~2~ in arterial blood ≈ 25–30%; up to 50–60% during physical work, and 60–70% in heart muscle even at rest.
s4732 The oxyhemoglobin dissociation curve
s4733 The relationship between the saturation of hemoglobin with O~2~ (saturation, SO~2~) and pO~2~ is expressed by an **S-shaped** curve. The S shape results from the **cooperative effect**: once one heme binds O~2~, the affinity of the others increases.
s4734 Figure 17.3
s4735 The oxyhemoglobin dissociation curve and its shifts (drawn after Costanzo L.S., 2018)
s4736 Physiological significance of the curve:
s4737 **The upper flat part** (pO~2~ 70–100 mm Hg) — in the lungs: even if pO~2~ falls slightly (altitude, lung diseases), the saturation remains high — oxygen supply is **reliable**.
s4738 **The steep lower part** (pO~2~ 10–40) — in the tissues: a small fall in pO~2~ releases a lot of O~2~ — the tissues **easily obtain** O~2~. **P~50~** is the pO~2~ at which hemoglobin is 50% saturated; normally ≈ **26–27 mm Hg.** An increase in P~50~ (a right shift of the curve) means that the affinity of Hb for O~2~ has decreased and the delivery of O~2~ to the tissues is facilitated.

s4767 6. Gas exchange in the tissues and tissue respiration
s4768 In the tissue capillaries O~2~ diffuses from the blood (pO~2~ 95–100) into the tissue (pO~2~ 20–40, 5–10 mm Hg in working muscle), and CO~2~ from the tissue (pCO~2~ 46–60) into the blood (40). Diffusion is affected by capillary density, the number of open capillaries, the distance to the cells and the tissue's O~2~ consumption. In muscles **myoglobin** stores O~2~ and carries it inside the cell (its affinity for O~2~ is higher than that of hemoglobin — P~50~ ≈ 1–5 mm Hg).
s4769 **Tissue (cellular) respiration** is the use of O~2~ as the final electron acceptor in the mitochondrial respiratory chain (cytochromes, cytochrome oxidase) and the synthesis of ATP by oxidative phosphorylation. For normal mitochondrial function an intracellular pO~2~ of only 1–3 mm Hg is sufficient (**critical pO~2~**).
s4770 **Cyanides** (bitter almonds, cyanide salts) block **cytochrome oxidase** — even if there is enough oxygen in the blood, the cells cannot use it (**tissue hypoxia**). Venous blood remains saturated with oxygen (the arteriovenous difference narrows) and the skin is pink.
s4771 7. Pulse oximetry
s4772 **Pulse oximetry** is a non-invasive, continuous method of determining arterial oxygen saturation (**SpO~2~**). The sensor (usually placed on a finger) transmits light of two wavelengths — **red (660 nm)** and **infrared (940 nm)**. Oxyhemoglobin absorbs more infrared light, deoxyhemoglobin more red light. The device analyzes only the **pulsatile** (arterial) component, calculates SpO~2~ from the ratio of absorption of the two lights and at the same time shows the pulse rate.

s4904 A 25-year-old woman complains of cough and nocturnal attacks of breathlessness. Spirometry: FVC — 88% of predicted, FEV~1~ — 58%, Tiffeneau index 62%. 20 minutes after salbutamol inhalation the FEV~1~ increased by 22% (350 ml).
s4905 What type of ventilatory disorder is this? What does the result of the bronchodilator test show?
s4906 An obstructive disorder: the FEV~1~ and Tiffeneau index (< 70%) are reduced, while the FVC is almost normal. When the bronchi narrow, airway resistance increases (according to Poiseuille's law, inversely to the fourth power of the radius), and rapid expiration of air becomes difficult. The bronchodilator test is positive (≥ 12% and ≥ 200 ml) — the obstruction is reversible; the spasm of the bronchial smooth muscle relaxed under the action of a β~2~-adrenomimetic. This is typical of bronchial asthma.
s4907 A mountaineer climbed to an altitude of 5000 m, where the atmospheric pressure is ≈ 405 mm Hg. A few days later his blood 2,3-DPG was found to have increased.
s4908 Calculate the approximate alveolar pO~2~ at this altitude. What is the adaptive significance of the increase in 2,3-DPG?
s4909 In inspired air pO~2~ = (405 − 47) × 0.21 ≈ 75 mm Hg; taking alveolar CO~2~ and ventilation into account, alveolar pO~2~ falls to ≈ 45–50 mm Hg (hyperventilation raises it slightly). At this pO~2~ the saturation is about 80–85%. Hypoxia enhances 2,3-DPG synthesis in erythrocytes; 2,3-DPG binds to deoxyhemoglobin and reduces its affinity for O~2~ — the dissociation curve shifts to the right. As a result, although O~2~ binding in the lungs decreases slightly, O~2~ release in the tissues is greatly facilitated — the oxygen supply of the tissues improves.
