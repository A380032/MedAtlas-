s5319 3. Bile formation and secretion
s5320 **Bile** is formed **continuously** in liver cells (hepatocytes) (**0.5–1.2 l** per day), but it is released into the duodenum only during **digestion**. Between meals bile accumulates **in the gallbladder** and is concentrated 5–10 times (water and electrolytes are reabsorbed).

s5342 **Bile formation (choleresis)** is enhanced by: bile acids returning to the blood (the strongest stimulus), **secretin** (water and bicarbonate), the vagus nerve, gastrin, and protein and fatty food.
s5343 **Bile release (cholekinesis)** is enhanced by: **cholecystokinin (CCK)** — contracts the gallbladder and relaxes the **sphincter of Oddi** (the main factor); the vagus nerve. The strongest stimulants are egg yolk, milk, meat and fats. The sympathetic nerves relax the gallbladder and contract the sphincter.
s5344 **Gallstone disease** — if the amount of cholesterol in bile increases relative to bile acids and phospholipids (lithogenic bile), cholesterol crystallizes and forms stones. After fatty food CCK contracts the gallbladder, and when a stone blocks the neck of the gallbladder, pain appears under the right costal margin (biliary colic). When the duct is completely blocked, bile does not enter the intestine: fats are not digested (**steatorrhea**), the stool is discolored (acholic stool), vitamin K is not absorbed (tendency to bleed), and **obstructive jaundice** develops.

s5381 Exercise 1: emulsification of fats by bile
s5382 Pour 2 ml of water into each of two test tubes.
s5383 Add 1 ml of bile (or a solution of bile salts) to tube 1 and 1 ml of water to tube 2.
s5384 Add 3–5 drops of vegetable oil to both, shake vigorously and let stand for 5 minutes.
s5385 Put a drop from each on a slide and examine under the microscope. **Result and analysis:** in the tube with bile the oil remains broken into small droplets for a long time (a stable emulsion), and small uniform droplets are seen under the microscope. In the tube with water the oil quickly separates to the surface. Bile acids reduce surface tension and emulsify fats.
s5386 Exercise 2: the importance of bile for the action of pancreatic lipase
s5387 Put 5 ml of milk (or fat emulsion) and phenolphthalein (pink in an alkaline medium) into each of three test tubes; bring them to a pale pink color with a weak alkali.
s5388 Add to tube 1 — pancreatin (lipase) solution + bile; tube 2 — pancreatin + water; tube 3 — water only.
s5389 Keep at 37 °C and observe the time of color change. **Result and analysis:** the pink color disappears fastest (fatty acids are formed and the medium becomes acidic) in tube 1, more slowly in tube 2; in tube 3 the color does not change. Bile activates lipase and, by emulsifying fats, increases the surface on which the enzyme acts.

s5489 After fatty food a 45-year-old obese woman developed severe pain under the right costal margin, followed by yellowing of the skin and sclerae, dark urine and pale stools. Blood conjugated bilirubin is increased.
s5490 What is the condition? Explain the signs on the basis of the physiology of bile.
s5491 Gallstone disease with blockage of the common bile duct by a stone — obstructive jaundice. Fatty food enhances the release of CCK in the duodenum; the gallbladder contracts, the stone moves and blocks the duct — biliary colic. Bile does not enter the intestine: the conjugated bilirubin in the liver returns to the blood (jaundice), and because it is water-soluble it passes into the urine (dark urine); no stercobilin is formed in the intestine (pale stools). Because bile acids are lacking, fats are not emulsified or digested (steatorrhea), and fat-soluble vitamins (vitamin K — blood coagulation) are not absorbed.
s5492 A patient who has consumed alcohol for many years has weight loss, abundant shiny, fatty, foul-smelling stools and bloating. Fecal elastase-1 is reduced.
s5493 What is the condition? Explain the mechanism of the signs.
s5494 Chronic pancreatitis and exocrine pancreatic insufficiency. The acinar tissue has been replaced by fibrosis, and the production of lipase, proteases and amylase has decreased. Because of lipase deficiency triglycerides are not broken down or absorbed — steatorrhea (fatty, shiny stools); protein digestion is impaired — creatorrhea; undigested substances ferment and putrefy in the large intestine under the action of bacteria — bloating, foul smell. Because nutrients are not absorbed, weight loss develops. Treatment: stopping alcohol and enzyme preparations (pancreatin) with meals.
