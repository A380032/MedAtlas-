s5900 5. Regulation of metabolism
s5901 Metabolism is regulated by **neurohumoral** means. The highest center is the **hypothalamus**: it contains the **hunger** (lateral nuclei) and **satiety** (ventromedial nuclei, arcuate nucleus) centers; the centers of thermoregulation, thirst and the endocrine system are also located there. The hypothalamus influences metabolism through the endocrine glands (insulin, glucagon, thyroid hormones, cortisol, GH, sex hormones) and through the autonomic nervous system.
s5902 **Regulation of appetite and food intake: ghrelin** (secreted in the stomach on an empty stomach — enhances the sensation of hunger); **leptin** (from adipose tissue — a signal about fat stores, reduces appetite); **insulin**, **CCK**, **GLP-1**, **PYY** (satiety signals); gastric distension (via the vagus).

s6040 A week after extensive burns, a patient is excreting 25 g of nitrogen per day in the urine while taking 75 g of protein with food. The patient is losing weight and the wounds are healing slowly.
s6041 Calculate and evaluate the nitrogen balance. Why has this condition developed, and what measures are needed?
s6042 Nitrogen intake = 75 / 6.25 = 12 g; nitrogen output ≥ 25 g; balance = 12 − 25 = −13 g/day — a sharply negative nitrogen balance (catabolism predominates). In burns, stress hormones (cortisol, catecholamines, glucagon) and inflammatory cytokines enhance protein breakdown (mainly in muscles); amino acids are used for gluconeogenesis and wound healing; protein is also lost from the burn surface. As a result muscle mass decreases and immunity and regeneration weaken. Measures: increase protein to 1.5–2 g/kg, provide sufficient energy (otherwise protein is used for energy), vitamins (C, A) and trace elements (zinc).
s6043 A student eats mainly bread, pasta and sweet tea every day, with little meat, milk, fruit or vegetables. His daily energy intake is 2800 kcal (expenditure 2400 kcal). In recent months his weight has increased, but he complains of fatigue and bleeding gums.
s6044 Evaluate the shortcomings of the ration from a physiological point of view.
s6045 The ration is excessive in energy (positive energy balance — weight gain) and far too rich in carbohydrates, especially rapidly absorbed (“free”) sugar. At the same time it is unbalanced: complete (animal) proteins, essential amino acids, calcium (milk) and iron (meat) are lacking; there are few fruit and vegetables — vitamin C deficiency (bleeding gums — impaired collagen synthesis) and little fiber. The fatigue may be related to deficiency of iron and B vitamins. Recommendation: match energy to expenditure, reduce sugar, add protein sources, dairy products and at least 400 g of fruit and vegetables a day.
s6046 A patient who has consumed alcohol for a long time and eaten poorly has disturbed gait, impaired eye movements and a sharp decline in memory. Thiamine was prescribed before giving him glucose solution.
s6047 Deficiency of which vitamin is this? Why is thiamine given before glucose?
s6048 Vitamin B~1~ (thiamine) deficiency — Wernicke's encephalopathy (with Korsakoff syndrome). Thiamine pyrophosphate is a cofactor of the enzymes pyruvate dehydrogenase and α-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase — it is necessary for the oxidation of carbohydrates. The brain obtains its energy mainly from glucose, so in thiamine deficiency the neurons suffer from an energy shortage. If glucose is given without thiamine, glucose metabolism increases and uses up the little thiamine that remains, and the neurological condition may worsen. In alcoholism thiamine intake is low, it is poorly absorbed and little is stored in the liver.

s6101 2. The nephron and renal blood flow
s6102 The **nephron** is the structural and functional unit of the kidney; each kidney has ≈ 1 million nephrons. The nephron consists of the **renal corpuscle** (the network of glomerular capillaries and the surrounding **Shumlyansky–Bowman capsule**) and the system of **tubules**: **proximal tubule → loop of Henle** (descending and ascending limbs) **→ distal tubule → collecting duct**.

s6104 Structure of the nephron (Alavia O.T., 2018)
s6105 **Types of nephron: cortical** nephrons — 80–85%, with a short loop of Henle; **juxtamedullary** nephrons — 15–20%, with a long loop extending deep into the medulla; they play the main role in concentrating urine.
s6106 The **juxtaglomerular apparatus (JGA)** consists of the **juxtaglomerular cells** in the wall of the afferent arteriole (they secrete renin), the **macula densa** of the distal tubule (it senses NaCl in the tubule) and the mesangial cells.
