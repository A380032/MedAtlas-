s4432 the **dorsal respiratory group** (in the region of the nucleus of the solitary tract) — mainly inspiratory neurons; it receives afferent information;
s4433 the **ventral respiratory group** — inspiratory and expiratory neurons; within it the **pre-Bötzinger complex** is the respiratory **rhythm generator** (neurons with pacemaker properties);
s4434 the **pneumotaxic center in the pons** — terminates inspiration on time and ensures the switch to expiration; its damage makes breathing deep and slow. After transection between the medulla oblongata and the spinal cord breathing stops; after transection above the pons breathing is preserved.

s4453 Reflex regulation
s4454 **Hering–Breuer reflex** — during inspiration the stretch receptors of the lungs are excited, and impulses travel along the vagus nerve to the respiratory center and stop inspiration (the switch from inspiration to expiration). In adults it is of little importance in quiet breathing and is activated in deep breathing (volume > 1–1.5 l); it is well expressed in newborns.

s4456 **Proprioceptors** (muscles and joints) — rapidly increase ventilation at the start of physical work.
s4457 **The cerebral cortex** — voluntary control of breathing (speech, singing, breath-holding), conditioned reflexes (increased breathing before the start).
s4458 Exercise 1: demonstrating the mechanism of breathing with the Donders model
s4459 Examine the model: the lungs of an animal are suspended by the trachea inside a glass bell jar (its lower part closed by a rubber membrane); the cavity of the jar is connected to a manometer.
s4460 Pull the rubber membrane down (imitating contraction of the diaphragm).
s4461 Observe the change in pressure inside the jar and in lung volume.

s4463 Exercise 2: pneumography — recording respiratory movements

s4468 Exercise 3: the Stange and Genchi tests
s4469 **Stange test**: the seated subject takes a deep (not maximal) breath, pinches the nose and holds the breath; the time is measured with a stopwatch.
s4470 **Genchi test**: the breath-holding time after a quiet expiration is measured.
s4471 Repeat the Stange test after 1 minute of hyperventilation. **Interpretation:** the Stange test is 40–60 s in healthy adults (60–120 s in athletes), the Genchi test 20–40 s. After hyperventilation the breath-holding time increases — blood CO~2~ decreases, and it takes longer to reach the “threshold” that excites the respiratory center.

s4579 A tall, thin 20-year-old young man suddenly developed sharp pain in the right side of the chest and shortness of breath. No breath sounds are heard on the right. On X-ray the right lung has collapsed toward its root, with air in the pleural cavity.
s4580 What is the condition? Why did the lung collapse? Why does this lung not expand during inspiration?
s4581 Spontaneous pneumothorax (a bulla on the lung surface has ruptured). Normally the pressure in the pleural cavity is lower than atmospheric — it keeps the lung “stuck” to the chest wall. When air enters, the pressure equalizes with atmospheric and the lung shrinks toward its root because of its own elastic recoil (elastic fibers and alveolar surface tension). Even when the chest expands during inspiration, no negative pressure is created in the pleural cavity — air simply enters the pleural cavity, and the lung does not expand passively. Treatment — removal of the air (puncture, drainage).
s4582 A baby born at 29 weeks of pregnancy developed rapid breathing a few hours after birth; the soft parts of the chest are drawn inward, there is grunting on expiration, and cyanosis is increasing.
s4583 What is the condition? Explain its mechanism on the basis of the function of surfactant.
s4584 Respiratory distress syndrome of the newborn. Surfactant is synthesized in sufficient amounts after 34–35 weeks; a premature baby lacks it. As a result the surface tension in the alveoli remains high: according to Laplace's law the small alveoli collapse during expiration (atelectasis), lung compliance falls sharply and each inspiration requires great effort — chest retraction, shortness of breath. No gas exchange takes place in the collapsed alveoli (shunt) — hypoxemia and cyanosis. Treatment — administration of surfactant preparations into the trachea and respiratory support.
s4585 A student who was nervous before an exam breathed rapidly and deeply. He developed dizziness and numbness of the fingers and around the lips. He was advised to breathe in and out of a paper bag.
s4586 Explain the mechanism of the signs. Why does breathing into a bag help?
s4587 Hyperventilation syndrome. Because of excessive ventilation a lot of CO~2~ is removed from the blood — hypocapnia and respiratory alkalosis. The decrease in CO~2~ constricts the cerebral vessels — cerebral blood flow decreases and dizziness occurs. Alkalosis enhances the binding of ionized calcium to proteins — free Ca^2+^ decreases and nerve excitability increases — paresthesias (numbness), and in severe cases cramps. When breathing into a bag, the expired CO~2~ is re-inhaled, blood pCO~2~ is restored and the signs disappear (but this method is used only briefly and only when it is clear that the cause is hyperventilation).

s4626 **Rapid shallow breathing is ineffective**: for example, with a TV of 200 ml and a respiratory rate of 40 /min the MV is 8 l/min (close to normal), but alveolar ventilation is (200 − 150) × 40 = 2 l/min — hypoventilation. Therefore **deep, slow breathing** is more effective.
s4627 Shift to the right — **“CADET, face Right!”**: **C**O~2~, **A**cid (H^+^), **D**PG (2,3-DPG), **E**xercise (physical work), **T**emperature — when these increase the curve shifts **to the right** and the tissues receive more oxygen.
s4628 The transport of O~2~ and CO~2~ is interrelated: **Bohr effect** — an increase in CO~2~ and H^+^ facilitates the release of O~2~ by Hb (in the tissues); **Haldane effect** — deoxyhemoglobin binds CO~2~ and H^+^ better than oxyhemoglobin, so in the tissues the release of O~2~ facilitates the uptake of CO~2~, and in the lungs oxygenation facilitates the release of CO~2~.
s4629 Limitations of pulse oximetry: in **carboxyhemoglobinemia** (carbon monoxide poisoning) and **methemoglobinemia** the result is wrong (falsely high or about ≈ 85%); peripheral vasospasm, cold hands, shock, movement, nail polish, bright ambient light and dark skin reduce accuracy. SpO~2~ shows only oxygenation — **it does not evaluate ventilation (CO~2~)**: in a patient receiving oxygen hypercapnia may be present even when SpO~2~ is normal.
