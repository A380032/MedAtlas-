s1315 The **internal environment** is formed by the fluids surrounding the cells: **blood**, **lymph** and **tissue fluid**. They constantly exchange with one another: blood plasma filters from the capillaries into the tissues and forms tissue fluid, part of which returns to the blood through the lymphatic vessels.
s1316 2. The blood system and the functions of blood
s1317 The **blood system** (G.F. Lang, 1939) is a single system consisting of the peripheral blood, the hematopoietic organs (red bone marrow, lymph nodes, spleen, thymus), the organs of blood destruction (spleen, liver) and the neurohumoral apparatus that regulates them.

s1336 Blood volume and composition
s1337 In adults the blood volume is **6–8%** of body mass, i.e., 4.5–6 l (≈ 70 ml/kg). At rest part of the blood is kept in **reservoirs** (spleen, liver, subcutaneous vessels, lungs) and joins the circulating blood during physical work or blood loss.
s1338 Blood consists of **plasma** (55–60%) and **formed elements** (40–45%). The ratio of the volume of formed elements to the total blood volume is called the **hematocrit**.

s1400 **Osmotic pressure** is the force with which dissolved substances (mainly ions) draw the solvent (water) to themselves through a semipermeable membrane. **NaCl** generates 60% of the osmotic pressure of plasma. Solutions whose osmotic pressure equals that of plasma are called **isotonic** (0.9% NaCl — “physiological saline”, 5% glucose); those with a lower pressure are **hypotonic**, and those with a higher one are **hypertonic**.
s1401 Figure 5.4
s1402 Erythrocytes in hypotonic, isotonic and hypertonic solutions (Alavia O.T., 2018)
s1403 **In a hypotonic solution** water enters the erythrocyte, which swells and bursts — **osmotic hemolysis** (“lacquered blood”).
s1404 **In a hypertonic solution** water leaves the erythrocyte, which shrinks — **plasmolysis** (crenation). **The osmotic resistance of erythrocytes** is their resistance to bursting in hypotonic solutions. Normally hemolysis begins at **0.46–0.48%** NaCl (minimal resistance) and is complete at **0.32–0.34%** NaCl (maximal resistance).
s1405 Only isotonic solutions are infused intravenously in large volumes. If a hypotonic fluid (for example, distilled water) is infused, hemolysis develops and the kidneys are damaged. In **hereditary microspherocytosis** (Minkowski–Chauffard disease) the erythrocytes are spherical and their osmotic resistance is sharply reduced — hemolysis begins already at 0.6–0.7% NaCl.
s1406 **Oncotic (colloid osmotic) pressure** is the part of osmotic pressure generated by plasma proteins; it is small (25–30 mm Hg) but very important, because proteins hardly pass through the capillary wall. Oncotic pressure **holds water inside the vessels** and returns tissue fluid to the blood in the venous part of the capillaries (the Starling balance).
s1407 The active reaction of blood (pH) and buffer systems
s1408 Blood pH is a very rigid homeostatic parameter: the limits compatible with life are 6.8–7.8. The constancy of pH is ensured by the **buffer systems**, the **lungs** (removal of CO~2~) and the **kidneys** (excretion of H^+^, reabsorption of HCO~3~^-^).

s1426 The **alkaline reserve** is the amount of bicarbonate in plasma; it shows the capacity to neutralize acids (normally 24 ± 2 mmol/l, 50–65 vol% CO~2~).
s1427 **Acidosis** is a fall in pH below 7.35, **alkalosis** a rise above 7.45. By origin they are **respiratory** (accumulation or excessive loss of CO~2~) and **metabolic** (nonrespiratory); if the pH is kept normal by the buffer systems, they are **compensated**; if the pH changes, they are **decompensated**.
s1428 5. Erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR)
s1429 When anticoagulated blood stands upright in a tube, the erythrocytes gradually settle and a layer of plasma forms above them. The **ESR** is the height of the plasma layer formed in one hour (mm/h).
s1430 **Mechanism:** erythrocytes carry a negative charge and repel each other. Sedimentation depends on the erythrocytes sticking together into “coin stacks” (rouleaux, aggregates): aggregates are heavier and settle faster. Aggregation is enhanced by **large, positively charged protein molecules** — **fibrinogen**, **γ-globulins**, paraproteins; **albumin** reduces it.

s1445 Experiment 1: determining the hematocrit
s1446 Rinse a hematocrit capillary with an anticoagulant (heparin).
s1447 Fill the capillary to 7/8 of its length with blood taken from a finger and seal one end with plasticine.
s1448 Place the capillary in a hematocrit centrifuge and spin for 1.5–3 minutes (or 30 minutes at 3000 rpm in an ordinary centrifuge).
s1449 Using the scale, determine the ratio of the height of the column of formed elements to the total column height. **Interpretation:** 40–48% in men, 36–42% in women. A decreased hematocrit indicates anemia or hydremia; an increased one — erythrocytosis or dehydration (hemoconcentration).
s1450 Experiment 2: determining the ESR by the Panchenkov method
s1451 Rinse a Panchenkov capillary with 5% sodium citrate solution, draw citrate up to the “P” mark (50 divisions) and expel it onto a watch glass.
s1452 Draw blood from a finger into the capillary up to the “K” mark twice, add it to the citrate on the watch glass (blood : citrate = 4 : 1) and mix.
s1453 Draw the mixture into the capillary up to the “K” (0) mark.
s1454 Place the capillary in the stand strictly upright and note the time.
s1455 After one hour measure the height of the plasma column in mm. **Interpretation:** 1–10 mm/h in men, 2–15 mm/h in women. A high result suggests an inflammatory process or a change in protein composition.
s1456 Experiment 3: determining the osmotic resistance of erythrocytes
s1457 Pour 1 ml of NaCl solutions from 0.7% to 0.2% (in steps of 0.02%) into test tubes.
s1458 Add 1 drop of blood to each tube, mix and leave for 1 hour (or centrifuge).
s1459 The tube in which the solution has turned pink but a sediment is still present — **minimal resistance** (onset of hemolysis).
s1460 The tube in which the solution is completely red and transparent (“lacquered blood”) and there is no sediment — **maximal resistance** (complete hemolysis). **Interpretation:** normally the minimal resistance is 0.46–0.48% and the maximal 0.32–0.34% NaCl.

s1468 Determine the pH: below 7.35 — acidosis, above 7.45 — alkalosis.

s1539 A patient with liver cirrhosis has fluid accumulation in the legs and the abdominal cavity (edema, ascites). Total blood protein is 52 g/l, albumin 24 g/l.
s1540 Explain the mechanism of the edema. Which parameter has changed?
s1541 The liver is the main organ that synthesizes albumin; in cirrhosis albumin synthesis decreases (24 g/l, normal 35–50 g/l). Albumin generates 80% of the oncotic pressure of plasma, so oncotic pressure falls. The force holding water inside the capillaries decreases: more fluid leaves at the arterial end and less returns at the venous end. As a result fluid accumulates in the tissues — oncotic (hypoproteinemic) edema. In cirrhosis, increased pressure in the portal vein also contributes to ascites.
s1542 A healthy woman at 32 weeks of pregnancy has an ESR of 42 mm/h. Other parameters are normal, and she has no complaints.
s1543 Is this a pathology? Explain the cause of the increased ESR.
s1544 This is a physiological state. In the second half of pregnancy the blood levels of fibrinogen and globulins rise, albumin decreases relatively, and the erythrocyte concentration falls slightly because of hydremia. Large protein molecules reduce the charge of erythrocytes and enhance their aggregation into “coin stacks” (rouleaux) — the aggregates settle faster. In pregnancy the ESR may rise to 40–50 mm/h.
s1545 By mistake a nurse infused 500 ml of distilled water intravenously into a patient. A few hours later the urine turned red-brown.
s1546 What condition has developed? Explain the mechanism. What solutions should be infused intravenously in large volumes?
s1547 Osmotic hemolysis. Distilled water is sharply hypotonic relative to blood; water enters the erythrocytes down the osmotic gradient, they swell and burst, and hemoglobin is released into the plasma. Free hemoglobin is excreted through the kidneys, colors the urine red-brown (hemoglobinuria) and may damage the renal tubules. Only isotonic solutions (0.9% NaCl, Ringer's solution, 5% glucose) are infused intravenously in large volumes.
