s0944 A 30-year-old woman complains that in the evening her eyelids droop, she tires quickly when speaking and swallowing food becomes difficult. After rest in the morning her condition improves. After injection of neostigmine (Proserin), muscle strength was temporarily restored.
s0945 What disease can be suspected? Explain the mechanism of the disease and the action of neostigmine.
s0946 Myasthenia gravis. In this disease autoantibodies are formed against N-cholinergic receptors, and the number of receptors on the postsynaptic membrane decreases. As a result the end-plate potential becomes smaller and the safety factor of the neuromuscular synapse falls; with repeated activity, when transmitter release decreases slightly, the EPP falls below threshold — the muscles tire quickly (worse toward evening). Neostigmine inhibits acetylcholinesterase, acetylcholine persists longer in the synaptic cleft and activates the remaining receptors more — muscle strength is temporarily restored.
s0947 A patient who had eaten home-canned food developed double vision, drooping eyelids, impaired swallowing and later weakness of the respiratory muscles. Sensation was not affected.
s0948 The action of which poison can be suspected? Which stage of synaptic transmission does it disrupt?
s0949 Botulinum toxin (botulism). The toxin enters the presynaptic terminal and cleaves the proteins that ensure fusion of acetylcholine-containing vesicles with the membrane — exocytosis of the neurotransmitter stops. Even when the action potential reaches the terminal, acetylcholine is not released, no end-plate potential arises and flaccid paralysis develops. Sensation is preserved because sensory pathways do not use cholinergic neuromuscular synapses. Paralysis of the respiratory muscles is life-threatening.
s0950 A student stimulated the nerve of a nerve-muscle preparation rhythmically for a long time. The muscle stopped contracting. He concluded that “the muscle is fatigued”.

s1028 The physiological properties of muscle are **excitability**, **conductivity**, **contractility**, **elasticity** (return to the original state after stretching) and **extensibility**; smooth muscles additionally have **plasticity** (keeping their tone unchanged in the stretched state) and **automaticity**.

s1030 2. Structure of the skeletal muscle fiber
s1031 Muscle → muscle bundles → **muscle fibers** (multinucleated cells, up to several cm long) → **myofibrils** → **sarcomeres**. The sarcomere is the part of a myofibril between two **Z lines** (2–2.5 µm long at rest), the functional unit of the contractile apparatus.
s1032 **Thick filaments** — myosin; located in the middle of the sarcomere, they form the **A band** (anisotropic, dark).
s1033 **Thin filaments** consist of actin, tropomyosin and troponin; they are attached to the Z line and form the **I band** (isotropic, light).
s1034 The light part in the middle of the A band containing only thick filaments is the **H zone**, with the **M line** at its center.

s1036 Structure of skeletal muscle: muscle — fiber — myofibril — sarcomere (Alavia O.T., 2018)
s1037 The membrane of the muscle fiber (sarcolemma) dips into the fiber as **transverse tubules (the T system)**. On both sides of them lie the terminal cisterns of the **sarcoplasmic reticulum (SR)**, forming a **triad**. The SR is the store of Ca^2+^ ions.
s1038 3. Mechanism of muscle contraction
s1039 Excitation–contraction coupling
s1040 The action potential generated at the neuromuscular synapse spreads along the sarcolemma and passes into the fiber through the **T tubules**.
s1041 The voltage-sensitive **dihydropyridine receptors** (L-type Ca^2+^ channels) in the T tubule change their conformation and mechanically open the **ryanodine receptors** (Ca^2+^ channels) of the SR.
s1042 Ca^2+^ leaves the SR for the sarcoplasm: its concentration rises from 10^-7^ to 10^-5^ mol/l.
s1043 Ca^2+^ binds to **troponin C**; the troponin–tropomyosin complex shifts and the myosin-binding sites on actin are exposed.
s1044 The sliding filament theory (A. Huxley, H. Huxley)
s1045 During contraction the filaments themselves do not shorten: **the thin actin filaments slide between the thick myosin filaments**. As a result the Z lines approach each other, **the I band and H zone narrow, while the width of the A band does not change**.

s1050 Relaxation
s1051 Relaxation is also an **active process**: the **Ca^2+^-ATPase (calcium pump)** in the SR membrane pumps Ca^2+^ back into the SR using the energy of ATP; once Ca^2+^ falls, troponin–tropomyosin covers the active sites and the muscle relaxes.

s1069 Single twitch
s1070 A single threshold or stronger stimulus evokes a **single twitch**. Its periods (in the frog gastrocnemius, ≈ 0.1 s in total) are the **latent period** (from the stimulus to the start of contraction, ≈ 0.01 s — for excitation–contraction coupling), the **contraction phase** (≈ 0.04 s) and the **relaxation phase** (≈ 0.05 s). The action potential (1–3 ms) is much shorter than the contraction — therefore the muscle can be excited again during the contraction.

s1089 In tetanus the contraction amplitude is 2–4 times greater than that of a single twitch, because Ca^2+^ is maintained at a high concentration in the sarcoplasm and all cross-bridges are active. In natural human movements, skeletal muscles contract **tetanically**: motor neurons send impulses at a frequency of 10–50 Hz.
s1090 Optimum and pessimum (N.E. Vvedensky)
s1091 As the stimulus frequency increases, the amplitude of tetanus grows and becomes greatest at a certain frequency — the **optimum**: each impulse falls into the phase of exaltation after the previous excitation. If the frequency is increased further, the impulses fall into the refractory period and the contraction decreases or stops — the **pessimum**. Pessimum is mainly related to the low lability of the neuromuscular synapse.
