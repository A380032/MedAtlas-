s8325 Neighbors noticed a household gas leak in a patient's home, but the patient himself smelled nothing. A year earlier he had injured the back of his head in a car accident, and since then food has seemed “tasteless”.
s8326 What disorder has developed? Explain its mechanism. Why does food seem “tasteless”?
s8327 Post-traumatic anosmia. When the back of the head is struck, the brain shifts back and forth and the delicate olfactory nerve fibers passing through the openings of the ethmoid bone are torn — impulses from the olfactory receptors no longer reach the bulb. The “flavor” of food consists mainly of retronasal smell (odorants passing from the mouth through the pharynx into the nasal cavity); the taste receptors distinguish only the five basic tastes (sweet, salty, sour, bitter, umami). When smell is lost, the complex flavor of food is not perceived. Anosmia is dangerous — the patient does not smell gas, smoke or spoiled food; installing gas and smoke detectors at home is recommended.
s8328 A footballer injured his leg during a match, but felt almost no pain until the end of the game. After the game the pain increased.
s8329 How can this phenomenon be explained?
s8330 Stress analgesia. The strong emotional and physical strain during competition activates the antinociceptive system: β-endorphin and enkephalins are released in the pituitary and CNS, and descending inhibitory pathways from the periaqueductal gray matter, the raphe nuclei (serotonin) and the locus coeruleus (noradrenaline) to the dorsal horns of the spinal cord stop the transmission of pain signals. Activation of the sympathoadrenal system also contributes. When the stress passes these influences decrease, while in the damaged tissue inflammatory mediators (prostaglandins, bradykinin) have sensitized the nociceptors — the pain increases.
s8331 A 50-year-old man complains of pressing pain behind the sternum during physical exertion, spreading to the left arm and under the scapula.
s8332 How can the spread of the pain to the left arm be explained?
s8333 This is referred pain — typical of angina pectoris. Visceral afferent fibers from the heart enter the Th~1~–Th~5~ segments of the spinal cord; somatic afferents from the skin of the inner surface of the left arm and the chest wall enter the same segments. They converge on the same projection neurons in the dorsal horn. Based on usual experience, the brain “interprets” the signals from these neurons as coming from a skin (somatic) source — the pain is felt in the left arm and scapular region (Zakharyin–Head zones). This sign is important in diagnosing myocardial ischemia.

s8378 1. The concept of higher nervous activity
s8379 **Higher nervous activity (HNA)** (I.P. Pavlov) is the joint activity of the cerebral cortex and the subcortical structures; it ensures the most perfect individual adaptation (behavior) of animals and humans to a changing external environment. Its main mechanism is **conditioned reflexes**. **Lower nervous activity**, by contrast, coordinates the activity of the internal organs through the spinal cord and brainstem (mainly unconditioned reflexes).
s8380 The conceptual basis of Pavlov's doctrine was created by **I.M. Sechenov** in his work “Reflexes of the Brain” (1863): mental activity is also reflex in nature.

s8400 2. Conditioned reflexes
s8401 Conditions for the formation of a conditioned reflex
s8402 **Coincidence in time of two stimuli**: an indifferent (neutral) stimulus (for example, light, a bell) must be given **before** the **unconditioned stimulus** (food) and applied **together with it several times** (reinforcement).
s8403 The conditioned signal must begin **1–5 s before** the unconditioned stimulus (if it is given after the unconditioned stimulus, the reflex is not formed).
s8404 The unconditioned stimulus must be biologically **stronger** (the animal must be hungry).
s8405 **An active, alert state of the cortex** and the absence of extraneous stimuli.
s8406 The indifferent stimulus must be of moderate strength.

s8409 Mechanism of formation of a conditioned reflex
s8410 According to Pavlov, when a conditioned reflex is formed, a **temporary connection** arises in the cortex between the **center of the conditioned signal** (for example, the visual center) and the **cortical representation of the unconditioned reflex** (the food center). The strong (dominant) unconditioned center “draws to itself” the excitation in the weaker center of the conditioned signal (Ukhtomsky's dominant). With repeated pairing this path is “worn in” — synaptic efficacy increases.

s8413 According to modern ideas, the temporary connection is formed not only in the cortex but in a **multilevel** system involving **cortical and subcortical** structures (thalamus, hippocampus, amygdala, reticular formation, cerebellum). Its cellular basis is **synaptic plasticity**: long-term potentiation (in the hippocampus — NMDA glutamate receptors, Ca^2+^), synthesis of new proteins and formation of new synapses (E. Kandel, Nobel Prize 2000).
