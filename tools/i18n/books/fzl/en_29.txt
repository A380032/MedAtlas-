s6135 the efferent arteriole has a smaller diameter than the afferent one — the pressure in the glomerulus is high;
s6136 **autoregulation**: when arterial pressure changes within **70–180 mm Hg**, renal blood flow and GFR hardly change (the myogenic mechanism — the Bayliss effect — and **tubulo- glomerular feedback** via the macula densa).
s6137 3. Mechanism of urine formation
s6138 Urine formation consists of three processes: **glomerular filtration**, **tubular reabsorption** and **tubular secretion**. Amount excreted = filtered − reabsorbed + secreted.
s6139 Glomerular filtration
s6140 Blood plasma, with almost all its components except proteins, is filtered from the glomerular capillaries into the capsular space — **primary urine** (ultrafiltrate) is formed. **The filtration barrier**: the fenestrated endothelium, the (negatively charged) basement membrane and the slit diaphragms between the foot processes of the podocytes. Substances with a molecular mass greater than 70,000 Da and negatively charged substances (albumin) hardly pass.
s6141 Figure 22.2
s6142 Forces creating the filtration pressure in the glomerulus (drawn after Costanzo L.S., 2018)
s6143 **Effective filtration pressure** = hydrostatic pressure in the glomerular capillaries − (plasma oncotic pressure + hydrostatic pressure in the capsule) ≈ 60 − (32 + 18) ≈ **10 mm Hg**.
s6144 **Glomerular filtration rate (GFR)** is the volume of primary urine formed by both kidneys per unit time: **≈ 125 ml/min** (≈ **180 l/day**). Of this, **99%** is reabsorbed and about **1.5 l** of final (secondary) urine is excreted. The **filtration fraction** is the ratio of GFR to renal plasma flow (≈ 20%).
s6145 **Factors affecting GFR:** a sharp fall in arterial pressure (< 70 mm Hg — filtration decreases; it stops at < 40–50), the tone of the afferent and efferent arterioles, plasma oncotic pressure (GFR increases in hypoproteinemia), the pressure in the capsule (GFR decreases when the urinary tract is obstructed), the number of active glomeruli and the filtration surface.
s6146 **GFR** is the main indicator of kidney function; it is evaluated by **creatinine clearance** or by formulas (CKD-EPI) based on blood creatinine, age and sex. If GFR < 60 ml/min/1.73 m^2^ persists for more than 3 months — **chronic kidney disease**; < 15 — end-stage renal failure (dialysis or transplantation).

s6168 Tubular secretion
s6169 The tubular epithelium **actively secretes** substances from the blood into the tubular lumen: **H^+^ ** (proximal and distal tubules, intercalated cells of the collecting ducts), **K^+^** (principal cells of the distal tubule and collecting ducts, under the action of aldosterone), **NH~3~/NH~4~^+^**, and organic acids and bases (para-aminohippuric acid, penicillin, many drugs, partly creatinine) — in the proximal tubule.
s6170 4. Mechanism of urine concentration
s6171 The human kidney can concentrate urine **up to 4 times** relative to plasma (up to 1200 mOsm/kg) or dilute it (down to 50 mOsm/kg). This is achieved by the **countercurrent multiplier system**:
s6172 In the descending and ascending limbs of the loop of Henle fluid flows in opposite directions.
s6173 The **ascending limb**, being impermeable to water, actively pumps NaCl into the interstitium — the interstitium becomes hypertonic.
s6174 The **descending limb** is permeable to water — water moves out into the hypertonic interstitium and the filtrate becomes concentrated.
s6175 Repeated along the loop, this process creates an **osmotic gradient** in the medulla from the cortex (300 mOsm/kg) to the tips of the papillae (1200 mOsm/kg). The return of **urea ** from the collecting ducts to the interstitium (urea recycling) also contributes greatly.
s6176 The **vasa recta**, acting as a countercurrent exchanger, also do not wash out the gradient.
s6177 When **vasopressin** is present, **aquaporin-2** channels are inserted into the walls of the collecting ducts and water is reabsorbed into the hypertonic interstitium — **concentrated urine**; without vasopressin — **dilute urine**.

s6223 Micturition
s6224 Urine passes from the renal pelvis through the ureters into the bladder by peristaltic contractions. When 250–300 ml has accumulated in the bladder (pressure above 15–20 cm H₂O), the stretch receptors are excited and the urge to urinate appears. **The center of the micturition reflex** is in the **sacral segments of the spinal cord (S~2~–S~4~)**: the parasympathetic (pelvic) nerves contract the bladder muscle (detrusor) and relax the internal sphincter; the sympathetic nerves (Th~11~–L~2~), conversely, help to store urine. The **external sphincter** (striated, pudendal nerve) is controlled voluntarily; the **pontine micturition center** (Barrington's center) and the cerebral cortex coordinate the reflex and provide voluntary control (developed by 2–3 years of age).
s6225 Exercise 1: calculating GFR from creatinine clearance
s6226 **Clearance** is the volume of plasma completely cleared of a given substance by the kidneys per unit time: C = (U × V) / P, where U is the concentration of the substance in urine, V the minute diuresis (ml/min) and P its concentration in plasma. The clearance of a substance that is only filtered and neither reabsorbed nor secreted (inulin, in practice creatinine) equals the GFR.
s6227 **Given:** daily urine 1440 ml (V = 1 ml/min); urine creatinine 9 mmol/l; plasma creatinine 0.08 mmol/l.
s6228 C = 9 × 1 / 0.08 = 112.5 ml/min.
s6229 **Interpretation:** normal 90–130 ml/min (decreases with age) — GFR is normal.
