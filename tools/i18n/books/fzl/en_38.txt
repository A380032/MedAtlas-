s7936 Axons of the spiral ganglion neurons (the cochlear part of nerve VIII) → **cochlear nuclei** in the medulla oblongata → **superior olivary complex** (pons; information from the two ears is compared for the first time) → lateral lemniscus → **inferior colliculi** (midbrain; auditory orienting reflexes) → **medial geniculate body** (thalamus) → **primary auditory cortex** (temporal lobe, Heschl's gyri, **areas 41–42**). The pathways partly cross, so each hemisphere receives information **from both ears** — unilateral cortical damage does not cause deafness.
s7937 **Binaural hearing and sound localization** — determining the direction of a sound source. Mechanisms: the **difference in arrival time** of the sound at the two ears (for low frequencies; differences down to 10 µs are detected) and the **difference in intensity** (for high frequencies — the head creates a “shadow”); these differences are analyzed by the superior olivary complex.
s7938 4. The vestibular apparatus
s7939 The **vestibular apparatus** is the organ that senses the position of the head in space and its movement (acceleration). It consists of the **vestibule** (otolith organs — the **utricle** and **saccule**) and three **semicircular canals** located in mutually perpendicular planes; all are filled with endolymph.

s7953 **How the semicircular canals work:** when rotation starts, the endolymph lags behind the canal wall because of inertia and bends the cupula in the direction **opposite** to the rotation; during uniform rotation the endolymph moves together with the canal — the cupula returns to its initial position; on a sudden stop the endolymph continues to move and bends the cupula in the **opposite** direction — **post-rotational sensations** (a feeling of rotating the other way) and **nystagmus** appear.

s7966 Exercise 1: determining hearing acuity with speech
s7967 The subject stands sideways to the examiner, closes the ear not being tested with a finger and closes the eyes (so as not to lip-read).
s7968 After expiration, using the residual air, the examiner **whispers** two-digit numbers (for example, 21, 47); starting from 6 m, he comes closer if the subject cannot repeat them.
s7969 Write down the greatest distance at which the subject repeated correctly; the other ear is also tested. **Interpretation:** normally whispered speech is heard from **6 m** or more, and ordinary conversational speech up to 20 m.
s7970 Exercise 2: the Rinne test
s7971 Place the stem of a vibrating tuning fork (128 or 512 Hz) on the mastoid process (bone conduction).
s7972 As soon as the subject stops hearing the sound, bring the tuning fork close to the ear canal (1–2 cm away) (air conduction).
s7973 Ask whether the sound is heard again. **Interpretation:** normally air conduction lasts longer than bone conduction (≈ 2 times) — **Rinne positive**. When the **sound-conducting** apparatus (outer and middle ear) is damaged, bone conduction is longer — **Rinne negative**. When the **sound-perceiving** apparatus (inner ear, nerve) is damaged, both are shortened but the ratio is preserved — Rinne positive.
s7974 Exercise 3: the Weber test (lateralization)
s7975 Place a vibrating tuning fork on the top of the head (in the midline).
s7976 Ask in which ear the sound is heard louder.
s7977 Close one ear with a finger and repeat the test. **Result and analysis:** normally the sound is heard equally in both ears (in the middle of the head). When the ear is closed or **conduction** is impaired (otitis, earwax plug), the sound lateralizes **toward the affected (closed) ear** — external noise does not act there and the sound coming through the bone is heard better. When **perception** is impaired, the sound lateralizes **toward the healthy ear**.
s7978 Exercise 4: testing the excitability of the vestibular apparatus
s7979 **Bárány rotation test** — as in topic 24: after 10 rotations in 20 s, the duration of nystagmus (normal 25–35 s), autonomic reactions and loss of balance are evaluated.
s7980 **Yashchenko (Voyachek) test** — after rotation, standing with the head bent forward and then walking along a straight line with the eyes open; the degree of deviation is evaluated.
s7981 Stability **in the Romberg position** is evaluated. **Interpretation:** prolonged nystagmus, severe nausea, pallor and sweating — high vestibular excitability (predisposition to motion sickness); important in occupational selection (pilots, cosmonauts, sailors).
s7982 Skill: determining the type of hearing loss from tuning fork tests
s7983 Perform the Rinne test on both ears.
s7984 Perform the Weber test.
s7985 Rinne negative + Weber toward the affected ear — **conductive** hearing loss (outer or middle ear).
s7986 Rinne positive (both shortened) + Weber toward the healthy ear — **sensorineural** hearing loss (inner ear or auditory nerve).
s7987 State that the result needs to be confirmed by audiometry.
s7988 Skill: reading an audiogram
s7989 Note that the horizontal axis shows frequency (125–8000 Hz) and the vertical axis the hearing threshold (dB, increasing from top to bottom).
s7990 Find the air conduction (o — right, x — left) and bone conduction lines.
s7991 Thresholds of 0–20 dB — normal; 20–40 — mild, 40–70 — moderate, 70–90 — severe, > 90 dB — profound hearing loss.
s7992 If there is a gap between the air and bone lines — conductive; if both are lowered together — a sensorineural disorder.

s8083 After a cold a child's right ear began to hurt, his hearing decreased and his temperature rose. The Rinne test is negative on the right and positive on the left; in the Weber test the sound is heard louder in the right ear.
s8084 What is the type and cause of the hearing loss? Explain the tuning fork tests.
s8085 Acute right-sided otitis media — conductive hearing loss. In children the Eustachian tube is short and wide, so infection easily passes from the nasopharynx to the middle ear; fluid accumulates in the middle ear and restricts the movement of the tympanic membrane and ossicles — air conduction of sound is impaired. Rinne negative: bone conduction (directly to the inner ear) is heard longer than air conduction. Weber lateralizes toward the affected ear: external noise does not enter the affected ear, and the sound coming through the bone is perceived as relatively louder; the inner ear is healthy.
s8086 A patient treated with gentamicin for a long time developed ringing in the ears, bilateral hearing loss and unsteadiness when walking. Rinne is positive on both sides, and there is no Weber lateralization.
s8087 What disorder has developed? Explain its mechanism.
s8088 The ototoxic (and vestibulotoxic) effect of aminoglycosides — bilateral sensorineural hearing loss. Gentamicin accumulates in the endolymph and damages the hair cells (first the outer hair cells at the base of the cochlea, which receive high frequencies) and the hair cells of the vestibular apparatus. Because the sound-conducting apparatus is healthy, Rinne is positive (air and bone conduction are shortened equally); because the damage is symmetrical, there is no lateralization in the Weber test. Damage to the vestibular hair cells leads to loss of balance. That is why doses and blood concentrations are monitored during treatment with aminoglycosides.
s8089 A passenger traveling by ship for the first time developed nausea, vomiting, pallor and cold sweat while sitting in the cabin. When he went out on deck and looked at the horizon, he felt better.
s8090 What is the condition? Explain its mechanism and why looking at the horizon helps.
s8091 Motion sickness (seasickness). When the ship rocks, the vestibular apparatus (otolith organs and semicircular canals) continuously registers changing accelerations, while in the cabin the eye sees motionless surroundings — a mismatch arises between vestibular, visual and proprioceptive information. Through the connections of the vestibular nuclei with the autonomic centers (the vomiting center, vagal nuclei), vestibulo-autonomic reactions develop: nausea, vomiting, pallor, sweating. When looking at the horizon, visual information matches vestibular information (the eye also sees the rocking) — the mismatch decreases and the symptoms ease.
