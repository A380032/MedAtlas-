s3717 A 72-year-old patient has an arterial pressure of 170/70 mm Hg. In youth his pressure was 120/80.
s3718 Calculate the pulse pressure and mean pressure. How can the rise in systolic and fall in diastolic pressure in old age be explained?
s3719 Pulse pressure = 170 − 70 = 100 mm Hg (normal 40–50). Mean pressure = 70 + 100/3 ≈ 103 mm Hg. In old age the walls of the aorta and large arteries lose their elastic fibers and stiffen (atherosclerosis, collagen accumulation). The shock-absorbing function weakens: in systole the stiff aorta dilates little — systolic pressure rises sharply; in diastole the aorta cannot recoil and push the blood onward — diastolic pressure falls. This condition is called isolated systolic hypertension and increases cardiovascular risk.
s3720 A patient in the surgical ward has heart failure: edema of the legs and distended neck veins. Blood albumin is normal.
s3721 What is the main mechanism of the edema? Explain it on the basis of the Starling forces.
s3722 In heart failure the heart cannot pump the venous blood adequately — blood accumulates in the veins and venous pressure rises (distended neck veins). The increased venous pressure raises the hydrostatic pressure at the venous end of the capillaries; reabsorption decreases and filtration increases. The oncotic pressure is normal (albumin is normal), so the main factor is the increase in hydrostatic pressure (congestive edema). Because of gravity, the edema appears in the lower parts of the body — the legs. In addition, sodium and water retention in the kidneys (RAAS activation) increases blood volume and aggravates the edema.
s3723 After standing motionless in formation for a long time, a student's vision darkened and he lost consciousness. After he was laid down and his legs were raised, he quickly came round.
s3724 Explain the mechanism of the fainting. Why did raising the legs help?
s3725 During prolonged motionless standing, gravity causes blood to pool in the leg veins; the skeletal muscle pump does not work — venous return decreases. According to the Frank–Starling mechanism, ventricular filling and stroke volume decrease and arterial pressure falls. When compensation by the baroreceptor reflex (sympathetic activation) is insufficient, cerebral blood flow decreases and fainting (orthostatic syncope) develops. In the horizontal position with the legs raised, venous return is restored, stroke volume and cerebral blood flow increase — the patient quickly comes round.

s3767 1. Energy exchange and methods of measuring it
s3768 Metabolism in the body consists of two processes: **assimilation (anabolism)** — synthesis of complex substances, which consumes energy; and **dissimilation (catabolism)** — breakdown of substances, which releases energy (topic 21). Ultimately all the energy that enters the body is converted into **heat** (apart from external mechanical work). Therefore energy expenditure can be estimated from the amount of heat released.

s3794 2. Basal metabolism
s3795 **Basal metabolism** is the minimum energy expenditure of the body needed for life (cellular metabolism, breathing, the work of the heart, kidneys and CNS, maintenance of body temperature). It is determined **under standard conditions** : in the morning, after waking, on an empty stomach (12–14 hours after the last meal), at physical and mental rest, lying down, at a **“comfort temperature”** (18–20 °C).
s3796 **Magnitude:** on average **1 kcal/kg/hour** (4.19 kJ); in a 70 kg man ≈ **1700 kcal/day** (7100 kJ), 10% less in women. Relative to body surface area: ≈ 40 kcal/m^2^/hour. **Rubner's surface law**: in warm-blooded animals of different mass, basal metabolism is proportional not to body mass but to **body surface area**.
s3797 **Factors affecting basal metabolism:** sex (higher in men), age (relatively high in children, low in the elderly), height and weight, **thyroid hormones** (the strongest — it increases by up to 50–100% in thyrotoxicosis and decreases by 30–40% in hypothyroidism), temperature (in fever it increases by ≈ 10–13% for every 1 °C), season, nutrition, pregnancy.
s3798 **Read's formula** gives an approximate estimate of the deviation of basal metabolism from the norm (%) from the pulse and arterial pressure: BM (%) = 0.75 × (pulse + pulse pressure × 0.74) − 72. Up to ± 10% is normal. This formula was used for screening thyroid function; today, for an accurate diagnosis, TSH and thyroid hormones are measured in the blood.
