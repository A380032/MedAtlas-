s5005 3. Digestion in the oral cavity
s5006 Food stays in the oral cavity for 15–20 s. Here food is processed **mechanically** (chewing), **moistened with saliva**, the **food bolus** is formed, partial chemical breakdown of carbohydrates begins and the taste of the food is evaluated.

s5029 **Regulation of salivation** is mainly **reflex** (nervous): unconditioned reflexes (receptors of the oral cavity — taste, mechanical, temperature) and conditioned reflexes (the sight and smell of food, mealtime, kitchen sounds). The center is the **salivary nuclei of the medulla oblongata**.
s5030 **Parasympathetic** influence (superior and inferior salivary nuclei; via cranial nerves VII and IX) — a large amount of watery, enzyme-rich saliva; vessels dilate.
s5031 **Sympathetic** influence (Th~2~–Th~4~, superior cervical ganglion) — a small amount of thick, mucin-rich saliva (dry mouth during excitement).
s5032 **Xerostomia** (dry mouth) — in Sjögren syndrome, radiotherapy of the head and neck, many drugs (antidepressants, antihistamines, M-cholinergic blockers), dehydration. Because the protective and remineralizing functions of saliva are lost, **caries increases sharply**, and candidiasis and disorders of taste and swallowing occur. **Sialorrhea** (excessive drooling) — in Parkinson's disease, cerebral palsy, and during teething.
s5033 Chewing
s5034 **Chewing** is the grinding of food, mixing it with saliva and forming the bolus. The chewing center is in the **medulla oblongata** and pons (the chewing rhythm generator); chewing can also be controlled voluntarily (cortex). The masticatory muscles are innervated by the motor fibers of the **trigeminal nerve** (cranial nerve V). Receptors — the oral mucosa, the tooth periodontium (pressure receptors — regulate the biting force), and proprioceptors of the masticatory muscles and the temporomandibular joint.
s5035 **Chewing pressure** is 10–15 kg on the incisors and up to 50–80 kg on the molars (limited by the endurance of the periodontium).
s5036 **Masticatiography** is the graphic recording of the movements of the lower jaw. The masticatiogram shows the phases of one chewing period: I — rest; II — taking food into the mouth; III — the beginning of chewing (adaptation); IV — the main (rhythmic) chewing phase; V — formation of the bolus and swallowing.
s5037 Figure 18.3
s5038 Masticatiogram: phases of the chewing period (Alavia O.T., 2018)
s5039 Swallowing
s5040 **Swallowing** is a complex reflex act that ensures the passage of the bolus from the oral cavity to the stomach (F. Magendie). It consists of three phases:
s5041 **Oral (voluntary) phase** — the tongue lifts the bolus toward the palate and pushes it into the pharynx.
s5042 **Pharyngeal (involuntary, rapid) phase** — the soft palate rises and closes the nasopharynx; the larynx rises and the **epiglottis** closes the laryngeal inlet; breathing stops reflexly (swallowing apnea); the upper esophageal sphincter relaxes. The center is in the medulla oblongata.
s5043 **Esophageal (involuntary, slow) phase** — a peristaltic wave carries the bolus to the stomach in 2–9 s (liquid 1–2 s); the lower esophageal sphincter relaxes reflexly.

s5046 When the swallowing center is damaged (stroke, bulbar palsy), **dysphagia** and entry of food into the airways (aspiration, aspiration pneumonia) are observed. In insufficiency of the lower esophageal sphincter the gastric contents return to the esophagus — **gastroesophageal reflux disease** (heartburn).
