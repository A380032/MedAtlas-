s4887 Calculate the MV and alveolar ventilation. Why is such breathing ineffective?

s4944 **Types of digestion** (by the source of enzymes): **intrinsic** (the body's own enzymes), **symbiotic** (enzymes of the intestinal microflora — breakdown of fiber) and **autolytic** (the food's own enzymes, for example in breast milk). s4945 **The conveyor principle of digestion:** each part of the digestive system processes the food in turn and prepares the next part; the activity of the preceding part influences that of the next (for example, the acid chyme formed in the stomach stimulates pancreatic secretion in the duodenum).

s4980 3. Digestion in the oral cavity
s4981 Food stays in the oral cavity for 15–20 s. Here food is processed **mechanically** (chewing), **moistened with saliva**, the **food bolus** is formed, partial chemical breakdown of carbohydrates begins and the taste of the food is evaluated.

s5004 **Regulation of salivation** is mainly **reflex** (nervous): unconditioned reflexes (receptors of the oral cavity — taste, mechanical, temperature) and conditioned reflexes (the sight and smell of food, mealtime, kitchen sounds). The center is the **salivary nuclei of the medulla oblongata**.
s5005 **Parasympathetic** influence (superior and inferior salivary nuclei; via cranial nerves VII and IX) — a large amount of watery, enzyme-rich saliva; vessels dilate.
s5006 **Sympathetic** influence (Th~2~–Th~4~, superior cervical ganglion) — a small amount of thick, mucin-rich saliva (dry mouth during excitement).
s5007 **Xerostomia** (dry mouth) — in Sjögren syndrome, radiotherapy of the head and neck, many drugs (antidepressants, antihistamines, M-cholinergic blockers), dehydration. Because the protective and remineralizing functions of saliva are lost, **caries increases sharply**, and candidiasis and disorders of taste and swallowing occur. **Sialorrhea** (excessive drooling) — in Parkinson's disease, cerebral palsy, and during teething.
s5008 Chewing
s5009 **Chewing** is the grinding of food, mixing it with saliva and forming the bolus. The chewing center is in the **medulla oblongata** and pons (the chewing rhythm generator); chewing can also be controlled voluntarily (cortex). The masticatory muscles are innervated by the motor fibers of the **trigeminal nerve** (cranial nerve V). Receptors — the oral mucosa, the tooth periodontium (pressure receptors — regulate the biting force), and proprioceptors of the masticatory muscles and the temporomandibular joint.
s5010 **Chewing pressure** is 10–15 kg on the incisors and up to 50–80 kg on the molars (limited by the endurance of the periodontium).
s5011 **Masticatiography** is the graphic recording of the movements of the lower jaw. The masticatiogram shows the phases of one chewing period: I — rest; II — taking food into the mouth; III — the beginning of chewing (adaptation); IV — the main (rhythmic) chewing phase; V — formation of the bolus and swallowing.
s5012 Figure 18.3
s5013 Masticatiogram: phases of the chewing period (Alavia O.T., 2018)
s5014 Swallowing
s5015 **Swallowing** is a complex reflex act that ensures the passage of the bolus from the oral cavity to the stomach (F. Magendie). It consists of three phases:
s5016 **Oral (voluntary) phase** — the tongue lifts the bolus toward the palate and pushes it into the pharynx.
s5017 **Pharyngeal (involuntary, rapid) phase** — the soft palate rises and closes the nasopharynx; the larynx rises and the **epiglottis** closes the laryngeal inlet; breathing stops reflexly (swallowing apnea); the upper esophageal sphincter relaxes. The center is in the medulla oblongata.
s5018 **Esophageal (involuntary, slow) phase** — a peristaltic wave carries the bolus to the stomach in 2–9 s (liquid 1–2 s); the lower esophageal sphincter relaxes reflexly.

s5020 The pharyngeal phase of swallowing: the epiglottis closes the airway (Alavia O.T., 2018)
s5021 When the swallowing center is damaged (stroke, bulbar palsy), **dysphagia** and entry of food into the airways (aspiration, aspiration pneumonia) are observed. In insufficiency of the lower esophageal sphincter the gastric contents return to the esophagus — **gastroesophageal reflux disease** (heartburn).
