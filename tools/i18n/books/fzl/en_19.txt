s4088 **Aldosterone** is essential for life: without it the body loses sodium and water and potassium accumulates — in acute adrenal insufficiency blood pressure falls and hyperkalemia can stop the heart. **Cortisol** is the main “stress hormone”: its secretion increases several times during stress.
s4089 There are many hormones that raise blood glucose (**counter-insulin** hormones): glucagon, adrenaline, cortisol, GH, thyroid hormones. Only one lowers it — **insulin**. That is why the body is better protected against hypoglycemia.
s4090 **“Estrogen builds, progesterone preserves”**: estrogen makes the endometrium grow (proliferation), and progesterone prepares it for pregnancy and maintains it (“pro-gestation” — for pregnancy).
s4091 1. The thyroid gland
s4092 The thyroid gland (20–30 g) lies on the anterior surface of the neck. Its structural and functional unit is the **follicle**: a vesicle lined by a single layer of thyrocytes and filled with **colloid** (thyroglobulin). Between the follicles lie the **parafollicular C cells**, which produce **calcitonin**.

s4094 Thyroid follicles (Alavia O.T., 2018)
s4095 Synthesis of thyroid hormones
s4096 Thyrocytes actively take up **iodide** (I^-^) from the blood through the Na^+^/I^-^ symporter.
s4097 Thyroid peroxidase oxidizes iodide and attaches it to tyrosine residues of **thyroglobulin** (monoiodotyrosine, diiodotyrosine).
s4098 Iodotyrosines combine to form **thyroxine (T~4~)** and **triiodothyronine (T~3~)**; they are stored in the colloid.
s4099 Under the action of TSH thyroglobulin is taken back up by endocytosis and proteolyzed, and T~4~ and T~3~ are released into the blood. 90% of the secreted hormone is T~4~, but the active form is **T~3~** (3–5 times more potent); T~4~ is converted to T~3~ in peripheral tissues by deiodinase. In the blood the hormones are carried mainly bound to proteins. Adults need ≈ 150 µg of iodine per day.

s4129 2. Regulation of calcium and phosphorus metabolism
s4130 Total blood calcium is **2.2–2.6 mmol/l**, ionized (active) calcium 1.1–1.3 mmol/l. Calcium is necessary for muscle contraction, mediator release, blood coagulation and excitability. It is regulated by three hormones.

s4178 The medulla
s4179 The chromaffin cells of the medulla are modified postganglionic sympathetic neurons; they are innervated by preganglionic sympathetic fibers (acetylcholine, N-cholinergic receptors). They secrete **adrenaline** (80%) and **noradrenaline** (20%). The effects of catecholamines resemble those of the sympathetic nervous system (the “fight or flight” reaction) and are discussed in detail in topic 26: increased HR and pressure, bronchodilation, glycogenolysis and lipolysis (hyperglycemia), pupil dilation, and redistribution of blood flow to the muscles.
s4180 Stress and the general adaptation syndrome
s4181 **Stress** (H. Selye, 1936) is the body's nonspecific adaptive reaction to strong stimuli (stressors). The **general adaptation syndrome** consists of three stages: the **alarm stage** (sharp activation of the sympathoadrenal system and the hypothalamus–pituitary–adrenal system), the **stage of resistance** and the **stage of exhaustion** (if the stressor acts for a long time, the adaptive capacities are exhausted and diseases develop). The main hormones are **catecholamines and cortisol**.

s4206 **Regulation of insulin secretion:** the main stimulus is a **rise in blood glucose** (glucose metabolism in β- cells → ATP ↑ → ATP-sensitive K^+^ channels close → depolarization → Ca^2+^ entry → exocytosis of insulin). Amino acids, intestinal hormones — **incretins** (GLP-1, GIP; that is why oral glucose releases more insulin than intravenous glucose) and the vagus nerve also enhance it; the sympathetic nerves (α~2~), adrenaline and somatostatin inhibit it.
s4207 **Diabetes mellitus** is chronic hyperglycemia resulting from insulin deficiency (type 1, autoimmune destruction of β cells) or insulin resistance with relative deficiency (type 2, often with obesity). Classic signs: **polyuria** (glucosuria — osmotic diuresis), **polydipsia** (thirst), **polyphagia**, weight loss. Diagnostic criteria: fasting venous plasma glucose ≥ 7.0 mmol/l, ≥ 11.1 mmol/l 2 hours into a glucose tolerance test, HbA~1c~ ≥ 6.5%.
s4208 5. The sex glands
s4209 Male sex glands (testes)
s4210 In the testes, **Leydig cells** produce **testosterone** under the influence of LH; **Sertoli cells** support spermatogenesis under the influence of FSH and testosterone and secrete **inhibin** (which inhibits FSH).
s4211 **Effects of testosterone:** formation of the male genital organs in the fetal period; primary and secondary sexual characteristics at puberty (deepening of the voice, growth of hair and beard, muscle mass); spermatogenesis; a strong **anabolic** effect (protein synthesis, muscle and bone mass); stimulation of erythropoiesis; sexual drive and behavior. In many tissues testosterone is converted into a more active form — **dihydrotestosterone**.

s4249 Exercise 3: testing the Chvostek and Trousseau signs (demonstration)
s4250 **Chvostek sign**: contraction of the facial muscles (corner of the mouth) when the exit point of the facial nerve in front of the ear is tapped lightly with a hammer.
s4251 **Trousseau sign**: spasm of the hand muscles (“obstetrician's hand”) when a blood pressure cuff is placed on the upper arm and kept above systolic pressure for 3 minutes.
s4252 If the signs are positive, neuromuscular excitability is increased (hypocalcemia).
