s4074 **Aldosterone** is essential for life: without it the body loses sodium and water and potassium accumulates — in acute adrenal insufficiency blood pressure falls and hyperkalemia can stop the heart. **Cortisol** is the main “stress hormone”: its secretion increases several times during stress.
s4075 There are many hormones that raise blood glucose (**counter-insulin** hormones): glucagon, adrenaline, cortisol, GH, thyroid hormones. Only one lowers it — **insulin**. That is why the body is better protected against hypoglycemia.
s4076 **“Estrogen builds, progesterone preserves”**: estrogen makes the endometrium grow (proliferation), and progesterone prepares it for pregnancy and maintains it (“pro-gestation” — for pregnancy).
s4077 1. The thyroid gland
s4078 The thyroid gland (20–30 g) lies on the anterior surface of the neck. Its structural and functional unit is the **follicle**: a vesicle lined by a single layer of thyrocytes and filled with **colloid** (thyroglobulin). Between the follicles lie the **parafollicular C cells**, which produce **calcitonin**.
s4079 Figure 15.1
s4080 Thyroid follicles (Alavia O.T., 2018)
s4081 Synthesis of thyroid hormones
s4082 Thyrocytes actively take up **iodide** (I^-^) from the blood through the Na^+^/I^-^ symporter.
s4083 Thyroid peroxidase oxidizes iodide and attaches it to tyrosine residues of **thyroglobulin** (monoiodotyrosine, diiodotyrosine).
s4084 Iodotyrosines combine to form **thyroxine (T~4~)** and **triiodothyronine (T~3~)**; they are stored in the colloid.
s4085 Under the action of TSH thyroglobulin is taken back up by endocytosis and proteolyzed, and T~4~ and T~3~ are released into the blood. 90% of the secreted hormone is T~4~, but the active form is **T~3~** (3–5 times more potent); T~4~ is converted to T~3~ in peripheral tissues by deiodinase. In the blood the hormones are carried mainly bound to proteins. Adults need ≈ 150 µg of iodine per day.

s4115 2. Regulation of calcium and phosphorus metabolism
s4116 Total blood calcium is **2.2–2.6 mmol/l**, ionized (active) calcium 1.1–1.3 mmol/l. Calcium is necessary for muscle contraction, mediator release, blood coagulation and excitability. It is regulated by three hormones.

s4134 **Hypoparathyroidism** (most often when the parathyroid glands are accidentally removed during thyroid surgery) — hypocalcemia: the excitability of nerves and muscles increases, **tetany** (cramps, “obstetrician's hand”, Chvostek and Trousseau signs), and in severe cases laryngospasm. In **hyperparathyroidism** — leaching of calcium from bone (osteoporosis, fractures), kidney stones, peptic ulcer. **Vitamin D deficiency** leads to rickets in children and osteomalacia in adults.

s4164 The medulla
s4165 The chromaffin cells of the medulla are modified postganglionic sympathetic neurons; they are innervated by preganglionic sympathetic fibers (acetylcholine, N-cholinergic receptors). They secrete **adrenaline** (80%) and **noradrenaline** (20%). The effects of catecholamines resemble those of the sympathetic nervous system (the “fight or flight” reaction) and are discussed in detail in topic 26: increased HR and pressure, bronchodilation, glycogenolysis and lipolysis (hyperglycemia), pupil dilation, and redistribution of blood flow to the muscles.
s4166 Stress and the general adaptation syndrome
s4167 **Stress** (H. Selye, 1936) is the body's nonspecific adaptive reaction to strong stimuli (stressors). The **general adaptation syndrome** consists of three stages: the **alarm stage** (sharp activation of the sympathoadrenal system and the hypothalamus–pituitary–adrenal system), the **stage of resistance** and the **stage of exhaustion** (if the stressor acts for a long time, the adaptive capacities are exhausted and diseases develop). The main hormones are **catecholamines and cortisol**.

s4183 Insulin is the body's only hypoglycemic hormone. Its effects: it enhances the entry of glucose into muscle and fat cells (by bringing GLUT4 transporters to the membrane); in the liver and muscle it increases glycogen synthesis and inhibits gluconeogenesis; it enhances lipogenesis, inhibits lipolysis and the formation of ketone bodies; it enhances protein synthesis; it increases the entry of K^+^ into cells.
s4184 **Regulation of insulin secretion:** the main stimulus is a **rise in blood glucose** (glucose metabolism in β cells → ATP ↑ → ATP-sensitive K^+^ channels close → depolarization → Ca^2+^ entry → exocytosis of insulin). Amino acids, intestinal hormones — **incretins** (GLP-1, GIP; that is why oral glucose releases more insulin than intravenous glucose) and the vagus nerve also enhance it; the sympathetic nerves (α~2~), adrenaline and somatostatin inhibit it.
s4185 **Diabetes mellitus** is chronic hyperglycemia resulting from insulin deficiency (type 1, autoimmune destruction of β cells) or insulin resistance with relative deficiency (type 2, often with obesity). Classic signs: **polyuria** (glucosuria — osmotic diuresis), **polydipsia** (thirst), **polyphagia**, weight loss. Diagnostic criteria: fasting venous plasma glucose ≥ 7.0 mmol/l, ≥ 11.1 mmol/l 2 hours into a glucose tolerance test, HbA~1c~ ≥ 6.5%.
s4186 5. The sex glands
s4187 Male sex glands (testes)
s4188 In the testes, **Leydig cells** produce **testosterone** under the influence of LH; **Sertoli cells** support spermatogenesis under the influence of FSH and testosterone and secrete **inhibin** (which inhibits FSH).
s4189 **Effects of testosterone:** formation of the male genital organs in the fetal period; primary and secondary sexual characteristics at puberty (deepening of the voice, growth of hair and beard, muscle mass); spermatogenesis; a strong **anabolic** effect (protein synthesis, muscle and bone mass); stimulation of erythropoiesis; sexual drive and behavior. In many tissues testosterone is converted into a more active form — **dihydrotestosterone**.

s4227 Exercise 3: testing the Chvostek and Trousseau signs (demonstration)
