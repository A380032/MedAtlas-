s6745 5. The role of the brainstem in regulating muscle tone
s6746 Decerebrate rigidity
s6747 When an animal's brainstem is transected **between the midbrain (red nucleus) and the vestibular nuclei** (between the colliculi), **decerebrate rigidity** develops (C. Sherrington): the **tone of all the extensor (antigravity) muscles rises sharply** — the legs are stretched straight, the head is thrown back and the tail raised; if the animal is set on its feet it stands like a “wooden” figure.
s6748 Figure 24.2
s6749 Decerebrate rigidity — a sharp increase in extensor muscle tone in a cat (Alavia O.T., 2018)
s6750 **Mechanism:** the transection interrupts the **inhibitory** influence of the red nucleus and the higher centers (cortex, basal ganglia) on the extensor muscles, while the **activating** influence of the vestibular nuclei (**Deiters' nucleus**) and the pontine reticular formation on the extensor motoneurons is preserved and becomes dominant. The rigidity is mediated mainly by activation of **γ-motoneurons** (γ-rigidity): if the dorsal roots are cut (afferent input from the muscle spindles is lost), the rigidity disappears. If the transection is made below the vestibular nuclei, rigidity does not develop.
s6751 In humans, in severe brain injuries, stroke and brainstem compression, a **decerebrate posture** (arms and legs extended, forearms rotated inward) and a **decorticate posture** (arms flexed, legs extended — injury above the red nucleus) are observed. The decerebrate posture is considered a prognostically worse sign (the motor response is scored on the Glasgow scale).

s6788 6. The reticular formation
s6789 The **reticular formation (RF)** is a diffuse network of neurons with highly branched dendrites and axons located throughout the brainstem (from the upper spinal cord to the diencephalon).
s6790 Its neurons receive information from all sensory systems (via collaterals), but this information is **nonspecific** (it reflects the strength rather than the type of stimulus).
s6791 **The ascending activating system** (H. Magoun, G. Moruzzi, 1949): the RF diffusely activates the cerebral cortex — it maintains **wakefulness**, attention and consciousness. When the RF is stimulated, a sleeping animal wakes up and on the EEG the slow rhythms are replaced by fast ones (desynchronization); when the RF is damaged (or the brain is transected below the midbrain), the animal falls into a state resembling permanent sleep (coma).
s6792 Figure 24.4
s6793 The ascending activating influence of the reticular formation: collaterals of the sensory pathways to the RF, and diffuse impulses from the RF to the cortex (Alavia O.T., 2018)
s6794 **Descending influences** (Magoun): the **medial (medullary) part** of the RF **inhibits** the activity of spinal motoneurons, while the **lateral (pontine and midbrain) part** **facilitates** (activates) it. These influences act mainly through the γ-motoneurons and regulate muscle tone and reflexes.
s6795 Figure 24.5
s6796 Descending facilitatory (b) and inhibitory (c) influences of the reticular formation: Ct — stimulator; a — initial state (Alavia O.T., 2018)
s6797 **Other functions of the RF:** participation in the respiratory and circulatory centers; regulation of autonomic functions; regulation of the sleep–wake cycle (noradrenergic — locus coeruleus, serotonergic — raphe nuclei); modulation of pain; attention and orienting reactions.
s6798 **Barbiturates** and general anesthetics cause sleep and narcosis mainly by inhibiting the ascending activating system of the RF. Damage to the brainstem RF (injury, stroke, tumors) leads to impaired consciousness — **stupor and coma**.

s6804 Exercise 2: examining brainstem reflexes and cranial nerves in humans
s6805 **Pupillary reflex** (nerve III, midbrain): cover the eye with the palm for a few seconds, then uncover it and observe the constriction of the pupil; check the crossed reaction in the other eye.
s6806 **Corneal (blink) reflex** (nerves V–VII, pons): when the cornea is touched carefully with the tip of a cotton wisp, the eye closes (instructor demonstration).
s6807 **Swallowing and pharyngeal reflex** (nerves IX–X, medulla oblongata): touching the posterior pharyngeal wall with a spatula — a gag movement.
s6808 **Oculocardiac (Dagnini–Aschner) reflex** (nerves V–X): light pressure on the eyeballs — slowing of the pulse (topic 12).
s6809 **Eye movements** (nerves III, IV, VI): the subject follows a hammer in all directions with the eyes.
s6810 **Facial muscles** (nerve VII): wrinkling the forehead, closing the eyes tightly, showing the teeth; **masticatory muscles** (nerve V): clenching the jaws.

s6812 Exercise 3: demonstrating statokinetic reflexes (Bárány chair)
s6813 The subject sits in a rotating chair with the head bent 30° forward and the eyes closed.
s6814 He is rotated evenly 10 times over 20 s and then stopped suddenly.
s6815 The subject opens his eyes and looks ahead — rhythmic movements of the eyeballs (**post-rotational nystagmus**) are observed; their duration is measured with a stopwatch.
s6816 **Interpretation:** normally post-rotational nystagmus lasts 25–35 s. Prolonged nystagmus or nausea and pallor indicate increased excitability of the vestibular apparatus (in occupational selection — pilots, sailors).

s6916 Which part of the brain is damaged? Which cranial nerves are affected? What is the main danger for the patient?

s6918 A patient with a severe brain injury is unconscious; his arms and legs are stretched straight, the forearms rotated inward and the head thrown back, and this posture intensifies with painful stimuli.
s6919 What is this condition? Explain its mechanism.
s6920 Decerebrate posture (decerebrate rigidity). The injury is below the red nucleus and above the vestibular nuclei (at the midbrain–pons level). The inhibitory influence of the red nucleus and higher centers on the extensor muscles has been interrupted, while the activating influence of the vestibular (Deiters') nuclei and the pontine reticular formation is preserved and dominant — extensor muscle tone has risen sharply (mainly through the γ-motoneurons). This posture indicates serious brainstem damage and a poor prognosis; the unconsciousness is due to damage to the ascending activating system of the reticular formation.
