s0289 1.8. Metaplasia
s0290 **Metaplasia** (Greek metaplasso — I form differently) is the **replacement of one type of mature (differentiated) cell by another type of mature cell**. It is a reversible process that represents adaptation by replacing a cell with one better able to withstand adverse conditions.
s0291 **Mechanism.** A mature cell does not directly turn into another type. Metaplasia results from the **reprogramming of the stem (cambial) cells** of the tissue: under the influence of cytokines, growth factors and extracellular matrix components, stem cells differentiate along a different pathway. Certain transcription factors and retinoic acid (vitamin A) play an important role in this process. Metaplasia occurs **only within one tissue type**: epithelium turns into epithelium, and connective tissue into another type of connective tissue.

s0307 Although metaplasia is an **adaptation**, it comes at a price: the squamous epithelium in a smoker's bronchus is resistant, but it **does not secrete mucus and has no cilia** — mucociliary clearance is impaired and infections become more frequent. Most importantly, if the stimulus that caused metaplasia persists, **dysplasia and cancer** develop: squamous metaplasia in the bronchus is the soil for **squamous cell lung carcinoma**, Barrett esophagus for **esophageal adenocarcinoma**, and intestinal metaplasia for **gastric adenocarcinoma**. Therefore metaplasia is considered a **precancerous (background) process**.

s0310 1.9. Dysplasia
s0311 **Dysplasia** (Greek dys — disorder, plasis — formation) is a disturbance of the proliferation and differentiation of epithelial cells, manifested by cellular atypia and disruption of tissue structure (histoarchitecture).
s0312 Signs of dysplasia:
s0313 variation in the shape and size of cells and nuclei (**pleomorphism**);
s0314 enlargement and hyperchromasia of nuclei, increased nuclear-to-cytoplasmic ratio;
s0315 increased number of mitoses, which also occur in the upper layers of the epithelium;
s0316 loss of cell polarity and of layering. By grade, dysplasia is **mild (I), moderate (II) and severe (III)**. Current classifications often use two grades: **low-grade** and **high-grade** intraepithelial neoplasia. When severe dysplasia involves the full thickness of the epithelium but does not cross the basement membrane, it is called **carcinoma in situ**.
s0317 1.10. Clinicopathological section
s0318 Adaptation — when is it beneficial and when harmful?
s0319 Adaptive processes allow the organism to survive under new conditions, but each of them turns into disease beyond a certain limit:
s0320 **Hypertrophy.** In a patient with arterial hypertension, left ventricular hypertrophy preserves cardiac function for years. However, the capillaries cannot adequately supply the thickened myocardium — the risk of decompensation, arrhythmias and sudden cardiac death increases. Left ventricular hypertrophy detected by ECG and echocardiography is an independent risk factor for cardiovascular complications.
s0321 **Hyperplasia.** In an elderly man with prostatic hyperplasia, urination becomes difficult, the bladder wall undergoes compensatory hypertrophy (trabeculated bladder), and then diverticula, urinary retention, cystitis and hydronephrosis develop.
s0322 **Atrophy.** A patient confined to bed for a long time develops muscle atrophy and osteoporosis; therefore early mobilization and rehabilitation are necessary.
s0323 **Metaplasia.** If squamous metaplasia is found in a bronchial biopsy of a long-term smoker, this is a strong argument for quitting: if smoking stops, the metaplasia can regress; if it continues, dysplasia and cancer develop.
s0324 Thanatogenesis and determination of the cause of death
s0325 At autopsy, the pathologist lists the diseases that led to death in the following order: **underlying disease** (the nosological entity that caused death directly or through its complications), **complications of the underlying disease**, **concomitant diseases** and **cause of death**. The clinical and pathological diagnoses are compared (the diagnoses agree or disagree), and the reasons for disagreement are discussed at a clinicopathological conference.
s0326 At the autopsy of a 62-year-old patient with essential hypertension: heart weight 520 g, left ventricular wall 2.2 cm, a fresh infarct focus in the anterior wall of the left ventricle. Structure of the pathological diagnosis: **underlying disease** — myocardial infarction; **background disease** — essential hypertension (concentric hypertrophy of the left ventricle); **complication** — acute heart failure. Here left ventricular hypertrophy is recorded as the **result** of adaptation that has passed into decompensation.
s0327 Glossary of terms
s0328 **Agenesis** — complete absence of an organ or its primordium as a result of disturbed ontogenesis.
s0329 **Aplasia** — persistence of an organ only as an early primordium, without its typical structure.
s0330 **Atrophy** (Greek a — negation, trophe — nourishment) — reduction in the size of cells, tissues and organs during life, with weakening of their function.
s0331 **Heterotopia** — presence or displacement of tissue in a site that is not typical for it.
s0332 **Hypertrophy** (Greek hyper — over, excess, trophe — nourishment) — increase in the size of a cell, tissue or organ.
s0333 **Hypoplasia** — incomplete (subnormal) development of an organ.
s0334 **Dysontogenesis** (disontogenesis) — disturbance of ontogenesis (individual development).
s0335 **Dysplasia** — a marked disturbance of epithelial proliferation and differentiation, with cellular atypia and disruption of histoarchitecture.
s0336 **Metaplasia** (Greek metaplasso — I form differently) — transformation of one type of tissue into another, related type of tissue.

s0352 Skill 1. Gross specimens
s0353 **“Cardiac hypertrophy (myocardial hypertrophy)”.** The heart is enlarged and its weight increased; the left ventricular wall is 2 cm or thicker (normally 0.8–1.2 cm), and the trabecular and papillary muscles are thickened. In the compensation stage the cavity is narrowed (concentric hypertrophy); in decompensation the heart is flabby, the apex is rounded and the cavity is dilated (eccentric hypertrophy, myogenic dilatation). Conclusion: compensatory (work) hypertrophy.
s0354 **“Bladder hypertrophy, prostatic hyperplasia”.** The prostate is enlarged and nodular; the bladder wall is thickened, and muscle bundles bulge beneath the mucosa in a network pattern (trabeculated bladder), sometimes with false diverticula. Conclusion: compensatory hypertrophy of the bladder wall.
s0355 **“Glandular hyperplasia of the endometrium”.** The uterus is enlarged; in its cavity the endometrium is unevenly thickened, with polypoid growths. Conclusion: hormonal (hyperestrogenic) hyperplasia.
s0356 **“Brown atrophy of the liver”.** The liver is small, with a sharp edge, a wrinkled capsule, firm, and brown on the cut surface. Conclusion: a manifestation of generalized atrophy (cachexia) — brown atrophy.
s0357 **“Brown atrophy of the heart”.** The heart is small, there is little subepicardial fat, the coronary arteries are tortuous, and the myocardium is brown. Conclusion: brown atrophy (accumulation of lipofuscin).
s0358 **“Hydronephrosis”.** The kidney is enlarged; the pelvis and calyces are dilated, forming cavities; the parenchyma is thinned, and the cortex and medulla are indistinguishable. Conclusion: pressure atrophy.
s0359 **“Internal hydrocephalus”.** The ventricles of the brain are markedly dilated, and the brain tissue is thinned. Conclusion: pressure atrophy.
s0360 **“Scar after myocardial infarction”.** Whitish, dense scar tissue in the left ventricular wall; the surrounding myocardium is thickened. Conclusion: regenerative hypertrophy (the myocardium around the scar).

s0364 **“Metaplasia of the bronchial epithelium”** (hematoxylin and eosin).
