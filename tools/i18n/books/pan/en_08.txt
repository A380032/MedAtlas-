s1040 3.2. Parenchymal protein degenerations (dysproteinoses)
s1041 In parenchymal protein degenerations, the metabolism of **proteins in the cell cytoplasm** is disturbed. They include **hyaline droplet, hydropic (vacuolar)** and **horny (keratinizing)** degenerations.
s1042 Hyaline droplet degeneration
s1043 **Large, homogeneous, eosinophilic, hyaline-like protein droplets** appear in the cytoplasm; they merge with one another and fill the whole cell. In this degeneration the cell organelles are destroyed, and the process is **irreversible**, often leading to **coagulative necrosis**.
s1044 Location and causes:
s1045 **Kidney** — in the epithelium of the proximal tubules. Cause: **nephrotic syndrome** (glomerulonephritis, amyloidosis, diabetic nephropathy, paraproteinemic nephropathy) — large amounts of protein pass through the glomerular filter, and the tubular epithelium takes it up by pinocytosis, but the lysosomes cannot digest it in time (**insufficiency of the vacuolar–lysosomal system**). The gross appearance is not specific — it is determined by the underlying kidney disease. Clinically — **proteinuria** and casts in the urine.
s1046 **Liver** — **alcoholic hyaline — Mallory bodies** in hepatocytes (aggregates of cytokeratin intermediate filaments); they occur in alcoholic hepatitis, primary biliary cirrhosis and Wilson disease.
s1047 **Plasma cells** — immunoglobulins accumulate in the cytoplasm to form **Russell bodies** (in chronic inflammation).
s1048 **Myocardium** — very rare.

s1053 Hydropic (vacuolar) degeneration
s1054 **Vacuoles filled with cytoplasmic fluid** appear in the cell. The cause is increased permeability of cell membranes, failure of the Na+/K+ pump, and disturbed water–electrolyte and protein metabolism; **hydrostatic pressure** inside the cell increases. The cell enlarges, its cytoplasm fills with clear vacuoles, and the nucleus is displaced to the periphery or becomes vacuolated.
s1055 When the vacuoles merge and occupy the whole cell, the cell turns into a fluid-filled balloon — **ballooning degeneration**. This condition is regarded as **focal liquefactive necrosis** and is **irreversible**.
s1056 **Skin epithelium** — smallpox, chickenpox, burns, edema;
s1057 **Renal tubules** — nephrotic syndrome, glycosuria (diabetes), potassium deficiency (hypokalemic nephropathy), toxic influences (ethylene glycol, some drugs) — **osmotic nephrosis**;
s1058 **Liver** — viral hepatitis (especially hepatitis B) — **ballooning degeneration**;
s1059 **Nerve cells, myocardium, adrenal cortex** — severe intoxications, hypoxia;
s1060 **Tumor** cells (for example, chordoma, the clear cell type of renal cell carcinoma). The gross appearance hardly changes. Microscopically, the fluid in the vacuoles **does not stain** with fat stains (Sudan III) — this distinguishes it from fatty degeneration. Outcome: reversible when mild; in ballooning degeneration — necrosis.

s1062 Hydropic degeneration of the liver (biopsy): hepatocytes are enlarged, with vacuolated, clear cytoplasm (Strukov, Serov, Fig. 2-5)
s1063 In liver biopsy in viral hepatitis B, **ballooning degeneration** and **Councilman bodies** (Topic 2) are the main signs of hepatocyte injury. Their number and distribution are taken into account in grading the activity of inflammation.
s1064 Horny (keratinizing) degeneration
s1065 Formation of **horny substance (keratin)** in excess or in a site where it is not normally formed.
s1066 **Hyperkeratosis** — excessive formation of horny substance in keratinizing stratified squamous epithelium: callus (calluses on the palm), ichthyosis (congenital hereditary hyperkeratosis — “fish skin”), warts.
s1067 **Leukoplakia** (Greek leukos — white) — keratinization of mucous membranes lined by nonkeratinizing stratified squamous epithelium (oral cavity, tongue, esophagus, cervix), in the form of a white patch.
s1068 In **keratinizing squamous cell carcinoma** — **“keratin pearls”** (concentric layers of horny substance).
s1069 **Leukoplakia** is a clinical term (“white patch”) behind which various histologic processes may lie: simple hyperkeratosis or dysplasia. Leukoplakia is considered a **precancerous** condition: some oral leukoplakias turn into squamous cell carcinoma. Therefore a biopsy is necessary in long-standing leukoplakia.
s1070 3.3. Parenchymal fatty degenerations (lipidoses)
s1071 Accumulation of **neutral fats (triglycerides)** in the cytoplasm of cells. In Robbins — **steatosis (fatty change)**. It occurs most often in cells actively involved in lipid metabolism — hepatocytes, cardiomyocytes and renal tubular epithelium.
s1072 **Methods for detecting fats:** on frozen sections (during paraffin processing, alcohol and xylene dissolve the fats — **empty vacuoles** remain in their place with hematoxylin and eosin):
s1073 **Sudan III** — stains fats **orange-red**;
s1074 **Scarlet red** — red;
s1075 **Sudan IV, Nile blue, osmic acid** — black.
s1076 Fatty degeneration of the liver
s1077 Fatty degeneration of the liver is the most common fatty degeneration. **Causes:**
s1078 **alcohol** — the most common cause (in developed countries);
s1079 **obesity, diabetes mellitus, metabolic syndrome** — **nonalcoholic fatty liver disease**;
s1080 **protein-energy malnutrition** (kwashiorkor) — synthesis of apoproteins decreases;
s1081 **hypoxia** — anemia, chronic heart failure;
s1082 **toxins and drugs** — CCl4, phosphorus, arsenic, glucocorticoids, tetracycline;
s1083 **infections and intoxications** — diphtheria, tuberculosis, sepsis;
s1084 acute fatty liver of pregnancy.
s1085 **Mechanism.** In the liver, fatty acids are handled as follows: they arrive from the blood → are converted to triglycerides → combine with apoproteins and are secreted into the blood as **lipoproteins**. If any link in this chain is disturbed, fat accumulates:
s1086 **Excessive delivery of fatty acids to the liver** — starvation, diabetes, alcohol (increased lipolysis in adipose tissue).
s1087 **Decreased oxidation of fatty acids** — hypoxia, mitochondrial damage (alcohol).
s1088 **Increased synthesis of triglycerides** — alcohol (excess NADH).
s1089 **Decreased synthesis of apoproteins** — CCl4, protein deficiency.
s1090 **Impaired secretion of lipoproteins** — alcohol, toxins. **Grossly:** the liver is enlarged and heavy (up to 3–6 kg), its surface is smooth, the edges are rounded, the consistency is soft and doughy, the color is **yellowish or clay-yellow**, and on cutting fat remains on the knife blade. Such a liver is called a “**goose liver**” (French foie gras — the fatty liver obtained by force-feeding geese).
s1091 **Microscopically:** **small-droplet** (initially) or **large-droplet** fat vacuoles in the cytoplasm of hepatocytes; a large vacuole pushes the nucleus to the periphery of the cell — the cell takes on a **“signet ring”** appearance. Fat droplets can rupture and form fatty cysts. By location: **centrilobular** (in the center of the lobule — hypoxia, alcohol, toxins), **periportal** (at the periphery of the lobule — phosphorus poisoning, protein deficiency), **diffuse**.

s1096 **Outcomes:** if the cause is removed, it is **reversible** (for example, 4–6 weeks after stopping alcohol). If it persists, fatty liver may progress to **steatohepatitis** (inflammation and hepatocyte necrosis), **fibrosis** and **liver cirrhosis**.
s1097 Nonalcoholic fatty liver disease is today the most widespread chronic liver disease in the world and is closely associated with obesity and type 2 diabetes mellitus. It is often detected incidentally on ultrasound (“increased echogenicity of the liver”). When it progresses to steatohepatitis, transaminases rise; the basis of treatment is weight loss and correction of metabolic disorders.
s1098 Fatty degeneration of the myocardium
s1099 **Causes:** mainly **chronic hypoxia** (severe anemia, chronic cardiovascular insufficiency) and **intoxications** (diphtheria, alcohol, phosphorus, arsenic poisoning). Mechanism — damaged mitochondria reduce the oxidation of fatty acids, and fat droplets accumulate in cardiomyocytes; in diphtheria, breakdown of membrane structures (**decomposition**) is also important.
s1100 **Grossly:** the heart is slightly enlarged, its cavities are dilated, the myocardium is flabby and clay-yellow. From the endocardial side, especially in the papillary muscles and trabeculae of the left ventricle, **yellow-white transverse stripes** are seen — the “**tiger heart**”. These stripes reflect a **focal** process: fatty degeneration develops in cardiomyocytes around the venules (which receive the least oxygen), while those around the arterioles are spared.
s1101 **Microscopically:** **very fine droplets** of fat in cardiomyocytes (**pulverized (dust-like) fatty change**), later small droplets; the cross-striation disappears.
s1102 **Outcome:** reversible when mild. When severe — necrosis of cardiomyocytes; myocardial contractility decreases — **heart failure**.

s1105 Fatty degeneration of the kidney
s1106 Neutral fats accumulate in the epithelium of the proximal and distal tubules. Mainly in **nephrotic syndrome** (hyperlipidemia and lipiduria — infiltration of fats), rarely in intoxications. The kidney is enlarged and soft; the cortex is swollen, gray, with yellow spots. It often occurs together with hyaline droplet and hydropic degeneration.
s1107 Hereditary lipidoses (storage diseases)
s1108 Because of hereditary deficiency of lysosomal enzymes, particular lipids accumulate (Topic 11):
s1109 **Gaucher disease** — glucocerebrosides accumulate (glucocerebrosidase deficiency); **Gaucher cells** with cytoplasm resembling “**crumpled tissue paper**” in the spleen, liver and bone marrow;
s1110 **Niemann–Pick disease** — sphingomyelin (sphingomyelinase deficiency); foamy macrophages;
s1111 **Tay–Sachs disease** — GM2 ganglioside (hexosaminidase A deficiency); neurons are affected, with a “**cherry-red spot**” on the retina.
s1112 3.4. Parenchymal carbohydrate degenerations
s1113 Carbohydrates in the cell exist mainly as **glycogen** and **glycoproteins** (mucoids, mucins). Glycogen stains magenta with the **PAS reaction** (Schiff reagent) and red with **Best carmine**. Glycogen is water-soluble, so the tissue is fixed in absolute alcohol.
s1114 Disturbed glycogen metabolism
s1115 **Diabetes mellitus** is the main example of acquired disturbance of glycogen metabolism:
s1116 in the **liver**, glycogen stores decrease (glucose does not enter the tissues), but glycogen accumulates in the **nuclei** of hepatocytes — the nucleus is clear and vacuolated (“**holed**” or “**empty nuclei**”);
s1117 in the **kidney** — because of glucosuria, glycogen accumulates in the epithelium of the loop of Henle and the narrow segment of the distal tubules — the epithelium is tall and its cytoplasm is clear and foamy (**Armanni–Ebstein change**);
s1118 **diabetic microangiopathy** — glycoproteins accumulate in the glomeruli, and **nodular (Kimmelstiel–Wilson) glomerulosclerosis** develops. **Hereditary glycogenoses** — hereditary deficiency of enzymes that break down or synthesize glycogen: **von Gierke disease** (type I, glucose-6-phosphatase deficiency — glycogen in the liver and kidney), **Pompe disease** (type II, lysosomal acid maltase — heart, skeletal muscles, liver; cardiomegaly), **McArdle disease** (type V, muscle phosphorylase — skeletal muscles, pain and cramps on exertion).
s1119 Disturbed glycoprotein metabolism — mucous degeneration
s1120 In mucus-producing cells, **mucins and mucoids** accumulate in excess or their physicochemical properties change. Mucus gives **metachromasia** with **thionine and cresyl violet** (red-violet color with a blue dye) and is PAS-positive.
s1121 **Catarrhal inflammation** — increased mucus secretion in mucous membranes (rhinitis, bronchitis, gastritis): goblet cells multiply, much mucus is produced, and cells desquamate. If mucus blocks the excretory ducts, **cysts** form.
s1122 **Cystic fibrosis (mucoviscidosis)** — a hereditary disease: because of a mutation in the CFTR gene, chloride channels are defective and exocrine glands secrete **thick, viscous mucus**. The pancreatic ducts, bronchi, intestine and bile ducts become plugged, and cysts, chronic inflammation and sclerosis develop (Topic 11).
s1123 **Colloid degeneration** — accumulation of colloid in colloid-forming glands (thyroid gland) — **colloid goiter**.
s1124 In **tumors** — mucin-producing adenocarcinomas (**“signet ring cell” carcinoma** — mucus inside the cell, nucleus pushed to the periphery).
s1125 3.5. Stromal-vascular (mesenchymal) protein degenerations
s1126 Stromal-vascular degenerations develop in **connective tissue** and in the walls of vessels. They occur mainly within the **histion** — the microcirculatory bed and the connective tissue around it (ground substance, fibers, cells). Therefore they are often associated with impaired **permeability of the blood–tissue barrier**.
s1127 Stromal-vascular protein degenerations include **mucoid swelling, fibrinoid change (swelling), hyalinosis** and **amyloidosis**. The first three are often **successive stages of one process**: mucoid swelling → fibrinoid swelling → hyalinosis. This sequence is called **disorganization** of connective tissue.
s1128 Mucoid swelling
s1129 **Mucoid swelling** is a **superficial and reversible disorganization** of connective tissue. **Glycosaminoglycans** (mainly hyaluronic acid) accumulate and are redistributed in the ground substance. Because glycosaminoglycans are hydrophilic, tissue and vascular permeability increases, plasma proteins (mainly globulins) and glycoproteins enter the ground substance — the ground substance **swells**. The collagen fibers are preserved but swell and split into fibrils.
s1130 **Microscopically:** the ground substance is basophilic; when stained with **toluidine blue**, it turns **violet-red** instead of dark blue — **metachromasia** (a sign of accumulation of chromotropic substances). There are no gross changes.
s1131 **Causes:** infectious-allergic diseases, **rheumatic diseases** (rheumatic fever, especially in the heart valves and endocardium), atherosclerosis, endocrinopathies.
