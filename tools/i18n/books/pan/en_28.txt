s3661 Grading and staging
s3662 **Grading** is the histologic assessment of a tumor by its **degree of differentiation** and the number of mitoses: **G1** (well differentiated), **G2** (moderately), **G3** (poorly differentiated), **G4** (undifferentiated, anaplastic).
s3663 **Staging** is the extent of spread of the tumor; according to the **TNM system**:
s3664 **T** (tumor) — size and local spread of the primary tumor (Tis — in situ, T1–T4);
s3665 **N** (nodus) — metastases to regional lymph nodes (N0 — none, N1–N3);
s3666 **M** (metastasis) — distant metastases (M0 — none, M1 — present). Clinical stages I–IV are determined on the basis of TNM. **Staging is more important than grading for prognosis and the choice of treatment.**
s3667 9.8. Epithelial tumors (non-organ-specific)
s3668 Benign epithelial tumors
s3669 **Papilloma** — from squamous or transitional epithelium; a papillary tumor (resembling a cauliflower or mulberry); each papilla has a vascular stromal core covered by epithelium; tissue atypia (thickened epithelium, hyperkeratosis). Skin, oral cavity, pharynx, urinary bladder, urinary tract.
s3670 **Adenoma** — from glandular epithelium; **acinar (alveolar)**, **tubular**, **trabecular**, **papillary**, **cystadenoma** (cystic — in the ovary), **fibroadenoma** (abundant stroma — breast). In mucous membranes — **adenomatous polyp**. Adenomatous polyps of the intestine are a precancerous condition.

s3679 Malignant epithelial tumors — carcinoma
s3680 **Cancer (carcinoma)** is the most common malignant tumor (80–90% of all malignant tumors). Mainly **after the age of 40**. It metastasizes first by the **lymphogenous** route.
s3681 The main histologic types:
s3682 **Carcinoma in situ** — cancer that has not broken through the basement membrane.
s3683 **Squamous cell (epidermoid) carcinoma** — in sites lined by stratified squamous epithelium (skin, mouth, esophagus, cervix) or at sites of metaplasia (bronchus). **Keratinizing** (well differentiated) — concentric layers of horny substance in the center of tumor cell cords — **“keratin pearls”** — and intercellular bridges; **nonkeratinizing** (moderately and poorly differentiated).
s3684 **Adenocarcinoma (glandular carcinoma)** — from glandular and columnar epithelium (stomach, intestine, breast, lung, endometrium, prostate); atypical glands of various shapes and sizes, often producing mucus. Types: tubular, papillary, **mucinous (colloid, mucoid) carcinoma**, **signet ring cell carcinoma** (mucus inside the cell, nucleus pushed to the periphery — diffuse gastric cancer).
s3685 **Basal cell carcinoma (basalioma)** — from the basal layer of the skin; on the face, in the elderly; **locally destructive growth**, almost never metastasizes.
s3686 **Transitional cell (urothelial) carcinoma** — in the urinary tract.
s3687 **Solid carcinoma** — a poorly differentiated carcinoma in the form of trabeculae.
s3688 **Small cell carcinoma** — composed of small, lymphocyte-like cells (lung); highly malignant, neuroendocrine.
s3689 **Medullary (brain-like) carcinoma** — abundant parenchyma, scant stroma, soft; **scirrhous (fibrous) carcinoma** — abundant stroma, hard.

s3750 9.10. Tumors of melanin-forming tissue
s3751 **Pigmented nevus (birthmark)** — a benign tumor-like collection of melanocytes; by location: **junctional** (at the border of the epidermis and dermis), **dermal (intradermal)** and **compound (mixed)**. **Dysplastic nevus** — atypical melanocytes; high risk of melanoma.
s3752 **Melanoma** is a **highly malignant** tumor arising from melanocytes; in the skin (often in a nevus or de novo), the eye (retina) and mucous membranes. Risk factors — ultraviolet rays (especially sunburns in childhood), fair skin, many nevi, family predisposition (CDKN2A), BRAF mutation. **Growth phases: radial** (horizontal, along the epidermis — low risk of metastasis) and **vertical** (deep into the dermis — high risk of metastasis). The main prognostic factor is the **thickness of the tumor (Breslow)**. Histologically: atypical melanocytes, numerous mitoses, melanin (there is also a nonpigmented — **amelanotic** — melanoma). It metastasizes very early by the lymphogenous and hematogenous routes (liver, lungs, brain, skin).

s3757 The “ABCDE” signs of melanoma (Topic 4) and any changing pigmented spot are indications for **excisional biopsy**. Early detection and removal of melanoma (Breslow thickness up to 1 mm) lead to almost complete cure; at a late stage the prognosis is unfavorable, but modern therapy (BRAF inhibitors, immune checkpoint inhibitors) considerably prolongs life.
s3758 9.11. Clinicopathological section
s3759 The pathologist and the oncologist: from diagnosis to treatment
s3760 The final diagnosis of a tumor is made only on the basis of **histologic** examination (biopsy, surgical specimen, cytology). The pathologist's report states: the histologic type of the tumor, its grade of differentiation (G), the depth of invasion, invasion of vessels and nerves, the resection margins (tumor present or absent), and the number of lymph nodes with metastases (pTNM). In modern diagnosis, **immunohistochemistry** (determining the origin of the tumor — cytokeratins for epithelium, vimentin for mesenchyme, CD45 for lymphoid tissue, S-100 and HMB-45 for melanoma) and **molecular tests** (for targeted therapy — estrogen and progesterone receptors and HER2 in breast cancer; EGFR and ALK in lung cancer; KRAS in colorectal cancer) are important.
s3761 **Tumor markers** are substances produced by tumor cells and detected in the blood; they are used mainly to monitor the effect of treatment and recurrence: **alpha-fetoprotein** (hepatocellular carcinoma, yolk sac tumor), **carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA)** (colorectal cancer), **PSA** (prostate cancer), **CA-125** (ovarian cancer), **CA 19-9** (pancreatic cancer), **chorionic gonadotropin (beta-hCG)** (choriocarcinoma, testicular tumors). They are not sufficient for diagnosis on their own.
s3762 Screening — early detection of cancer
s3763 **Screening programs** are used to detect precancerous changes and early cancer: cervical smear (**Papanicolaou test**) and papillomavirus testing, **mammography** (breast cancer), **colonoscopy** and fecal occult blood testing (colorectal cancer), low-dose computed tomography (lung cancer in heavy smokers). Prevention: quitting smoking, **vaccination against papillomavirus and hepatitis B**, treatment of H. pylori, sun protection.
s3764 **Oncology** (Greek onkos — tumor) — the science that studies tumors.
s3765 **Tumor** (blastoma — Greek blastano — I sprout; neoplasma — new formation; tumor) — a pathological process characterized by uncontrolled proliferation of cells.
s3766 **Adenoma** (Greek aden — gland, -oma — tumor) — a benign tumor arising from glandular epithelium.
s3767 **Adenocarcinoma** (aden — gland, carcinoma — a malignant tumor of epithelium) — glandular cancer arising from glandular (prismatic) epithelium.
s3768 **Basal cell carcinoma (basalioma)** — a locally destructive tumor arising from the basal cells of the epidermis.
s3769 **Paget disease of the breast** — a malignant tumor of the nipple and areola of the breast.
s3770 **Hidradenoma** — a benign tumor arising from the secretory epithelium of skin glands.
s3771 **Carcinoid** — a neuroendocrine tumor arising from the enterochromaffin (Kulchitsky) cells of the gastric and intestinal mucosa.
s3772 **Melanoma** — a malignant tumor of melanin-forming tissue.
s3773 **Neurinoma (schwannoma)** (Greek neuron — nerve, -oma) — a benign tumor arising from the Schwann cells (lemmocytes) of the nerve fiber sheath.
s3774 **Nevus** — a benign tumor-like lesion of melanin-forming tissue.
s3775 **Papilloma** (Latin papilla — nipple) — a benign papillary tumor arising from stratified squamous or transitional epithelium.
s3776 **Cancer (carcinoma)** — a malignant tumor of epithelium.
s3777 **Carcinoma in situ** — a noninvasive cancer (not breaking through the basement membrane) but with marked cellular atypia.
s3778 **Clear cell (hypernephroid) carcinoma** — the most common malignant tumor of the kidney.
s3779 **Seminoma (dysgerminoma)** — a malignant germ cell tumor (seminoma in the testis, dysgerminoma in the ovary).
s3780 **Syringoadenoma** — a benign tumor of the sweat glands of the skin.
s3781 **Thecoma** — a benign tumor arising from the sex cord stroma of the ovary.
s3782 **Trichoepithelioma** — a benign tumor arising from hair follicles.
s3783 **Choriocarcinoma (chorionepithelioma)** — a malignant tumor arising from the trophoblast after abortion, tubal pregnancy or hydatidiform mole.
s3784 **Exophytic growth** (Greek exo — outside, phyton — growth) — growth of a tumor into the lumen of an organ.
s3785 **Endophytic growth** (Greek endon — inside) — growth of a tumor into the wall of an organ.
s3786 **Expansive growth** (Latin expansus — expanded) — growth of a tumor by itself, compressing the surrounding tissues.
s3787 **Infiltrative (invasive) growth** — growth of tumor cells into the surrounding tissues with their destruction.
s3788 **Metastasis** (Greek metastasis — transfer) — transfer of tumor cells to another site with formation of a new focus.
s3789 **Recurrence** (Latin recidivus — returning) — reappearance of a tumor at the site of its removal.
s3790 **Anaplasia** (Greek ana — backward, plasis — formation) — loss of differentiation.
s3791 **“Uterine fibromyoma”.** The uterus is enlarged, with sharply demarcated, round, firm, whitish nodules of various sizes in its wall; a whorled fibrous pattern on the cut surface. Conclusion: benign mesenchymal tumor (leiomyoma).
s3792 **“Fibroadenoma of the breast”.** A sharply demarcated, encapsulated, firm, whitish-gray, lobulated nodule. Conclusion: benign fibroepithelial tumor.
s3793 **“Ovarian cystadenoma”.** In place of the ovary — a thin-walled, smooth, unilocular or multilocular cyst filled with clear or mucinous fluid. Conclusion: benign epithelial tumor.
s3794 **“Skin papilloma”.** A sharply demarcated papillary (cauliflower-like) lesion protruding from the skin surface. Conclusion: benign epithelial tumor.
s3795 **“Colonic polyp”.** Pedunculated, gray-pink, lobulated lesion(s) on the mucosal surface. Conclusion: adenomatous polyp (precancerous).
s3796 **“Gastric cancer”.** Ulcerated tissue with raised edges (“saucer-shaped”) or diffusely thickened (linitis plastica), firm and whitish, in the gastric wall. Conclusion: malignant epithelial tumor.
s3797 **“Cancer metastases in the liver”.** The liver is enlarged, with sharply demarcated whitish-gray nodules of various sizes, with an “umbilicated” depression in the center. Conclusion: hematogenous metastases.
s3798 **“Osteosarcoma”.** A tumor in the bone metaphysis that destroys the bone and grows into the surrounding soft tissue, with indistinct borders; on the cut surface it is whitish-brown, with foci of hemorrhage and necrosis. Conclusion: malignant mesenchymal tumor.
