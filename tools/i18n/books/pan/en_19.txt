s2669 7.1. Classification of circulatory disorders
s2670 Circulatory disorders are divided into two large groups:
s2671 **Disorders of blood supply (blood filling):** arterial hyperemia, venous hyperemia (congestion), anemia (ischemia).
s2672 **Disorders of the rheological properties of blood and of vessel wall permeability:** bleeding and hemorrhage, plasmorrhagia, stasis, thrombosis, embolism, DIC, shock. In addition, **disorders of lymph circulation** (lymphostasis, lymphedema) and **changes in the amount of tissue fluid** (edema, exsiccosis) are also considered in this section. Circulatory disorders are **generalized** (heart failure, shock) and **local** (thrombosis, infarction).
s2673 7.2. Arterial hyperemia
s2674 **Arterial hyperemia (active hyperemia)** is the filling of an organ or tissue with blood as a result of increased arterial inflow. It is an **active** process: the arterioles dilate (neurogenic and humoral influences). The tissue is **red** (bright), warm and slightly enlarged.
s2675 **General arterial hyperemia (plethora)** — an increase in circulating blood volume (erythremia — Vaquez disease, multiple transfusions).
s2676 **Local arterial hyperemia** is physiological (physical work, blushing with embarrassment) or pathologic. **Types of pathologic hyperemia:**
s2677 **angioneurotic (neuroparalytic)** — loss of the influence of vasoconstrictor nerves or excitation of vasodilator nerves (reddening of the facial skin);
s2678 **collateral** — increased blood flow through collateral pathways when a main artery is occluded;
s2679 **vacant (ex vacuo)** — filling of tissue with blood when barometric pressure falls (for example, when medical cupping glasses are applied);
s2680 **postanemic** — a sudden rush of blood when a compressed artery is released (for example, the abdominal vessels when ascitic fluid is removed rapidly); it can lead to cerebral anemia and fainting;
s2681 **inflammatory hyperemia** — the most common type (Topic 5);
s2682 hyperemia due to an **arteriovenous fistula**.
s2683 7.3. Venous hyperemia (congestion)
s2684 **Venous hyperemia (passive hyperemia, congestion)** is an increase of blood in an organ or tissue as a result of **impaired venous outflow**. It is a **passive** process. The veins and capillaries dilate and fill with deoxygenated blood — the tissue becomes **bluish-red** (**cyanosis**), and its temperature falls.
