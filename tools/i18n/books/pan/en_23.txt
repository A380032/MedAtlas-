s3108 8.1. Structure of the immune system
s3109 The immune system protects the organism from foreign substances (antigens) and distinguishes **“self”** from **“nonself”**. It consists of **innate (nonspecific)** and **acquired (adaptive, specific)** immunity.
s3110 **Innate immunity** — epithelial barriers, phagocytes (neutrophils, macrophages), dendritic cells, **natural killer (NK) cells**, the complement system; it recognizes microbes rapidly but nonspecifically (Toll-like receptors).
s3111 **Acquired immunity** — **lymphocytes** and their products; specific and with memory. **Humoral immunity** — B lymphocytes and antibodies; **cell-mediated immunity** — T lymphocytes.
s3112 Organs of the immune system:
s3113 **central (primary)** — the **thymus** (T lymphocytes mature) and **bone marrow** (B lymphocytes; in birds, the bursa of Fabricius — hence the name “bursa-dependent” or B lymphocytes);
s3114 **peripheral (secondary)** — lymph nodes, spleen, mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue (tonsils, Peyer patches of the intestine — MALT).
s3115 Main cells:
s3116 **T lymphocytes** (thymus-dependent): **CD4^+^ helper T cells** (they produce cytokines and regulate the immune response: Th1, Th2, Th17), **CD8^+^ cytotoxic T lymphocytes (killer T cells)** — kill virus-infected and tumor cells, **regulatory T lymphocytes**
s3117 **(suppressor T cells)** — suppress the immune response and maintain tolerance to self antigens. In the lymph node they lie in the paracortical zone, in the spleen around the arterioles. • **B lymphocytes** — under the action of antigen they turn into **plasma cells** and produce antibodies (immunoglobulins — IgG, IgM, IgA, IgE, IgD). They are located in the follicles of lymph nodes and in the follicles of the splenic white pulp. • **Macrophages and dendritic cells** — process antigen and **present** it to T lymphocytes (antigen-presenting cells). • **NK cells** — kill virus-infected and tumor cells without prior sensitization. **The major histocompatibility complex (MHC; in humans — HLA)** — proteins on the cell surface: **class I** (on all nucleated cells; presents antigen to CD8+ T lymphocytes) and **class II** (on antigen-presenting cells; to CD4+ T lymphocytes). HLA molecules determine tissue compatibility in transplantation; some HLA types are associated with predisposition to autoimmune diseases (for example, HLA-B27 — ankylosing spondylitis, HLA-DR4 — rheumatoid arthritis).
s3118 Changes in the immune organs in response to antigenic stimulation
s3119 Under the action of antigen the **lymph nodes and spleen** enlarge: in the **humoral** response — **follicular hyperplasia** (large germinal centers) and an increase in plasma cells (**plasmacytic transformation**), especially in the medullary cords of the lymph node; in the **cellular** response — **hyperplasia of the paracortical (T) zone**.
s3120 **Accidental (stress) transformation (involution) of the thymus** — rapid shrinkage of the thymus under the action of glucocorticoids in severe stress, infections, intoxication and tumors: lymphocytes in the cortex disappear by apoptosis (a **“starry sky”** picture — macrophages that have engulfed lymphocyte debris), then the border between the cortex and medulla disappears (inversion), and Hassall corpuscles become cystically dilated. **Thymomegaly** — congenital enlargement of the thymus; in older literature, the **“thymicolymphatic state”** (status thymicolymphaticus) was described as a condition with hyperplasia of the thymus, spleen and lymph nodes and hypoplasia of the adrenal glands, predisposing children to sudden death; it is now regarded as a form of congenital immunodeficiency.
