s3543 **Expansive growth** — the tumor grows “by itself”, **compressing** and pushing aside the surrounding tissue; the atrophic tissue and condensed stroma around it form a **pseudocapsule**; the tumor border is sharp. It is characteristic of **benign** tumors.
s3544 **Infiltrative (invasive, destructive) growth** — tumor cells **grow into the surrounding tissue, around vessels and nerves, and destroy it**; the border is indistinct. It is characteristic of **malignant** tumors; the name “**cancer**” (Latin cancer, Greek karkinos — crab) comes from the tumor spreading like the legs of a crab.
s3545 **Appositional growth** — by transformation of surrounding normal cells into tumor cells (for example, multicentric growth). **In relation to a hollow organ: exophytic** — growing into the lumen of the organ (polyp, papillary tumor); **endophytic** — growing into the wall of the organ, in depth (often more malignant, ulcerates).
s3546 **By the number of foci of growth: unicentric** (from one focus) and **multicentric** (from several foci).

s3555 Invasion and metastasis
s3556 **Metastasis** (Greek metastasis — transfer) is the detachment of tumor cells from the primary focus, their transfer to another site and the formation there of a new tumor focus — a **secondary tumor (metastasis)**. **Metastasis is the most reliable sign of a malignant tumor**; benign tumors do not metastasize. About 30% of malignant tumors have already metastasized at the time of diagnosis.
s3557 **Stages of the invasion–metastasis cascade:** 1) detachment of tumor cells from one another (decreased E-cadherin); 2) attachment to the **basement membrane and extracellular matrix** and its destruction by **matrix metalloproteinases** (collagenase IV); 3) migration; 4) entry into a vessel (**intravasation**); 5) survival in the blood together with platelets (tumor emboli); 6) exit from the vessel (**extravasation**) and growth at the new site (angiogenesis).
s3558 Routes of metastasis:
s3559 **Lymphogenous** — the most characteristic early route for **carcinomas**: the regional lymph nodes are involved first. For example, in breast cancer — the axillary nodes; in gastric cancer — the left supraclavicular lymph node (**Virchow metastasis**), the ovaries (**Krukenberg metastasis**), the rectouterine pouch (**Schnitzler metastasis**). The **sentinel lymph node** is the first lymph node to receive lymph from the tumor; its biopsy is used in choosing treatment.
s3560 **Hematogenous** — characteristic of **sarcomas**, and of carcinomas in later stages. Through the portal vein — to the **liver** (tumors of the digestive tract); through the venae cavae — to the **lungs**. Common targets: liver, lungs, bones, brain, adrenal glands. Some tumors tend to metastasize to **bones**: cancers of the prostate, breast, lung, kidney and thyroid.
s3561 **Implantation (contact, across serous cavities)** — when the tumor reaches the serosa, cells fall into the peritoneal or pleural cavity and spread over the entire serous membrane (**carcinomatosis**): cancers of the ovary, stomach and colon; often with hemorrhagic exudate (ascites, pleurisy).
s3562 **Perineural** — along nerves (pancreatic and prostate cancer).
s3563 **Mixed. Recurrence (relapse)** — reappearance of a tumor **at the same site** after its removal or treatment; the cause is residual tumor cells or multicentric growth.

s3600 Effects of tumors on the organism
s3601 **Local effects:** **compression** of surrounding tissues and organs (brain, bile ducts — obstructive jaundice, intestine — obstruction), **obstruction** of hollow organs, destruction and **ulceration**, **bleeding** (tumor breakdown — anemia), secondary infection, **rupture** or **perforation** of an organ, **pathologic bone fracture**.
s3602 **Systemic effects: cancer cachexia** (TNF and other cytokines, loss of appetite — Topic 1), anemia, fever, intoxication, **hormonal** effects (endocrine tumors).
s3603 **Paraneoplastic syndromes** are signs not directly related to the location of the tumor or its metastases; they are often related to ectopic production of hormones or cytokines by the tumor. Examples: **Cushing syndrome** (small cell lung carcinoma — ACTH), **hypercalcemia** (squamous cell lung carcinoma, breast cancer — parathyroid hormone–related protein), the **syndrome of inappropriate antidiuretic hormone secretion** (small cell carcinoma — hyponatremia), **Trousseau syndrome** (migratory thrombophlebitis — adenocarcinoma of the pancreas and lung), **acanthosis nigricans**, **clubbing of the fingers** and hypertrophic osteoarthropathy, **myasthenia-like syndrome** (Lambert–Eaton), polycythemia (renal cell carcinoma — erythropoietin).
s3604 9.6. Carcinogenesis
s3605 **Carcinogenesis** is the process of transformation of a normal cell into a tumor cell. It is based on **genetic damage** (mutations) and **epigenetic changes**; cancer is a **genetic disease**.
s3606 Carcinogenic factors
s3607 **1. Chemical carcinogens.** The first observation was made by the English surgeon **P. Pott** (1775): scrotal skin cancer in chimney sweeps (exposure to soot). The Japanese scientists Yamagiwa and Ichikawa (1915) induced cancer experimentally by painting coal tar on a rabbit's ear.
s3608 **Direct-acting agents** — react with DNA without modification: alkylating agents (some **chemotherapy drugs** — cyclophosphamide; they can cause secondary leukemias).
s3609 **Indirect-acting agents (procarcinogens)** — act after activation in the body (cytochrome P-450): **polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons** (**benzopyrene** — tobacco smoke, tar, smoked products; lung and bladder cancer), **aromatic amines and azo dyes** (aniline dyes — bladder cancer), **aflatoxin B1** (nuts and grain contaminated with the fungus Aspergillus flavus — liver cancer), **nitrosamines** (gastric cancer), vinyl chloride (hepatic angiosarcoma), **asbestos** (mesothelioma, lung cancer), arsenic, chromium, nickel, benzene (leukemia). **Stages of chemical carcinogenesis: initiation** — irreversible DNA damage (mutation) by the carcinogen; **promotion** — proliferation of the mutated cell under the action of **promoters** (hormones, phorbol esters, drugs) (a promoter is not itself carcinogenic and produces a tumor only if it acts after initiation); **progression**.
s3610 2. Physical carcinogens — radiation.
s3611 **Ultraviolet rays** (sun, UVB) — form **pyrimidine dimers** in DNA: **basal cell** and **squamous cell carcinoma** of the skin, **melanoma**; the risk is high in fair-skinned people and in xeroderma pigmentosum (defect of DNA repair).
s3612 **Ionizing radiation** (X-rays, gamma rays, radioactive substances) — double-strand breaks of DNA: **leukemias** (after Hiroshima and Nagasaki), **thyroid cancer** (in children after the Chernobyl accident — radioactive iodine), lung cancer (uranium miners, radon), bone sarcomas. **3. s3632 Molecular basis of carcinogenesis
s3633 Carcinogenesis is a **multistep** process: several mutations must accumulate in a single cell. Mutations occur in four groups of **target genes**:
s3634 **Proto-oncogenes → oncogenes** — genes that normally stimulate cell growth and division (growth factors, their receptors, signaling proteins, transcription factors). As a result of mutation, amplification or translocation, an **activated oncogene** gives a constant growth signal. Oncogenes act **dominantly** (mutation of one allele is enough). Examples: **RAS** (the most frequently mutated oncogene — cancers of the pancreas, colon and lung), **HER2/ERBB2** amplification (breast cancer — treated with trastuzumab), **MYC** (Burkitt lymphoma — t(8;14)), **BCR-ABL** (chronic myeloid leukemia — t(9;22), the “Philadelphia chromosome”; treated with imatinib), **BRAF** (melanoma), **EGFR** (lung adenocarcinoma). 2. **Tumor suppressor genes** — genes that normally **inhibit** cell growth; for a tumor to develop, **both alleles** must be inactivated (**Knudson “two-hit” hypothesis**). The main ones: **TP53** — the “guardian of the genome”: when DNA is damaged, it arrests the cell cycle and initiates repair, and if repair is impossible — apoptosis; it is mutated in more than 50% of human tumors; **RB** — controls the G~1~→S transition of the cell cycle; **APC** (colon), **BRCA1/2**, **NF1**, **VHL** (renal cell carcinoma), **PTEN**, **CDKN2A** (p16). 3. **Genes regulating apoptosis** — overexpression of **BCL2** (follicular lymphoma — t(14;18)), disturbed apoptotic mechanisms (Topic 2). 4. **DNA repair genes** — their defects accelerate the accumulation of mutations in other genes (**genomic instability**): mismatch repair genes (Lynch syndrome), BRCA1/2, nucleotide excision repair (xeroderma pigmentosum). In addition, **epigenetic changes** (hypermethylation of gene promoters — “silencing” of suppressor genes), **microRNAs** and activation of **telomerase** (limitless division) are important.
s3635 Hallmarks of cancer (Hanahan and Weinberg)
s3636 The main properties of cancer cells (“hallmarks of cancer”):
s3637 **Self-sufficiency** in growth signals (oncogenes).
s3638 **Insensitivity** to growth-inhibitory signals (inactivation of suppressor genes).
s3639 **Evasion of apoptosis**.
s3640 **Limitless replicative potential** (activation of telomerase).
s3641 **Angiogenesis** (VEGF) — necessary for a tumor larger than 1–2 mm.
s3642 **Invasion and metastasis**.
s3643 **Altered cellular metabolism** (Warburg effect — aerobic glycolysis).
s3644 **Evasion of immune surveillance** (expression of PD-L1 — immune checkpoint inhibitors act against this). Enabling factors: **genomic instability** and **tumor-promoting inflammation**.

s3647 9.7. Classification and naming of tumors
s3648 Tumors are classified mainly by the **histogenetic** principle (tissue of origin), by **degree of malignancy** and by **location** (WHO classification). **Naming rules:**
s3649 **Benign tumors** — tissue name + the suffix **“-oma”**: fibroma, lipoma, chondroma, osteoma, leiomyoma, hemangioma. Benign epithelial tumors: **papilloma** (from squamous and transitional epithelium, papillary), **adenoma** (from glandular epithelium), cystadenoma (cystic adenoma).
s3650 **Malignant epithelial tumors** — **cancer (carcinoma)**: squamous cell carcinoma, **adenocarcinoma** (glandular carcinoma), transitional cell carcinoma and others.
s3651 **Malignant mesenchymal tumors** — **sarcoma** (Greek sarx — flesh — for its “fish-flesh” appearance on the cut surface): fibrosarcoma, liposarcoma, chondrosarcoma, osteosarcoma, leiomyosarcoma, rhabdomyosarcoma, angiosarcoma.
s3652 **Tumors of hematopoietic and lymphoid tissue** — leukemias and lymphomas.
s3653 **Exceptions** (ending in “-oma” although malignant): **melanoma, seminoma, lymphoma, mesothelioma, hepatoma**; named after an author or a distinctive feature: Wilms tumor (nephroblastoma), Ewing sarcoma, Hodgkin lymphoma.
s3654 **Teratoma** — a tumor composed of tissues of several germ layers (ecto-, meso-, endoderm) (from totipotent germ cells of the ovary and testis): **mature teratoma (dermoid cyst)** — benign; **immature teratoma** — malignant.
s3655 **Mixed tumors** — arising from one tissue but differentiating in different directions (pleomorphic adenoma of the salivary gland).
s3656 **Hamartoma** — disorganized tumor-like growth of tissues native to the organ (a developmental anomaly); **choristoma** — normal tissue in an unusual location (heterotopia — pancreatic tissue in the gastric wall).
