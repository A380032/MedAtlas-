s4338 The period of organogenesis (**weeks 3–8**) is the period **most sensitive** to teratogens: an agent acting at this time disrupts the formation of organ primordia and causes a **congenital malformation**. The time of teratogen exposure determines the **type** of malformation — each organ has its own “critical period” (for example, the heart — weeks 3–6, the palate — weeks 6–9). An agent acting in the fetal period causes not a malformation but a **disease** (infection, inflammation).
s4339 11.1. Features of childhood and the perinatal period
s4340 A child's organism is not a scaled-down copy of an adult's: at each age the structure and course of diseases are distinctive. From the standpoint of pathological anatomy, the following periods are important:
s4341 **antenatal (prenatal) period** — from fertilization to birth;
s4342 **perinatal period** — from the **22nd week** of pregnancy (fetal weight 500 g) to **7 days** after birth (168 hours): it includes the antenatal, intranatal (during delivery) and early neonatal periods;
s4343 **neonatal period** — up to **28 days** after birth;
s4344 **infancy** — up to 1 year; then the periods of childhood. The causes of death in children change with age: **under 1 year** — congenital malformations, prematurity and low birth weight, respiratory distress syndrome, perinatal infections and sepsis, sudden infant death syndrome, asphyxia; **1–4 years** — congenital malformations, accidents, infections (pneumonia, diarrhea), tumors; **5–14 years** — accidents, malignant tumors (leukemias, brain tumors), congenital malformations.

s4366 11.3. Congenital malformations
s4367 **Congenital malformations** are permanent changes in the shape, structure and function of an organ or body part present at birth. They occur in about **3%** of newborns and are one of the main causes of infant mortality.
s4368 Types of anomalies (by the disturbance of morphogenesis):
s4369 **Malformation** — abnormal formation of an organ as a result of a disturbance of its intrinsic genetic program (heart defects, polydactyly, cleft lip and palate);
s4370 **Disruption** — secondary destruction of a previously normally formed organ by an external factor (for example, **amniotic bands** — amputation of fingers or limbs);
s4371 **Deformation** — a change of shape due to mechanical pressure (a narrow uterine cavity, **oligohydramnios** — clubfoot);
s4372 **Sequence** — a chain of secondary changes arising from one primary defect: the **Potter sequence** — renal agenesis → absence of urine → **oligohydramnios**
s4373 → compression of the fetus → flat face, limb deformities, **pulmonary hypoplasia** (the cause of death); • **Syndrome** — a set of several defects caused by one cause (Down syndrome). **Morphologic terms** (Topic 1): **agenesis** (complete absence of an organ), **aplasia** (the organ as a primordium), **hypoplasia** (incompletely developed), **atresia** (closure of an orifice or canal — esophageal, anal atresia), **stenosis** (narrowing), **ectopia** (an organ in an unusual location — the testis remaining in the abdominal cavity — cryptorchidism), **heterotopia** (tissue elements in another organ), **persistence** (survival of embryonic structures — patent ductus arteriosus), **dysraphia** (failure of closure of embryonic clefts — spina bifida with meningocele, cleft lip), **atavism** (appearance of structures typical of phylogenetic ancestors), **gigantism**, **polydactyly** (extra fingers), **syndactyly** (fused fingers).
s4374 **Genetic factors** (about 25%): chromosomal diseases (Down, Edwards and Patau syndromes), monogenic mutations;
s4375 **Environmental factors — teratogens** (about 10%): – **infections** — **TORCH**: toxoplasmosis, others (syphilis, VZV, parvovirus B19, Zika), **rubella** (cataract, heart defects, deafness — the Gregg triad), **cytomegalovirus** (microcephaly, periventricular calcification), **herpes**; – **drugs and chemicals** — **thalidomide** (phocomelia — shortening of the limbs), warfarin, antiepileptic drugs (valproate — neural tube defects), retinoids (isotretinoin), ACE inhibitors, **alcohol**; – **maternal diseases** — **diabetes mellitus** (heart defects, neural tube defects, macrosomia — **diabetic fetopathy**), phenylketonuria; – **radiation**, nutritional deficiency (**folic acid** deficiency — **neural tube**
