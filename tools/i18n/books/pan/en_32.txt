s4300 A 6-year-old child complains of fever, cough and weakness. X-ray: a small subpleural focus in the lower part of the upper lobe of the right lung and enlargement of the hilar lymph nodes. s4357 The period of organogenesis (**weeks 3–8**) is the period **most sensitive** to teratogens: an agent acting at this time disrupts the formation of organ primordia and causes a **congenital malformation**. The time of teratogen exposure determines the **type** of malformation — each organ has its own “critical period” (for example, the heart — weeks 3–6, the palate — weeks 6–9). s4358 11.1. Features of childhood and the perinatal period
s4359 A child's organism is not a scaled-down copy of an adult's: at each age the structure and course of diseases are distinctive. From the standpoint of pathological anatomy, the following periods are important:
s4360 **antenatal (prenatal) period** — from fertilization to birth;
s4361 **perinatal period** — from the **22nd week** of pregnancy (fetal weight 500 g) to **7 days** after birth (168 hours): it includes the antenatal, intranatal (during delivery) and early neonatal periods;
s4362 **neonatal period** — up to **28 days** after birth;
s4363 **infancy** — up to 1 year; then the periods of childhood. The causes of death in children change with age: **under 1 year** — congenital malformations, prematurity and low birth weight, respiratory distress syndrome, perinatal infections and sepsis, sudden infant death syndrome, asphyxia; **1–4 years** — congenital malformations, accidents, infections (pneumonia, diarrhea), tumors; **5–14 years** — accidents, malignant tumors (leukemias, brain tumors), congenital malformations.

s4385 11.3. Congenital malformations
s4386 **Congenital malformations** are permanent changes in the shape, structure and function of an organ or body part present at birth. They occur in about **3%** of newborns and are one of the main causes of infant mortality.
s4387 Types of anomalies (by the disturbance of morphogenesis):
s4388 **Malformation** — abnormal formation of an organ as a result of a disturbance of its intrinsic genetic program (heart defects, polydactyly, cleft lip and palate);
s4389 **Disruption** — secondary destruction of a previously normally formed organ by an external factor (for example, **amniotic bands** — amputation of fingers or limbs);
s4390 **Deformation** — a change of shape due to mechanical pressure (a narrow uterine cavity, **oligohydramnios** — clubfoot);
s4391 **Sequence** — a chain of secondary changes arising from one primary defect: the **Potter sequence** — renal agenesis → absence of urine → **oligohydramnios**
