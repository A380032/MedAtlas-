s4011 Forms and morphology:
s4012 **Mild form** — **serous-catarrhal** inflammation of the upper respiratory tract; degeneration and desquamation of the epithelium; basophilic and fuchsinophilic inclusions in the epithelium.
s4013 **Moderate form** — catarrhal inflammation spreads to the trachea, bronchi and bronchioles; in the lung — **focal influenza pneumonia** — serous-hemorrhagic exudate, macrophages and desquamated epithelium in the alveoli; sometimes **hyaline membranes**.
s4014 **Severe form:** a) **toxic influenza** — severe intoxication: **serous-hemorrhagic** inflammation and necrosis in the respiratory tract, **hemorrhagic pneumonia** and edema in the lung, edema and small hemorrhages in the brain; b) **influenza with pulmonary complications** — a secondary bacterial (staphylococcal, pneumococcal) infection is added, producing the **“large variegated influenza lung”**: focal hemorrhagic pneumonia, abscesses, bronchiectasis, pleurisy. Complications: pneumonia, lung abscess, otitis, sinusitis, meningitis, encephalitis, myocarditis, vascular thromboses.

s4019 Measles
s4020 **Measles** is a highly contagious acute infection, occurring mainly in children, caused by an RNA virus of the paramyxovirus family. The virus enters through the respiratory tract, multiplies in lymphoid tissue and spreads with viremia.
s4021 **in the oral mucosa** (on the buccal mucosa opposite the lower molars) — whitish spots with a red halo — **Belsky–Filatov–Koplik spots** (the pathognomonic early sign of the disease, before the rash);
s4022 **skin rash (exanthem)** — a maculopapular rash beginning on the face and behind the ears and spreading downward; perivascular lymphocytic infiltrate in the dermis; later **branny desquamation** and pigmentation;
s4023 **respiratory tract** — catarrhal-purulent inflammation; in severe cases **necrotizing laryngitis and tracheitis** — “**false croup**”;
s4024 **lung** — **giant cell interstitial pneumonia**: multinucleated (Warthin–Finkeldey) giant cells in the alveolar walls and spaces, with eosinophilic inclusions in their nuclei and cytoplasm; secondary bacterial pneumonia;
s4025 **lymphoid organs** — follicular hyperplasia, **Warthin–Finkeldey giant cells**.
s4026 Complications: pneumonia (the main cause of death in children), **noma** (wet gangrene of the facial soft tissues in debilitated children), encephalitis, later **subacute sclerosing panencephalitis**; immunosuppression (the virus damages lymphocytes — reactivation of tuberculosis). **Prevention — vaccination**.

s4031 Herpesvirus infections
s4032 **Herpes simplex (HSV-1, HSV-2)** — **vesicles** on the skin and mucous membranes (lips, genitals): epithelial cells swell (ballooning degeneration) and rupture, forming an intraepidermal vesicle; **glassy eosinophilic intranuclear inclusions (Cowdry type A bodies)** and multinucleated giant cells in epithelial cells. The virus persists **latently in sensory ganglia** and recurs. Severe forms: **herpetic encephalitis** (hemorrhagic-necrotizing inflammation of the temporal lobes), generalized infection in newborns, esophagitis and pneumonia in immunodeficiency.
s4033 **Chickenpox and herpes zoster (VZV)** — chickenpox in children: a vesicular skin rash (morphology similar to herpes); the virus persists latently in the dorsal root ganglia and later recurs as **herpes zoster (shingles)** (painful vesicles along a nerve).
s4034 **Cytomegalovirus infection (CMV)** — infected cells are **markedly enlarged (cytomegaly)**, with a large basophilic intranuclear inclusion surrounded by a clear halo — the “**owl's eye**”. The salivary glands, lungs, kidneys, liver and brain are most often affected. **Congenital** (intrauterine) infection — microcephaly, periventricular calcification, jaundice, hepatosplenomegaly, deafness; **in immunodeficiency** (AIDS, transplantation) — pneumonia, retinitis, colitis.
s4035 **Epstein–Barr virus** — **infectious mononucleosis** (fever, tonsillitis, lymphadenopathy, splenomegaly, atypical lymphocytes in the blood); association with tumors (Topic 9).

s4044 10.3. Bacterial infections
s4045 Staphylococcal and streptococcal infections
s4046 **Staphylococci** (Staphylococcus aureus) — gram-positive cocci arranged like bunches of grapes; they produce **coagulase**, hemolysins, leukocidin and **enterotoxins**. They cause mainly **purulent-necrotizing** inflammation that **tends to be localized** (abscess):
s4047 skin — **furuncle** (purulent-necrotizing inflammation of a hair follicle and sebaceous gland), **carbuncle** (fusion of several furuncles), panaritium, impetigo;
s4048 **lung** — staphylococcal pneumonia, especially in children and after influenza: abscesses and **staphylococcal destruction of the lung** (air spaces — bullae, pyopneumothorax);
s4049 bone — **osteomyelitis**; heart — **infective endocarditis**; sepsis;
s4050 toxin-mediated diseases — **food poisoning** (enterotoxin), **toxic shock syndrome**, **scalded skin syndrome** in newborns (exfoliative toxin). **Streptococci** (Streptococcus pyogenes — group A beta-hemolytic) — chain-forming cocci; they produce hyaluronidase and streptokinase and cause **diffuse** inflammation of tissue (**phlegmon**): **tonsillitis (angina)**, pharyngitis, **erysipelas** (diffuse edematous inflammation of the dermis, sharply demarcated redness), impetigo, necrotizing fasciitis, sepsis. **Postinfectious immune complications: rheumatic fever** (molecular mimicry) and **poststreptococcal glomerulonephritis** (type III).
s4051 **Scarlet fever** is a childhood disease caused by group A streptococci producing a pyrogenic (erythrogenic) toxin: **necrotic tonsillitis** (necrosis of the tonsils), regional **lymphadenitis**, a fine punctate rash (pale nasolabial triangle — Filatov sign), **“raspberry tongue”**, followed by desquamation of the skin in large sheets. Severe **toxic** and **septic** forms; late complications (in weeks 2–3) — glomerulonephritis, rheumatic fever (the allergic period).

s4056 Diphtheria
s4057 **Diphtheria** is an acute infection caused by Corynebacterium diphtheriae; the main damaging factor is the **exotoxin** (it stops protein synthesis in cells). **Fibrinous inflammation** develops at the portal of entry (Topic 5):
s4058 **in the pharynx and tonsils** (stratified squamous epithelium) — **diphtheritic** inflammation: a thick gray **membrane** firmly attached to the underlying tissue; much toxin is absorbed, and the soft tissues of the neck swell;
s4059 **in the larynx, trachea and bronchi** (columnar epithelium) — **croupous** inflammation: an easily detachable membrane — **true croup**, asphyxia. **Systemic action of the toxin: toxic myocarditis** — fatty degeneration of cardiomyocytes (“tiger heart”), foci of myolysis, interstitial inflammation — **sudden cardiac arrest** in the 2nd week; **parenchymal neuritis** (paralysis of the soft palate, eye muscles and diaphragmatic nerves — in weeks 3–6); hemorrhage and necrosis in the adrenal glands; nephrosis. **Causes of death:** early — asphyxia (true croup) and toxic shock; late — cardiac paralysis (myocarditis), diaphragmatic paralysis.

s4064 Meningococcal infection
s4065 **Meningococcus** (Neisseria meningitidis) is a gram-negative diplococcus; it is transmitted by airborne droplets; mainly in children. Forms:
s4066 **meningococcal nasopharyngitis** — catarrhal inflammation;
s4067 **purulent meningitis (leptomeningitis)** — the leptomeninges are thickened, dull and soaked with yellow-green pus — a **“purulent cap”** (mainly over the convex surface and base of the brain); microscopically, a diffuse neutrophilic infiltrate in the meninges; complications — **hydrocephalus** (obliteration of the CSF pathways), cerebral edema and herniation, encephalitis, abscess;
s4068 **meningococcemia (meningococcal sepsis)** — severe **septic shock**, **DIC**, a **hemorrhagic rash** on the skin (“stellate” necrotic rash), bilateral hemorrhage into the adrenal glands — **Waterhouse–Friderichsen syndrome** (acute adrenal insufficiency) — leads rapidly to death.

s4075 10.4. Tuberculosis
s4076 **Tuberculosis** is a chronic infectious disease caused by Mycobacterium tuberculosis (Koch bacillus, 1882); it affects mainly the lungs but can involve any organ. Mycobacteria are **acid-fast** (red bacilli with the Ziehl–Neelsen method) and resistant to phagocytosis because of their waxy cell wall. Transmission is mainly by **airborne droplets** (from a patient with open tuberculosis), less often through the intestine (cow's milk — M. bovis). According to WHO, tuberculosis is one of the leading causes of death from a single infectious agent in the world; it runs a severe course together with **HIV infection**.
s4077 Pathogenesis
s4078 Mycobacteria are engulfed by alveolar macrophages, but they block the fusion of the phagosome with the lysosome and multiply inside the macrophage (**incomplete phagocytosis**). After about 3 weeks **cell-mediated immunity** develops: **Th1 lymphocytes** produce IFN-gamma and activate macrophages — these turn into **epithelioid cells** and **giant cells** and form a **granuloma** (Topic 6); at the same time **type IV hypersensitivity** develops — **caseous necrosis** and a positive tuberculin (Mantoux) test. Thus in tuberculosis immunity and hypersensitivity appear simultaneously.
s4079 Tissue reactions in the tuberculous focus
s4080 **Alterative reaction** — caseous necrosis predominates (when immunity is low);
s4081 **Exudative reaction** — serous-fibrinous or purulent exudate that rapidly turns into caseous necrosis (when hypersensitivity is high);
s4082 **Productive reaction** — tuberculous granulomas (tubercles) (when immunity is good). Outcomes of the tuberculous focus: **favorable** — encapsulation, calcification (petrification), ossification, scar; **unfavorable** — liquefaction of the caseous mass and formation of a cavity (**cavern**), enlargement of the focus and dissemination.
s4083 Clinicomorphologic forms
s4084 Three main types of tuberculosis are distinguished:
s4085 **1. Primary tuberculosis** — on the first infection (in a nonsensitized organism), most often in children. Its morphologic expression is the **primary tuberculous complex**:
s4086 **primary affect (Ghon focus)** — in the lung parenchyma, usually **in the lower part of the upper lobe or the upper part of the lower lobe**, beneath the pleura, a focus of caseous pneumonia 1–1.5 cm in size (with perifocal inflammation around it);
s4087 **lymphangitis** — tuberculous tubercles along the lymphatic vessels;
s4088 **regional lymphadenitis** — caseous necrosis of the hilar and peribronchial lymph nodes (larger than the primary affect). **Outcomes:** a) **healing** (in most people) — the foci are encapsulated and calcified: the calcified primary affect (**Ghon focus**) and the calcified lymph nodes together form the **Ranke complex** (visible on X-ray); mycobacteria may survive inside for years; b) **progression**: growth of the primary affect — **primary lung cavity**, caseous pneumonia; **lymphogenous** spread (bronchoadenitis, extensive lymph node involvement); **hematogenous** spread (miliary tuberculosis, meningitis) — especially in young children and in immunodeficiency; c) **chronic course**.
s4089 **2. Hematogenous tuberculosis** — spread through the blood from foci that persisted after the primary infection (most often from lymph nodes). Forms:
s4090 **generalized hematogenous tuberculosis** — **miliary tuberculosis**: millet-sized (1–2 mm) whitish-yellow tubercles in many organs (lungs, liver, spleen, kidneys, meninges); in severe cases — **tuberculous sepsis** (necrotic foci without reaction);
s4091 **hematogenous tuberculosis with predominant lung involvement** — bilateral symmetric miliary or large-focal dissemination in the lungs;
s4092 **extrapulmonary hematogenous tuberculosis** — individual organs: **bones and joints** (spine — **Pott disease**, cold abscesses, gibbus), **kidney** (cavities, “pyonephrosis”), genital organs (epididymitis, salpingitis — infertility), adrenal glands (**Addison disease**), **tuberculous meningitis** (gelatinous exudate at the base of the brain), skin, lymph nodes (scrofula). **3.
