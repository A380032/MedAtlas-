s2685 Generalized venous congestion
s2686 **The cause is heart failure** (myocarditis, cardiosclerosis, heart defects, myocardial infarction, hypertension). It is acute or chronic.
s2687 **Acute generalized venous congestion** — in acute heart failure (myocardial infarction, acute myocarditis): capillary hypoxia and permeability increase, with **plasmorrhagia, edema and diapedetic hemorrhages**, degeneration and necrosis in the parenchyma; in the lung — **pulmonary edema**.
s2688 **Chronic generalized venous congestion** — in chronic heart failure. Prolonged hypoxia leads to **parenchymal atrophy** and **stromal sclerosis** (activation of fibroblasts) — the organs become **firm** — **cyanotic (congestive) induration**. Cyanosis of the skin, edema (anasarca), transudate in the cavities (ascites, hydrothorax, hydropericardium).
s2689 Changes in the organs:
s2690 **1. Liver — “nutmeg liver”.** In right ventricular failure, blood stagnates in the inferior vena cava; the hepatic veins, then **the central veins of the lobules and the sinusoids of the central zones** fill with blood. Hepatocytes of the central zone undergo **atrophy and necrosis** because of hypoxia (the lobule center is the least supplied with oxygen), whereas hepatocytes in the periphery undergo **fatty degeneration**. As a result the liver is enlarged and firm, and on the cut surface it is mottled **red-brown (center) and yellowish (periphery)** — resembling the cut surface of a **nutmeg**. If the process is prolonged, **nutmeg fibrosis** and then **cardiac (nutmeg) cirrhosis** develop.

s2695 **2. Lung — brown induration. Left ventricular failure** and especially **mitral stenosis** cause blood stagnation in the pulmonary circulation. The capillaries of the interalveolar septa dilate, and **diapedetic hemorrhages** occur; macrophages engulf red cells that have entered the alveoli and accumulate **hemosiderin** (**“heart failure cells”**, Topic 4); because of chronic hypoxia, **sclerosis** develops in the interalveolar septa. The lung becomes **brown (hemosiderosis)** and **firm (induration)**. Pulmonary hypertension leads to right ventricular hypertrophy.
s2696 **3. Kidneys — cyanotic induration**: the kidney is enlarged, firm and bluish, with a dark red medulla.
s2697 **4. Spleen — cyanotic induration**: enlarged, firm, dark cherry-colored.
s2698 **5. Skin and limbs** — cyanosis, edema, trophic ulcers.

s2703 Local venous congestion
s2704 It occurs when venous outflow from a particular area is impaired: when a vein is occluded by a thrombus or embolus, or compressed from outside (tumor, scar, cast, bandage). Examples: congestion in the **portal venous system in liver cirrhosis** (**portal hypertension**: ascites, splenomegaly, esophageal varices); **Budd–Chiari syndrome** (thrombosis of the hepatic veins); edema and cyanosis of the leg in thrombosis of the leg veins.
s2705 In **portal hypertension**, blood passes from the portal vein to the inferior and superior venae cavae through **portocaval anastomoses**: the veins of the esophagus and gastric cardia (**varices** — sudden massive bleeding from them is one of the main causes of death in patients with cirrhosis), the rectal veins (**hemorrhoids**), and the veins of the anterior abdominal wall (“**caput medusae**”).
s2706 7.4. Anemia (ischemia)
s2707 **Local anemia (ischemia)** is a **decrease or cessation of arterial blood flow** to an organ or tissue. Generalized anemia is **anemia** proper (blood diseases). Causes of local ischemia:
s2708 **angiospastic** — arterial spasm (stress, cold, Raynaud syndrome, cocaine);
s2709 **obturative** — occlusion of the arterial lumen by a thrombus, embolus or atherosclerotic plaque;
s2710 **compressive** — compression of an artery from outside (tumor, tourniquet, ligature);
s2711 **due to redistribution of blood** — ischemia of other areas during postanemic hyperemia. The outcomes depend on the speed and duration of ischemia and the sensitivity of the tissue: **acute ischemia** — degeneration and **infarction** (necrosis); **chronic ischemia** — **atrophy** of the parenchyma and **sclerosis** of the stroma (for example, atherosclerotic nephrosclerosis, chronic cerebral ischemia — dementia).
s2712 7.5. Bleeding and hemorrhage
s2713 **Bleeding (hemorrhage)** is the escape of blood **from a vessel or a cardiac chamber**: **external** (to the outside) and **internal** (into a body cavity or tissue). Accumulation of blood in tissue is called **hemorrhage (extravasation)**.
s2714 Types of bleeding by mechanism:
s2715 **Bleeding due to rupture** (per rhexin) — rupture of the wall of a vessel or the heart: trauma, rupture of an aneurysm, cardiac rupture in myocardial infarction, rupture of a cerebral vessel in essential hypertension.
s2716 **Bleeding due to erosion (arrosive)** (per diabrosin) — destruction of the vessel wall by inflammation, necrosis or tumor: at the floor of a gastric ulcer, in a tuberculous cavity, during tumor breakdown, in ectopic (tubal) pregnancy.
s2717 **Diapedetic bleeding** (per diapedesin) — escape of red cells from microvessels with **increased permeability** without disruption of the vessel wall: hypoxia, vasculitis, infections, intoxications, hemorrhagic diatheses (thrombocytopenia, hemophilia), DIC. Usually **small, pinpoint** hemorrhages.

s2722 Types of hemorrhage:
s2723 **hematoma** — destruction of tissue with formation of a cavity filled with blood (brain, muscles, subcutaneous tissue);
s2724 **hemorrhagic infiltration (imbibition)** — soaking of tissue with blood while its structure is preserved;
s2725 **bruise (ecchymosis)** — a flat hemorrhage in the skin or mucosa larger than 1–2 cm;
s2726 **petechiae** — pinpoint hemorrhages of 1–2 mm (in serous and mucous membranes; asphyxia, thrombocytopenia, sepsis);
s2727 **purpura** — hemorrhages up to 3–5 mm (vasculitis, thrombocytopenia). **Accumulation of blood in cavities:** hemothorax (in the pleural cavity), **hemopericardium** (in the pericardium — **cardiac tamponade**), hemoperitoneum (in the peritoneal cavity), hemarthrosis (in a joint). Bleeding from organs: **nose** (epistaxis), **lung** (hemoptysis — coughing up blood), **stomach** (hematemesis — vomiting of blood, “coffee grounds”), **intestine** (melena — black, tarry stools), **urinary tract** (hematuria), **uterus** (metrorrhagia, menorrhagia).
s2728 **Outcomes:** resorption of blood (small hemorrhages); accumulation of hemosiderin with formation of a **“rust-colored” cyst** (in the brain); organization and encapsulation; suppuration. The significance depends on the volume and rate of blood loss (if more than 20% of the total blood volume is lost rapidly — **hemorrhagic shock**) and on the location (brainstem, pericardium).
s2729 7.6. Plasmorrhagia and stasis
s2730 **Plasmorrhagia** is the escape of blood plasma from the vascular lumen with **soaking** of the vessel wall and surrounding tissues (**plasmatic imbibition**). Mainly in microcirculatory vessels — essential hypertension, atherosclerosis, rheumatic diseases, diabetes mellitus. The vessel wall is thickened and homogeneous; the outcome is **hyalinosis** (arteriolohyalinosis) or **fibrinoid necrosis** (Topic 3).
s2731 **Stasis** (Greek stasis — standstill) is the **cessation of blood flow** in microcirculatory vessels (mainly capillaries). In the capillaries, red cells stick together to form homogeneous columns (**sludge phenomenon** — red cell aggregation), but there is no hemolysis or clotting (unlike a thrombus). Causes: infections, intoxications, shock, cold or heat. Stasis is reversible (blood flow is restored); if prolonged, it leads to necrosis.

s2736 7.7. Edema
s2737 **Edema** is an **increase in intercellular (interstitial) tissue fluid**. Accumulation of fluid in cavities is called **dropsy** (Greek hydrops): **hydrothorax** (pleura), **hydropericardium**,
s2738 **ascites** (peritoneal cavity, hydroperitoneum), **hydrocele** (tunica vaginalis of the testis). Diffuse edema of the subcutaneous tissue — **anasarca**. Edema fluid is a **transudate** (low protein, clear; Topic 5).
s2739 Mechanisms of edema (Starling forces):
s2740 **Increased hydrostatic pressure in capillaries** — heart failure (generalized venous congestion), venous thrombosis, portal hypertension.
s2741 **Decreased plasma oncotic pressure (hypoproteinemia)** — **nephrotic syndrome** (albumin is lost in the urine), **liver cirrhosis** (albumin synthesis decreases), protein-energy malnutrition (“hunger edema”).
s2742 **Increased vascular permeability** — inflammation, allergy (Quincke edema (angioedema)), toxins.
s2743 **Impaired lymph flow** (**lymphedema**) — involvement of lymph nodes by tumor or their removal (arm edema after breast cancer surgery), parasites (filariasis — **elephantiasis**), postirradiation sclerosis.
s2744 **Sodium and water retention** — kidney failure, secondary hyperaldosteronism (activation of the renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system in heart failure). **Types of edema: cardiac edema** (begins in the legs — gravitational, worse in the evening; anasarca); **renal edema** (begins in the face and eyelids, worse in the morning; widespread in nephrotic syndrome); **hepatic edema** (mainly ascites); **hunger edema**; **inflammatory and allergic edema**; **lymphedema**.
s2745 **Morphology:** edematous skin is pale and doughy, and a pit remains when pressed with a finger. Edematous organs are enlarged and heavy, and fluid flows from the cut surface. **Pulmonary edema** — the lung is heavy (2–3 times normal), and frothy pink fluid flows from the cut surface; the alveoli are filled with pale pink protein-containing fluid; in left ventricular failure (acute — myocardial infarction, hypertensive crisis) and toxic and allergic influences. **Cerebral edema** — the brain is enlarged, the gyri are flattened and the sulci narrowed; **displacement (herniation)** of the brain and **impaction into the foramen magnum** (herniation of the cerebellar tonsils into the foramen magnum) — the brainstem is compressed, and the respiratory and cardiac centers stop functioning — death.
s2746 **The significance of edema** depends on its location: edema of the legs causes discomfort and predisposes to trophic ulcers; **laryngeal edema** (Quincke edema) — asphyxia; **pulmonary edema** — acute respiratory failure (emergency care: oxygen, diuretics); **cerebral edema** and brain herniation — a cause of death. With prolonged edema, sclerosis develops in the tissue.
s2747 **Exsiccosis (dehydration)** — a decrease in body water: severe diarrhea (cholera, food poisoning), vomiting, profuse sweating, diabetes mellitus and diabetes insipidus. The skin is dry and wrinkled, the eyes are sunken, and the blood thickens; in severe cases — hypovolemic shock.
s2748 7.8. Hemostasis and thrombosis
s2749 Normal hemostasis
s2750 **Hemostasis** is a complex system that stops bleeding when a vessel is injured and at the same time keeps the blood fluid in intact vessels. Its participants are the **endothelium, platelets** and **coagulation factors**. Stages of hemostasis:
s2751 **Brief arteriolar vasoconstriction** (under the action of endothelin).
s2752 **Primary hemostasis** — **platelet adhesion** to the exposed subendothelial matrix at the site of injury (via **von Willebrand factor**), their activation (release of ADP and thromboxane A2 from granules) and **aggregation** — a **platelet plug** forms.
s2753 **Secondary hemostasis** — **tissue factor** (factor III) triggers the coagulation cascade (via factor VII), and **thrombin** is formed; thrombin converts fibrinogen to **fibrin** and further activates platelets.
s2754 **Stabilization and contraction (retraction) of the clot**, followed by its dissolution by **fibrinolysis** (plasmin). Normal endothelium has **antithrombotic** properties: prostacyclin (PGI2) and NO (inhibit platelet aggregation), thrombomodulin (turns thrombin into an activator of protein C), heparin-like molecules (activate antithrombin III), tissue plasminogen activator (t-PA).
s2755 Thrombosis
s2756 **Thrombosis** is **clotting** of blood (formation of a clot) in the vascular lumen or cardiac chambers **during life**. The clot formed is a **thrombus**. Thrombosis is a pathologic activation of hemostasis.
s2757 Causes of thrombosis — Virchow's triad:
s2758 **Endothelial injury** — the most important factor (it alone can cause thrombosis): atherosclerotic plaques, endocardial injury in myocardial infarction, vasculitis, hypertension, trauma, bacterial toxins, cigarette smoke, hypercholesterolemia.
s2759 **Abnormal blood flow** — **slowing (stasis)** (in veins — heart failure, immobility, varicose veins; in the cardiac chambers — atrial fibrillation, dilated ventricles, aneurysms) or **turbulence** (in arteries — at plaques and bifurcations).
s2760 **Increased coagulability of blood (hypercoagulability, thrombophilia)**: – **primary (hereditary)** — **factor V mutation (Leiden)** — the most common; prothrombin
s2761 gene mutation, deficiency of antithrombin III, protein C and protein S; – **secondary (acquired)** — prolonged bed rest, surgery and trauma, **malignant tumors**
s2762 (Trousseau syndrome — migratory thrombophlebitis), pregnancy and the postpartum period, **oral contraceptives**, smoking, obesity, **antiphospholipid syndrome**, heparin-induced thrombocytopenia, nephrotic syndrome.
s2763 **Stages of thrombus formation (morphogenesis):** 1) **agglutination** of platelets (adhesion and aggregation); 2) **coagulation of fibrinogen**, formation of fibrin; 3) **agglutination** of red cells (trapping in the fibrin network); 4) **precipitation** of plasma proteins.
s2764 Types of thrombi
s2765 By structure and appearance:
s2766 **White thrombus** — composed of platelets, fibrin and leukocytes; it forms **slowly**, in places with **rapid** blood flow — in **arteries** and on heart valves (in endocarditis).
s2767 **Red thrombus** — composed of a fibrin network with entrapped red cells; it forms where clotting is **rapid** and blood flow is **slow** — in **veins**.
s2768 **Mixed (laminated) thrombus** — the most common; it has a **head** (with the structure of a white thrombus, attached to the vessel wall), a **body** (mixed — alternating white and red layers: **lines of Zahn**) and a **tail** (red thrombus, free). It occurs in veins, the aorta and cardiac aneurysms.
s2769 **Hyaline thrombi** — in microcirculatory vessels, composed of hemolyzed red cells, platelets and precipitated proteins; a homogeneous mass; in **DIC** and shock. **By relation to the vessel lumen: mural (parietal)** — partially occludes the lumen; **occlusive (obturating)** — completely occludes the lumen.
