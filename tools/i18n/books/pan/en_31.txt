s4102 10.5. Syphilis
s4103 **Syphilis** is a chronic infection with a wavelike course caused by Treponema pallidum; it is transmitted mainly **sexually**, and also vertically (transplacentally) and parenterally. Treponemes cannot be seen with ordinary stains — **silver impregnation**, dark-field microscopy, immunofluorescence are used; diagnosis is mainly by **serologic** tests (nontreponemal — RW (Wassermann reaction), RPR; treponemal — RIF, TPHA).
s4104 In every stage of syphilis the main morphologic feature is **damage to small vessels**: **obliterating endarteritis** (endothelial proliferation, thickening of the vessel wall) and a **perivascular lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate** (abundance of **plasma cells** is characteristic).

s4130 10.6. Sepsis
s4131 **Sepsis** is life-threatening **organ dysfunction** caused by a **dysregulated host response** to infection (Sepsis-3, 2016). In traditional pathological anatomy, sepsis is a **nonspecific** (similar with different agents), **noncyclic** generalized infectious disease characterized by constant or periodic entry of agents and toxins into the blood from a local infectious focus.
s4132 Pathological concepts of sepsis:
s4133 **Septic focus (primary focus)** — a local focus of purulent inflammation from which the agent enters the blood; it often coincides with the **portal of entry**; the septic focus often shows **purulent lymphangitis** and **thrombophlebitis** (purulently dissolved thrombi — a source of bacterial embolism).
s4134 **Types of sepsis by portal of entry: therapeutic (parasepsis)** — from infection of internal organs; **tonsillogenic**; **surgical** (postoperative, wound); **uterine (obstetric)** — after childbirth and abortion; **otogenic**; **odontogenic**; **urogenic**; **umbilical sepsis** (in newborns); **cryptogenic** — when no focus is found.
s4135 Clinicomorphologic forms:
s4136 **Septicemia** — severe **intoxication** without metastatic purulent foci: hemorrhages in the skin and mucous membranes, **septic spleen** (enlarged, soft, bright red on the cut surface, yielding abundant pulp on scraping), hyperplasia of lymph nodes, degeneration and interstitial inflammation in parenchymal organs, hemolysis (jaundice), DIC; **septic shock**.
s4137 **Septicopyemia** — the main feature is **metastatic abscesses** in various organs (lungs, kidneys, myocardium, brain, liver) (the result of bacterial embolism, for example, **embolic purulent nephritis** — Topic 7); most often caused by staphylococci and Pseudomonas aeruginosa.
s4138 **Septic (bacterial) endocarditis** — **polypous-ulcerative endocarditis** of the heart valves (most often aortic and mitral; tricuspid in drug users): large thrombotic vegetations on the valves, destruction (perforation), microbial colonies; **thromboembolic syndrome** (infarcts of the kidney, spleen, brain), **immune complex syndrome** (glomerulonephritis, vasculitis, Roth spots, Osler nodes). Acute (staphylococcus, on a healthy valve) and subacute (viridans streptococcus — Streptococcus viridans, on a previously damaged valve) forms.
s4139 **Chronic sepsis** — prolonged suppuration (osteomyelitis, carious teeth), cachexia, amyloidosis.
s4140 **Septic shock** is the main mechanism of death from sepsis: systemic vasodilation, endothelial damage, DIC and “shock organs” under the action of endotoxin and cytokines (TNF, IL-1) (Topic 7). The most important principle of treatment is giving antibiotics **within the first hour** and **eliminating the septic focus** (opening an abscess, removing an infected catheter or tissue).
s4141 10.7. Fungal and protozoal infections (in brief)
s4142 **Candidiasis** — Candida albicans (an opportunistic yeast-like fungus); it develops with antibiotics, glucocorticoids, diabetes mellitus, immunodeficiency (AIDS) and in newborns. White plaques in the oral cavity (“**oral thrush**”), the esophagus (candida esophagitis), the vagina; in severe cases **candida sepsis** (microabscesses in the kidney, brain, heart). Microscopically — yeast cells and **pseudohyphae** (PAS-positive).
s4143 **Aspergillosis** — Aspergillus fungi: **aspergilloma** (a “fungus ball” in old cavities), **allergic bronchopulmonary aspergillosis**, **invasive aspergillosis** (in neutropenia — invades vessels, causing hemorrhagic infarcts). The hyphae are thin, septate and branch **at acute angles**.
s4144 **Malaria** — caused by Plasmodium (falciparum, vivax, malariae, ovale); transmitted by female Anopheles mosquitoes. Plasmodia multiply in red cells and destroy them — **hemolysis**, attacks of fever. **Hemomelanin (malarial pigment)** accumulates in macrophages (Topic 4): the **spleen** is enlarged and gray-black (“**slate spleen**”), and the liver and bone marrow are dark. **Tropical malaria** (P. falciparum) — **cerebral malaria**: infected red cells stick to cerebral capillaries and occlude them — hemorrhages, necrosis (Dürck granulomas), coma; acute kidney injury, severe anemia.
s4145 **Leishmaniasis** — Leishmania (protozoa), transmitted by sand flies (Phlebotomus). **Cutaneous leishmaniasis** (“**pendinka**”, Borovsky disease — found in Central Asia) — ulcerating nodules in the skin; **visceral leishmaniasis (kala-azar)** — leishmaniae multiply in macrophages: marked **splenomegaly**, hepatomegaly, bone marrow involvement (pancytopenia), cachexia; **Leishman–Donovan bodies** in macrophages.

s4154 10.8. Clinicopathological section
s4155 The pathological diagnosis of an infectious disease
s4156 The diagnosis of a patient who died of an infectious disease states: the **underlying disease** (nosological form, clinicomorphologic variant, laboratory-confirmed agent), **complications**, **concomitant diseases**. When an infectious disease is suspected, material is taken at autopsy for **bacteriologic, virologic and histobacterioscopic** examination; a special biosafety regime is used for highly dangerous infections; when an infectious disease is identified, the sanitary-epidemiological service is notified.
s4157 Example: “Underlying disease: fibrocavernous pulmonary tuberculosis (bacteriologically confirmed), a cavity in the upper lobe of the right lung. Complications: erosion of a vessel in the cavity wall, pulmonary hemorrhage, aspiration of blood. Concomitant disease: secondary amyloidosis of the kidneys and spleen.”
s4158 **Prevention of infections — vaccination.** The national immunization schedule against measles, diphtheria, pertussis, tetanus, poliomyelitis, hepatitis B, tuberculosis (BCG), papillomavirus infection and others prevents severe infectious diseases and deaths in children. In areas where vaccination coverage has declined, epidemics of measles and diphtheria are reappearing.
s4159 **Cytomegaly** (Greek kytos — cell, megalos — large) — a disease characterized by the appearance of large cells (cytomegalic cells) in the epithelium.
s4160 **Exanthem** (Greek exanthein — to blossom) — skin rash.
s4161 **Enanthem** (Greek en — inside, anthein — to blossom) — rash on mucous membranes.
s4162 **Staphylococci** (Greek staphyle — bunch of grapes, kokkos — grain) — gram-positive cocci arranged like bunches of grapes (family Staphylococcaceae).
s4163 **Streptococci** (Greek streptos — chain) — chain-forming, spherical or oval, non-spore-forming, gram-positive, facultatively anaerobic bacteria (family Streptococcaceae).
s4164 **Diphtheria** (Greek diphthera — leather membrane) — an acute infectious disease distinguished by the formation of a membrane as a result of fibrinous inflammation.
s4165 **Meningococcus** (Neisseria meningitidis) — gram-negative diplococci of the genus Neisseria.
s4166 **Candidiasis** (oral thrush) — a fungal infection caused mainly by Candida albicans; the fungi are opportunistic and cause disease when immunity is reduced.
s4167 **Aspergillosis** — the general name of all diseases caused by fungi of the genus Aspergillus.
s4168 **Malaria** (Italian mala aria — “bad air”) — a group of vector-borne infectious diseases caused by Plasmodium and transmitted by the bite of female Anopheles mosquitoes.
s4169 **Leishmaniases** (named after W. Leishman) — a group of mainly zoonotic, vector-borne parasitic diseases with natural foci, caused by protozoa of the genus Leishmania and found in tropical and subtropical regions.
s4170 **Primary affect** — the initial change that develops at the portal of entry of an infection.
s4171 **Cavern (cavity)** (Latin caverna — cave) — a cavity formed as a result of discharge of necrotic tissue.
s4172 **Tubercle** (Latin tuberculum — small knob) — the tuberculous granuloma.
s4173 **Chancre** (French chancre — sore) — the primary affect of syphilis, a painless ulcer with a firm base.
s4174 **Syphilides** — skin and mucosal rashes in secondary syphilis.
s4175 **Sepsis** (Greek sepsis — putrefaction) — a generalized infectious disease with organ dysfunction resulting from a dysregulated response to infection.
s4176 **Septicopyemia** (Greek pyon — pus) — sepsis with metastatic purulent foci.
s4177 **“Primary tuberculous complex”.** A yellowish caseous focus 1–1.5 cm in size beneath the pulmonary pleura (primary affect); the hilar lymph nodes are enlarged and cheese-like on the cut surface. Conclusion: primary tuberculosis.
s4178 **“Miliary tuberculosis of the lung”.** Numerous millet-sized whitish-yellow tubercles in the lung. Conclusion: generalized hematogenous tuberculosis.
s4179 **“Fibrocavernous tuberculosis”.** A cavity with a thick, dense wall in the lung apex, its inner surface lined by caseous mass; sclerosis and foci of different ages around it; the pleura is thickened. Conclusion: secondary pulmonary tuberculosis.
s4180 **“Staphylococcal destruction of the lung”.** Irregular abscesses and air spaces in the lung tissue. Conclusion: staphylococcal pneumonia.
s4181 **“Purulent leptomeningitis”.** The leptomeninges are thick, dull and soaked with yellow-green pus. Conclusion: purulent meningitis (meningococcal).
s4182 **“The spleen in malaria”.** The spleen is enlarged, firm and gray-black (slate-colored) on the cut surface. Conclusion: malaria (hemomelanosis).
s4183 **“The liver in visceral syphilis”** and **“syphilitic aortic aneurysm”** — see Topic 6.
