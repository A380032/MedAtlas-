There is no safe dose of alcohol. Taking **folic acid** before pregnancy and in the first months significantly reduces the risk of neural tube defects.

s4406 Monogenic (Mendelian) diseases
s4407 Diseases caused by mutation of a single gene; by mode of inheritance:
s4408 **Autosomal dominant** — **one** mutant allele is enough for the disease; it occurs in every generation; 50% of the patient's children are affected. Most often defects of **structural proteins** and receptors: **Marfan syndrome** (fibrillin-1 — tall stature, arachnodactyly, lens dislocation, **aortic aneurysm and dissection**), **familial hypercholesterolemia** (LDL receptor — early atherosclerosis), **neurofibromatosis** (NF1), polycystic kidney disease (adult type), achondroplasia, Huntington disease.
s4409 **Autosomal recessive** — when **both** alleles are mutated; the parents are healthy carriers (heterozygotes); each child has a 25% chance of being affected; more common in **consanguineous marriages**. Most often **enzyme** defects: **phenylketonuria**, **galactosemia**, **cystic fibrosis**, **lysosomal storage diseases** (Gaucher, Niemann–Pick, Tay–Sachs — Topic 3), **glycogenoses**, hemochromatosis (Topic 4), Wilson disease, sickle cell anemia, thalassemia, albinism.
s4410 **X-linked (recessive)** — mainly **boys** are affected, the mother is a carrier: **hemophilia A and B**, **Duchenne muscular dystrophy** (dystrophin), glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase deficiency, Bruton agammaglobulinemia, Lesch–Nyhan syndrome. **Phenylketonuria** — deficiency of the enzyme phenylalanine hydroxylase: phenylalanine and its products accumulate and **damage the brain** (intellectual disability, seizures, demyelination); melanin is low (fair skin, light hair); it is prevented by neonatal **screening** and a phenylalanine-free diet.
s4411 **Galactosemia** — deficiency of galactose-1-phosphate uridyltransferase: after milk intake galactose-1-phosphate accumulates — **fatty degeneration of the liver** and cirrhosis, **cataract**, brain damage, renal tubular damage; it is prevented by a milk-free diet.
s4412 **Cystic fibrosis (mucoviscidosis)** — mutation of the **CFTR** gene (most often deltaF508) — chloride channel function is impaired: exocrine glands secrete **thick, viscous mucus**, while the sweat glands excrete much sodium chloride (the **sweat test** is the basis of diagnosis). It is the most common lethal autosomal recessive disease in white populations. **Morphology:**
s4413 **pancreas** (85–90%) — the ducts are plugged with mucus and dilated (**cysts**), with atrophy and fibrosis of the acini — exocrine insufficiency (fatty stools, weight loss, deficiency of fat-soluble vitamins);
s4414 **lungs** — the most severe involvement and the main cause of death: the bronchi are plugged with mucus, **chronic bacterial infections** (staphylococcus, **Pseudomonas aeruginosa**), **bronchiectasis**, abscesses, pulmonary fibrosis, cor pulmonale;
s4415 **intestine** — **meconium ileus** in newborns;
s4416 **liver** — focal biliary cirrhosis; **reproductive system** — congenital absence of the vas deferens in men (infertility); sweat glands — no morphologic changes.

s4423 11.5. Prematurity and fetal growth restriction
s4424 A **premature (preterm)** infant is born **before 37 weeks** of pregnancy. A **low-birth-weight** infant weighs **less than 2500 g** at birth. Prematurity is the most important cause of neonatal death (respiratory distress syndrome, necrotizing enterocolitis, sepsis, intracerebral hemorrhages).
s4425 Risk factors for prematurity: **premature rupture of membranes**, **intrauterine infection** (chorioamnionitis — one of the most important causes), anomalies of the uterus, cervix and placenta (placenta previa, premature placental separation), multiple pregnancy.
s4426 **Intrauterine growth restriction (small-for-gestational-age fetus)** — causes: **fetal factors** (chromosomal diseases, congenital malformations, infections — symmetric restriction), **placental factors** (placental infarcts, vascular insufficiency — asymmetric: the brain relatively spared), **maternal factors** (preeclampsia, arterial hypertension, malnutrition, smoking, alcohol, drug abuse).
s4427 **Signs of prematurity and immaturity** (at autopsy): low weight and length, scant subcutaneous fat, fine hairs on the skin (lanugo), soft ear and nasal cartilages, nails not reaching the fingertips, undescended testes in boys, labia majora not covering the labia minora in girls; immature internal organs (embryonic glomeruli in the lungs and kidneys, few sulci and gyri in the brain).
s4428 11.6. Diseases of the perinatal period
s4429 Birth injury
s4430 **Birth injury** is mechanical damage to the infant's tissues during delivery. Risk factors: a large fetus, a narrow pelvis, abnormal fetal presentation (breech presentation), precipitate or prolonged labor, use of obstetric forceps and vacuum extractor, prematurity.
s4431 **Caput succedaneum** — edema and hemorrhage of the soft tissues of the presenting part of the head (resolves quickly);
s4432 **Cephalohematoma** — hemorrhage beneath the periosteum of the skull; its border does not cross the bone sutures;
s4433 **Intracranial hemorrhages** — **subdural hematoma** (tears of the falx cerebri and tentorium cerebelli, rupture of veins — marked molding of the head), **subarachnoid** hemorrhage; **in premature infants** — **intraventricular and periventricular (germinal matrix) hemorrhages** (immature vessels, hypoxia, fluctuations in blood pressure);
s4434 **spinal cord** injury, **brachial plexus** injury (Erb palsy), clavicle fracture, hemorrhage into the liver and adrenal glands.
s4435 **Asphyxia (hypoxia)** — oxygen deficiency in utero or during delivery: umbilical cord entanglement, placental separation, abnormal labor. Morphology: venous congestion, **petechial hemorrhages** (pleura, pericardium, thymus, internal organs), **aspiration of amniotic fluid** (meconium — in the lungs), **hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy** in the brain (later **cerebral palsy** — CP).
s4436 Respiratory distress syndrome (hyaline membrane disease)
s4437 **Neonatal respiratory distress syndrome** is the main disease of premature infants and one of the causes of their death. The main cause is **lung immaturity** and **surfactant deficiency**. Surfactant (phospholipids, mainly lecithin) is produced by type II alveolar cells and reduces surface tension in the alveoli; its adequate synthesis begins after the **35th week** of pregnancy.
s4438 **Risk factors:** prematurity (in 60% of infants born before 28 weeks), **maternal diabetes mellitus** (insulin suppresses surfactant synthesis), cesarean section (absence of labor stress and glucocorticoids), male sex, twins.
s4439 **Pathogenesis:** surfactant deficiency → the alveoli collapse with each expiration (**atelectasis**) → hypoxia and hypercapnia → acidosis → pulmonary vasoconstriction and damage to the endothelium and epithelium → leakage of plasma proteins (fibrinogen) into the alveoli → **hyaline membranes form from fibrin and necrotic cell debris** → gas exchange worsens further (a vicious circle).
s4440 **Morphology:** the lungs are of normal size but **firm, airless, red-violet and liver-like**, and **sink** in water. Microscopically: alternating areas of **atelectasis** and dilated alveoli; **eosinophilic, homogeneous hyaline membranes** lining the respiratory bronchioles, alveolar ducts and alveoli; little inflammation. Reparative changes in infants who survived more than 48 hours.
s4441 **Complications: bronchopulmonary dysplasia** (prolonged mechanical ventilation and oxygen toxicity — chronic lung damage), **retinopathy of prematurity**, patent ductus arteriosus, intraventricular hemorrhages, necrotizing enterocolitis.
s4442 Giving **glucocorticoids** (dexamethasone, betamethasone) before delivery to a pregnant woman at risk of preterm birth accelerates surfactant synthesis in the fetal lungs; **exogenous surfactant** is given to the infant through the trachea, and breathing is supported. These measures have sharply reduced deaths from respiratory distress syndrome. Lung maturity can be assessed by the **lecithin/sphingomyelin ratio** in amniotic fluid (above 2:1).

s4447 Necrotizing enterocolitis
s4448 **Necrotizing enterocolitis** is inflammation and necrosis of the small and large intestine, mainly in **premature infants** (especially after formula feeding is started). Intestinal **ischemia**, **bacterial colonization**, immaturity of the intestinal barrier and enteral feeding are important in its pathogenesis. The **terminal ileum, cecum and ascending colon** are most often affected: the intestine is dilated, its wall thinned and dark red-black (hemorrhagic infarction and necrosis), with **gas bubbles in the submucosa** (**pneumatosis intestinalis** — a diagnostic sign on X-ray), perforation and peritonitis. As a consequence — intestinal strictures.

s4451 Perinatal infections
s4452 Infection of the fetus and newborn is transmitted by two routes:
s4453 **Transcervical (ascending)** — the vaginal and cervical microflora (group B streptococcus, Escherichia coli, mycoplasmas) infect the amniotic fluid and membranes — **chorioamnionitis** (neutrophilic infiltrate in the membranes) and **funisitis** (inflammation of the umbilical cord); the fetus swallows or aspirates the infected amniotic fluid — **congenital pneumonia**, sepsis. Chorioamnionitis is an important cause of premature rupture of membranes and **preterm birth**.
s4454 **Transplacental (hematogenous)** — agents in the maternal blood cross the placenta: **TORCH** — toxoplasmosis, others (syphilis, listeriosis, parvovirus B19, HIV, hepatitis B), rubella, cytomegalovirus, herpes. Their clinicomorphologic manifestations are common: fever, encephalitis, chorioretinitis, hepatosplenomegaly, pneumonitis, myocarditis, hemolytic anemia, rashes; depending on the period of pregnancy at which infection occurred — congenital malformations or fetal disease. **Neonatal sepsis: early** (in the first 7 days of life, most often in the first 4–5 days) — agents transmitted from the mother (**group B streptococcus**, Escherichia coli, Listeria) — pneumonia, sepsis, meningitis; **late** (from 7 days to 3 months) — hospital infections (Listeria, Candida, staphylococci), **umbilical sepsis** (the umbilical wound is the portal of entry: purulent omphalitis, purulent thrombophlebitis and arteritis of the umbilical vessels).

s4457 Hemolytic disease of the newborn and hydrops fetalis
s4458 **Hydrops fetalis** is **generalized edema** of the fetal body and accumulation of fluid in body cavities during intrauterine development. There are **immune** and **nonimmune** types.
s4459 **Immune hydrops — hemolytic disease of the newborn** — **antigenic incompatibility** between maternal and fetal blood: an Rh-positive fetus of an Rh-negative mother (most often the **D antigen**), less often ABO incompatibility. During a previous pregnancy, abortion or delivery, fetal red cells enter the maternal blood and sensitize her; in the next pregnancy maternal **IgG antibodies** cross the placenta and destroy fetal red cells (**type II hypersensitivity**, Topic 8). Therefore the first pregnancy is usually not affected.
