s1132 **Outcome:** if the cause is removed, the tissue is completely **restored**; otherwise it progresses to **fibrinoid swelling**.

s1137 Fibrinoid change (fibrinoid swelling and fibrinoid necrosis)
s1138 **Fibrinoid swelling** is a **deep and irreversible disorganization** of connective tissue: the ground substance and collagen fibers are destroyed, vascular permeability increases sharply, and plasma proteins, including **fibrinogen**, enter the tissue. As a result **fibrinoid** forms — a complex substance containing proteins (fibrin), polysaccharides, collagen breakdown products and often immune complexes.
s1139 **Microscopically:** the collagen fibers appear as a homogeneous, dense, **eosinophilic** structureless mass; special reactions for fibrin (Schueninov, Weigert) are positive; with the **van Gieson** method it stains **yellow** (not red like collagen); PAS-positive; metachromasia is weak or absent. Around fibrinoid swelling there is a macrophage reaction (for example, **Aschoff–Talalaev granulomas** in rheumatic fever).
s1140 The end of fibrinoid swelling is **fibrinoid necrosis** (Topic 2): complete destruction of connective tissue.
s1141 Types and causes:
s1142 **Infectious-allergic** — in hyperergic reactions (tuberculosis, Arthus phenomenon);
s1143 **Allergic (immunopathological)** — in **rheumatic diseases** (rheumatic fever, systemic lupus erythematosus, polyarteritis nodosa, scleroderma) and **vasculitis**;
s1144 **Angioneurotic** — fibrinoid necrosis of the walls of arterioles and arteries in **malignant arterial hypertension**;
s1145 **In chronic inflammation** — at the **floor of a chronic ulcer** of the stomach and duodenum, in appendicitis. **Outcome:** fibrinoid necrosis → sclerosis or **hyalinosis**. Organ function is impaired (for example, acute kidney injury in malignant hypertension, valvular defects in rheumatic fever).
s1146 Hyalinosis
s1147 **Hyalinosis** (Greek hyalos — transparent, glassy) is the accumulation in connective tissue of a mass **resembling hyaline cartilage, homogeneous, translucent and dense** — **hyaline**. Hyaline is a **fibrillar protein** containing plasma proteins (fibrin, immunoglobulins, complement), lipids and glycoproteins. It is **eosinophilic**, PAS-positive, stains yellow or red with van Gieson, and is resistant to acids, alkalis and enzymes.
s1148 Hyalinosis is often the **end result** of plasmorrhagia, fibrinoid swelling, inflammation, necrosis and sclerosis. It is divided into **vascular hyalinosis** and **connective tissue hyalinosis**.
s1149 **1. Vascular hyalinosis** — mainly in **small arteries and arterioles**. Hyaline accumulates beneath the endothelium; the vessel wall thickens, and the lumen narrows or is completely occluded. Hyaline formation results from leakage of plasma into the vessel wall (**plasmorrhagia**) and accumulation of basement membrane material.
s1150 Three types of vascular hyaline:
s1151 **simple hyaline** — from unchanged or slightly changed plasma components; in **benign hypertension** and atherosclerosis;
s1152 **lipohyaline** — rich in lipids and beta-lipoproteins; in **diabetes mellitus**;
s1153 **complex hyaline** — from immune complexes, fibrin and breakdown products of the vessel wall; in **immunopathological diseases** (rheumatic diseases). Vascular hyalinosis is **systemic** (in many organs) and is most pronounced in the vessels of the **kidney, brain, retina and pancreas** in hypertension and diabetes. Physiological hyalinosis occurs in the splenic arterioles (in adults and the elderly, related to the blood-storing function of the spleen) and in the ovarian corpus luteum after the menstrual cycle.

s1158 In arterial hypertension and diabetes mellitus, hyalinosis of the renal arterioles leads to ischemia and sclerosis of the glomeruli (**arteriolosclerotic nephrosclerosis**): the kidney becomes small, its surface finely granular — the “**primary contracted kidney**”, and chronic kidney disease develops as a result. Hyalinosis of the cerebral arterioles makes their walls brittle and increases the risk of **hemorrhagic stroke**.
s1159 **2. Connective tissue hyalinosis** — collagen fibers swell, fuse with one another and turn into a dense, homogeneous, cartilage-like mass; the cells are compressed and atrophy. It is usually the end result of fibrinoid change, inflammation and sclerosis. **Examples:**
s1160 **scars** (keloid scar), fibrous adhesions of serous cavities;
s1161 **heart valves** — after rheumatic endocarditis (the valves thicken and become deformed — a heart defect);
s1162 **splenic capsule** — after perisplenitis: the capsule is thickened, dense, milky white and translucent — the “**glazed (sugar-coated) spleen**”;
s1163 **pleura** (outcome of pleurisy with hyalinosis), atherosclerotic plaques, organized thrombi and infarcts. **Outcome:** usually **irreversible**; sometimes hyaline may be resorbed in keloid scars. Its functional significance depends on location: arteriolar hyalinosis — atrophy and sclerosis of the organ; hyalinosis of the heart valves — a heart defect.
s1164 Amyloidosis (in brief)
s1165 **Amyloidosis** is a stromal-vascular protein degeneration characterized by deposition in tissues of **amyloid** — a fibrillar protein with a **beta-pleated sheet** structure formed from **misfolded proteins**. This degeneration develops by the mechanism of **perverted synthesis**. Amyloid stains red-orange with **Congo red** and shows **apple-green** birefringence in polarized light; it turns blue-violet with iodine and sulfuric acid (Virchow reaction) and gives **metachromasia** (red with methyl violet).
s1166 The causes, classification (AL, AA, ATTR and others), pathogenesis and organ changes of amyloidosis (sago and lardaceous spleen, “large white amyloid kidney”) are discussed in detail together with diseases of the immune system in **Topic 8**.
s1167 3.6. Stromal-vascular fatty degenerations
s1168 This group includes disorders of the metabolism of **neutral fats** and of **cholesterol and its esters**.
s1169 Disorders of neutral fat metabolism
s1170 An increase in adipose tissue stores can be **generalized** or **local**.
s1171 **General obesity (adipositas)** — excessive accumulation of neutral fats in the subcutaneous tissue, omentum, mesentery, mediastinum and epicardium. The **degree of obesity** is currently assessed by the **body mass index (BMI)**: BMI = weight (kg) / height^2^ (m^2^).

s1183 **Causes and types of obesity: alimentary** (overeating, physical inactivity), **cerebral** (damage to the hypothalamus — tumor, trauma, infection), **endocrine** (Cushing syndrome, hypothyroidism, hypogonadism, insulinoma), **hereditary** (mutations of the leptin or leptin receptor gene, Laurence–Moon–Biedl syndrome). Obesity is also distinguished by **enlargement** of fat cells (hypertrophic obesity — in adults, a relatively benign course) or **an increase in their number** (hyperplastic obesity — from childhood, difficult to treat).
s1184 **Fatty infiltration of the heart (adiposity of the heart)** — in general obesity the subepicardial adipose tissue increases, envelops the heart like a “sheath” and grows into the **myocardial stroma**, between the muscle fibers. The cardiomyocytes are compressed and undergo **atrophy**. The wall of the **right ventricle** is especially affected — it can be completely replaced by adipose tissue, which may lead to **cardiac rupture**. This is an example of false hypertrophy (Topic 1).

s1187 Local accumulation of fat:
s1188 **Dercum disease** (painful lipomatosis) — painful nodular deposits of fat in the subcutaneous tissue of the limbs and trunk;
s1189 **vacant (ex vacuo) fatty replacement** — growth of adipose tissue in place of an atrophic organ (for example, in renal atrophy the perirenal adipose tissue grows and takes its place);
s1190 **lipodystrophy** — loss or increase of adipose tissue at insulin injection sites.
s1191 In obesity, adipose tissue is not simply a “store” but an active endocrine organ: it produces leptin, adiponectin, resistin and inflammatory cytokines (TNF, IL-6). Therefore obesity (especially the abdominal, visceral type) increases the risk of **insulin resistance, type 2 diabetes mellitus, arterial hypertension, atherosclerosis, nonalcoholic fatty liver disease**, obstructive sleep apnea, osteoarthritis and some cancers (endometrium, breast, colon).
s1192 Disorders of the metabolism of cholesterol and its esters
s1193 Disorders of the metabolism of cholesterol and its esters are most evident in **atherosclerosis**: cholesterol and lipoproteins accumulate in the intima of arteries (infiltration mechanism), forming **foam cells** (lipid-laden macrophages and smooth muscle cells) and **atheromatous plaques**. Atherosclerosis is studied in detail in systemic pathology.
s1194 Other examples:
s1195 **xanthomas** — collections of cholesterol-laden macrophages in the skin and tendons (yellow nodules); in familial hypercholesterolemia;
s1196 **xanthelasma** — yellow patches on the eyelids;
s1197 **cholesterolosis** — cholesterol-laden macrophages in the gallbladder mucosa (“**strawberry gallbladder**”).

s1200 3.7. Stromal-vascular carbohydrate degenerations
s1201 These degenerations are associated with disturbed metabolism of **glycoproteins and glycosaminoglycans**. A mucus-like substance accumulates in place of the collagen fibers of connective tissue — **mucinous change of tissues**. Connective, adipose, cartilaginous and bone tissues become translucent and mucoid, and their cells become stellate.
s1202 **Causes:** dysfunction of the endocrine glands — **myxedema** (hypofunction of the thyroid gland: mucus accumulates in the skin and in the stroma of the heart and other organs — “mucous edema”), cachexia (mucinous change of adipose tissue), chronic heart failure.
s1203 **Outcome:** the process can be reversible; if prolonged, liquefactive necrosis and cavities (**mucous cysts**) form.

s1234 Clinical examples
s1235 **Nephrotic syndrome.** A 6-year-old child has edema, 4 g of protein in the urine per day and hyperlipidemia. Kidney biopsy shows **hyaline droplet**, **hydropic** and **fatty degeneration** together in the epithelium of the proximal tubules — all of them result from **infiltration** of the tubules by proteins and lipids that have passed through the glomerular filter. When the underlying disease is treated and proteinuria decreases, the tubular changes largely regress.
s1236 **Diphtheritic myocarditis.** Diphtheria exotoxin stops protein synthesis in cardiomyocytes and impairs the oxidation of fatty acids — **“tiger heart”**; myocardial contractility decreases, and there is a risk of sudden cardiac arrest in the 2nd week of the disease.
s1237 **Malignant hypertension.** A patient with a blood pressure of 240/140 mm Hg develops acute kidney injury; the biopsy shows **fibrinoid necrosis** of the afferent arterioles and hyperplastic arteriolosclerosis with an “onion skin” appearance.
s1238 In **rheumatic fever** all stages of connective tissue disorganization can be observed: in an acute attack — **mucoid swelling** of the heart valves (reversible; with proper treatment the valve recovers), then **fibrinoid change** and Aschoff–Talalaev granulomas, and then **sclerosis and hyalinosis** — the valve cusps thicken and fuse with one another, and a heart defect such as **mitral stenosis** forms. Therefore early diagnosis of rheumatic fever and prevention of its recurrence (benzathine benzylpenicillin (bicillin) prophylaxis) are very important.
s1239 **Degeneration (dystrophy)** (Greek dys — disorder, trophe — nourishment) — the appearance of structural changes in cells and intercellular substance as a result of disturbed tissue metabolism.
s1240 **Trophism** — the set of mechanisms that ensure tissue metabolism and structural organization.
s1241 **Infiltration** — excessive entry of substances from blood and lymph into the cell or intercellular substance.
s1242 **Decomposition (phanerosis)** — breakdown of complex substances of cells and tissues, so that their components become “visible”.
s1243 **Dysproteinosis** — a degeneration associated with disturbed protein metabolism.
s1244 **Lipidosis** — a degeneration associated with disturbed fat (lipid) metabolism.
s1245 **Thesaurismosis** (Greek thesaurismos — storing) — a storage disease of substances due to hereditary enzyme deficiency.
s1246 **Hyaline** (Greek hyalos — glass) — a dense, homogeneous, translucent protein mass resembling hyaline cartilage.
s1247 **Hyperkeratosis** — excessive formation of horny substance.
s1248 **Leukoplakia** (Greek leukos — white, plax — plate) — keratinization of a nonkeratinizing mucous membrane (“white patch”).
s1249 **Metachromasia** — staining of tissue in a color different from that of the dye (violet-red with toluidine blue).
s1250 **Mucoid swelling** — a superficial, reversible disorganization of connective tissue with accumulation of glycosaminoglycans.
s1251 **Fibrinoid** — a complex fibrin-like substance containing fibrin, plasma proteins and tissue breakdown products.
s1252 **Plasmorrhagia** — leakage of plasma into and beyond the vessel wall.
s1253 **Steatosis** — accumulation of neutral fats (triglycerides) in parenchymal cells.
s1254 **Xanthoma** (Greek xanthos — yellow) — a yellow nodule composed of cholesterol-laden macrophages.
s1255 **Myxedema** — accumulation of a mucus-like substance in tissues in hypofunction of the thyroid gland.
s1256 **“Fatty degeneration of the liver (‘goose liver’)”.** The liver is enlarged, with rounded edges, a smooth surface and a soft, doughy consistency, yellowish or clay-yellow; on cutting, fat remains on the knife blade. Conclusion: parenchymal fatty degeneration.
