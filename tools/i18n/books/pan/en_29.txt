s3964 **Sepsis terminology has changed.** In the textbooks of Strukov and Abdullakhodjaeva, sepsis is divided into **septicemia** (without metastatic purulent foci) and **septicopyemia** (with metastatic abscesses), and “bacteremia” and “septic endocarditis” are considered separately. s3965 10.1. General characteristics of infectious diseases
s3966 **Infectious (communicable) diseases** are diseases caused by pathogenic microorganisms (viruses, bacteria, fungi, protozoa, helminths, prions) and often transmitted from person to person. Their development depends on the interaction of the **causative agent** (virulence, dose, tropism), the **host** (immune status, age, nutrition, comorbidities) and **environmental** conditions.
s3967 General features of an infectious disease:
s3968 **Portal of entry** — the site where the agent enters the organism: skin (wound, insect bite), respiratory tract (airborne droplets), digestive tract (fecal–oral), genitourinary tract (sexual), parenteral route (blood, injection), transplacental (vertical).
s3969 **Primary infectious complex** — changes at the portal of entry (**primary affect**) + **lymphangitis** as the agent spreads through the lymph + involvement of the regional lymph nodes (**lymphadenitis**). For example, the primary tuberculous complex, the hard chancre and regional lymphadenitis in syphilis.
s3970 **Routes of spread** — contiguous (to neighboring tissues), canalicular (along the bronchi, urinary and bile ducts), lymphogenous, hematogenous, perineural.
s3971 **Local and systemic changes** — local: inflammation; systemic: hyperplasia of lymphoid organs, degeneration in parenchymal organs, rash, intoxication.
s3972 **Cyclic course** — incubation period → prodromal period → period of main manifestations → recovery or complications.
s3973 Mechanisms by which microorganisms damage tissue
s3974 **Direct cell damage** — viruses multiply inside the cell and destroy it (cytopathic effect), alter cell metabolism and induce apoptosis;
s3975 **Toxins** — **exotoxins** (proteins secreted by bacteria: diphtheria, tetanus, botulinum and cholera toxins) and **endotoxin** (the lipopolysaccharide of the gram-negative bacterial wall — fever, septic shock, DIC);
s3976 **Enzymes** — proteases, hyaluronidase (spread of streptococci), coagulase (staphylococci);
s3977 **Immune-mediated damage** — hypersensitivity reactions (type IV in tuberculosis; type III in poststreptococcal glomerulonephritis; molecular mimicry in rheumatic fever).

s3996 10.2. Viral infections
s3997 Viruses are obligate intracellular parasites. The common features of most viral infections: **mononuclear (lymphocytic) infiltration**, **interstitial inflammation**, **degeneration and necrosis** of epithelial cells, **viral inclusions** (intranuclear or cytoplasmic), **multinucleated giant cells**, and addition of a secondary **bacterial infection**.
s3998 Influenza
s3999 **Influenza** is an acute respiratory infection caused by RNA-containing **influenza viruses (A, B, C)** and transmitted by airborne droplets. Because of the variability of the **hemagglutinin (H)** and **neuraminidase (N)** antigens of virus A, epidemics and **pandemics** occur (the 1918 “Spanish flu” — 50 million deaths). The virus damages the epithelium of the respiratory tract (**epitheliotropic**) and has a **toxic** effect on the vessels (increased permeability, hemorrhages).
