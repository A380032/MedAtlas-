s2416 **Reparative (restorative) regeneration** — restoration of tissue damaged by pathological processes. It is of two kinds:
s2417 – **restitution (complete regeneration)** — the defect is filled with **exactly the same** tissue; mainly in labile and stable tissues (epithelium, connective tissue, bone) and when the damage is small; – **substitution (incomplete regeneration)** — the defect is filled with **connective tissue**
s2418 **(scar)**, while the remaining parenchymal cells enlarge (**regenerative hypertrophy**) and maintain the function of the organ. For example, in myocardial infarction the necrotic area is replaced by a scar, and the surrounding cardiomyocytes hypertrophy. 3. **Pathologic regeneration** — a disturbance of the regeneration process: regeneration is **excessive** (hyperregeneration) or **insufficient** (hyporegeneration), or the tissue turns into **another type** (metaplasia). **Forms of regeneration** (D.S. Sarkisov): **cellular regeneration** — an increase in the number of cells (by mitosis; epithelium, connective tissue, bone); **intracellular regeneration** — an increase in the number and size of intracellular structures (organelles) (heart, skeletal muscles, neurons — nondividing cells). In most organs both forms occur together.
s2419 Mechanisms of regeneration
s2420 Regeneration is controlled by **growth factors** and the **extracellular matrix (ECM)**:
s2421 **growth factors** — stimulate cell proliferation, migration and differentiation: epidermal growth factor (**EGF**) and transforming growth factor alpha (**TGF-alpha**) — epithelium; platelet-derived growth factor (**PDGF**) — migration of fibroblasts, smooth muscle cells and macrophages; vascular endothelial growth factor (**VEGF**) — **angiogenesis**; fibroblast growth factor (**FGF**) — angiogenesis and fibroblasts; **hepatocyte growth factor (HGF)** — liver regeneration; **transforming growth factor beta (TGF-beta)** — the main factor of **fibrosis** (increases collagen synthesis and reduces its breakdown) and it suppresses inflammation;
s2422 **extracellular matrix** — collagen, elastin, fibronectin, laminin, proteoglycans; it serves as a scaffold for the tissue and stores growth factors. **If the basement membrane and scaffold are preserved**, complete regeneration is possible; if the scaffold is destroyed, a scar forms;
s2423 **stem (cambial) cells** — tissue stem cells (for example, intestinal crypts, the basal layer of the skin, hepatic ductal cells).
s2424 Regeneration of various tissues
s2425 **Blood** — physiological regeneration is very active; after blood loss the bone marrow becomes hyperplastic, the yellow marrow of the long bones turns into red marrow, and foci of hematopoiesis outside the marrow (**extramedullary hematopoiesis**) appear.
s2426 **Blood vessels** — capillaries are restored by **budding** (endothelial proliferation — angiogenesis); large vessels regenerate only in their inner layer, and defects of the media and adventitia are replaced by a scar.
s2427 **Connective tissue** — through **granulation tissue** (below).
s2428 **Epithelium** — stratified squamous epithelium (skin) is completely restored from the edges of the defect and from the basal layer; the epithelium of mucous membranes (stomach, intestine) is rapidly restored from cambial cells in the crypts and glands.
s2429 **Liver** — has a high capacity for regeneration (**restitution**): if 2/3 of the liver is removed, the remaining part reaches its original mass within a few weeks. But in chronic injury regeneration is disturbed: pseudolobules form and **cirrhosis** develops.
s2430 **Bone** — a **bony callus** forms at the fracture site: first fibrin and granulation tissue (**preliminary connective tissue callus**), then an **osteoid-cartilaginous callus**, then a **coarse-fibered bony callus**, and finally lamellar bone. If the fragments are mobile, much cartilage forms and healing slows; if connective tissue remains between the fragments, a **false joint (pseudarthrosis)** forms.
s2431 **Skeletal muscles** — if the sarcolemma is preserved, they are restored by **satellite cells**; large defects are replaced by a scar.
s2432 **Cardiac muscle** — cardiomyocytes do not divide; the defect is replaced by a **scar**, with intracellular regeneration and **regenerative hypertrophy** in the remaining cardiomyocytes.
s2433 **Nervous tissue** — in the central nervous system neurons are not restored (the defect is replaced by **gliosis** — a glial scar or a cyst). In peripheral nerves, if the neuron body is preserved, the axon is restored by growing from its central end (1–2 mm per day); if the path of growth is blocked, an **amputation neuroma** (a nodule of nerve fibers and connective tissue) forms.
s2434 Granulation tissue and scar formation
s2435 **Granulation tissue** is young, newly formed connective tissue **rich in capillaries and fibroblasts**. Its gross appearance: a **red-pink, soft, granular** (Latin granulum — grain; the granules are capillary loops) surface that bleeds easily. Granulation tissue begins to appear **3–5 days** after injury.
s2436 The **six layers** of granulation tissue (after N.N. Anichkov, from the surface to the depth):
s2437 **Superficial leukocytic-necrotic layer** — leukocytes, debris, fibrin.
s2438 **Superficial layer of vascular loops** — vertically arranged capillary loops with polyblasts (various cells) between them.
s2439 **Layer of vertical vessels** — perivascular fibroblasts.
s2440 **Maturing layer** — fibroblasts begin to lie horizontally, and collagen fibers form.
s2441 **Layer of horizontal fibroblasts**.
s2442 **Fibrous layer** — mature connective tissue.
s2443 Stages of scar formation:
s2444 **Angiogenesis** — new capillaries bud from existing vessels (under the action of VEGF); the new capillaries are “leaky” — which is why granulation tissue is edematous.
s2445 **Migration and proliferation of fibroblasts**, synthesis of collagen and other ECM components (under the action of TGF-beta, PDGF, FGF).
s2446 **Remodeling** — collagen matures and is reorganized, the number of vessels decreases, and granulation tissue turns into a **dense, whitish, poorly vascularized scar**. The balance between collagen synthesis and degradation (matrix metalloproteinases) determines the size of the scar.

s2451 Wound healing
s2452 Healing of a skin wound is the classic example of tissue repair. Four types are distinguished:
s2453 **Direct closure of an epithelial defect** — a superficial defect involving only the epithelium is closed by ingrowth of epithelium (erosions of the cornea and mucous membranes).
s2454 **Healing under a scab** — a superficial defect is covered with a scab of dried blood, fibrin and lymph; epithelium grows beneath it, and the scab falls off in 3–5 days.
s2455 **Healing by first intention (primary union)** — wounds with closely apposed edges, **clean (uninfected)**, with little tissue loss (for example, a sutured surgical incision): the incisional gap fills with fibrin; neutrophils appear on day 1, macrophages and granulation tissue by day 3, collagen bridges by day 5, and by the 2nd week the wound is completely united by a scar; the epithelium covers the defect in 24–48 hours. The result is a **thin, almost invisible scar**. 4. **Healing by second intention (secondary union)** — wounds with a **large tissue defect**, widely separated edges, **infection** and much necrotic tissue: intense inflammation and abundant **granulation tissue** form; **wound contraction** occurs due to **myofibroblasts** (within 6 weeks the defect may shrink to 5–10% of its original size); the result is a **wide, coarse scar**.

s2458 Factors influencing repair
s2459 **Local factors: infection** (the most important factor — it prolongs inflammation), mechanical factors (motion of the wound edges), **inadequate blood supply** (atherosclerosis, varicose veins, diabetes), **foreign bodies** (sutures, bone fragments), the type, size and location of the wound.
s2460 **Systemic factors: nutrition** (protein, **vitamin C** — necessary for collagen synthesis, zinc deficiency), **diabetes mellitus**, **glucocorticoids** (suppress inflammation and TGF-beta, reducing fibrosis), age (healing slows in the elderly), immune status.
s2461 Pathologic regeneration and disorders of repair
s2462 **Keloid scar** (Greek chele — crab claw) — excessive collagen formation: the scar **extends beyond** the wound margins and bulges like a tumor, and it tends to recur. There is a hereditary predisposition (more common in people of African descent). Microscopically — broad, hyalinized collagen bundles.
s2463 **Hypertrophic scar** — collagen is excessive, but the scar does not extend beyond the wound margins and may shrink with time.
s2464 **“Proud flesh”** (exuberant granulation tissue) — granulation tissue protrudes above the skin level and interferes with epithelialization.
s2465 **Contractures** — scars after burns contract and limit joint movement.
s2466 **Insufficient repair** — wound opening (**wound dehiscence**, especially in the abdominal wall), chronic (trophic) ulcers.
s2467 **Desmoid (aggressive fibromatosis)** — tumor-like proliferation of fibroblasts, a borderline tumor.
s2468 **False joint** and **amputation neuroma** (above).

s2473 Organization, encapsulation, sclerosis and cirrhosis
s2474 **Organization** — replacement of necrotic foci, thrombi, fibrinous exudate (fibrinous inflammation) or a hematoma by connective tissue. For example, **organization and canalization of a thrombus** — granulation tissue grows into the thrombotic mass from the wall, new vascular channels appear in it, and blood flow is partially restored (Topic 7).
s2475 **Encapsulation** — formation of a connective tissue capsule around a nonresorbable dead mass, foreign body or parasite.
s2476 **Sclerosis** (Greek sklerosis — hardening) — growth of connective tissue in an organ, displacing the parenchyma and making the organ firm (cardiosclerosis, nephrosclerosis, pneumosclerosis). Its causes: chronic inflammation (interstitial inflammation), organization of necrotic foci, chronic hypoxia, activation of fibroblasts.
s2477 **Cirrhosis** (Greek kirrhos — tawny, orange-yellow) — the final stage, in which sclerosis is accompanied by **restructuring** of the parenchyma (pseudolobules, regenerative nodules) and **deformation** of the organ (liver cirrhosis, pneumocirrhosis).
s2478 6.6. Clinicopathological section
s2479 A granuloma has been found — what is the next step?
s2480 If **epithelioid cell granulomas** are found in a lymph node biopsy, the pathologist seeks answers to the following questions:
s2481 **Is there necrosis?** Granulomas with caseous necrosis — first of all **tuberculosis** (or a fungal infection); uniform, noncaseating, “stamped-out” granulomas — **sarcoidosis** is more likely.
s2482 **Is the causative agent found? Ziehl–Neelsen** staining (acid-fast bacilli), PAS and Grocott silver stain (fungi), PCR (mycobacterial DNA).
s2483 **Is there a foreign body?** It is seen in polarized light (talc, sutures). The answers to these questions completely change the treatment: in tuberculosis — antituberculosis drugs; in sarcoidosis — glucocorticoids (giving glucocorticoids in tuberculosis is dangerous — the infection spreads!).
s2484 **Chronic inflammation and cancer** are interrelated: tumors develop more often against a background of long-standing chronic inflammation — Helicobacter pylori gastritis — gastric adenocarcinoma and MALT lymphoma; chronic viral hepatitis B and C — hepatocellular carcinoma; ulcerative colitis — colorectal cancer; Barrett esophagus — adenocarcinoma; sinus tracts of chronic osteomyelitis — squamous cell carcinoma. Mechanism: continuous cell proliferation, DNA damage by ROS, cytokines and growth factors.
s2485 Wound healing and diabetes mellitus
s2486 In diabetes mellitus, wounds heal slowly and become infected easily. The causes: **micro- and macroangiopathy** (reduced blood supply), **neuropathy** (because of loss of sensation the patient does not feel the injury — the “diabetic foot”), **impaired neutrophil function** in hyperglycemia (chemotaxis and phagocytosis are impaired), and impaired collagen synthesis. As a result, chronic trophic ulcers and gangrene develop (Topic 2).
s2487 **Granuloma** (Latin granulum — grain, Greek -oma — tumor) — a localized focus of productive inflammation; mainly a collection of activated macrophages.
s2488 **Gumma** (Latin gummi — gum) — the specific infectious granuloma in syphilis.
s2489 **Leproma** (Greek lepra — leprosy, -oma — tumor) — the specific infectious granuloma in leprosy.
s2490 **Tuberculoma** (Latin tuberculum — small knob) — a focus of caseous necrosis in the lung surrounded by a connective tissue capsule, with elements of calcification.
s2491 **Infiltrate** (Latin in — into, filtratum — filtered) — entry of various cellular elements into a tissue, making it firm.
s2492 **Condyloma** (Greek kondyloma — growth) — papillary growth of squamous epithelium and the underlying stroma.
s2493 **Polyp** (Greek poly — many, pus — foot) — papillary (pedunculated) outgrowth of the mucosal epithelium and the underlying tissue.
s2494 **Specific inflammation** (Latin species — kind) — chronic inflammation caused by a particular biological agent and having characteristic morphologic features (not singled out in current international classifications).
s2495 **Sclerosis** (Greek sklerosis — hardening) — growth of connective tissue in an organ, displacing the parenchyma.
s2496 **Cirrhosis** (Greek kirrhos — tawny) — growth of connective tissue and restructuring of the parenchyma with deformation of the organ.
s2497 **Myofibrosis** (Greek mys — muscle, Latin fibrosus — fibrous) — small-focal cardiosclerosis.
s2498 **Fibrosis** (Latin fibra — fiber) — excessive formation of connective tissue fibers.
s2499 **Stenosis** (Greek stenos — narrow) — narrowing of a canal or orifice.
s2500 **Aneurysm** (Greek aneurysma — dilatation) — a localized bulging dilatation of the wall of the heart or a vessel.
s2501 **Regeneration** (Latin regeneratio — renewal) — restoration of the structural elements of cells and tissues.
s2502 **Restitution** — complete restoration of a defect by the same tissue.
s2503 **Substitution** — replacement of a defect by connective tissue (scar).
s2504 **Organization** — replacement of necrosis, a thrombus or exudate by connective tissue.
