s4579 **Carbon monoxide** — a colorless, odorless, highly toxic gas; it forms carboxyhemoglobin with hemoglobin and causes tissue hypoxia.
s4580 **Nitrates and nitrites** — salts of nitric and nitrous acids; in excess they cause methemoglobinemia and lead to the formation of nitrosamines (carcinogens).

s4586 **Smog** — a toxic haze consisting of a mixture of gases, smoke and dust over industrial plants and cities.

s4695 An infant born at 29 weeks of pregnancy and weighing 1250 g developed shortness of breath, grunting, chest retractions and cyanosis a few hours after birth. The mother has diabetes mellitus and did not receive glucocorticoids before delivery. The infant died on day 3. At autopsy the lungs are firm, airless, red-violet and sink in water.
s4696 1) Your diagnosis? 2) What changes are expected microscopically? 3) Pathogenesis of the disease and risk factors.
s4697 1) Neonatal respiratory distress syndrome — hyaline membrane disease. 2) Alternating atelectasis and dilated alveoli; eosinophilic hyaline membranes (fibrin + necrotic epithelial debris) lining the respiratory bronchioles, alveolar ducts and alveoli; little inflammation. 3) Surfactant deficiency due to lung immaturity → collapse of the alveoli (atelectasis) → hypoxia, acidosis → damage to the endothelium and epithelium → leakage of plasma proteins → hyaline membranes. Risk factors: prematurity, maternal diabetes mellitus (insulin suppresses surfactant synthesis), absence of antenatal glucocorticoids.
s4698 The third pregnancy of an Rh-negative woman. She did not receive anti-D immunoglobulin after the previous pregnancies. The fetus died at 32 weeks. At autopsy: generalized edema of the fetal body, ascites, hydrothorax; the liver and spleen are markedly enlarged; the placenta is large, edematous and pale. Microscopically, numerous islands of erythroid cells in the liver.
s4699 1) Your diagnosis? 2) The mechanism of the disease. 3) If the fetus had been born alive, what dangerous complication could have developed? What is the prevention?
s4700 1) Hemolytic disease of the newborn, the generalized hydrops form (immune hydrops fetalis). 2) During previous pregnancies, Rh-positive fetal red cells entered the maternal blood and sensitized her; the mother's anti-D IgG antibodies crossed the placenta and destroyed the fetal red cells (type II hypersensitivity) → severe anemia → hypoxia, heart failure and liver damage (hypoproteinemia) → generalized edema; extramedullary hematopoiesis in the liver and spleen in response to anemia (hepatosplenomegaly, erythroblastosis). 3) Kernicterus (nuclear jaundice) — unconjugated bilirubin damages the subcortical nuclei of the brain, leading to severe neurological sequelae. Prevention — giving the Rh-negative mother anti-D immunoglobulin within 72 hours after the birth of an Rh-positive child, after abortion, and at the 28th week.
s4701 The mother of a 3-year-old child felt a large hard mass in the child's abdomen while bathing him. Computed tomography: a sharply demarcated tumor 10 cm in size in the left kidney. s4702 1) Your diagnosis? 2) Explain the association with aniridia. 3) What determines the prognosis?
