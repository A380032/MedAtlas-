s1753 A 50-year-old man has an enlarged liver, diabetes mellitus has been diagnosed, and his skin is bronze-gray; his libido is decreased. Serum ferritin is sharply elevated, and transferrin saturation with iron is 85%. Liver biopsy: golden-brown granular pigment in the cytoplasm of hepatocytes, fibrosis of the portal tracts.
s1754 1) Your diagnosis? 2) How is the pigment detected, and in which cells has it accumulated? 3) State the mechanism and complications of the disease.
s1755 1) Hereditary (primary) hemochromatosis — “bronze diabetes”. 2) The pigment is hemosiderin; it stains blue (Prussian blue) with the Perls reaction; it has accumulated in parenchymal cells (hepatocytes, pancreatic cells, cardiomyocytes) — this distinguishes hemochromatosis from hemosiderosis. 3) Mutation of the HFE gene leads to decreased hepcidin and a continuous increase in intestinal iron absorption. Iron generates free radicals, damages cells and causes fibrosis. Complications: pigment cirrhosis of the liver, hepatocellular carcinoma, diabetes mellitus, cardiomyopathy (arrhythmia, heart failure), hypogonadism, arthropathy. Treatment — regular phlebotomy.
s1756 A 58-year-old man woke up at night with sudden, extremely severe pain in the joint of his right big toe; the joint is red and swollen. Yesterday at a wedding he ate a lot of meat and drank alcohol. Blood uric acid is high. He has had such attacks several times before, and there are small hard white nodules on his auricle.
s1757 1) Your diagnosis? 2) What are the nodules on the auricle, and what is their microscopic structure? 3) To which type of degeneration does the disease belong, and what are its complications?
s1758 1) Gout — an attack of acute gouty arthritis (triggered by purine-rich food and alcohol). 2) Tophi — collections of sodium urate crystals surrounded by foreign body giant cells, macrophages, lymphocytes and fibrosis (a granulomatous reaction). Complications: chronic gouty arthritis and joint deformity, gouty nephropathy, uric acid stones and kidney failure.
s1759 A 60-year-old woman has been diagnosed with a parathyroid adenoma; blood calcium and parathyroid hormone are high. The patient died of kidney failure. s1809 Inflammation is a **protective** reaction: without it, infections would spread uncontrollably and wounds would not heal. At the same time, inflammation can also be **harmful**: it underlies many diseases (anaphylactic reactions, rheumatoid arthritis, atherosclerosis, pulmonary fibrosis). Being able to distinguish the protective and damaging sides of the inflammatory process is the basis of clinical thinking.
s1810 **Alterative inflammation** is given as a separate type in the textbooks of Strukov and Abdullakhodjaeva: in it tissue damage (degeneration, necrosis) predominates, while exudation and proliferation are weak (for example, the myocardium in diphtheria, parenchymal organs in tuberculosis and viral hepatitis). The Robbins textbook does not distinguish such a type — these changes are considered as necrosis and cell injury. Many current sources also use the term **necrotic inflammation** instead of alterative inflammation.
s1811 The main signs and their mediators: **fever — IL-1, TNF, PGE~2~**; **pain — bradykinin, PGE~2~**; **vasodilation — histamine, prostaglandins, NO**; **permeability — histamine, bradykinin, C3a/C5a, leukotrienes**; **chemotaxis — C5a, LTB~4~, IL-8, bacterial peptides** (“**5-4-8**” — C**5**a, LTB**4**, IL-**8**).
s1812 Stages of leukocyte extravasation — **“Roll, Adhere, Transmigrate, Chase”**: **R**olling — **S**electins; **A**dhesion — **I**ntegrins (ICAM-1); **T**ransmigration — **P**ECAM-1 (CD31); **C**hemotaxis — following the signal (C5a, LTB4, IL-8). In short: **“S-I-P”**.
s1813 In **diphtheria** the type of inflammation depends on location: in the **pharynx and tonsils** (stratified squamous epithelium) — **diphtheritic** inflammation: the membrane is firmly attached and does not block the airway, but much toxin is absorbed (myocarditis, neuritis); in the **larynx, trachea and bronchi** (columnar epithelium) — **croupous** inflammation: the membrane detaches easily and blocks the airway — **true croup**, asphyxia. This is why both types can occur in one disease.
s1814 5.1. The concept of inflammation
s1815 **Inflammation** (Latin inflammatio, Greek phlogosis) is a **complex protective-adaptive vascular-mesenchymal reaction** of the organism that arises in response to an injurious agent and is aimed at eliminating or limiting it and restoring the damaged tissue. Inflammation occurs only in multicellular organisms with blood vessels; at its center are the **microcirculatory bed** and **leukocytes**.
