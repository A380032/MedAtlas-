s1670 **“Brown induration of the lung”.** The lung is enlarged and firm; on the cut surface it is rust-brown with small brown spots. Conclusion: local hemosiderosis and sclerosis (chronic venous congestion — mitral valve defect).
s1671 **“Cyst in place of a cerebral hemorrhage (‘rust-colored cyst’)”.** A smooth-walled cavity in the brain tissue, with a rust-brown wall. Conclusion: local hemosiderosis.
s1672 **“Calcification of the aortic valve”.** The valve cusps are thickened, deformed and stone-hard, with whitish calcium masses; the valve orifice is narrowed. Conclusion: dystrophic calcification (calcific aortic stenosis).
s1673 **“Atherocalcinosis”.** Hard, whitish, brittle calcified plaques in the aortic intima; they grate when cut. Conclusion: dystrophic calcification.
s1674 **“Gallbladder stones (cholelithiasis)”.** The gallbladder wall is thickened; its cavity contains multifaceted yellowish (cholesterol) or small black (pigment) stones. Conclusion: gallstone disease, chronic cholecystitis.
s1675 **“Kidney stones, hydronephrosis”.** A large stone (sometimes staghorn) in the renal pelvis; the pelvis and calyces are dilated, and the parenchyma is thinned. Conclusion: nephrolithiasis, hydronephrosis.
s1676 **“Gout”.** Nodules with a white, chalky mass (tophi) around a joint and in the auricle. Conclusion: gout.
s1677 **“Uric acid infarct in the kidney of a newborn”.** Yellowish-red streaks in the medulla directed toward the papillae. Conclusion: uric acid infarct (a physiological state).
s1678 **“Pulmonary hemosiderosis (brown induration of the lung)”** (hematoxylin and eosin and Perls reaction). The interalveolar septa are thickened (sclerosis), and the capillaries are dilated and filled with blood; macrophages with golden-brown granular pigment in the alveolar spaces and septa (“heart failure cells”); the pigment is blue with the Perls reaction. Conclusion: local hemosiderosis.
s1679 **“Cerebral hemorrhage”** (hematoxylin and eosin). Hemolyzed blood surrounded by hemosiderin-laden macrophages; orange hematoidin crystals in the center. Conclusion: hemorrhage, hemosiderosis and hematoidin.
s1680 **“Calcification of the myocardium (calcium metastases)”** (hematoxylin and eosin). Dark violet-blue, amorphous calcium deposits in some cardiomyocytes and in the stroma between them. Conclusion: metastatic calcification.
s1681 **“Gouty tophus”** (hematoxylin and eosin). Amorphous or needle-shaped crystalline masses (urate) surrounded by foreign body giant cells, macrophages, lymphocytes and fibrosis. Conclusion: gout.
s1682 **“Skin in Addison disease”** (hematoxylin and eosin). Numerous brown-black melanin granules in the cells of the basal layer of the epidermis, melanophages in the dermis. Conclusion: generalized acquired hyperpigmentation (melanosis).
s1683 **“Brown atrophy of the liver (lipofuscinosis)”** (hematoxylin and eosin). Fine yellowish-brown granules around the nuclei of hepatocytes (see Topic 1). Conclusion: accumulation of lipofuscin.

s1764 A 50-year-old man has an enlarged liver, diabetes mellitus has been diagnosed, and his skin is bronze-gray; his libido is decreased. Serum ferritin is sharply elevated, and transferrin saturation with iron is 85%. Liver biopsy: golden-brown granular pigment in the cytoplasm of hepatocytes, fibrosis of the portal tracts.
s1765 1) Your diagnosis? 2) How is the pigment detected, and in which cells has it accumulated? 3) State the mechanism and complications of the disease.
s1766 1) Hereditary (primary) hemochromatosis — “bronze diabetes”. 2) The pigment is hemosiderin; it stains blue (Prussian blue) with the Perls reaction; it has accumulated in parenchymal cells (hepatocytes, pancreatic cells, cardiomyocytes) — this distinguishes hemochromatosis from hemosiderosis. 3) Mutation of the HFE gene leads to decreased hepcidin and a continuous increase in intestinal iron absorption. Iron generates free radicals, damages cells and causes fibrosis. Complications: pigment cirrhosis of the liver, hepatocellular carcinoma, diabetes mellitus, cardiomyopathy (arrhythmia, heart failure), hypogonadism, arthropathy. Treatment — regular phlebotomy.
s1767 A 58-year-old man woke up at night with sudden, extremely severe pain in the joint of his right big toe; the joint is red and swollen. Yesterday at a wedding he ate a lot of meat and drank alcohol. Blood uric acid is high. He has had such attacks several times before, and there are small hard white nodules on his auricle.
s1768 1) Your diagnosis? 2) What are the nodules on the auricle, and what is their microscopic structure? 3) To which type of degeneration does the disease belong, and what are its complications?
s1769 1) Gout — an attack of acute gouty arthritis (triggered by purine-rich food and alcohol). 2) Tophi — collections of sodium urate crystals surrounded by foreign body giant cells, macrophages, lymphocytes and fibrosis (a granulomatous reaction). In the synovial fluid, needle-shaped crystals that are negatively birefringent in polarized light are found inside neutrophils. 3) A disorder of nucleoprotein metabolism (mixed degeneration). Complications: chronic gouty arthritis and joint deformity, gouty nephropathy, uric acid stones and kidney failure.
s1770 A 60-year-old woman has been diagnosed with a parathyroid adenoma; blood calcium and parathyroid hormone are high. The patient died of kidney failure. At autopsy the skeletal bones are softened; there are numerous calcium deposits in the lungs, gastric mucosa and renal tubules, and stones in the kidneys.
s1771 1) What is the change in the organs called? 2) Why are these particular organs affected? 3) How can it be distinguished from dystrophic calcification?
s1772 1) Metastatic calcification (calcium metastases) — hypercalcemia due to hyperparathyroidism; nephrocalcinosis and nephrolithiasis in the kidney. 2) The lungs, stomach and kidneys excrete acid, so their tissue is more alkaline, which creates favorable conditions for the precipitation of calcium salts. 3) In dystrophic calcification, blood calcium is normal and calcium is deposited only in injured or necrotic tissue (atherosclerotic plaque, caseous focus, valve); in metastatic calcification there is hypercalcemia, and calcium is deposited widely in healthy tissues.

s1820 Inflammation is a **protective** reaction: without it, infections would spread uncontrollably and wounds would not heal. At the same time, inflammation can also be **harmful**: it underlies many diseases (anaphylactic reactions, rheumatoid arthritis, atherosclerosis, pulmonary fibrosis). Being able to distinguish the protective and damaging sides of the inflammatory process is the basis of clinical thinking.
s1821 **Alterative inflammation** is given as a separate type in the textbooks of Strukov and Abdullakhodjaeva: in it tissue damage (degeneration, necrosis) predominates, while exudation and proliferation are weak (for example, the myocardium in diphtheria, parenchymal organs in tuberculosis and viral hepatitis). The Robbins textbook does not distinguish such a type — these changes are considered as necrosis and cell injury. Many current sources also use the term **necrotic inflammation** instead of alterative inflammation.
s1822 The main signs and their mediators: **fever — IL-1, TNF, PGE~2~**; **pain — bradykinin, PGE~2~**; **vasodilation — histamine, prostaglandins, NO**; **permeability — histamine, bradykinin, C3a/C5a, leukotrienes**; **chemotaxis — C5a, LTB~4~, IL-8, bacterial peptides** (“**5-4-8**” — C**5**a, LTB**4**, IL-**8**).
s1823 Stages of leukocyte extravasation — **“Roll, Adhere, Transmigrate, Chase”**: **R**olling — **S**electins; **A**dhesion — **I**ntegrins (ICAM-1); **T**ransmigration — **P**ECAM-1 (CD31); **C**hemotaxis — following the signal (C5a, LTB4, IL-8). In short: **“S-I-P”**.
s1824 In **diphtheria** the type of inflammation depends on location: in the **pharynx and tonsils** (stratified squamous epithelium) — **diphtheritic** inflammation: the membrane is firmly attached and does not block the airway, but much toxin is absorbed (myocarditis, neuritis); in the **larynx, trachea and bronchi** (columnar epithelium) — **croupous** inflammation: the membrane detaches easily and blocks the airway — **true croup**, asphyxia. This is why both types can occur in one disease.
s1825 5.1. The concept of inflammation
s1826 **Inflammation** (Latin inflammatio, Greek phlogosis) is a **complex protective-adaptive vascular-mesenchymal reaction** of the organism that arises in response to an injurious agent and is aimed at eliminating or limiting it and restoring the damaged tissue. Inflammation occurs only in multicellular organisms with blood vessels; at its center are the **microcirculatory bed** and **leukocytes**.
