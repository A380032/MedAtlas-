s2286 Main function
s2287 Killing of microbes (ROS, NO, lysosomal enzymes), amplification of inflammation (IL-1, TNF, IL-12)
s2288 Suppression of inflammation, repair and fibrosis (growth factors: PDGF, TGF-beta), angiogenesis
s2289 Result
s2290 Defense, but tissue damage
s2291 Repair, scar
s2292 Functions of macrophages in chronic inflammation: **phagocytosis** of microbes and dead tissue; **presentation** of antigen to T lymphocytes; production of **cytokines** that regulate inflammation; control of **repair and fibrosis** by stimulating fibroblasts and angiogenesis. Macrophages and T lymphocytes activate each other and maintain the chronicity of inflammation (IFN-gamma ↔ IL-12).
s2293 Lymphocytes, plasma cells and other cells
s2294 **T lymphocytes** — CD4+ helper T cells regulate inflammation through cytokines: **Th1** (IFN-gamma — classical activation of macrophages; tuberculosis, autoimmune diseases), **Th2** (IL-4, IL-5, IL-13 — eosinophils and alternatively activated macrophages; allergy, parasites), **Th17** (IL-17 — recruits neutrophils).
s2295 **B lymphocytes and plasma cells** — produce antibodies. The plasma cell has an eccentrically placed nucleus with “**cartwheel**” chromatin and basophilic cytoplasm; immunoglobulins can accumulate in its cytoplasm to form **Russell bodies**. In long-standing inflammation, lymphocytes, plasma cells and antigen-presenting cells form **lymphoid follicles** (**tertiary lymphoid organs** — for example, in the synovium in rheumatoid arthritis, in Hashimoto thyroiditis).
s2296 **Eosinophils** — numerous in IgE-mediated allergic reactions and **parasitic infections**; the **major basic protein** in their granules is toxic to parasites but also damages the epithelium.
s2297 **Mast cells** — release histamine and other mediators and participate in allergic and chronic inflammation.
s2298 **Neutrophils** — may also be present in chronic inflammation (for example, chronic osteomyelitis, chronic lung damage) — “**acute-on-chronic**” inflammation.
s2299 Multinucleated giant cells
s2300 Multinucleated cells 40–50 µm or more in diameter formed by fusion of macrophages (epithelioid cells):
s2301 **Pirogov–Langhans cells** — the nuclei (20 or more) are arranged at the periphery of the cell in a **horseshoe or ring** shape; characteristic of the tuberculous granuloma but also found in other granulomas;
s2302 **foreign body giant cells** — the nuclei are scattered **haphazardly** in the cytoplasm (often in the center); the cytoplasm may contain foreign particles;
s2303 **Touton cells** — a ring of nuclei in the center and foamy (lipid-laden) cytoplasm at the periphery; in xanthomas and fat necrosis.

s2308 6.3. Types of productive (proliferative) inflammation
s2309 In A.I. Strukov's classification, in **productive (proliferative) inflammation** cell proliferation predominates; it usually runs a chronic course. Four types are distinguished:
s2310 **1. Interstitial (diffuse) inflammation** — a **diffuse infiltrate** of lymphocytes, macrophages and plasma cells accumulates in the **stroma** of parenchymal organs (myocardium, liver, kidney, lung); degenerative and atrophic changes in the parenchyma. Examples: **interstitial myocarditis** (diphtheria, viral infections, rheumatic fever), interstitial nephritis, interstitial pneumonia, chronic hepatitis. Outcome — **diffuse sclerosis** of the organ (cardiosclerosis, nephrosclerosis, pneumosclerosis) and **cirrhosis**.
s2311 **2. Granulomatous inflammation** — accompanied by the formation of granulomas (in detail below).
s2312 **3. Productive inflammation forming polyps and condylomata acuminata** — in chronic inflammation of mucous membranes (or of surfaces lined by squamous epithelium), the stroma and epithelium grow and protrude outward:
s2313 **inflammatory polyps** — in the mucosa of the nasal cavity, maxillary sinus, stomach, colon and cervix; polyps must be distinguished from tumors (adenomatous polyp);
s2314 **condylomata acuminata** — in the squamous epithelium of the anus and external genitalia, most often associated with **papillomavirus** (types 6 and 11). **4. Productive inflammation around animal parasites** — around echinococcus, cysticercus and trichinella: macrophages, eosinophils, giant cells and a **fibrous capsule** form around the parasite (for example, the capsule around an echinococcal cyst).

s2318 Gastric polyp: a soft, gray-reddish lesion with small papillae on a thin stalk on the greater curvature (TMA study guide, 2018)

s2321 6.4. Granulomatous inflammation
s2322 **Granulomatous inflammation** is a distinctive form of chronic inflammation whose main feature is the formation of **granulomas**. A **granuloma** (Latin granulum — grain, Greek -oma — tumor; i.e., a “nodule”) is a **localized focal** productive inflammation up to 1–2 mm in diameter, consisting mainly of a collection of **activated macrophages** (epithelioid cells), often surrounded by lymphocytes. Granuloma formation is the organism's attempt to **wall off** an agent that is difficult to eradicate.
s2323 Morphogenesis of the granuloma
s2324 Accumulation of **monocytes** in the focus of inflammation.
s2325 Their transformation into **macrophages** and formation of a macrophage granuloma.
s2326 Transformation of macrophages into **epithelioid cells** — large cells with pale pink, finely granular cytoplasm, indistinct borders and an elongated oval nucleus (resembling a “shoe sole”); they lie closely packed like epithelium. Epithelioid cells are poorly adapted to phagocytosis and better adapted to secretion (cytokines, enzymes).
s2327 Fusion of epithelioid cells (or macrophages) to form **multinucleated giant cells**.
s2328 By mechanism there are two types of granulomas:
s2329 **Immune granulomas** — **type IV hypersensitivity** (T cell–mediated immune response): Th1 lymphocytes produce **IFN-gamma**, activate macrophages and transform them into epithelioid cells. In tuberculosis, syphilis, leprosy, sarcoidosis, fungal infections and Crohn disease.
s2330 **Nonimmune granulomas (foreign body granulomas)** — without an immune response, around large inert foreign bodies (suture material, talc, coal particles, keratin, urate crystals). Epithelioid cells are few, **foreign body giant cells** are numerous; the foreign body is often visible in polarized light.

s2345 Specific granulomas
s2346 **Specific inflammation** is chronic inflammation caused by a particular agent (tuberculosis, syphilis, leprosy, scleroma) that forms granulomas of characteristic structure. Its general features: a specific causative agent; a **wavelike** chronic course (periods of exacerbation and remission); granulomas of characteristic structure; frequent **necrosis** (caseous or coagulative); a change in the tissue reaction depending on the immune status of the organism (with high resistance — a productive reaction, with low resistance — an alterative and exudative reaction).
s2347 **1. The tuberculous granuloma (tubercle)** — the causative agent is Mycobacterium tuberculosis (Koch bacillus). Structure (from the center to the periphery):
s2348 in the center — a focus of **caseous necrosis** (a structureless, granular, pink mass);
s2349 around it — a layer of **epithelioid cells** (arranged like a palisade);
s2350 among the epithelioid cells — **Pirogov–Langhans giant cells**;
s2351 at the periphery — a layer of **lymphocytes**, a small number of plasma cells and macrophages;
s2352 there are **no blood vessels** inside the granuloma (silver impregnation shows a network of argyrophilic fibers);
s2353 with Ziehl–Neelsen staining, **acid-fast red bacilli** are found in epithelioid and giant cells. A granuloma without caseous necrosis is an **epithelioid cell (proliferative) tubercle**. Granulomas merge to form large foci; when they are surrounded by a capsule — a **tuberculoma**. Scattered small (millet-sized) granulomas in the organs — **miliary tuberculosis**.

s2362 **2. The syphilitic granuloma — gumma** (Latin gummi — gum) — the causative agent is Treponema pallidum; characteristic of **tertiary syphilis**. Structure:
s2363 in the center — an extensive focus of **coagulative (gummatous) necrosis**: a gummy, stretchy mass in which the outlines of vessels are sometimes preserved (unlike caseous necrosis);
s2364 around it — **lymphocytes, plasma cells** (very numerous), macrophages, fibroblasts, few epithelioid and giant cells;
s2365 **productive endovasculitis** — the walls of small vessels thicken and their lumen narrows (**obliterating endarteritis**), with plasma cells around them (**perivasculitis**);
s2366 at the periphery — a vascular connective tissue capsule. Gummas occur in the liver, bones, skin, brain, heart and other organs; on healing they leave a **deep stellate scar** (in the liver — **“hepar lobatum” (lobulated liver)**). Besides gummas, tertiary syphilis also produces **gummatous infiltration** (diffuse) — most importantly **syphilitic mesaortitis**: a lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate around the vasa vasorum in the media of the ascending aorta and arch, destruction of elastic fibers and a “**shagreen skin**” (tree-bark) appearance of the intima; its complications — **aortic aneurysm** and aortic valve insufficiency.

s2375 **3. The leprosy granuloma — leproma** (Greek lepra — leprosy) — the causative agent is Mycobacterium leprae (Hansen bacillus). Structure: macrophages, epithelioid and giant cells, lymphocytes, plasma cells. Inside macrophages, numerous mycobacteria accumulate in the form of a “**cigarette pack**”; fat vacuoles appear in them, and large **Virchow (lepra) cells** form. Lepromas occur in the skin, mucous membranes, **peripheral nerves**, lymph nodes and internal organs.
s2376 Depending on the immune status, leprosy has two main forms:
s2377 **lepromatous form** — low immunity (Th2 response): numerous Virchow cells **filled with mycobacteria**, skin nodules (“**leonine face**”), highly contagious;
s2378 **tuberculoid form** — high immunity (Th1 response): tuberculosis-like epithelioid cell granulomas, **few** mycobacteria, mainly nerves are affected (loss of sensation, trophic ulcers).

s2381 **4. The scleroma granuloma** — the causative agent is Klebsiella rhinoscleromatis (Volkovich–Frisch bacillus). Mainly in the upper respiratory tract — the nose (**rhinoscleroma**), larynx and trachea. Structure: plasma cells, epithelioid cells, lymphocytes, **hyaline (Russell) bodies**; most characteristic are **Mikulicz cells**: large macrophages with pale, foamy cytoplasm containing Volkovich–Frisch bacilli. Outcome — coarse sclerosis and scars, narrowing of the airways (stenosis, risk of asphyxia).

s2412 6.5. Repair (regeneration)
s2413 **Regeneration** (Latin regeneratio — renewal) is the **restoration (re-formation)** of the structural elements of cells and tissues. Restoration of damaged tissue is called **repair**, and it occurs in two ways: **regeneration** (by proliferation of cells) and **scar formation** (replacement by connective tissue).
s2414 Types of regeneration
s2415 **Physiological regeneration** — continuous renewal of worn-out cells throughout life: blood cells, epithelium of the skin and mucous membranes, endometrium (menstrual cycle).
