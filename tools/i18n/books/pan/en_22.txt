s2843 **Cerebral infarction (ischemic stroke)** — atherosclerosis, thrombosis or embolism (from the heart); white (gray softening) or red (hemorrhagic transformation); outcome — a cyst.
s2844 **Pulmonary infarction** — red (hemorrhagic), wedge-shaped, its base facing the pleura; fibrin deposits on the pleura (**fibrinous pleurisy**); cause — pulmonary thromboembolism + left ventricular failure; complications — **infarct pneumonia**, abscess, gangrene.
s2845 **Renal infarction** — white with a red rim, wedge-shaped; most often embolic (from the left heart, aorta); outcome — a depressed scar.
s2846 **Splenic infarction** — white, wedge-shaped; fibrin on its capsule (perisplenitis).
s2847 **Intestinal infarction** — red (hemorrhagic); when a mesenteric artery (embolism, thrombosis) or vein (thrombosis) is occluded; it rapidly progresses to **gangrene** and **peritonitis**.

s2858 7.11. Shock
s2859 **Shock** is a state of **systemic hypoperfusion**: as a result of reduced cardiac output or an effective reduction in circulating blood volume, the blood supply and oxygen delivery to tissues are impaired, and cellular hypoxia and cell injury develop. Arterial blood pressure falls.

s2878 Stages of shock:
s2879 **Compensated (nonprogressive) stage** — reflex compensatory mechanisms (sympathetic system, catecholamines, renin–angiotensin, ADH) maintain perfusion of vital organs (brain, heart): tachycardia, peripheral vasoconstriction (cold, pale skin), renal water retention. In septic shock, however, the skin may be warm and flushed.
s2880 **Progressive stage** — tissue hypoxia, anaerobic glycolysis, **lactic acidosis**; the arterioles dilate, blood pools in the microcirculation, and cardiac output falls further; the endothelium is damaged and **DIC** develops.
s2881 **Irreversible stage** — cells and tissues are irreversibly injured; treatment is ineffective — death.
s2882 **Morphologic changes in shock (“shock organs”):** disturbed microcirculation (redistribution of blood, sludge, stasis, hyaline microthrombi — DIC), hemorrhages, degeneration and necrosis. The main “shock organs”:
s2883 **shock lung** — **diffuse alveolar damage** (acute respiratory distress syndrome): the lung is heavy, dark red and edematous, with **hyaline membranes** in the alveolar walls, atelectasis and hemorrhages; acute respiratory failure;
s2884 **shock kidney** — **acute tubular necrosis** (necrotic nephrosis): the cortex is pale and swollen, the medulla dark red; necrosis of the proximal tubular epithelium; **acute kidney injury** (oliguria, anuria);
s2885 **shock liver** — centrilobular necrosis and fatty degeneration;
s2886 **heart** — subendocardial hemorrhages, small foci of necrosis (contraction band necrosis);
s2887 **brain** — hypoxic encephalopathy, ischemic necrosis (in border zones);
s2888 **digestive tract** — mucosal hemorrhages and **acute erosions and ulcers** (“stress ulcers”), hemorrhagic enteropathy;
s2889 **adrenal glands** — lipid depletion of the cortex (consumption of cortisol), bilateral hemorrhage (Waterhouse–Friderichsen syndrome). As a result, **multiple organ dysfunction syndrome** develops.
s2890 7.12. Disorders of lymph circulation
s2891 **Lymphatic insufficiency is mechanical** (obstruction of lymphatic vessels by tumor, scar or parasites, or their congenital aplasia), **dynamic** (a sharp increase in tissue fluid formation) and **resorptive** (changes in tissue proteins and substances). As a result **lymphostasis**, dilatation of lymphatic vessels (**lymphangiectasia**) and **lymphedema** develop. In chronic lymphedema, **sclerosis** develops in the skin and subcutaneous tissue and the part becomes markedly enlarged — **elephantiasis** (Topic 1). When the thoracic duct is injured or obstructed, **chylous ascites** and **chylothorax** (accumulation of milky lymph — chyle) develop.
s2892 7.13. Clinicopathological section
s2893 The “silent killer” in hospital — deep vein thrombosis
s2894 A 68-year-old woman underwent surgery for a femoral neck fracture and was on bed rest for 10 days. When she got up for the first time to go to the toilet, she suddenly became short of breath and cyanotic and died. At autopsy there was a saddle thromboembolus in the pulmonary trunk and its main branches, and occlusive red thrombi in the deep veins of the left calf. The patient had all components of Virchow's triad: **slowing of blood flow** (immobility), **endothelial injury** (surgery, trauma) and **hypercoagulability** (postoperative state, old age). Therefore **prevention of thrombosis** after surgery (early mobilization, compression stockings, low-molecular-weight heparins) is mandatory.
s2895 **Knowing the evolution of myocardial infarction has forensic and clinical significance**: the “age” of an infarct can be determined from its histologic features — neutrophils (1–3 days), macrophages and granulation tissue (3–10 days), scar (6–8 weeks). **Cardiac rupture** occurs most often on **days 3–7** — the period when the necrotic focus has been softened by macrophages but not yet replaced by a scar.

s2932 **“Nutmeg liver”.** The liver is enlarged and firm with a smooth surface; on the cut surface red-brown (lobule centers) and yellowish (periphery) spots alternate — resembling a nutmeg. Conclusion: chronic generalized venous congestion.
s2933 **“Brown induration of the lung”.** The lung is enlarged and firm, brown-rust colored on the cut surface. Conclusion: chronic venous congestion in the pulmonary circulation (see Topic 4).
s2934 **“Cerebral hemorrhage (hematoma)”.** A cavity filled with a dark red blood clot in the region of the subcortical nuclei; the brain tissue is destroyed; the blood may have ruptured into the ventricles. Conclusion: bleeding due to rupture — hematoma (most often in essential hypertension).
s2935 **“Mural thrombus in the aorta”.** Atherosclerotic plaques in the aortic intima with dull, rough, layered gray-red masses firmly attached to the wall over them. Conclusion: mural mixed thrombus.
s2936 **“Pulmonary thromboembolism”.** Dry, dull, worm-like red-gray thromboemboli in the pulmonary trunk and its branches, not attached to the wall. Conclusion: thromboembolism.
s2937 **“Ischemic infarction of the spleen”.** A whitish-yellow, firm, triangular focus beneath the capsule with a red rim around it and fibrin on the capsule. Conclusion: white infarct.
s2938 **“Hemorrhagic infarction of the lung”.** A wedge-shaped, dark red, firm focus with its base facing the pleura; fibrin deposits on the pleura. Conclusion: red infarct.
s2939 **“Myocardial infarction”.** An irregular, yellowish-white, dull focus surrounded by a red rim in the wall of the left ventricle; there may be a mural thrombus on the endocardium. Conclusion: white infarct with a red rim.
s2940 **“Cancer metastases in the liver”.** Numerous sharply demarcated whitish-gray nodules of various sizes in the liver. Conclusion: a consequence of tumor cell embolism (hematogenous metastases).
s2941 **“Nutmeg liver”** (hematoxylin and eosin). In the center of the lobule the central vein and sinusoids are markedly dilated and filled with red cells; the central hepatocytes are atrophic or necrotic; fatty degeneration of peripheral hepatocytes. Conclusion: chronic venous congestion.
s2942 **“Venous congestion of the skin”** (hematoxylin and eosin). The veins and capillaries of the dermis are markedly dilated and filled with blood, with diapedetic hemorrhages and edema in places. Conclusion: venous congestion.
s2943 **“Stasis in cerebral capillaries”** (hematoxylin and eosin). The capillaries are dilated; red cells have stuck together to form homogeneous columns; perivascular edema. Conclusion: stasis.
s2944 **“Mixed thrombus in a vein”** (hematoxylin and eosin). A mass in the venous lumen attached to the wall, with alternating layers of platelets, fibrin (pink), leukocytes and red cells. Conclusion: mixed thrombus.
s2945 **“Fat embolism of pulmonary capillaries”** (Sudan III). The capillaries of the interalveolar septa are occluded by orange-red fat droplets. Conclusion: fat embolism.
s2946 **“Ischemic infarction of the kidney”** (hematoxylin and eosin). In the infarct area the outlines of tubules and glomeruli are preserved, but nuclei are absent (coagulative necrosis); hemorrhage and leukocyte infiltration at the border (demarcation zone). Conclusion: white infarct with a red rim.
s2947 **“Hemorrhagic infarction of the lung”** (hematoxylin and eosin). In the area of necrosis the alveoli are filled with red cells, and the nuclei of the interalveolar septa do not stain. Conclusion: red infarct.
