s3497 9.1. The concept of a tumor
s3498 A **tumor** (tumor, neoplasma, blastoma) is a pathological process characterized by **uncontrolled, unlimited (autonomous) proliferation** of cells, in which the **growth, differentiation and morphology** of the cells are disturbed (**atypia**), and these properties are **hereditarily** transmitted to the progeny of tumor cells. According to the definition of the British pathologist R. Willis: “A tumor is an abnormal mass of tissue, the growth of which exceeds and is uncoordinated with that of the normal tissues and persists in the same excessive manner after cessation of the stimuli which evoked the change”.
s3499 The science that studies tumors is **oncology** (Greek onkos — tumor). Tumors are the second leading cause of death in the world after cardiovascular diseases.
s3500 **Tumor progression** — as the tumor develops, new mutations accumulate and its properties (growth rate, invasion, metastasis, resistance to treatment) **become progressively more aggressive**; as a result, subclones with different properties appear within the tumor (**heterogeneity**).
s3501 Precancerous processes
s3502 A tumor usually develops from healthy tissue not suddenly but through **precancerous changes**:
s3503 **Background processes** — chronic inflammation, hyperplasia, metaplasia (Topic 1), disturbed regeneration.
s3504 **Focal hyperplasia and dysplasia** — **dysplasia** (mild, moderate, severe) — cellular atypia and disruption of tissue structure; severe dysplasia is a true precancerous condition.
s3505 **Noninvasive cancer — carcinoma in situ** (cancer in place) — the full thickness of the epithelium is replaced by atypical cells, but the **basement membrane is intact** (no invasion); it does not metastasize.
s3506 **Invasive cancer** — the tumor breaks through the basement membrane and grows into the stroma; the possibility of metastasis appears.

s3528 9.2. Structure of a tumor
s3529 Every tumor consists of two parts:
s3530 **Parenchyma** — the **tumor cells** that make up the tumor itself; the biological properties of the tumor (malignant or benign) and its **name** depend on the parenchyma.
s3531 **Stroma** — connective tissue, blood vessels, nerves and immune cells; it nourishes and supports the parenchyma. The stroma forms **under the influence of the tumor cells** (growth factors: VEGF, PDGF, FGF). The stroma often contains a **lymphocytic infiltrate** — a sign of an antitumor immune response (favorable for prognosis). By the ratio of stroma to parenchyma: **organoid tumors** — the parenchyma and stroma are clearly separated (most epithelial tumors); **histioid tumors** — the stroma is scant, only in the form of vessels
s3532 (most mesenchymal tumors). If the stroma is abundant, the tumor is **hard** (**scirrhous** — carcinoma with a fibrous stroma, for example breast cancer — **desmoplasia**); if scant, it is **soft** (**medullary**, “brain-like” carcinoma).
s3533 **Gross appearance:** a nodule (encapsulated or unencapsulated), a polyp (pedunculated), mushroom-shaped, papillary (cauliflower-like), a plaque, a cyst (unilocular or multilocular), a diffuse infiltrate, an ulcer (with raised crater edges). Tumors range in size from millimeters to several tens of kilograms. On the cut surface, a **“fish-flesh”** appearance (sarcomas) and foci of necrosis and hemorrhage (malignant tumors) are seen.
s3534 9.3. Tumor atypia
s3535 **Atypia** — the properties of a tumor cell and tissue that differ from those of the normal cell and tissue.
s3536 **Morphologic atypia:** – **tissue atypia** — disturbance of the ratio of parenchyma to stroma, of the epithelial layers, of the shape and
s3537 size of glands and of the orientation of fibers; the cells look normal. It is characteristic of **benign** tumors. – **cellular atypia** — characteristic of **malignant** tumors (in detail below). 2. **Biochemical atypia** — altered metabolism: increased glycolysis even in the presence of oxygen (**Warburg effect**), increased synthesis of proteins and nucleic acids. 3. **Functional atypia** — tumor cells lose their normal function or acquire **unusual functions** (for example, lung cancer produces hormones). 4. **Antigenic atypia** — new antigens appear on tumor cells (tumor-specific antigens, **embryonic antigens** — alpha-fetoprotein, carcinoembryonic antigen); they are used in diagnosis as **tumor markers**.
s3538 Differentiation and anaplasia
s3539 **Differentiation** is the **degree of resemblance** of tumor cells, in structure and function, to the normal tissue from which they arose. A **well-differentiated** tumor closely resembles its tissue of origin (for example, most benign tumors, well-differentiated adenocarcinoma); a **poorly differentiated** one shows little resemblance. **Anaplasia** (Greek “backward formation”) is the loss of differentiation; an important sign of malignancy.
s3540 Signs of cellular atypia (anaplasia):
s3541 **pleomorphism** — variation in the shape and size of cells and nuclei, **tumor giant cells**;
s3542 **nuclear atypia** — nuclei are enlarged and **hyperchromatic** (dark-staining), with coarse chromatin and large nucleoli; **increased nuclear-to-cytoplasmic ratio** (normally 1:4–1:6, in cancer up to 1:1);
s3543 **numerous mitoses** and **atypical (pathologic) mitoses** (tripolar, quadripolar);
s3544 **loss of polarity** — the cells are arranged haphazardly;
s3545 foci of **necrosis** (tumor vessels cannot keep pace with rapid growth).
