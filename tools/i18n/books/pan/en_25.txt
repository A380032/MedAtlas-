s3233 **A-beta (beta-amyloid)** — in brain plaques in Alzheimer disease;
s3234 **AIAPP (amylin)** — in the pancreatic islets in type 2 diabetes mellitus;
s3235 **AE (endocrine)** — procalcitonin (in medullary carcinoma of the thyroid).
s3236 Methods of detecting amyloid
s3237 **Gross** — the **Virchow reaction**: when Lugol solution (iodine) and sulfuric acid are applied to the cut surface of an organ, amyloid turns **blue-violet** or green; with iodine alone — brown-orange.
s3238 **Congo red** — the main method: amyloid stains **red-orange**; in **polarized light** — **apple-green birefringence** (dichroism) — pathognomonic for amyloid.
s3239 **Metachromasia** — **red** with methyl (gentian) violet.
s3240 **Thioflavin T** — yellow-green fluorescence on fluorescence microscopy.
s3241 **Immunohistochemistry and mass spectrometry** — determination of the amyloid **type** (AL, AA, ATTR); necessary for choosing treatment.
s3242 With hematoxylin and eosin, amyloid is an **extracellular**, **homogeneous, amorphous, eosinophilic (pale pink)** mass.

s3274 Pathogenesis of amyloidosis
s3275 Amyloidosis is a **disease of protein misfolding**. Normally, misfolded proteins are corrected inside the cell by chaperones or degraded in proteasomes. In amyloidosis there are **two mechanisms**:
s3276 **A normal protein is produced in excess** and is prone to misfolding — **AA** (SAA increases in chronic inflammation; monocytes cannot degrade it adequately), **AL** (excess monoclonal light chains), A-beta-2-microglobulin (not removed by dialysis).
s3277 **A mutant protein** is prone to misfolding — **ATTR** (hereditary). The misfolded proteins form oligomers and fibrils and accumulate **outside cells**. Amyloid **mechanically compresses** the tissue and narrows the vessels, leading to parenchymal **atrophy**; the oligomers also have a direct toxic effect on cells. Amyloid is produced mainly with the participation of **macrophages** (amyloidoblasts), plasma cells, fibroblasts and cardiomyocytes.
s3278 **Stages** in the development of amyloidosis (V.V. Serov): the **preamyloid stage** — plasmacytic transformation of cells of the reticuloendothelial system, appearance of a clone of amyloidoblasts; **amyloid formation** — synthesis of the fibrillar protein and assembly of fibrils.
s3279 Changes in the organs
s3280 **1. Kidneys** — affected most often and most severely (especially in AA amyloidosis). Amyloid accumulates in the **glomerular mesangium and capillary basement membranes**, then in the tubular basement membranes and arteriolar walls; the glomeruli are gradually completely replaced by amyloid masses. **Stages:** latent → **proteinuric** → **nephrotic** (nephrotic syndrome: heavy proteinuria, hypoproteinemia, edema, hyperlipidemia) → **azotemic (uremic)**.
s3281 In the nephrotic stage the kidneys are enlarged, firm and whitish, with a waxy luster on the cut surface — the **“large white (lardaceous) amyloid kidney”**; hyaline droplet, hydropic and fatty degeneration in the tubules.
s3282 In the final stage the kidneys are small, firm and contracted — the **“amyloid contracted kidney”**; **chronic kidney disease** is the most common **cause of death** in amyloidosis. **2. Spleen** — enlarged and firm. Two types:
s3283 **“Sago spleen”** — amyloid accumulates in the **lymphoid follicles**; on the cut surface, translucent gray-white granules resembling **sago grains** (or boiled rice) are seen (early stage);
s3284 **“Lardaceous (waxy) spleen”** — amyloid accumulates **diffusely** in the **red pulp**, along the sinusoids and reticular fibers; on the cut surface it is uniform, with a waxy luster, red-brown. **3. Liver** — enlarged, firm, shiny, whitish-yellow (“lardaceous”); amyloid is deposited in the **spaces of Disse** (around the sinusoids), in vessel walls and in the portal tracts; the hepatocytes are compressed and atrophy. Liver function is usually only slightly impaired (portal hypertension is rare).
s3285 **4. Heart** — the main organ affected in **AL** and **ATTR** amyloidosis: the heart is enlarged, with thickened, firm, waxy walls; amyloid is deposited between cardiomyocytes, in vessel walls and in the endocardium. **Restrictive cardiomyopathy**, arrhythmias (involvement of the conduction system) and heart failure are the main **cause of death** in AL amyloidosis. Low voltage on the ECG — inconsistent with the wall thickness.
s3286 **5. Adrenal glands** — amyloid in the cortex along vessels and reticular fibers; with bilateral involvement — **adrenal insufficiency** (Addison disease, Topic 4).
s3287 **6. Other organs: tongue** — **macroglossia** (in AL amyloidosis); **digestive tract** — diarrhea, malabsorption; **peripheral nerves** — polyneuropathy; **skin** — “**raccoon eyes**” around the eyelids (purpura, AL); **joints** and carpal tunnel syndrome; vessels (bleeding tendency).

s3298 **Diagnosis of amyloidosis** — **biopsy** when clinically suspected: the most convenient and safe is **abdominal fat pad aspiration biopsy**; rectal mucosa, gingival, kidney or heart biopsy; Congo red + polarized light, then determination of the amyloid type.
s3299 **Outcome and treatment:** amyloidosis is usually considered a **progressive** and irreversible process, but if the underlying cause is removed, the process may stop and even partially regress: **AA** — treatment of the chronic inflammation (treating tuberculosis or osteomyelitis, TNF inhibitors in rheumatoid arthritis), colchicine in familial Mediterranean fever; **AL** — elimination of the plasma cell clone (chemotherapy, bone marrow transplantation); **ATTR** — transthyretin-stabilizing drugs and liver transplantation.
s3300 If **proteinuria appears in a patient with a chronic inflammatory disease** (for example, long-standing tuberculosis, osteomyelitis, bronchiectasis or rheumatoid arthritis), **AA amyloidosis** should be considered first. Formerly, tuberculosis and chronic suppurative diseases were the main causes of amyloidosis; today, in developed countries, they are rheumatoid arthritis and inflammatory bowel diseases.
s3301 **Unexplained heart failure, nephrotic syndrome and macroglossia in an elderly patient** — AL amyloidosis (multiple myeloma should be sought).
s3302 8.7. Clinicopathological section
s3303 Three diseases — not one mechanism
s3304 **Poststreptococcal glomerulonephritis** (type III): 2–3 weeks after tonsillitis a child develops edema, hematuria (“meat-wash” colored urine) and arterial hypertension; immune complexes (“humps” — subepithelial deposits) and neutrophils in the glomeruli.
s3305 **Goodpasture syndrome** (type II): a young man with hemoptysis and rapidly progressive kidney failure; antibodies against **type IV collagen in the basement membrane** of the pulmonary alveoli and renal glomeruli; on immunofluorescence — **linear** IgG deposits along the basement membrane.
s3306 **Contact dermatitis** (type IV): an itchy vesicular rash under a nickel-containing watchstrap after 48 hours; an infiltrate of lymphocytes and macrophages in the dermis.
s3307 **Accidental** (Latin accidens — chance) — occurring by chance; accidental transformation of the thymus — its rapid involution under the influence of stress.
s3308 **Allergy** (Greek allos — other, ergon — action) — altered (increased) reactivity of the organism to an antigen.
s3309 **Amyloid** (Latin amylum — starch) — a glycoprotein consisting mainly of fibrillar protein.
s3310 **Bursa-dependent (B) lymphocytes** (Latin bursa — pouch) — lymphocytes that mature in the bone marrow and carry out humoral immune reactions; so named because in birds they form in the bursa of Fabricius.
s3311 **Thymus-dependent (T) lymphocytes** — lymphocytes that mature in the thymus and provide cell-mediated immunity.
s3312 **Helper T cells** (English help) — helper lymphocytes that regulate the immune response (CD4+).
s3313 **Killer T cells** (English killer) — cytotoxic lymphocytes that kill target cells (CD8+).
s3314 **Suppressor T cells** (Latin suppressio — suppression) — (regulatory) lymphocytes that suppress the immune response.
s3315 **Hypoplasia** — insufficient development of an organ or tissue.
s3316 **Thymicolymphatic state** (status thymicolymphaticus) — the old name of a congenital immunodeficiency with hyperplasia of the thymus, spleen and lymph nodes and hypoplasia of the adrenal glands.
s3317 **Autoimmunity** — an immune response of the organism against its own antigens.
s3318 **Tolerance** (Latin tolerantia — endurance) — absence of a response of the immune system to a particular antigen (normally — to self antigens).
s3319 **Immunodeficiency** — a deficiency of one or more components of the immune system.
s3320 **Opportunistic infection** (Latin opportunus — convenient) — an infection caused by opportunistic microbes in a person with reduced immunity.
s3321 **Pannus** (Latin pannus — piece of cloth) — synovial granulation tissue that destroys cartilage in rheumatoid arthritis.
s3322 **“Splenic amyloidosis — sago spleen”.** The spleen is enlarged and firm; on the cut surface there are translucent gray granules resembling sago grains (amyloid in the follicles). Conclusion: secondary (AA) amyloidosis.
s3323 **“Splenic amyloidosis — lardaceous spleen”.** The spleen is enlarged and firm; on the cut surface it is uniform, shiny, red-brown and waxy. Conclusion: amyloidosis (diffuse deposition in the red pulp).
s3324 **“Renal amyloidosis — large white kidney”.** The kidneys are enlarged, firm and whitish-gray, with a waxy luster on the cut surface; the cortex is widened. Conclusion: amyloid nephrosis (nephrotic stage).
