s3146 8.3. Transplant rejection
s3147 When an allogeneic organ (from another person) is transplanted, the recipient's immune system recognizes the donor **HLA** antigens as foreign. Types of rejection:
s3148 **Hyperacute** — within minutes to hours: the recipient has **preexisting** antibodies against donor antigens (ABO incompatibility, previous transplantation, transfusion, pregnancy) — type III/II; thrombosis and fibrinoid necrosis in the vessels, **ischemic necrosis** of the organ. It is now rare thanks to pretransplant crossmatching.
s3149 **Acute** — days, weeks, months: **cellular** (CD8+ cytotoxic T lymphocytes — interstitial lymphocytic infiltration, tubular damage — “tubulitis”) and **humoral** (antibodies — vasculitis, endothelial damage, neutrophils in capillaries, C4d deposits) mechanisms; it responds to immunosuppressive therapy.
s3150 **Chronic** — months, years: thickening of the arterial intima (**transplant arteriopathy**), interstitial fibrosis, parenchymal atrophy; gradual loss of organ function. **“Graft-versus-host” disease** — when bone marrow (immunocompetent cells) is transplanted, donor T lymphocytes attack the recipient's tissues: the skin (rash), liver (jaundice) and intestine (diarrhea) are affected.
s3151 8.4. Autoimmune diseases
s3152 In **autoimmune diseases**, tissues are damaged as a result of an immune response against the organism's **own antigens** (autoantibodies and self-reactive T lymphocytes). Normally **immunologic tolerance** to self antigens is maintained:
s3153 **central tolerance** — lymphocytes that recognize self antigens are eliminated by apoptosis in the thymus and bone marrow (**clonal deletion**);
s3154 **peripheral tolerance** — anergy, suppression by **regulatory T lymphocytes**, activation-induced death (Fas–FasL). **Causes of loss of tolerance: genetic predisposition** (HLA genes, PTPN22, AIRE and others); **infections** — **molecular mimicry** (a microbial antigen resembles a self antigen — streptococcal M protein and cardiac antigens in rheumatic fever), tissue damage and exposure of hidden antigens; hormonal factors (most autoimmune diseases are more common in **women**), drugs and chemicals, ultraviolet light (SLE).
s3155 Classification:
s3156 **organ-specific** — Hashimoto thyroiditis, Graves disease, type 1 diabetes mellitus, autoimmune gastritis (pernicious anemia), myasthenia gravis, multiple sclerosis, Goodpasture syndrome;
s3157 **systemic (non-organ-specific)** — **systemic lupus erythematosus**, **rheumatoid arthritis**, **systemic sclerosis (scleroderma)**, dermatomyositis, Sjögren syndrome, polyarteritis nodosa (most of them in the older classification are “**collagen diseases**” or **rheumatic diseases** — systemic involvement of connective tissue, the disorganization of Topic 3).
s3158 Systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE)
s3159 **SLE** is a systemic autoimmune disease affecting many organs and associated with **type III** hypersensitivity; mainly in **young women** (female:male ≈ 9:1). Its main feature is **antinuclear antibodies**
s3160 **(ANA)**; specific ones are antibodies against **double-stranded DNA** and the **Smith (Sm) antigen**. The antibodies damage nucleated cells and form immune complexes.
s3161 **vessels** — **fibrinoid necrosis** and acute necrotizing **vasculitis** in small arteries and arterioles; immune complex deposits;
s3162 **kidneys — lupus nephritis** (in more than 50% of patients, one of the main causes of death): immune complexes are deposited in the glomeruli (subendothelially), and the capillary basement membrane thickens — “**wire loops**”; proliferative glomerulonephritis, **hyaline thrombi**, fibrinoid necrosis;
s3163 **skin** — a **“butterfly” erythema** on the face (on the cheeks and bridge of the nose), photosensitivity; immunoglobulin and complement deposits at the dermoepidermal junction;
s3164 **joints** — nonerosive synovitis (arthritis);
s3165 **serous membranes** — fibrinous pericarditis, pleurisy;
s3166 **heart** — **Libman–Sacks endocarditis** (small sterile vegetations on both surfaces of the valves), myocarditis;
s3167 **spleen** — concentric periarterial fibrosis around the follicular arteries (**“onion skin”**);
s3168 **blood** — hemolytic anemia, thrombocytopenia, leukopenia (type II); **antiphospholipid antibodies** — thromboses, miscarriage;
s3169 **brain** — vasculitis, thrombosis, psychiatric disturbances. Histologic signs: **hematoxylin bodies** (LE bodies — nuclei altered by antibodies), **LE cells** in the blood (a neutrophil that has engulfed a hematoxylin body).

s3174 Rheumatoid arthritis
s3175 **Rheumatoid arthritis** is a chronic systemic autoimmune disease that mainly affects the **joints** (the small joints of the hands and feet — metacarpophalangeal and proximal interphalangeal joints, symmetrically). Mechanism — **type IV** (CD4+ T lymphocytes, TNF) and **type III** (immune complexes). In the blood — **rheumatoid factor** (IgM against the Fc portion of IgG) and **anti–cyclic citrullinated peptide antibodies (ACPA)**.
s3176 **Morphology:** chronic inflammation of the synovium — **hyperplasia of synovial cells**, lymphocytes, plasma cells, lymphoid follicles, new vessels; granulation tissue forming villous projections — the **pannus** — grows into the cartilage and destroys it and the bone (**erosions**). Outcome — fibrous and bony **ankylosis**, joint deformities (ulnar deviation of the fingers, “**swan neck**”). Extra-articular — **rheumatoid nodules** (subcutaneous, at the elbow: fibrinoid necrosis in the center surrounded by palisading macrophages), vasculitis, pulmonary fibrosis, **AA amyloidosis**.
s3177 Systemic scleroderma (systemic sclerosis)
s3178 **Systemic sclerosis** is a disease characterized by activation of the immune system, damage to small vessels and **progressive fibrosis of the skin and internal organs**. Fibroblasts are activated by TGF-beta and other cytokines. The **skin** is thickened, dense and immobile; the fingers are thin and claw-like (**sclerodactyly**); the face is masklike; **Raynaud phenomenon**. **Internal organs:** esophagus (fibrosis, impaired motility), lungs (interstitial fibrosis, pulmonary hypertension), kidneys (thickening of the arterial intima — “**scleroderma renal crisis**”), heart. Antibodies: **Scl-70** (diffuse form), **anticentromere** (limited form — **CREST syndrome**: calcinosis, Raynaud phenomenon, esophageal dysmotility, sclerodactyly, telangiectasias).
s3179 Hashimoto thyroiditis
s3180 **Hashimoto thyroiditis (autoimmune thyroiditis)** is an organ-specific autoimmune disease and the most common cause of **hypothyroidism** (in iodine-sufficient areas). Antibodies against thyroid peroxidase and thyroglobulin; mainly a **type IV** (CD8+ T lymphocytes kill thyrocytes) and type II mechanism. **Morphology:** the thyroid gland is diffusely enlarged (goiter), firm and whitish-gray on the cut surface; a dense **lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate** in the parenchyma, **lymphoid follicles with germinal centers**, follicular atrophy; thyrocytes are large, with eosinophilic granular cytoplasm — **Hürthle (Askanazy) cells**; fibrosis. Complications — hypothyroidism (myxedema), risk of **B-cell lymphoma**.

s3187 8.5. Immunodeficiency states
s3188 **Immunodeficiency** is a decrease in the organism's resistance to infections and tumors as a result of a deficiency of one or more components of the immune system. It is **primary (congenital, hereditary)** or **secondary (acquired)**. The common clinical feature is **recurrent, severe and opportunistic infections** (caused by opportunistic microbes) and a predisposition to tumors and autoimmune diseases.

s3218 HIV infection and AIDS
s3219 **Acquired immunodeficiency syndrome (AIDS)** is the final stage of infection caused by the **human immunodeficiency virus (HIV)**. HIV is a **retrovirus** (lentivirus); mainly HIV-1. **Routes of transmission:** sexual, parenteral (injecting drug use, contaminated instruments, blood transfusion), vertical (from mother to child — during pregnancy, childbirth and breastfeeding).
s3220 **Pathogenesis:** through its **gp120** protein the virus binds to the **CD4** molecule and coreceptors (**CCR5, CXCR4**) and infects **CD4^+^ T lymphocytes**, macrophages and dendritic cells. The viral RNA is converted to DNA by **reverse transcriptase** and integrated into the cell genome. CD4+ T lymphocytes gradually die — cell-mediated immunity is profoundly impaired; macrophages become a **reservoir** of the virus and carry it to the brain.
s3221 Stages:
s3222 **Acute (primary) infection** — at 3–6 weeks: a mononucleosis-like syndrome (fever, lymphadenopathy, rash), high viremia, a transient decrease in CD4+ cells; seroconversion.
s3223 **Chronic (latent, clinically silent) phase** — on average 7–10 years: few symptoms, **persistent generalized lymphadenopathy**; the virus replicates actively in lymphoid organs, and CD4+ cells gradually decrease.
s3224 **AIDS** — CD4+ T lymphocytes **fewer than 200/µL**: **opportunistic infections**, **tumors**, cachexia, neurological disorders.
s3225 Morphology of AIDS:
s3226 **lymph nodes** — first follicular hyperplasia, then **follicular involution** and a **sharp decrease** in lymphocytes (“depletion” of lymphoid tissue);
s3227 **opportunistic infections** — **Pneumocystis jirovecii** pneumonia (the most common; foamy eosinophilic exudate in the alveoli), candidiasis (mouth, esophagus), cytomegalovirus infection (retina, intestine), atypical mycobacterioses and tuberculosis (the main cause of death in our region), cryptococcal meningitis, toxoplasmosis (brain abscesses), cryptosporidiosis (diarrhea), progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy;
s3228 **tumors** — **Kaposi sarcoma** (associated with HHV-8; red-violet patches and nodules on the skin), **B-cell lymphomas** (especially primary brain lymphoma), cervical and anal cancer (papillomavirus);
s3229 **central nervous system** — HIV encephalopathy (microglial nodules, multinucleated giant cells) — **HIV-associated dementia**;
s3230 **cachexia** (“wasting syndrome”).
s3231 Modern **highly active antiretroviral therapy (ART)** stops HIV replication, restores CD4+ cell counts and prevents the development of AIDS — the life expectancy of people living with HIV can be almost normal. Therefore **early diagnosis** (HIV testing) and treatment are most important. ART is used during pregnancy to prevent mother-to-child transmission.
s3232 8.6. Amyloidosis
s3233 **Amyloidosis** is a **stromal-vascular protein degeneration** (Topic 3) characterized by **extracellular** deposition in tissues of **amyloid** — a fibrillar protein with a beta-pleated sheet structure formed from misfolded proteins. The name amyloid (Latin amylum — starch) was given by R. Virchow: he saw that this substance stains with iodine like starch.
s3234 Structure and composition of amyloid
s3235 Amyloid is a complex **glycoprotein**. On electron microscopy, amyloid consists of nonbranching, randomly arranged **fibrils** 7.5–10 nm in diameter. On X-ray crystallography the fibrils have a **cross-beta-pleated sheet** structure — this structure determines the characteristic staining of amyloid and its resistance to proteolysis. Composition of amyloid: the **fibrillar protein (F component)** — 95% (varying with the type of amyloidosis) and the **plasma component (P component)** — 5% (serum amyloid P component, glycosaminoglycans).
s3236 The main fibrillar proteins:
s3237 **AL (amyloid light chain)** — immunoglobulin **light chains** (usually lambda); produced by **plasma cells**;
s3238 **AA (amyloid-associated)** — derived from the acute phase protein **serum amyloid A (SAA)** synthesized in the liver; it increases in chronic inflammation under the action of IL-1 and IL-6;
s3239 **ATTR (transthyretin)** — a thyroxine- and retinol-transporting protein; mutant (hereditary) or normal (senile);
s3240 **A-beta-2-microglobulin** — in patients on hemodialysis;
