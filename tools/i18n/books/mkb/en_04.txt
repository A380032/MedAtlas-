s0719 **Consequences:**
s0720 the shape is not constant (**pleomorphism**): round, pear-shaped, filamentous forms;
s0721 **practically not stained by the Gram method** (Romanowsky–Giemsa is used);
s0722 **naturally resistant to beta-lactam antibiotics** (penicillin stops cell wall synthesis, and there is no wall) — macrolides, tetracyclines and fluoroquinolones are used in treatment;
s0723 can pass through bacterial filters;
s0724 grows slowly on specially enriched medium and forms tiny colonies with a depressed center, a **“fried-egg”** appearance.
s0725 Medically important: *Mycoplasma pneumoniae* (atypical pneumonia), *Mycoplasma hominis* and *Ureaplasma urealyticum* (urogenital infections).
s0726 **Chlamydiae** — gram-negative prokaryotes that have a cell wall but little peptidoglycan; **obligate intracellular parasites**, because they cannot synthesize ATP themselves (“energy parasites”). Their developmental cycle consists of alternation between two forms:
s0727 **Elementary body (EB)** — a small, dense, metabolically inactive, **infectious** form that lives outside the cell; it enters the cell.
s0728 **Reticulate body (RB)** — a large, metabolically active form that divides inside the cell, but is not infectious.
s0729 Cycle: EB enters the cell → prevents phagolysosome formation → turns into RB and multiplies → turns into new EBs → the cell breaks down and EBs are released. The whole cycle lasts 48–72 hours.

s0732 **Main species and diseases:** *C. trachomatis* — trachoma, urogenital chlamydiosis, neonatal conjunctivitis; *C. psittaci* — ornithosis (from birds); *C. pneumoniae* — respiratory infection.
s0733 **Diagnosis:** inoculation into cell culture (the reference method, rarely used in practice), **PCR** (the main method today), direct immunofluorescence, antibody detection by ELISA; seeing intracellular inclusions in a smear with Romanowsky–Giemsa.
s0734 **Rickettsiae** — small, polymorphic (coccoid or rod-shaped) gram-negative bacteria, **obligate intracellular parasites**. Most are transmitted by **arthropods** (lice, ticks, fleas) — which is why the infections they cause are called **vector-borne** (topic 26).
s0735 Main ones: *Rickettsia prowazekii* — epidemic typhus (via lice); *R. typhi* — endemic (murine, rat) typhus (via fleas); *Coxiella burnetii* — Q fever (also transmitted by the airborne route).
s0736 **Staining:** Romanowsky–Giemsa, the **Zdrodovsky** method (rickettsiae red, cell cytoplasm blue). **Diagnosis** is made mainly by serological methods (CFT, PHA, ELISA) and PCR.
s0737 **Actinomycetes** — gram-positive bacteria that form branching filamentous outgrowths (mycelium). They were formerly considered fungi, but their prokaryotic structure (a wall with peptidoglycan, 70S ribosomes, no nucleus) places them among the bacteria; they are sensitive to antibacterial antibiotics.
s0738 *Actinomyces israelii* — a member of the normal oral microflora; when immunity falls or after injury it causes **actinomycosis** (chronic granulomatous inflammation, fistulas). Yellow **druses** (sulfur granules; clumps of mycelium) visible to the naked eye are found in the pus — this is the classic diagnostic sign.
s0739 *Nocardia* — partially acid-fast; pulmonary and cutaneous nocardiosis, often in patients with reduced immunity.
s0740 A chronic, indurated infiltrate with fistulous openings in the maxillofacial region + yellow granules (druses) in the pus — a picture typical of actinomycosis. Seeing the druses on ordinary microscopy speeds up the diagnosis.

s0895 All bacteria that cause disease in humans are **chemoorganoheterotrophs**: they obtain both carbon and energy from ready-made organic substances.
s0896 *Haemophilus influenzae* needs **factor X (hemin)** and **factor V (NAD)** to grow. That is why it does not grow on ordinary blood agar, but grows well on **chocolate agar** (heated blood — the erythrocytes break down and release the factors). This requirement is taken into account directly in routine laboratory work.
s0897 In determining antibiotic susceptibility and testing a disinfectant, a **24-hour (young, logarithmic-phase)** culture is used. In an old culture the cells are polymorphic, and the result comes out wrong.
s0898 Remember the types of media by function: **S**elective — **S**ingles out the one you want to grow; **D**ifferential — **D**istinguishes (tells organisms apart); **T**ransport — **T**o carry the specimen safely.
s0899 Chemical composition of bacteria
s0900 **Water** makes up 75–85% of the bacterial cell (the larger part of it is free water — the medium and solvent for reactions). The main components of the dry residue:
s0901 **proteins** (50–80%) — structural proteins, enzymes, toxins, antigens;
s0902 **nucleic acids** — DNA (nucleoid, plasmid) and RNA (r-, m-, t-RNA);
s0903 **carbohydrates** — cell wall, capsule, reserve substances;
s0904 **lipids** — membrane, mycolic acid in mycobacteria, lipid A in gram-negative bacteria;
s0905 **minerals** — phosphorus, potassium, magnesium, iron, sulfur and trace elements.

s0932 **Entry of substances into the cell** occurs by passive diffusion, facilitated diffusion, **active transport** (spending energy, against the concentration gradient) and group translocation. Active transport is the main route.
s0933 **Growth factors** — substances that the bacterium cannot synthesize itself and must obtain ready-made: some amino acids, vitamins (B group), purine and pyrimidine bases, heme. Bacteria that need them are called **auxotrophs**, and those that do not are called **prototrophs**.
s0934 Growth and reproduction
s0935 Bacteria multiply by **binary fission**. The time between two cell divisions is the **generation time**; in *E. coli* it is 20–30 minutes under favorable conditions, and in *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* up to 14–24 hours.
s0936 The **growth curve in liquid medium** consists of four phases:
s0937 **Lag phase (adaptation)** — the number of cells does not increase; the enzyme system adapts to the new medium.
s0938 **Logarithmic (exponential) phase** — the number of cells increases in geometric progression; the bacteria are most active, their morphology is typical, and they are most sensitive to antibiotics and disinfectants. Cultures for research are taken from this phase.
s0939 **Stationary phase** — balance between multiplication and death; the cell count is at its maximum (M-concentration). Many secondary metabolites, toxins, pigments and spores are formed in this phase.
s0940 **Death (decline) phase** — nutrients run out, metabolites accumulate, the number of living cells falls, and forms change (pleomorphism, involution forms).
s0941 **Growth on solid medium** — in the form of colonies. A colony is a visible collection of cells that has arisen by multiplication from a single cell; therefore **a single colony = a source of pure culture**.
s0942 Nutrient media
s0943 **Requirements for a nutrient medium:** it must be sterile; contain the necessary nutrients and growth factors; have an optimal pH (7.2–7.6 for most pathogens); be isotonic; have moisture and transparency; have a standard composition.
