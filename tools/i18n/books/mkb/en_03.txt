s0478 The acid is washed off with water and the smear is fixed.
s0479 It is stained with carbol fuchsin, heated until steam rises (5–7 minutes), and rinsed.
s0480 It is counterstained with methylene blue (3–5 minutes), rinsed and dried.
s0481 It is examined under the immersion objective: **red spore + blue vegetative cell**.
s0482 **Capsule** — a mucous layer with a distinct border on top of the cell wall. If thicker than 0.2 µm it is a **macrocapsule** (visible with a light microscope); a thinner one is called a **microcapsule**. A layer without a distinct border that is easily removed is the **slime layer (glycocalyx)**; it forms the basis of the biofilm and is important in infections associated with catheters, prostheses and implants.
s0483 **Chemical composition:** a polysaccharide in most bacteria (pneumococcus, Klebsiella, meningococcus, *Haemophilus influenzae*), a polypeptide in the anthrax bacillus.
s0484 **Significance:**
s0485 resists phagocytosis — a **virulence factor** (unencapsulated strains are not virulent);
s0486 determines the **serovars within a species**: pneumococcus has more than 90 capsular serotypes, and vaccines are made precisely from these polysaccharides;
s0487 protects against desiccation and immune factors.
s0488 Most bacteria form the capsule **in the body** or on media with added serum, and lose it on ordinary artificial media. That is why a smear prepared from **pathological material** (sputum, CSF, blood) is usually used to see the capsule.
s0489 **Burri–Hiss method:** India ink is added to the material and smeared (the background becomes black); after fixation, it is stained with fuchsin. As a result, a **red cell** on a **black background** is seen, and around it a **colorless halo — the capsule**.
s0490 Flagella and motility
s0491 **Flagellum** — a filamentous appendage made of flagellin protein and anchored to the cytoplasmic membrane; it gives the bacterium motility and **chemotaxis** (toward nutrients, away from harmful substances). The flagellum is used as the **H-antigen** in serological identification.

s0513 **Methods of determining motility:**
s0514 **Hanging drop** — living cells in a drop between a concave slide and a coverslip are observed by phase-contrast or dark-field microscopy.
s0515 **Crushed (wet-mount) drop** — a coverslip is placed over a drop on an ordinary slide; simpler, but the drop dries quickly.
s0516 **Stab inoculation into semisolid agar** — motile bacteria spread sideways from the stab line and make the medium cloudy; nonmotile ones grow only along the stab line. This method is the most widely used in routine practice.
s0517 Pili and inclusions
s0518 **Pili (fimbriae)** — numerous protein appendages that are shorter and thinner than flagella. They are divided into two groups:
s0519 **common-type pili (adhesins)** — ensure attachment of the cell to the epithelium; this is the first step of infection (for example, the P pili of E. coli in urinary tract infection, gonococcal pili);
s0520 **sex (F) pili** — ensure the transfer of DNA between donor and recipient cells during conjugation (topic 11).
s0521 **Inclusions** — reserve substances in the cytoplasm: **volutin (metachromatic granules)**, glycogen, starch, lipid droplets, sulfur granules. The most important for diagnosis is **volutin**: it is located at the ends of the diphtheria bacillus, and with the **Neisser** stain the cell appears yellow-brown while the volutin granules appear **dark blue-black**. This is the classic sign in the bacterioscopic diagnosis of diphtheria.

s0525 Independent work: Fungi — morphology, structure and methods of study
s0526 **Fungi** — **eukaryotic**, chlorophyll-free, heterotrophic organisms. They differ from bacteria in having a formed nucleus, membrane-bound organelles, **80S** ribosomes, a cell wall made of **chitin and glucan**, and **ergosterol** in the membrane (the target of antifungal drugs). That is why antibacterial antibiotics do not act on fungi.

s0549 Practical skill: choosing complex staining methods
s0550 In the laboratory, the staining method is chosen according to the **structure being sought**:
s0551 Shape, arrangement and Gram group needed → **Gram method**.
s0552 Acid-fast rods (tuberculosis, leprosy) → **Ziehl–Neelsen** (red rods, blue background) or the fluorescent method with auramine.
s0553 Spore → **Ozheshko** (red spore, blue cell).
s0554 Capsule → **Burri–Hiss** (black background, colorless halo).
s0555 Volutin granules (diphtheria) → **Neisser**.
s0556 Spirochetes → **Romanowsky–Giemsa**, **Morozov** (silver impregnation) or dark-field.
s0557 Fungi → **KOH preparation**, PAS, calcofluor.
s0558 In complex methods, the order of steps and the timing must be strictly observed. In the Ozheshko method, the spore is not stained unless it is treated with acid; in the Neisser method, if the counterstain is held too long, the volutin granules merge with the background and become invisible.
