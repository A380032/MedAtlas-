s3021 Antigens
s3022 **Antigen (Ag)** — a genetically foreign substance that, on entering the body, can provoke an immune response (antibody formation and/or a cellular response) and can bind **specifically** with the products of that response.
s3023 **Main properties:**
s3024 **Antigenicity** — the ability to bind with the products of the immune response;
s3025 **Immunogenicity** — the ability to provoke an immune response (it is high if the molecular mass is large and the chemical structure is complex);
s3026 **Specificity** — determined by the **epitope (antigenic determinant)**, the small part of the antigen molecule that binds directly to the antibody.
s3027 **Types:**
s3028 **Complete antigens** — both immunogenic and antigenic (proteins);
s3029 **Haptens** — small molecules; they do not provoke an immune response by themselves but do so when joined to a carrier protein; they bind to antibody independently (many drugs can cause allergy as haptens).

s3076 **Dynamics of the immune response.** **Primary response:** latent period 3–5 days, first **IgM**, then IgG; the titer is low and falls quickly. **Secondary response (due to memory):** the latent period is short (1–2 days), a large amount of **IgG** forms at once, and the titer is high and persists for a long time. For this reason **revaccination** is done in vaccination.
s3077 Serological reactions: the general principle
s3078 **Serological reactions** are reactions based on the specific binding between antigen and antibody *in vitro*.
s3079 **They are set up for two purposes:**
s3080 **Detecting an unknown antigen with a known antibody** (identifying the pathogen) — for this a **diagnostic serum** is needed;
s3081 **Detecting unknown antibodies with a known antigen** (in the patient’s serum) — for this a **diagnosticum** is needed.
s3082 **Reagents:**
s3083 **Diagnostic serum** — serum containing known antibodies, obtained by immunizing animals with a known antigen (agglutinating, precipitating, luminescent, enzyme-conjugated and others).
s3084 **Diagnosticum** — a preparation of a known antigen (a suspension of killed microbes, an erythrocyte diagnosticum, an antigen extract).
s3085 **General conditions of the reaction:** the antigen and antibody must match, an electrolyte (physiological saline) must be present, the pH and temperature must be optimal (often 37 °C), and the ratio of the components must be correct.
s3086 The main serological reactions
s3087 5.1. Agglutination reaction (AR)
s3088 **Principle:** a corpuscular (large, particulate) antigen — bacterial cells, erythrocytes — sticks together under the action of antibody and forms a visible granular precipitate.
s3089 **Variants:**
s3090 **On a glass slide (preliminary, rapid)** — mixing the isolated culture with diagnostic serum → granularity appears; for identification;
s3091 **Extended (in a tube)** — serially diluting the patient’s serum and adding a diagnosticum → the **titer** is determined (Widal reaction — in typhoid fever; Wright reaction — in brucellosis);
s3092 **Indirect (passive) hemagglutination (PHA)** — the antigen is “loaded” onto the surface of erythrocytes; high sensitivity;
s3093 **Coagglutination** — antibodies are attached to staphylococcal protein A;
s3094 **Latex agglutination** — antibodies are attached to latex particles (rapid tests).

s3096 Agglutination reactions: the mechanism, the titration series in a tube and the preliminary reaction on a glass slide; positive and negative result. Source: Muhamedov I.M. et al., 2019.
s3097 5.2. Precipitation reaction (PR)
s3098 **Principle:** a **soluble (molecular)** antigen combines with antibody and forms visible turbidity or a precipitate. The result is seen best in the **equivalence zone**, where the ratio of antigen and antibody is optimal.
s3099 **Variants:** ring precipitation (a white ring at the boundary of two liquids in a tube — the Ascoli reaction), **double immunodiffusion (Ouchterlony)** in gel, radial immunodiffusion (Mancini — measuring the amount of immunoglobulin), immunoelectrophoresis, **flocculation** (measuring toxoid activity).

s3102 5.3. Complement fixation test (CFT)
s3103 **It consists of two systems:**
s3104 **Test system** — patient serum (antibody) + known antigen + complement;
s3105 **Indicator (hemolytic) system** — sheep erythrocytes + hemolytic serum.
s3106 **Result:** if an antigen–antibody complex forms in the test system, the complement is **bound** there and does not reach the indicator system → **there is no hemolysis** → the reaction is **positive**. If no complex forms, the complement stays free and lyses the erythrocytes → **there is hemolysis** → the reaction is **negative**.

s3108 Scheme of setting up the complement fixation test (with the serum of a sick and a healthy person) and the principle of the immunofluorescence reaction. Source: Muhamedov I.M. et al., 2019.
s3109 5.4. Reactions based on labeled antibodies
s3110 **Immunofluorescence reaction (IF, Coons method)** — the antibody is labeled with a fluorochrome (FITC); in a fluorescence microscope it glows bright green. In the **direct** method the labeled antibody is applied directly to the antigen; in the **indirect** method an ordinary antibody is added first, then a labeled antibody against it (more sensitive).
s3111 **Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)** — the antibody or antigen is labeled with an **enzyme**; when substrate is added a color appears, and the color intensity is measured in a photometer. Direct, indirect and “sandwich” variants exist. It is currently the most widespread serological method.

s3114 **Radioimmunoassay (RIA)** — labeling with an isotope; very sensitive, but replaced by ELISA in practice because of the radiation hazard.
s3115 **Immunoblot (Western blot)** — antigens are separated by electrophoresis, transferred to a membrane and treated with the patient’s serum; as a result the **antibody against each antigen appears as a separate band**. That is why it is used as a **confirmatory (verification)** test (for example, in HIV diagnosis — topic 29).

s3118 **Neutralization test (NT)** — the antibody removes the biological activity of a toxin or virus; the result is assessed in a living system (animal, cell culture).
