s4370 Yersiniosis
s4371 **Causative agents:** *Yersinia enterocolitica* (yersiniosis) and *Y. pseudotuberculosis* (pseudotuberculosis). (*Y. pestis* is covered in topic 24.)
s4372 **Features:** gram-negative rods; **psychrophilic** — **multiply at low temperature (4–8 °C)**, so they accumulate in refrigerated products and vegetables (“refrigerator infection”). Motile at 25–28 °C, nonmotile at 37 °C; urease positive.

s4376 **Diagnosis:**
s4377 **Material:** stool, blood, surgical material (lymph nodes, appendix).

s4380 **Identification:** biochemical tests (urease +), testing motility at two temperatures, serological identification.
s4381 **Serological method:** dynamics of the titer in paired sera by IHA/ELISA.
s4382 Independent work: Campylobacter and Helicobacter
s4383 6.1. *Campylobacter jejuni*
s4384 **Features:** gram-negative, **“gull-wing”**, S-shaped or “swallow-wing” curved rod; polar flagellum, highly motile; **microaerophilic** (5–10% O₂ + 10% CO₂) and **thermophilic** (grows well at 42 °C); oxidase positive.
s4385 **Epidemiology:** the source is poultry and other animals; it is transmitted through undercooked poultry, unpasteurized milk and contaminated water.
s4386 **Clinical picture:** acute gastroenteritis (often bloody diarrhea, severe abdominal pain, fever) — one of the main causes of bacterial diarrhea worldwide; a complication is **Guillain–Barré syndrome** (through cross-reactive antigens).
s4387 **Diagnosis:** stool → **selective medium** (with added antibiotics) → **microaerophilic conditions, 42 °C** → oxidase and motility; in a wet mount the characteristic “darting” motion; modern methods — antigen tests and PCR.
s4388 6.2. *Helicobacter pylori*
s4389 **Features:** gram-negative, **spiral/S-shaped** curved rod; a tuft of flagella at one end (highly motile); **microaerophilic**; its most important feature is **strong urease activity**.
s4390 Figure 20.5
s4391 *Helicobacter pylori* — spiral-shaped gram-negative bacteria in the gastric mucosa. Source: Muhamedov I.M. et al., 2019.
s4392 **Pathogenesis:** urease splits urea to form **ammonia** — it neutralizes the acid around the bacterium and allows it to survive in the acidic gastric environment; motility and the spiral shape ensure penetration into the mucous layer; adhesins and the **CagA** and **VacA** factors damage the epithelium.
s4393 **Clinical significance:** chronic gastritis, gastric and duodenal ulcer disease; in the long term, it is linked to the risk of gastric cancer and MALT lymphoma.

s4411 Practical skill: examining stool for intestinal infection
s4412 Stool is collected **before antibiotics**, if possible in the first days of the illness, into a sterile container; if there are pathological admixtures (mucus, blood), the sample is taken from exactly that part.
s4413 The sample is delivered within 2 hours; if delayed, **transport medium** (Cary–Blair) is used.
