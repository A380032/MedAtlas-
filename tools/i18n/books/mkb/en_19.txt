s4371 Yersiniosis
s4372 **Causative agents:** *Yersinia enterocolitica* (yersiniosis) and *Y. pseudotuberculosis* (pseudotuberculosis). (*Y. pestis* is covered in topic 24.)
s4373 **Features:** gram-negative rods; **psychrophilic** — **multiply at low temperature (4–8 °C)**, so they accumulate in refrigerated products and vegetables (“refrigerator infection”). Motile at 25–28 °C, nonmotile at 37 °C; urease positive.

s4377 **Diagnosis:**
s4378 **Material:** stool, blood, surgical material (lymph nodes, appendix).
s4379 **“Cold enrichment”** — storing the material in phosphate buffer **at 4 °C for 1–3 weeks**; Yersinia multiply while the accompanying flora is suppressed. It is then inoculated onto selective medium (CIN agar, Endo).
s4380 **Incubation** is carried out at 25–28 °C (not 37 °C).
s4381 **Identification:** biochemical tests (urease +), testing motility at two temperatures, serological identification.
s4382 **Serological method:** dynamics of the titer in paired sera by IHA/ELISA.
s4383 Independent work: Campylobacter and Helicobacter
s4384 6.1. *Campylobacter jejuni*
s4385 **Features:** gram-negative, **“gull-wing”**, S-shaped or “swallow-wing” curved rod; polar flagellum, highly motile; **microaerophilic** (5–10% O₂ + 10% CO₂) and **thermophilic** (grows well at 42 °C); oxidase positive.
s4386 **Epidemiology:** the source is poultry and other animals; it is transmitted through undercooked poultry, unpasteurized milk and contaminated water.
s4387 **Clinical picture:** acute gastroenteritis (often bloody diarrhea, severe abdominal pain, fever) — one of the main causes of bacterial diarrhea worldwide; a complication is **Guillain–Barré syndrome** (through cross-reactive antigens).
s4388 **Diagnosis:** stool → **selective medium** (with added antibiotics) → **microaerophilic conditions, 42 °C** → oxidase and motility; in a wet mount the characteristic “darting” motion; modern methods — antigen tests and PCR.
s4389 6.2. *Helicobacter pylori*
s4390 **Features:** gram-negative, **spiral/S-shaped** curved rod; a tuft of flagella at one end (highly motile); **microaerophilic**; its most important feature is **strong urease activity**.
s4391 Figure 20.5
s4392 *Helicobacter pylori* — spiral-shaped gram-negative bacteria in the gastric mucosa. Source: Muhamedov I.M. et al., 2019.
s4393 **Pathogenesis:** urease splits urea to form **ammonia** — it neutralizes the acid around the bacterium and allows it to survive in the acidic gastric environment; motility and the spiral shape ensure penetration into the mucous layer; adhesins and the **CagA** and **VacA** factors damage the epithelium.
s4394 **Clinical significance:** chronic gastritis, gastric and duodenal ulcer disease; in the long term, it is linked to the risk of gastric cancer and MALT lymphoma.

s4411 Before breath and stool tests, proton pump inhibitors, antibiotics and bismuth preparations are stopped for a set period — otherwise the result will be a **false negative**. Serology stays positive for a long time even after treatment, so it is not suitable for monitoring eradication.
s4412 Practical skill: examining stool for intestinal infection
s4413 Stool is collected **before antibiotics**, if possible in the first days of the illness, into a sterile container; if there are pathological admixtures (mucus, blood), the sample is taken from exactly that part.
s4414 The sample is delivered within 2 hours; if delayed, **transport medium** (Cary–Blair) is used.
s4415 Inoculation: Endo/Levine (general), Ploskirev and bismuth sulfite agar (Salmonella/Shigella), selective media (for Campylobacter — 42 °C under microaerophilic conditions).
s4416 After 18–24 hours the colonies are assessed: lactose-positive (colored) and lactose-negative (colorless) colonies are separated.
s4417 A pure culture is picked from suspicious colonies → Kligler, the biochemical series → **serological identification** (diagnostic sera).
s4418 Where necessary, PCR (diarrheagenic *E. coli*, Campylobacter, viral diarrheas) and an antibiogram.
s4419 The final answer states the species of the causative agent, its serovar (if any) and its antibiotic susceptibility.
