s6236 Practical skill: interpreting the markers of parenteral hepatitis and HIV
s6237 **Screening panel:** HBsAg, anti-HCV, HIV (4th-generation ELISA).
s6238 **If HBsAg is positive:** anti-HBc IgM (is it acute?), HBeAg and anti-HBe (degree of replication), **HBV DNA**; in an HBsAg-positive patient **anti-HDV** is also tested.
s6239 **If anti-HCV is positive:** **HCV RNA (PCR)** — to confirm active infection; if positive, genotyping.
s6240 **If the HIV screening is positive:** a **confirmatory test** by the set algorithm (immunoblot and/or a molecular method); after confirmation, the **CD4⁺ count** and **viral load**.
s6241 **If a negative result was obtained after a risky contact:** taking the “window period” into account, the test is repeated at set intervals.
s6242 **At all stages:** confidentiality, counseling (before and after the test), observing safe care rules.

s6378 **Routes of transmission:** direct contact (saliva, vesicle fluid), sexual (HSV-2), airborne (VZV), vertical and transfusion (CMV), through saliva — the “kissing disease” (EBV).
s6379 **Clinically important conditions:**
s6380 **Neonatal herpes** — infection with HSV-2 in the birth canal; it may give a severe, disseminated form;
s6381 **Congenital CMV infection** — hearing loss, damage to the nervous system;
s6382 **Infectious mononucleosis (EBV)** — fever, tonsillitis, lymphadenopathy, splenomegaly, **atypical mononuclear cells** in the blood;
s6383 **Shingles (reactivation of VZV)** — a painful vesicular rash along a nerve, on one side.
s6384 **Material:** vesicle fluid and scrapings from the base, saliva, cerebrospinal fluid, blood, biopsy; in newborns — from several sites.
s6385 **PCR — the main modern method**: especially in encephalitis (HSV in the cerebrospinal fluid), neonatal infection and CMV; **quantitative PCR** to monitor viral load in transplant patients.
s6386 **Cytologic method** — the Tzanck preparation: in a smear taken from the base of the rash, **multinucleated giant cells** and intranuclear inclusions.
s6387 **Serologic** — IgM (new infection), IgG (past infection); in EBV, the heterophile antibody test (for mononucleosis) and a panel of specific antibodies.
s6388 **Virus isolation** — in cell culture (CPE: rounding, syncytia, inclusions); rarely used in practice.
s6389 **Immunofluorescence and antigen tests** — rapid methods.
s6390 **Prophylaxis and control:** **vaccines against chickenpox and shingles** are available; there is no widely used vaccine against HSV and CMV. Antiviral drugs control reactivation but do not eliminate the latent virus — treatment is prescribed by a physician.
s6391 Poxviruses (*Poxviridae*)
s6392 **General properties:** **the largest viruses** (250–300 nm), visible under the light microscope with special staining; **double-stranded DNA**; **complex symmetry**, brick-shaped. Unlike other DNA viruses, they **multiply in the cytoplasm** (because they have their own enzymes).
s6393 Figure 30.3
s6394 The structure of the smallpox virus: the outer envelope, tubular structures, lateral bodies and the nucleocapsid (DNA + protein). Source: Muhamedov I.M. et al., 2019.
s6395 **Smallpox (variola):** historically one of the most severe infectious diseases; **declared eradicated worldwide by the World Health Organization in 1980** — the first and only complete eradication in the history of medicine. The main reasons: the virus circulates only in humans, a stable antigenic structure, an effective vaccine and a mass vaccination strategy.
s6396 **Monkeypox (mpox):** a zoonotic infection of the genus *Orthopoxvirus*; the reservoir is rodents and primates (West and Central Africa). Transmission: contact with a sick animal, from person to person by **close direct contact** (rash lesions, body fluids, prolonged face-to-face contact), contaminated objects; the vertical route is also possible.
s6397 **Clinical picture:** fever, headache, muscle pain and **enlargement of lymph nodes** (a sign that differs from smallpox), then a rash: macule → papule → vesicle → pustule → scab. The rash lesions in a given area are usually **at the same stage**.
s6398 **PCR (the main method)** — scrapings from a rash lesion, vesicle fluid, scab;
s6399 electron microscopy (brick-shaped virions), antigen tests — supplementary;
s6400 serology — of limited value because of cross-reactivity among orthopoxviruses;
s6401 work is carried out under the set biosafety regime.
s6402 **Control:** isolation of the patient, monitoring of contacts, protective equipment; smallpox vaccines give cross-protection against orthopoxviruses and are used in risk groups on indication.
s6403 Independent work: Nosocomial (hospital-acquired) infections
s6404 **Definition:** **healthcare-associated infections** — infections that were not present in the patient at hospital admission and were not in the incubation period, and that were acquired in a medical institution (also the occupational infections of medical staff).
s6405 **Main causes:**
s6406 **patient factor** — reduced immunity, severe underlying disease, age, diabetes mellitus;
s6407 **invasive procedures** — catheters, artificial ventilation, operations, drains (breach of natural barriers);
s6408 **microbial factor** — **multidrug-resistant** “hospital strains”; biofilm formation;
s6409 **organizational factors** — breaches of hand hygiene, breaches of the sterilization and disinfection regime, crowded rooms, unjustified use of antibiotics.
