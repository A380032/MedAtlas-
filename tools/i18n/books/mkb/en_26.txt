s5759 Coronaviruses (*Coronaviridae*)
s5760 **Structure:** enveloped, **single-stranded, positive-sense RNA** viruses; the genome is the largest among RNA viruses. The **S (spike) protein** in the envelope forms crown-like projections — hence the name.
s5761 **Members:** seasonal coronaviruses (mild ARVI), **SARS-CoV**, **MERS-CoV** and **SARS-CoV-2** (COVID-19).
s5762 **Pathogenesis and clinical picture:** the S protein binds to the cell receptor; the epithelium of the respiratory tract and other tissues are damaged. The clinical picture ranges from mild ARVI to severe pneumonia and systemic complications; in severe forms, excessive activation of the immune response and disturbance of the coagulation system are important.
s5763 **PCR (real-time)** — the gold standard; material: a nasopharyngeal swab;
s5764 **Rapid antigen tests** — convenient for screening, with lower sensitivity;
s5765 **Serology (IgM/IgG)** — for assessing past infection and the immune response; not the main method for acute diagnosis;
s5766 **Isolation in cell culture** — only in special laboratories.
s5767 **Prophylaxis:** vaccination, a mask, hand hygiene, ventilation, isolation measures.
s5768 Independent work: Avian and swine influenza, RS virus, rhinoviruses
s5769 **Avian influenza (for example, A/H5N1, A/H7N9):** the natural reservoir is wild waterfowl; it is transmitted to humans mainly **by direct contact with sick birds**; person-to-person transmission is limited. It can run with severe lung damage. The main danger is the appearance, as a result of **reassortment**, of a new variant easily transmitted from person to person (antigenic shift).
s5770 **Swine influenza (A/H1N1pdm09):** the strain of the 2009 pandemic — the product of **redistribution** of segments of human, swine and avian influenza viruses. It now circulates among the seasonal influenza strains and is included in the seasonal vaccine.
s5771 **The pig — the “mixing vessel”:** because pig cells carry receptors for both avian and human influenza viruses, the pig is a favorable environment for reassortment.
s5772 **RS virus:** **the main cause of bronchiolitis and pneumonia** in children under 1 year; it causes seasonal rises; immunity is incomplete — reinfection is possible. Diagnosis — antigen tests and PCR; special immunoprophylaxis is used in risk groups.
s5773 **Rhinoviruses (*Picornaviridae*):** **nonenveloped**, single-stranded positive RNA; there are more than 100 serotypes — so durable immunity does not form and a person falls ill several times a year. The optimal temperature for multiplication is **33–35 °C** — so they multiply mainly **in the upper respiratory tract** (the nasal mucosa). The most frequent cause of the common cold; diagnosis is usually not required.
s5774 **Rhinovirus — many serotypes + loves low temperature → a frequently recurring “common cold”; a vaccine is hard to develop.**
s5775 Practical skill: collecting material for a respiratory virus
s5776 The material is taken **in the first days of the illness** (best on day 1–3) — viral shedding is highest in this period.
s5777 **Nasopharyngeal swab:** the swab is advanced along the nasal passage **to the posterior wall**, held for a few seconds and withdrawn with rotation; a throat swab is taken from the tonsils and the posterior wall.
s5778 The swab is placed in **viral transport medium** and its tip is broken off.
s5779 The sample is kept at **2–8 °C** and delivered quickly; if prolonged storage is needed, it is frozen by the set procedure (repeated freezing and thawing is prohibited).
s5780 On the requisition: the date of onset, vaccination status, travel/contact history.
s5781 In the laboratory: PCR (often a multiplex panel), an antigen test where necessary, and in epidemiological surveillance — virological isolation.
s5782 **Safety:** aerosols may form — protective equipment and the prescribed regime are observed.

s5884 In poliomyelitis the paralysis is **asymmetric and flaccid**, and **sensation is not impaired** — this is related to the selective damage to the motor neurons.
s5885 In the poliomyelitis eradication program both vaccines are used: OPV interrupts the circulation of the virus, while IPV gives safe individual protection. They are used according to the national immunization schedule.
s5886 In rabies **time is the decisive factor**: prophylaxis must begin before clinical signs appear. Therefore in any case of a bite it is necessary to seek medical help immediately.
s5887 **“A and E — Alimentary/Enteral”**: both are transmitted through the intestine and do not become chronic. **B, C, D** are transmitted by blood and sexual routes and may become chronic (topic 29).
s5888 Picornaviruses (*Picornaviridae*)
s5889 **General features:** very small (22–30 nm), **nonenveloped**, with an icosahedral capsid, **single-stranded, positive-sense RNA** viruses. Because they are nonenveloped they are **resistant** to the external environment, acid and bile acids — which is why most are transmitted by the **fecal-oral** route.

s5906 2.1. Poliomyelitis
s5907 **Causative agent:** poliovirus (serotypes 1, 2, 3).
s5908 **Transmission:** **fecal-oral** (the main route), less often airborne; the source is a sick person and a virus excretor.
s5909 **Pathogenesis (stages):**
s5910 **Alimentary stage** — the virus multiplies in the lymphoid tissue of the pharynx and intestine (tonsils, Peyer’s patches);
s5911 **Viremia** — it enters the blood; in most cases the process ends here;
s5912 **Neural stage** — the virus crosses the blood–brain barrier and infects the **motor neurons in the anterior horns of the spinal cord**.
s5913 **Clinical forms:** inapparent (asymptomatic, the most frequent), abortive (general infectious signs), meningeal (serous meningitis) and the **paralytic** form — **asymmetric, flaccid (peripheral) paralysis**, often in the legs; sensation is preserved.

s5915 **Virological method** — infection of a cell culture → **cytopathic effect** → typing by the neutralization reaction; **molecular typing** to distinguish it from the vaccine strain.
s5916 **PCR** — fast and sensitive.
s5917 **Serological** — the dynamics of the titer in paired sera by neutralization or ELISA.

s5929 Rhabdoviruses: rabies (*Rhabdoviridae*, *Lyssavirus*)
s5930 **Structure:** a **bullet-shaped**, enveloped virus; the genome is **single-stranded, negative-sense, nonsegmented RNA**; helical nucleocapsid; the envelope bears **G glycoprotein** spikes (the main protective antigen, receptor binding).
s5931 Figure 28.1
s5932 The structure of the rabies virus: a bullet-shaped virion, supercapsid, G glycoprotein spikes, matrix protein and helical nucleoprotein. Source: Muhamedov I.M. et al., 2019.
s5933 **Epidemiology:** a zoonosis; the source is wild and domestic animals (dog, cat, fox, wolf, bats). Transmission — through **a bite by a sick animal or saliva getting on a mucous membrane**.
s5934 **Pathogenesis:** the virus multiplies at the entry site → moves **along peripheral nerves to the center** → multiplies in the central nervous system (especially the hippocampus and brainstem) and causes encephalitis → then spreads **along nerves outward**, including to the **salivary glands** (which is why saliva is infectious).
s5935 **Incubation period** — usually 1–3 months; **it is shorter if the bite is near the head and deep**.
s5936 **Clinical picture:** a prodromal period (discomfort at the bite site, restlessness) → an excitation period (**hydrophobia** — fear of water and spasm on swallowing, aerophobia, photophobia, excitability) → a paralytic period. Once clinical signs have developed, the disease is considered practically untreatable.
s5937 **Morphological sign:** in the cytoplasm of nerve cells **Babes–Negri bodies** (topic 12) — viral inclusions; found on examining brain tissue after death.
s5938 **In animals:** examination of brain tissue — immunofluorescence (the standard method), detection of Babes–Negri bodies, PCR;
s5939 **In humans (during life):** immunofluorescence on a nuchal skin biopsy, **PCR** on saliva and cerebrospinal fluid, a smear from the cornea; the methods have limited sensitivity, so repeat samples are taken;
s5940 After death — examination of brain tissue.
s5941 **Emergency prophylaxis (postexposure):** **wash the wound immediately with soap and plenty of water** and treat it with an antiseptic; then, as prescribed, the **vaccine** and, when indicated, **specific immunoglobulin** are used. The animal is placed under observation. All measures are determined by a physician on the basis of the current protocol.
s5942 Hepatitis viruses transmitted by the fecal-oral route
s5943 4.1. Hepatitis A virus (HAV)
s5944 **Features:** the family *Picornaviridae* (hepatoviruses); **nonenveloped**, single-stranded positive RNA; **resistant** in the external environment (to acid, bile, drying and the usual disinfection regimes).
s5945 Figure 28.2
s5946 The structure of the hepatitis A virus and the papillomavirus (schematic image). Source: Muhamedov I.M. et al., 2019.
s5947 **Transmission:** **fecal-oral** — contaminated water and food (especially unwashed vegetables and fruit, seafood), contact-household. Viral shedding is **highest before jaundice appears** — so the patient is most infectious until jaundice begins.
s5948 **Clinical picture:** the incubation period is 2–6 weeks; a pre-icteric period (fever, loss of appetite, nausea), an icteric period, recovery. In children it often runs **asymptomatically**. It **does not become chronic**, carriage does not develop; acute liver failure is rare.
s5949 **Diagnosis:** **anti-HAV IgM** — a sign of acute infection (the main marker); **anti-HAV IgG** — past infection or immunity after vaccination; PCR (stool, blood) — in epidemiological investigation.
s5950 **Prophylaxis:** sanitary and hygienic measures (water supply, food control, hand hygiene), the **inactivated vaccine**; immunoglobulin for contacts on indication.
s5951 4.2. Hepatitis E virus (HEV)
s5952 **Features:** *Hepeviridae*; nonenveloped, single-stranded positive RNA; transmitted by the fecal-oral route, mainly **through water**; some genotypes are zoonotic (pigs and others, via undercooked meat).
s5953 **Clinical picture:** resembles hepatitis A; usually does not become chronic (an exception may occur in the severely immunocompromised). **The main feature is a severe course in pregnant women** and a high risk of acute liver failure.
s5954 **Diagnosis:** anti-HEV IgM and IgG, PCR (blood, stool).
