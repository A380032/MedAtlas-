s2547 **Enveloped (supercapsid)** viruses are unstable in the external environment: they are sensitive to lipid solvents, alcohol and soap. **Non-enveloped** viruses are much more resistant and adapted to transmission by the fecal-oral route (enteroviruses, hepatitis A, rotavirus).
s2548 Remember the stages in order: **A–P–U–S–A–R**: **A**dsorption → **P**enetration → **U**ncoating → **S**ynthesis → **A**ssembly → **R**elease.
s2549 The basis of modern virological diagnosis is **PCR** (and real-time PCR): it is fast and sensitive, detects the virus without culturing it and quantitatively assesses the viral load. The classic virological method (isolation) has remained mainly for scientific purposes and for selecting vaccine strains.
s2550 In virology, **the time of collecting the material** is decisive: virus shedding is often highest in the first days of the illness. In material taken late, even PCR may be negative, so it is supplemented with serology.
s2551 The structure and chemical composition of viruses
s2552 **Viruses** are acellular forms of life; they are **obligate intracellular parasites**. Their main distinguishing features:
s2553 they contain only **one** type of nucleic acid — **DNA or RNA** (not both together);
s2554 they have no protein-synthesizing system (ribosomes) or energy-producing system of their own — they use those of the host cell;
s2555 they multiply not by binary fission but by the **disjunctive (separated)** method: the components are synthesized separately and then assembled;
s2556 they pass through bacterial filters;
s2557 they are not susceptible to antibiotics.
s2558 **Virion** is the mature, extracellular virus particle. Structure:
s2559 **Nucleoid (genome)** — DNA or RNA; single-stranded or double-stranded, linear or circular, whole or segmented.
s2560 **Capsid** — the protein shell, built of **capsomers**; it protects the genome and takes part in attachment to the cell and in antigenic properties.
s2561 **Supercapsid (outer envelope)** — present in some viruses; it is formed from the host cell membrane and equipped with viral glycoproteins (spikes).
s2562 **Types of symmetry:** **helical** (the nucleocapsid is coiled in a spiral — influenza, rabies), **cubic (icosahedral)** (adenovirus, poliovirus, herpes), **complex (mixed)** (smallpox virus, most bacteriophages).

s2567 **Principles of classification:** type of nucleic acid (DNA/RNA), strandedness (single/double), type of symmetry, presence of an envelope, virion size, site of replication (nucleus or cytoplasm), antigenic properties.
s2568 The interaction of a virus with the cell
s2569 **Three kinds of outcome:**
s2570 **Productive (lytic)** — the full cycle is completed, new virions form, and the cell often dies.
s2571 **Abortive** — the cycle stalls (the cell is unsuitable or the virus is defective); no new virions form.
s2572 **Integrative (virogeny)** — the viral genome integrates into the cell chromosome and replicates with it (retroviruses, hepatitis B, some oncoviruses).
s2573 Stages of the productive cycle
s2574 **Adsorption** — binding of the viral spikes to **specific receptors** on the cell surface. This very stage determines the tissue and species specificity (tropism) of the virus.
s2575 **Penetration (entry)** — endocytosis through the receptor, or fusion of the envelope with the cell membrane.
s2576 **Uncoating (“undressing”)** — the genome is freed from the envelope and capsid and exposed.
s2577 **Eclipse (hidden) period and synthesis** — the viral genome and proteins are synthesized in the host cell’s systems; during this time no whole virion can be found in the cell.
s2578 **Assembly (morphogenesis)** — the nucleocapsid is assembled from the components.
s2579 **Release** — by cell lysis (non-enveloped viruses) or by **budding** (enveloped viruses take their envelope from the membrane).

s2627 Independent work: Uses of bacteriophages in practice
s2628 **1. Identification and epidemiological typing (phage typing).** A set of type-specific phages is applied to the isolated culture; from which phages produce lysis, the **phagovar** (phage type) is determined. This helps to distinguish strains within the same species and to find the source of infection (for example, in identifying the focus of a hospital staphylococcal infection).
s2629 **2. Phage diagnosis.** Isolating **the phage itself** from the material being examined also indirectly shows the presence of the pathogen (for example, detection of coliphages in water).
s2630 **3. Phage therapy.** Preparations of live virulent phages are used to treat infections (staphylococcal, streptococcal, dysentery, typhoid fever, Proteus, *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* phages, polyvalent pyobacteriophage). Advantages: narrow and precise action (does not disturb the normal microflora), and it may also act on antibiotic-resistant strains. Limitations: narrow spectrum (the phage must match the strain), development of phage resistance in bacteria, problems of standardization. The order of prescribing and using the preparation is determined by the physician.
s2631 **4. Phage prophylaxis.** Used on epidemic indications (for contacts in a focus).
s2632 **5. Genetic engineering and research.** Phages are a means of transferring genetic material (transduction), vectors, and also a classic model of molecular biology.
s2633 The main difference between phage therapy and an antibiotic: an antibiotic is a **chemical substance with broad action**; a phage is a **live, highly selective** biological agent that lyses only the bacteria it matches.
s2634 Practical skill: the general algorithm of a virological examination
s2635 **Collecting material** — according to the stage of the disease and the tropism of the virus: nasopharyngeal wash/swab for respiratory viruses, stool for enteroviruses, vesicle fluid for herpes, cerebrospinal fluid for neuroinfections, blood serum for serology.
s2636 **Transport** — on ice (2–8 °C), in a special transport medium, fast; many viruses are unstable in the external environment.
s2637 **Rapid diagnosis** — detecting **viral antigen** (ELISA, immunofluorescence, immunochromatography) or **nucleic acid** (PCR) in the material.
s2638 **Virological method** — inoculation into cell culture/chick embryo → indication (CPE, HA, hemadsorption) → identification with specific sera (HI, NT).
s2639 **Serological method** — a ≥4-fold rise in antibody titer in **paired sera**; IgM — recent infection, IgG — past infection or a sign of immunity.
s2640 **Interpreting the result** — together with clinical and epidemiological data.
