s4548 The causative agent of cholera — *Vibrio cholerae*
s4549 **Morphology:** gram-negative, **comma-shaped** curved rod (a vibrio); **monotrichous** — highly motile thanks to a single polar flagellum (directed movement like a “shoal of fish”); nonspore-forming, noncapsulated.
s4550 Figure 21.3
s4551 Cholera vibrios — comma-shaped motile gram-negative bacteria. Source: Muhamedov I.M. et al., 2019.
s4552 **Cultural and biochemical properties:**
s4553 **alkaliphilic (alkali-loving)** — grows well at pH 8.5–9.0; therefore **alkaline peptone water** is used as the enrichment medium: the vibrio forms a **thin pellicle** on the surface and outgrows the accompanying flora;
s4554 the selective solid medium is **TCBS agar** (yellow colonies) or alkaline agar (transparent, bluish colonies);
s4555 **oxidase positive** (unlike enterobacteria!), ferments sucrose and mannose, does not ferment arabinose.
s4556 **Serological groups:** O1 (classical and **El Tor** biovars; Ogawa and Inaba serovars) and **O139** — of epidemic significance. Other (non-O1) vibrios usually cause mild gastroenteritis.
s4557 **The main pathogenicity factor — cholera toxin (choleragen):** it consists of A and B subunits; the B subunit binds to a receptor on the intestinal epithelium, and the A subunit enters the cell and **permanently activates adenylate cyclase** → **cAMP** rises sharply → **water and electrolytes** (Na⁺, K⁺, Cl⁻, HCO₃⁻) leave the cell into the intestinal lumen.
s4558 **Pathogenesis:** the vibrio **does not enter the epithelium**, stays in the intestinal lumen and produces toxin. Therefore:
s4559 the stool looks like **“rice water”** — watery, odorless, with whitish flecks; no blood or mucus;
s4560 inflammation and high fever are usually absent;
s4561 the main danger is **severe dehydration**, loss of electrolytes, metabolic acidosis and hypovolemic shock.
s4562 **Laboratory diagnosis (rapid and staged):**
s4563 **Material:** stool, vomit; in fatal cases — a piece of intestine; water and environmental samples (in epidemiological investigation). The requirements of the **especially dangerous infection** regime are observed.
s4564 **Rapid methods:** seeing **motility** in a wet mount (dark-field/phase-contrast) and the **cessation of motility** with specific serum; immunofluorescence, latex agglutination, PCR.
s4565 **Enrichment:** **alkaline peptone water**, 37 °C, 6–8 hours → subculture from the pellicle on the surface.
s4566 **Inoculation:** TCBS or alkaline agar.
s4567 **Identification:** oxidase, biochemical properties, agglutination with O1/O139 sera; additional tests to determine the biovar (classical/El Tor) (phage susceptibility, hemagglutination, polymyxin susceptibility).
s4568 Cholera is an **especially dangerous (quarantine) infection**: when it is suspected, the material is collected and transported in a special manner, and the health system is notified immediately. Laboratory work is carried out under the prescribed biosafety regime.
