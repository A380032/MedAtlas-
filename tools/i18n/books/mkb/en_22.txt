s4899 **Listeriosis** can be linked to products stored in the refrigerator, because the causative agent multiplies at low temperature. It is dangerous for pregnant women, newborns and the immunocompromised; in diagnosis, blood and cerebrospinal fluid culture is decisive.

s4915 Salmonelloses
s4916 **Causative agents:** the many serovars of *Salmonella enterica*, the most frequent being **Enteritidis** and **Typhimurium**. (The typhoid–paratyphoid group was covered in topic 22.)
s4917 **Properties:** gram-negative, peritrichous (motile) rods; **lactose-negative**, they ferment glucose with production of **acid and gas**, **H₂S positive** (the Kligler butt blackens), urease negative, indole negative.
s4918 **Epidemiology:** the main source is **animals and poultry**; transmission factors are undercooked eggs and egg dishes, poultry and beef, dairy products; in addition, the contact-household route (especially in children) and contact with reptiles.
s4919 **Pathogenicity factors:** adhesins, **invasion factors** (entry into the intestinal epithelium), enterotoxin and cytotoxin, **endotoxin (LPS)**; virulence plasmids in *S.* Typhimurium and others.
s4920 **Clinical forms:**
s4921 **Gastrointestinal (common)** — fever, abdominal pain, vomiting, watery or mucous diarrhea;
s4922 **Generalized** — typhoid-like and septic forms; in newborns, the elderly and the immunocompromised;
s4923 **Bacterial carriage** — acute or chronic.
s4924 **Material:** stool, vomit, gastric lavage, remains of suspect food; in a generalized form — **blood** (hemoculture), urine.
s4925 **Enrichment:** selenite or magnesium medium (for stool).
s4926 **Inoculation:** Endo, Ploskirev, **bismuth sulfite agar** (black colonies).
s4927 **Identification:** Kligler (slant red, butt yellow, **gas +, H₂S +**), the biochemical series, motility.
s4928 **Serological identification:** agglutination with O and H sera according to the **Kauffmann–White scheme** — first polyvalent, then group-specific and type-specific sera.
s4929 **Additional:** antibiogram, PCR; in epidemiological investigation — examination of food and environmental samples.
s4930 **Causative agent:** *Clostridium botulinum* — a gram-positive, **obligate anaerobe**, **subterminal spore**-forming rod (“tennis racket” shape); motile.

s4933 **Botulinum toxin:** one of the most potent biological poisons in nature; **protein in nature**, **heat-labile** (broken down on boiling), but resistant to gastric acid and proteases. Serological types A, B, E, F (more common in humans) and others.
s4934 **Mechanism of action:** the toxin binds to peripheral cholinergic nerve endings and **stops the release of acetylcholine** → neuromuscular transmission is impaired → **flaccid (peripheral) paralysis** develops.
s4935 Compare the two clostridial neurotoxins: **tetanus toxin — blocks inhibitory mediators → spastic (tonic) convulsions**; **botulinum toxin — blocks acetylcholine → flaccid paralysis**. In both, consciousness is preserved.
s4936 **Routes of transmission and forms:**
s4937 **Foodborne botulism** — toxin accumulates in products in which anaerobic conditions have been created, such as home-canned sealed preserves, smoked fish and sausage;
s4938 **Wound botulism** — multiplication of the pathogen in a wound;
s4939 **Infant botulism** — germination of spores in the intestine in children under 1 year (therefore **honey is not recommended before age 1**).
s4940 **Clinical picture:** the incubation period is usually 12–36 hours (from 2 hours to several days, depending on the dose of toxin). At first nausea and abdominal discomfort may occur, then the typical **neurological signs**:
s4941 **visual disturbance** — “a net before the eyes,” double vision (diplopia), drooping of the eyelid (ptosis), dilated pupil;
s4942 **swallowing and speech disturbance** — dysphagia, dysphonia, dry mouth;
s4943 **symmetric, descending flaccid paralysis**; in severe cases — **paralysis of the respiratory muscles** (the main cause of death).
s4944 **Fever is usually absent and consciousness is preserved** — this distinguishes botulism from many infections.
s4945 **Material:** blood serum, vomit, gastric lavage, stool, **remains of suspect food**.
s4946 **The main method — detecting the toxin:** the classic variant is the **biological test (neutralization test)**: the material is mixed with type-specific antitoxic sera and injected into a laboratory animal; the serum that protects shows the type to which the toxin belongs. Modern variants — ELISA and other in vitro methods.
s4947 **Isolating the pathogen** — anaerobic culture (Kitt–Tarozzi and others); detecting toxin genes by PCR.
s4948 **In practice diagnosis rests on clinical and epidemiological data** — waiting for the laboratory answer must not delay treatment.
s4949 The decisive treatment in botulism is giving **antitoxic serum** as **early** as possible: the serum neutralizes only toxin **in the blood that is not bound to nerve**. The effect of bound toxin cannot be reversed — so no time is lost. In severe cases breathing is supported. All prescriptions are made by a physician.
s4950 **Prophylaxis:** following the technology of home canning, not using suspect preserves (with a bulging lid or a changed smell), heating the product sufficiently before consumption (the toxin is heat-labile), not giving honey to children under 1 year.

s5093 The causative agents of anthrax, plague and tularemia belong to the group of **especially dangerous infections**. When they are suspected, the material is collected in a special manner, transported in a prescribed container, and processed **in specially authorized laboratories** under a high biosafety regime; the health system is notified immediately. Such cultures are not handled in an ordinary diagnostic laboratory.
s5094 Anthrax toxin is **three-component**: PA is the carrier and “door-opener,” while LF and EF are the effector parts. The protective antigen (PA) is the main component of vaccines.
s5095 The **Ascoli reaction** is the classic way of making a diagnosis in material in which the causative agent cannot be grown or is dead (dried skin, wool, old meat): the antigen persists because it is heat-stable.
s5096 To remember brucellosis: **“milk + undulant fever + sweating + joint pain”**; in serology — **Wright** (tube, titer) and **Huddleson** (slide, rapid).
s5097 General concept of zoonoses
s5098 **Zoonoses** are diseases whose source of infection is **animals**. A human is often the “final host,” and person-to-person transmission is limited or absent.
s5099 **Routes of transmission:** direct contact (a sick animal, its skin and wool products), alimentary (meat, milk), airborne (dust, aerosol), vector-borne (arthropods).
