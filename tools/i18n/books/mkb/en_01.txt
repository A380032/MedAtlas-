s0018 The science of microbiology: subject, branches and tasks
s0019 **Microbiology** (Gr. *mikros* — small, *bios* — life, *logos* — study) is the science that studies the structure, vital activity, heredity and ecology of tiny living organisms that cannot be seen with the naked eye, and their relationship with humans, animals, plants and the environment. The world of microorganisms includes **bacteria, archaea, fungi, protozoa** (eukaryotes) and the acellular forms — **viruses** and **prions**.
s0020 **Medical microbiology** studies the microorganisms that cause disease in humans (pathogenic and opportunistic) and the diagnosis, treatment and prevention of the infectious diseases they cause. It is divided into the following branches:
s0021 **bacteriology** — studies bacteria;
s0022 **virology** — viruses;
s0023 **mycology** — fungi;
s0024 **protozoology** — disease-causing protozoa;
s0025 **immunology** — the body’s defense system.

s0037 History of microbiology and the contribution of Uzbek scientists
s0038 Several stages are distinguished in the development of microbiology:
s0039 **Empirical (heuristic) period** — before the discovery of microbes, speculation about the “invisible causes” of diseases (Hippocrates; Avicenna (Ibn Sina) wrote in *“The Canon of Medicine”* about tiny particles that spread disease).
s0040 **Morphological period** — Antonie van **Leeuwenhoek** (1676) was the first to see “animalcules” (bacteria) in a microscope he built himself.
s0041 **Physiological period** — Louis **Pasteur** proved that fermentation and putrefaction are the work of microbes and laid the foundations of sterilization and vaccination; Robert **Koch** developed methods of isolating pure cultures, solid culture media, immersion microscopy and “Koch’s postulates.”
s0042 **Immunological period** — I.I. **Mechnikov** created the theory of phagocytosis and P. **Ehrlich** created the theory of humoral immunity.
s0043 **Molecular-genetic period** — the discovery of antibiotics (A. Fleming) and the development of virology, genetics and biotechnology.

s0066 The microbiological laboratory: structure, equipment and safety
s0067 Because a microbiological laboratory works with infectious materials, it has a special regime. It usually consists of the following rooms: specimen reception, preparation (staining, media preparation), inoculation (transfer boxes), incubator room, autoclave (sterilization) and washing room.
s0068 **Main equipment:**
s0069 **immersion microscope** — for examining stained smears;
s0070 **incubator** — for growing inoculated media at 37 °C;
s0071 **autoclave** — sterilization under steam pressure (121 °C, 1 atm of excess pressure);
s0072 **dry-heat sterilizer (oven)** — for sterilizing glassware at 160–180 °C;
s0073 **bactericidal lamps (UV)**, **laminar-flow box** — for disinfecting the air and the work area;
s0074 **centrifuge, refrigerator, pH meter, bacteriological loop and Petri dishes.**
s0075 **Working rules (in brief):** work wearing a lab coat and gloves; treat all inoculated material as potentially infectious; sterilize the loop each time by flaming; after work, disinfect the work area and wash the hands; discard used material only after decontaminating it in an autoclave; eating and touching the face are forbidden in the laboratory.
s0076 Microscopy methods
s0077 Because bacteria are very small (0.5–10 µm), they can be seen only under a microscope. The resolving power of the light microscope is ≈ 0.2 µm; the maximum useful magnification is ≈ 1000× (10× eyepiece × 100× immersion objective).

s0104 Morphology of bacteria
s0105 Bacteria differ in shape, size and arrangement after division. These features are important in the preliminary identification of the causative agent.

s0107 Main forms of spherical bacteria: 1-micrococci; 2, 3, 4-diplococci; 5-tetracocci; 6-sarcinae; 7-staphylococci; 8-streptococci (Aliyev Sh.R. et al., 2013)
s0108 Figure 1.6
s0109 Main forms of rod-shaped and curved bacteria: 1-E. coli; 2-diplobacteria; 3-streptobacteria; 4-streptobacilli; 5, 6-clostridia; 7-causative agent of diphtheria; 8-diphtheroids; 9-causative agent of plague; 10-coccobacilli; 11-fusobacteria; 12-mycobacterium; 13-vibrios; 14-spirilla; 15-treponemes (Aliyev Sh.R. et al., 2013)
s0110 **Cocci** (spherical): depending on the plane of division and whether the cells separate afterward — micrococcus (single), **diplococcus** (pair: pneumococcus, gonococcus, meningococcus), **streptococcus** (chain), **staphylococcus** (grape-like cluster), tetracoccus (group of four), **sarcina** (packet of 8–16 cells).
s0111 **Rod-shaped**: single (monobacterium), paired (diplobacterium), chain (streptobacterium). The ends may be blunt, pointed or thickened.
s0112 **Curved**: **vibrio** (comma-shaped — causative agent of cholera), **spirillum** (1–3 large coils), **spirochete** (many fine coils — syphilis, leptospirosis).

s0115 Practical skill: examining a smear under immersion microscopy
s0116 Algorithm:
s0117 Point the microscope toward the light source, raise the condenser to its upper position and open the diaphragm.
s0118 Secure the stained and dried smear on the stage.
s0119 Place **one drop of immersion oil** on the smear.
s0120 Rotate the nosepiece so that the **immersion objective (90–100×)** touches the oil (the objective is immersed in the oil).
s0121 Looking from the side, carefully bring the objective close to the smear with the coarse focus knob, then looking through the eyepiece bring the image into sharp focus with the **fine focus knob**.
s0122 Move the field of view and assess the shape, arrangement and staining of the cells.
s0123 When finished, clean the objective with a soft tissue.
s0124 When working with the immersion objective, use only the **fine focus knob** — if you turn the coarse focus knob sharply, the objective will break the slide and damage the lens.

s0204 Case 1
s0205 A smear taken from a purulent wound was stained by the Gram method; under the microscope, spherical cells arranged in grape-like clusters and stained dark purple were found.
s0206 What morphological group and what probable causative agent can be considered?
s0207 Spherical (coccus), staphylococcal arrangement, gram-positive cells — probably *Staphylococcus aureus*. A frequent causative agent of purulent infections.
s0208 Case 2
s0209 A patient is suspected of having syphilis (lues). Fluid taken from the base of an ulcerative lesion must be examined alive, without staining.
s0210 Which microscopy method is used and why?
s0211 Dark-field microscopy. *Treponema pallidum* is a delicate, motile spirochete that takes ordinary dyes poorly; against a dark background it is seen alive as a bright spiral.
s0212 Case 3
s0213 While working with the immersion objective, a student is looking for the image by sharply turning the coarse focus knob without looking through the eyepiece. As a result, the slide broke.
s0214 Which rule was violated?
s0215 When working with the immersion objective, bring the objective close carefully while watching from the side, and perform fine focusing only with the fine focus knob. Sharp turning of the coarse focus knob damages the lens and the specimen.
