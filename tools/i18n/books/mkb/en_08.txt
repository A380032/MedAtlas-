s1672 Practical skill: microbiological examination of water
s1673 7.1. Determining the total microbial count (pour plate method)
s1674 The water sample is collected in a sterile container and examined within 2 hours (or within 6 hours if kept cooled).
s1675 1 mL of water is placed in a sterile Petri dish.
s1676 10–12 mL of melted meat-peptone agar cooled to 45 °C is poured over it and mixed by rotating the dish.
s1677 After the agar sets, the dish is inverted and incubated at 37 °C for 24 hours.
s1678 The colonies that have grown are counted — the result is expressed in **CFU/mL** (colony-forming units).
s1679 7.2. Membrane filter method (determining coliforms)
s1680 A certain volume of water (usually 100–333 mL) is passed through a **membrane filter** — the bacteria are retained on the surface of the filter.
s1681 The filter is removed with sterile forceps and placed on the surface of **Endo medium**.
s1682 It is incubated at 37 °C for 24 hours.
s1683 The **red colonies with a metallic sheen** that have grown on the filter are counted as lactose-positive coliforms; they are confirmed with a smear and an oxidase test.
s1684 The coli index is calculated from the result.

s1687 7.3. Titration (fermentation) method
s1688 Different volumes of water are inoculated into lactose medium; from the tubes with gas formation, subcultures are made onto Endo medium, and the coli index is calculated from the confirmed results using a special table (most probable number).

s1772 What does the result mean and what measures are taken?
s1773 The water is contaminated with fresh feces; intestinal pathogens are likely to be present. Use of the water is stopped, the source of contamination is identified, the well is cleaned and disinfected, and use is permitted after repeat testing.
s1774 An operating-room air sample was examined: the total microbial count was above the norm and hemolytic streptococcus was found.

s1800 **Low temperature does not kill, it preserves.** That is why freezing is not a method of disinfection; on the contrary, it is used to store cultures and samples.
s1801 **95% alcohol disinfects less effectively than 70% alcohol**: high-concentration alcohol immediately coagulates the protein on the cell surface, forms a crust and cannot penetrate inside.
s1802 Remember the targets of protein synthesis inhibitors: **“30 — AT, 50 — CLM”**: **A**minoglycoside and **T**etracycline — **30S**; **C**hloramphenicol, **L**incosamide, **M**acrolide — **50S**.
s1803 The selective action (selective toxicity) of an antibiotic is based on the difference between the bacterial and the human cell: humans have no peptidoglycan wall (which is why beta-lactams are relatively safe) and the ribosome is 80S (not 70S). For this reason, the groups acting on the wall are usually considered the least toxic.

s1856 Table 9.2. Main sterilization methods
s1857 **Asepsis** is a system of measures to **prevent** microbes from entering a wound, tissue or an object that must be sterile (sterilized instruments, gloves, mask, laminar flow cabinet, preparation of the operative field).
s1858 **Antisepsis** is the elimination or reduction of microbes **already present** in a wound or living tissue (treatment of skin and mucous membranes).
s1859 **Groups of disinfectant and antiseptic substances:** halogens (chlorine and iodine preparations), alcohols (70% ethanol is the most effective), oxidizing agents (hydrogen peroxide, potassium permanganate), aldehydes (formaldehyde, glutaraldehyde), phenols, salts of heavy metals, surfactants (chlorhexidine, quaternary ammonium compounds), dyes (brilliant green).
s1860 Antibiotics
s1861 **Antibiotics** are substances produced by microorganisms, plants or animals, or obtained synthetically or semisynthetically, that stop the growth of other microorganisms or kill them.
s1862 **Classification:**
s1863 **By origin:** natural (penicillin, streptomycin), semisynthetic (ampicillin, amoxicillin), synthetic (sulfonamides, fluoroquinolones).
s1864 **By the nature of action:** **bactericidal** (kill — beta-lactams, aminoglycosides, fluoroquinolones, vancomycin) and **bacteriostatic** (stop multiplication — tetracyclines, macrolides, chloramphenicol (levomycetin), sulfonamides).
s1865 **By spectrum of action:** narrow-spectrum (mainly Gram-positive or mainly Gram-negative) and broad-spectrum.

s1887 Determining susceptibility to antibiotics
s1888 5.1. Disk diffusion method (Kirby–Bauer)
s1889 A suspension of standard turbidity (0.5 McFarland) is prepared from a pure culture.
s1890 The suspension is spread evenly (in three directions) over the surface of **Mueller–Hinton agar** with a swab.
s1891 **Disks** impregnated with antibiotics are placed with forceps at specified distances.
s1892 It is incubated at 35–37 °C for 16–18 hours.
s1893 The **diameter of the zone of growth inhibition** around the disk is measured with a ruler and interpreted using a standard table: **S — susceptible**, **I — intermediate (susceptible at an increased dose)**, **R — resistant**.
s1894 Figure 9.1
s1895 Determining susceptibility to antibiotics: zones of growth inhibition around the disks in the disk diffusion method and reading of the result. Source: Muhamedov I.M. et al., 2019.
s1896 The diameter of the zone by itself does not mean “good or bad”: every antibiotic and every species has its **own cutoff value**. That is why the result is interpreted only according to the current standard table.
s1897 5.2. Dilution method — MIC and MBC
s1898 **MIC (minimum inhibitory concentration)** is the lowest concentration of an antibiotic that stops visible growth. **MBC (minimum bactericidal concentration)** is the lowest concentration that kills a specified proportion of the microbes. MIC is determined by serial dilution in liquid or solid medium; in practice the E-test (gradient strip) is also used.
s1899 **Clinical significance:** MIC helps in choosing the drug dose and route of administration; in severe infections (sepsis, endocarditis, meningitis) a quantitative MIC value is preferable to a qualitative “S/I/R” answer.
s1900 Bacteriocins
s1901 **Bacteriocins** are protein substances produced by bacteria that act against **closely related species**. They are named after the producing species: colicins (*E. coli*), staphylococcins, pyocins. They are encoded on plasmids.
s1902 **Significance:** a factor of competition (antagonism) between microbial populations; **bacteriocin typing** is a method of distinguishing strains in epidemiological investigation; a natural preservative in the food industry (nisin); being studied as new antibacterial agents.
s1903 Independent work: Mechanisms of resistance to antibiotics
s1904 **Types of resistance:**
s1905 **Natural (innate)** — arises from the structure of the species and is present in all its representatives (mycoplasmas — to beta-lactams; the outer membrane of gram-negative bacteria — to many drugs).
s1906 **Acquired** — arises as a result of mutation or gene transfer; this is the type that causes clinical problems.
s1907 **Genetic basis of acquired resistance:** chromosomal mutations and **horizontal gene transfer** — conjugation (R plasmids), transduction, transformation, transposons and integrons (topic 11).

s1925 **Factors that lead to the spread of resistance:** unjustified prescription of antibiotics (in viral infections), not completing the course of treatment, wrong choice of dose, mass use in livestock farming, spread of resistant strains inside hospitals.
s1926 **Prevention:** prescribing antibiotics **based on an antibiogram**, completing the course, infection control and hand hygiene, antibiotic stewardship programs, monitoring of resistance.
s1927 **Bacteriological assessment of sterilization and disinfection.** The quality of sterilization is monitored with **biological indicators** (an ampoule or strip containing spores of a resistant test microorganism): after the cycle is finished, the indicator is cultured and there must be no growth. Physical (temperature, pressure, time record) and chemical (indicator strips) monitoring are also used in addition. The quality of disinfection is assessed by culturing wash samples taken from treated surfaces — no sanitary indicator microbes should be found.
