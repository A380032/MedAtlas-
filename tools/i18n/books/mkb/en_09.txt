s1928 Practical skill: setting up and reading the disk diffusion method
s1929 From a 24-hour pure culture, prepare a suspension in sterile saline and adjust its turbidity to the **0.5 McFarland** standard.
s1930 Dip a sterile swab into the suspension and press out the excess liquid against the wall of the tube.
s1931 Spread the swab evenly over the surface of **Mueller–Hinton agar** in **three directions** (rotating the dish 60° each time); also cover the edges.
s1932 Wait 3–5 minutes until the surface is dry.
s1933 Place the antibiotic disks with sterile forceps at least 24 mm apart and press them lightly so that they stick to the surface.
s1934 Invert the dish and incubate at 35–37 °C for 16–18 hours.
s1935 Measure the diameter of the zone of growth inhibition from the bottom of the dish with a ruler in **mm**; take the whole diameter of the zone.
s1936 Compare the result with the current standard table, mark it as **S / I / R** and write it in the report.
s1937 If the suspension is denser than the standard, the zone becomes smaller (false resistance); if it is thinner, the zone becomes larger (false susceptibility). That is why adjusting the turbidity is the main condition for a reliable result.

s2020 A patient has a purulent infection; the *S. aureus* isolated was found to be oxacillin-resistant in the antibiogram.
s2021 What does this result mean and what is important in treatment?
s2022 A methicillin (oxacillin)-resistant staphylococcus — MRSA. The resistance is linked to an altered penicillin-binding protein, so **all beta-lactams** are considered ineffective. Treatment is carried out according to the antibiogram with drugs of another group; the patient is placed under infection control measures.
s2023 In a laboratory, after the autoclave cycle was finished, the color of the chemical indicator changed, but growth was detected when the biological indicator was cultured.
s2024 What is the conclusion?
s2025 Sterilization was in fact not achieved. The chemical indicator only shows that the temperature reached the set value; the exposure time or the penetration of steam into the material may have been insufficient. The autoclave is sent for technical inspection and the material is re-sterilized.
s2026 For an acute respiratory viral infection, a doctor prescribed a broad-spectrum antibiotic to a patient; when the patient felt better, he stopped the drug on day 3.
s2027 What two mistakes are there here, and what is the consequence?
s2028 First, an antibiotic in a viral infection is unjustified (it does not act on viruses). Second, the course was not completed. Both mistakes lead to the selection of resistant strains and to disruption of the normal microflora (dysbacteriosis).

s2056 If a microorganism is isolated from a site that should normally be **sterile** (blood, cerebrospinal fluid), this is almost always a sign of **pathology** — even if the isolated species is “a member of the normal microflora.”
s2057 In the large intestine **anaerobes predominate**: **BI-BA** — **Bi**fidobacteria and **Ba**cteroides. Although *E. coli* is mentioned often, it makes up **less than 1%** of the total mass.
s2058 When colonization resistance is disrupted (especially after a broad-spectrum antibiotic), the vacated place is taken by resistant or opportunistic species. Antibiotic-associated diarrhea and pseudomembranous colitis caused by *Clostridioides difficile* are the clinical expression of this condition.
s2059 A stool culture reflects only part of the **luminal** microflora of the intestine, and not all anaerobes can be cultured. That is why the result of a “dysbacteriosis analysis” is interpreted together with the clinical picture; prescribing treatment on the basis of the analysis alone is not correct.
s2060 General concept of the normal microflora
s2061 **The normal microflora (microbiota)** is the totality of microorganisms that permanently inhabit the organs and tissues of a healthy body that are in contact with the external environment. The number of microbial cells in the human body is comparable to the number of body cells; most of them are located in the large intestine.
s2062 **Two parts:**
s2063 **Resident (permanent, obligate) microflora** — of stable composition, characteristic of the organ (bifidobacteria, lactobacilli, bacteroides, *E. coli*);
s2064 **Transient microflora** — species that come from the external environment and do not persist for long; among them there are also opportunistic pathogens.
s2065 **Sterile sites (microbe-free in the norm):** blood, cerebrospinal fluid, internal organs, synovial fluid, the bladder and the upper parts of the urinary tract, the uterine cavity, the alveolar part of the lung.

s2099 Functions of the normal microflora
s2100 **Colonization resistance (protection)** — occupies the space and the nutrients, preventing pathogens from settling; produces bacteriocins, organic acids and hydrogen peroxide.
s2101 **Shaping immunity** — stimulates the gut-associated lymphoid tissue (GALT), supports the production of secretory IgA, provides “training” of the immune system.
s2102 **Metabolic and digestive function** — ferments undigested carbohydrates, forms short-chain fatty acids (an energy source for the intestinal epithelium), takes part in the metabolism of bile acids and cholesterol.
s2103 **Vitamin synthesis** — vitamin K, B vitamins (B₁₂, folate, biotin).
s2104 **Detoxification** — breaks down certain toxic substances and speeds up their excretion.
s2105 **Morphokinetic effect** — influences the normal development of the intestinal mucosa and peristalsis.
s2106 Dysbacteriosis (dysbiosis)
s2107 **Dysbacteriosis** is a persistent disturbance in the qualitative and quantitative composition of the normal microflora: the resident representatives decrease, and opportunistic pathogens and unusual species increase. It is not an independent nosological entity but a **syndrome** — the cause must always be sought.
s2108 **Causes:** antibiotics and other drugs (chemotherapy, immunosuppressants), acute intestinal infections, chronic gastrointestinal diseases, improper nutrition, operations, immunodeficiency, stress and irradiation; in a newborn — disruption of the breastfeeding regimen.

s2125 **Laboratory diagnosis:**
s2126 **Classic bacteriological method** — serial dilution of stool (from 10⁻¹ to 10⁻⁹) and culture on selective and differential media; for each group (bifidobacteria, lactobacilli, *E. coli* and its variants, enterococci, staphylococci, Proteus, Klebsiella, fungi) the **CFU/g** is calculated and compared with the norm.
s2127 **Gas-liquid chromatography** — assessing the state of the anaerobic microbiota from the spectrum of short-chain fatty acids in stool.
s2128 **Molecular methods** — PCR, sequencing of the 16S rRNA gene; they also cover species that cannot be cultured and show the composition of the microbiota more fully.
s2129 **Additional** — examination of biopsy material from the intestinal mucosa (mucosa-associated microflora).
s2130 Independent work: Probiotics, prebiotics and synbiotics
s2131 **Probiotics** are **live microorganisms** that, when taken in adequate amounts, have a beneficial effect on health. By composition:
s2132 **monocomponent** — a single strain (bifidumbacterin, lactobacterin, colibacterin);
s2133 **polycomponent (symbiotic)** — several strains (Bifiform and similar preparations);
s2134 **self-eliminating antagonists** — species that do not multiply in the intestine but displace pathogens (preparations based on *Bacillus*, *Saccharomyces boulardii*);
s2135 **sorbed** — strains immobilized on a sorbent.
s2136 **Mechanism of action:** restoring colonization resistance, producing organic acids and bacteriocins, strengthening the barrier function of the epithelium, stimulating local immunity (secretory IgA).
s2137 **Prebiotics** are substances that are not digestible but stimulate the growth of the beneficial microflora (lactulose, inulin, oligofructose).
s2138 **Synbiotics** are combined preparations of a probiotic and a prebiotic.
s2139 **Use:** prevention of antibiotic-associated diarrhea, recovery after acute intestinal infections, supporting the formation of the microbiota in newborns.
s2140 The effect of a probiotic is **strain-specific**: a benefit proven for one strain is not automatically transferred to the whole species or to another preparation. In patients with severely reduced immunity, preparations of live microorganisms are used with caution.
s2141 Practical skill: algorithm for examining stool for dysbacteriosis
s2142 Stool is collected into a **sterile container**, before antibiotics are taken (or 2–3 days after stopping them); the sample is delivered to the laboratory within 2 hours.
s2143 1 g of stool is mixed in 9 mL of sterile solution — a 10⁻¹ dilution; serial dilutions are made in the same way up to 10⁻⁹.
s2144 From each dilution, the appropriate media are inoculated: Blaurock (bifidobacteria), MRS (lactobacilli), Endo/Levine (E. coli and enterobacteria), egg-yolk salt agar (staphylococci), enterococcus agar, Sabouraud (fungi), blood agar (hemolytic variants).
s2145 For anaerobes, culture is done under anaerobic conditions for 48–72 hours; aerobes for 24–48 hours, at 37 °C.
s2146 The colonies are counted and multiplied by the dilution factor — the result is given in **CFU/g**.
s2147 Identification is done (Gram stain, biochemical tests); hemolytic and lactose-negative variants are recorded separately.
s2148 The results are compared with age norms and a conclusion is given on the degree of dysbacteriosis.
