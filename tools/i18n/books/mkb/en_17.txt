s3923 A patient who received a deep puncture wound to the foot during field work developed, after 8 days, difficulty opening the mouth, drawing of the facial muscles, and then generalized convulsions. Consciousness is intact.
s3924 What is the probable diagnosis and how is it made?

s3952 The main danger of diphtheria is **asphyxia** (the laryngeal membrane blocks the airway — “true croup”) and the late complications: **myocarditis**, polyneuritis, nephrosis. That is why antitoxic serum is given on clinical suspicion, without waiting for the laboratory answer.
s3953 In the diagnosis of diphtheria, **determining toxigenicity is essential**: nontoxigenic corynebacteria can occur in the throat as carriage and do not cause disease.
s3954 In pertussis the effectiveness of the bacteriological method falls sharply depending on the stage of the disease: the best result is obtained **in the catarrhal stage and at the beginning of the spasmodic stage**; later only PCR and serology give information.
s3955 Distinguish the Neisseria by carbohydrate: **MeninGoMaltose** — meningococcus: **G**lucose **+ M**altose; gonococcus — only **G**lucose.
s3956 The causative agent of diphtheria — *Corynebacterium diphtheriae*
s3957 **Morphology:** gram-positive, straight or slightly curved rod; the ends are thickened owing to **volutin granules** (resembling a matchstick); the cells lie at an angle to each other, forming the shape of **the Latin letters V, X, Y** (**pleomorphism and pleomorphic arrangement**). Nonmotile, nonspore-forming, noncapsulated.
s3958 Figure 18.1
s3959 The diphtheria bacillus stained by the Neisser method: dark volutin granules at the ends of the cells and angular arrangement. Source: Muhamedov I.M. et al., 2019.
s3960 **Staining:** Gram (positive), **Neisser** (the cell is yellow-brown, the volutin granules dark blue-black), **Löffler’s blue**.
s3961 **Cultural properties:** facultative anaerobe; fastidious — requires **serum medium** (Löffler’s coagulated serum) and **tellurite-containing medium** (selective; the tellurite is reduced, making the colony **black/gray**).
s3962 **Biovars:** *gravis* (large, rough, “resembling a daisy”), *mitis* (small, smooth, black, shiny), *intermedius*.
s3963 Figure 18.2
s3964 Colonies of the gravis (left) and mitis (right) biovars of *C. diphtheriae*. Source: Muhamedov I.M. et al., 2019.
s3965 **Pathogenicity factor — diphtheria exotoxin.** The toxin is encoded by **prophage genes**: only **lysogenic (toxigenic)** strains cause disease (phage conversion — topic 11). The toxin’s mechanism of action is blocking **elongation factor EF-2** in the cell by ADP-ribosylation → **stopping of protein synthesis** → cell necrosis.
s3966 **Pathogenesis:** the pathogen stays **local** at the portal of entry (throat, nose, rarely the eye, skin, wound); the toxin is absorbed into the blood and damages **the heart muscle, nervous system and kidneys**. The local change is fibrinous inflammation, with formation of a **dense gray-white membrane**; when the membrane is removed, the mucosa bleeds.
s3967 **Material:** a swab from the throat and nose taken **at the edge of the membrane**; before antibiotics and throat rinses are used, on an empty stomach.
s3968 **Bacterioscopy** — Neisser method: rods with volutin granules arranged at angles (**presumptive result**).
s3969 **Bacteriological (main):** inoculation onto tellurite-containing and serum media → black colonies → pure culture → biochemical identification (cystinase-positive — **Pizu test**; urease-negative — differs from *C. ulcerans*).
s3970 **Determining toxigenicity (the decisive step):** **precipitation in gel (Elek method)** — precipitation lines between a paper strip impregnated with antitoxin and the culture; also detecting the **tox gene** by PCR, and the biological test.
s3971 **Serological:** assessing the level of antitoxic immunity (PHA).
s3972 Figure 18.3
s3973 Scheme of the bacteriological examination of the diphtheria causative agent: collecting material, inoculation onto tellurite and serum media, identification, determining toxigenicity, susceptibility to antibiotics. Source: Aliyev Sh.R. et al., 2013.
s3974 **Prophylaxis:** planned vaccination with **toxoid** (as part of combination vaccines, according to the national schedule). In treatment, **antitoxic serum** and antibiotics are used (as prescribed by a physician).
s3975 The causative agent of pertussis (whooping cough) — *Bordetella pertussis*
s3976 **Morphology:** very small, ovoid gram-negative rod (coccobacillus); nonmotile, encapsulated.
s3977 **Cultural properties:** **obligate aerobe**, extremely fastidious. The classic medium is **Bordet–Gengou** (potato-glycerin blood agar) and **charcoal-casein agar**; growth is slow (3–5 days), the colonies are small and shiny — resembling a **“mercury droplet”** or a pearl.
s3978 **Pathogenicity factors:** **pertussis toxin** (disrupts the adenylate cyclase system), filamentous hemagglutinin and pertactin (adhesion), **tracheal cytotoxin** (damages the ciliated epithelium), adenylate cyclase–hemolysin, LPS.
s3979 **Pathogenesis:** the pathogen **adheres to the respiratory epithelium** and does not enter the blood; the movement of the cilia is disrupted, mucus is not cleared, and toxins constantly stimulate the cough center. As a result, **spasmodic (paroxysmal) coughing fits** — a series of cough bursts and a deep whooping inspiration (reprises), often ending in vomiting.
s3980 **Stages of the disease:** catarrhal (resembling an acute respiratory viral infection — **the most contagious stage**), spasmodic (typical fits), recovery.
s3981 **Material:** a swab taken from the nasopharynx **from the posterior wall** (special swab), the **“cough plate”** method (an open dish is held in front of the patient’s mouth while he coughs).
s3982 **Bacteriological** — immediate inoculation onto special medium; the pathogen is very unstable in the external environment, so **inoculation is done at the patient’s bedside**.
s3983 **PCR** — currently the most sensitive and practical method, especially in the catarrhal stage.
s3984 **Serological** — detecting antibodies by ELISA; significant in the late stage.
s3985 Figure 18.4
s3986 Scheme of the microbiological examination of the pertussis causative agent: collecting material, inoculation onto special media, identification and serological examination. Source: Aliyev Sh.R. et al., 2013.
s3987 **Prophylaxis:** vaccine (whole-cell or acellular) — within the complex immunization schedule.
s3988 The meningococcus — *Neisseria meningitidis*
s3989 **Morphology:** gram-negative **diplococcus** with flattened facing sides — resembling a **“coffee bean”** or a kidney bean; in cerebrospinal fluid and blood smears it is often located **inside leukocytes** (intracellularly). Encapsulated, nonmotile.
s3990 **Cultural properties:** fastidious aerobe; requires **serum agar** and chocolate agar; grows well in an atmosphere with **5–10% CO₂**; **oxidase-positive**, breaks down glucose and maltose (differs from the gonococcus, which breaks down only glucose).
s3991 **Pathogenicity factors:** the **capsule** (antiphagocytic; determines the serological groups — A, B, C, W, Y and others), pili (adhesion), **LPS (endotoxin)** — the main cause of severe shock, IgA protease.
s3992 **Clinical forms:** inflammation of the nasopharynx (nasopharyngitis) and **healthy carriage** (the most widespread form), **generalized forms** — meningococcemia (heterogeneous rash, shock) and **purulent meningitis**.
s3993 **Material:** cerebrospinal fluid (lumbar puncture), blood, nasopharyngeal swab, scraping from a rash element.
s3994 **Transporting the material** — **warm**, without cooling, immediately: the meningococcus is extremely sensitive to low temperature and drying.
s3995 **Bacterioscopy** — Gram smear from the CSF sediment: intracellular gram-negative diplococci in leukocytes → a rapid presumptive answer.
s3996 **Bacteriological** — serum/chocolate agar, CO₂ conditions; identification — oxidase, carbohydrates (glucose + maltose), serological grouping.
s3997 **Rapid methods** — detecting the capsular antigen in CSF by **latex agglutination**, and **PCR**.
s3998 In meningococcal infection, collecting and culturing the CSF must not be delayed; at the same time, in severe cases antibacterial treatment is started without waiting for the laboratory answer. After antibiotics are started the culture may be negative — in which case **PCR and antigen tests** are valuable.
s3999 **Prophylaxis:** conjugate and polysaccharide vaccines (according to serological groups), preventive measures for contacts in a focus — as prescribed by a physician.

s4001 5.1. *Streptococcus pneumoniae* (pneumococcus)
s4002 **Morphology:** gram-positive **lancet-shaped diplococcus** with the ends pointing in opposite directions; **encapsulated** (clearly visible in pathological material).

s4010 **Diagnosis:** sputum, cerebrospinal fluid, blood → bacterioscopy (encapsulated diplococci) → blood agar → optochin and bile tests → serotyping; antigen tests and PCR in cerebrospinal fluid.
s4011 **Prophylaxis:** polysaccharide and **conjugate** vaccines (for the reason explained in topic 15, the conjugate form is preferable in children).
s4012 5.2. *Haemophilus influenzae*
s4013 **Morphology:** very small gram-negative coccobacillus, pleomorphic; type b is encapsulated.
s4014 **Cultural property:** requires **factor X (hemin)** and **factor V (NAD)** — therefore it does not grow on ordinary blood agar but grows on **chocolate agar** (topic 5). When it grows next to *S. aureus*, the **“satellite” phenomenon** is seen — the staphylococcus lyses erythrocytes and releases factor V.
s4015 **Diseases:** type b (Hib) — meningitis, epiglottitis, pneumonia and sepsis in children; nonencapsulated strains — otitis, sinusitis, exacerbations of bronchitis.
s4016 **Diagnosis:** cerebrospinal fluid, blood, sputum → chocolate agar + CO₂ → determining the requirement for factors X and V (disk method) → serotyping; PCR and antigen tests.
s4017 **Prophylaxis:** the **Hib conjugate vaccine** has sharply reduced the frequency of Hib meningitis in children.
