s3639 **Special features of laboratory diagnosis:**
s3640 **Collecting material** — not with a swab but as a **puncture sample or tissue**, taken with limited contact with air;
s3641 **Transport** — in a special anaerobic transport medium, fast;

s3645 Practical skill: collecting material from a purulent wound

s3647 Remove the dried surface layer or crust — the material is taken from the **deep, active** part.
s3648 The preferred method is taking a **puncture sample with a syringe** (especially in a closed abscess and when anaerobes are suspected); a swab is a less informative option.
s3649 Label the sample: patient, site, time of collection, whether antibiotics have been taken.
s3650 Deliver it to the laboratory in a transport medium as fast as possible (within 1–2 hours); a special medium is essential for anaerobic examination.

s3756 In tetanus consciousness **is not impaired** — the patient feels everything. This is because the toxin acts selectively on inhibitory neurons.
s3757 In tetanus **no immunity develops**: the amount of toxin that causes disease is too small for an immune response. That is why **vaccination** is done even after recovery from the disease.
s3758 The triad of gas gangrene: **gas** (crepitus) + **necrosis** + **severe intoxication**. In tetanus, by contrast, **convulsions**, with consciousness intact.
s3759 In anaerobic wound infections **the time factor** is decisive: the earlier the surgical treatment is done, the better the outcome. Treatment cannot be delayed to wait for the laboratory answer.
s3760 General description: clostridia
s3761 **Clostridia** (*Clostridium*) are gram-positive, **spore-forming**, **obligate anaerobic** rods. The position of the spore and the shape of the cell are important signs in preliminary identification (topic 3). The natural habitat is soil and the intestines of humans and animals.

s3764 The causative agent of tetanus — *Clostridium tetani*
s3765 **Morphology:** gram-positive, thin, long rod; **peritrichous** (motile); forms a **round, terminally located spore** — the cell takes the shape of a **“drumstick”** or a “matchstick.”
s3766 Figure 17.2
s3767 The causative agent of tetanus: gram-positive rods with terminal spores — the “drumstick” appearance. Source: Muhamedov I.M. et al., 2019.
s3768 **Cultural properties:** obligate anaerobe; in Kitt–Tarozzi medium it grows, making the medium turbid and giving a characteristic smell; on blood agar it gives thin, “creeping” growth and hemolysis.
s3769 **Pathogenicity factors — two exotoxins:**
s3770 **Tetanospasmin (neurotoxin)** — the main toxin; it reaches the central nervous system along the nerve fibers and through the blood, and **stops the release of inhibitory mediators (glycine and GABA)**. As a result the inhibition reaching the muscles is lost and a constant **tonic contraction (spasm)** develops;
s3771 **Tetanolysin** — lyses erythrocytes and contributes to local tissue damage.
s3772 **The main condition of pathogenesis:** the pathogen stays **local** in the wound and does not spread into the blood — the clinical picture is determined only by the **toxin**. That is why tetanus is a disease of **toxinemia**.
s3773 **Clinical picture:** contraction of the masseter muscles (**trismus**), drawing of the facial muscles (“risus sardonicus”), difficulty swallowing, then generalized tonic convulsions and arching of the trunk (**opisthotonus**); consciousness is preserved.
s3774 Causative agents of gas gangrene
s3775 **The main pathogen — *Clostridium perfringens***; in addition *C. novyi*, *C. septicum* and *C. histolyticum* take part. It often occurs as a **mixed (several species)** infection.
s3776 ***C. perfringens* features:** large gram-positive rod, **nonmotile** (rare among clostridia), **encapsulated**; in the body it often does not form spores. On blood agar it gives **double-zone hemolysis**, and in milk medium **“stormy fermentation”** (violent gas formation).
s3777 **Pathogenicity factors:** numerous toxins and enzymes — **alpha-toxin (lecithinase)** (damages the membrane, the main factor), collagenase, hyaluronidase, proteases, DNase, neuraminidase; **enterotoxin** (in some strains — food poisoning).
s3778 **Pathogenesis:** when an oxygen-free environment forms in the tissue (a crushed wound with impaired circulation), clostridia multiply; enzymes dissolve tissue, **gas** forms from the fermentation of carbohydrates (crepitus under the skin), necrosis spreads, and severe intoxication develops.
s3779 **In tetanus the diagnosis is made mainly clinically** — waiting for the laboratory result delays treatment. Laboratory examination has an auxiliary role: wound discharge, necrotic tissue and operative material are examined; detection is possible by bacterioscopy, anaerobic culture and the **toxin neutralization test**.
s3780 **Laboratory diagnosis in gas gangrene:**
s3781 **Material:** a piece of tissue taken from the wound, discharge from the edge of the necrosis, puncture fluid, blood (when generalized).
s3782 **Bacterioscopy** — Gram stain: large gram-positive rods, capsule; gives a rapid direction.
s3783 **Anaerobic culture** — Kitt–Tarozzi medium, Wilson–Blair medium (black colonies), blood agar; in an anaerostat for 24–48 hours.
s3784 **Identification** — morphology, lecithinase activity (a zone on egg-yolk agar), the reaction in milk medium, biochemical tests, determining the toxin type with the neutralization test.
s3785 **Modern methods** — detecting toxin genes by PCR, MALDI-TOF.

s3805 Principles of specific prophylaxis and treatment
s3806 **Prevention of tetanus:**
s3807 **Planned (active)** — **tetanus toxoid** (as part of combination vaccines), according to the national immunization schedule, later with booster vaccinations;
s3808 **Emergency (when a wound is received)** — depending on the vaccination history, toxoid and/or ready-made antibodies (passive immunization) are given; the exact scheme is determined by a **physician** on the basis of the current protocol;

s3830 **General features of pathogenesis:** endogenous origin; a fall in the oxygen level in the tissue (ischemia, necrosis, foreign body, surgery) is the decisive condition; it often occurs **together with aerobes (synergism)**.
s3831 **Signs that raise suspicion:** a foul smell of the pus, gas formation, necrosis, no growth on ordinary culture even though the smear shows many microbes, poor response to commonly used antibiotics.

s3834 Practical skill: collecting and culturing material for an anaerobic infection
s3835 The skin around the wound is cleaned with an antiseptic; wait until the antiseptic dries.
s3836 The material is taken as a **puncture sample with a syringe** or as a **piece of tissue** from the edge of the necrosis; the syringe must be free of air.
s3837 The sample is placed in **anaerobic transport medium**; it is not cooled and is delivered as fast as possible.
s3838 In the laboratory the following are inoculated at the same time: anaerobic blood agar, Kitt–Tarozzi medium, Wilson–Blair medium and an **aerobic control** plate (to distinguish whether the culture is aerobic or anaerobic).
s3839 In an anaerostat at 37 °C for 48–72 hours; the aerobic plate — 24 hours.
s3840 Smears (Gram) and differential tests are made from the colonies that grew; a culture that did not grow on the aerobic plate but grew anaerobically is judged to be an **obligate anaerobe**.
