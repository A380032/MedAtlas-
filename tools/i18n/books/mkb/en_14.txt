s3148 They are fused with **myeloma cells** (myeloma cells divide without limit but do not produce the required antibody).
s3149 In a special selective medium only the fused cells — **hybridomas** — survive.
s3150 The hybridomas are separated into individual clones, the clone that produces the required antibody is selected and multiplied without limit.
s3151 As a result a cell line that is **“immortal + produces the required antibody”** is obtained.
s3152 **Advantages:** absolute specificity, standardization (the same from batch to batch), and the possibility of obtaining unlimited amounts.
s3153 **Uses:**
s3154 **In diagnosis** — ELISA, immunofluorescence, immunochromatographic rapid tests, detecting CD markers by flow cytometry (topic 15), determining blood groups, measuring hormones and tumor markers;
s3155 **In treatment** — targeted therapy (directed at a chosen target): in oncology, autoimmune diseases and some infections; preventing rejection in transplantation;
s3156 **In research** — detecting, purifying and studying proteins.
s3157 **Types:** mouse, chimeric, humanized and fully human antibodies — progressively “humanized” to reduce the response to the foreign protein (indicated by the corresponding suffixes in their names).
s3158 **Polyclonal** serum — many kinds of antibodies to many epitopes (the product of natural immunization). **Monoclonal** — one kind of antibody to one epitope (the product of a hybridoma). The accuracy of standard diagnostic test systems is ensured precisely by monoclonality.
s3159 Practical skill: setting up the extended agglutination reaction
s3160 Physiological saline is poured into a row of tubes and the patient’s serum is serially **diluted two-fold** (1:50, 1:100, 1:200, 1:400, 1:800...).
s3161 The same volume of **diagnosticum** is added to each tube.
s3162 Control tubes are set up: a **serum control** (serum + physiological saline, without diagnosticum) and an **antigen control** (diagnosticum + physiological saline).
s3163 They are left in a thermostat at 37 °C for 2 hours, then at room temperature for 18–20 hours.
s3164 The result is assessed: clearing of the liquid with a granular sediment at the bottom is positive; depending on the amount of sediment it is graded from “++++” to “+”.
s3165 The **titer** is the highest dilution that gives a positive result. There must be no reaction in the control tubes.
s3166 The result can be read **only if the controls are correct**. If there is spontaneous sediment in the antigen control (the diagnosticum is of poor quality), the whole reaction is considered unreliable.

s3270 General scheme of adaptive immunity
s3271 Adaptive immunity forms after an encounter with antigen, is **specific** and leaves **immunological memory**. It appears in two forms:
s3272 **Humoral response** — B lymphocytes → plasma cells → **antibodies**; effective against extracellular bacteria and toxins;
s3273 **Cellular response** — T lymphocytes; directed against **intracellular** pathogens (viruses, *Mycobacterium tuberculosis*), tumor cells and the transplant.
s3274 **Three stages of the immune response:** recognition and presentation of the antigen (APC) → activation, proliferation and differentiation of lymphocytes → the effector stage (antibody production or destruction of the target cell) and the formation of **memory cells**.

s3301 B lymphocytes and the humoral response
s3302 In humans B lymphocytes mature in the **bone marrow**. Markers: **CD19, CD20, CD21**; on the surface there is **membrane-bound immunoglobulin (IgM and IgD)** — the B cell receptor.
s3303 **Stages of the humoral response:**
s3304 The receptor on the surface of the B cell recognizes the antigen directly and engulfs it;
s3305 It shows a piece of the antigen with **MHC II** to a T helper (Tfh);
s3306 With the help of the T helper (cytokines + CD40L), the B cell is activated and multiplies;
s3307 Some become **plasma cells** (the antibody “factory”), others remain as **memory B cells**;
s3308 In the germinal centers of the lymph node, **class switching** (IgM → IgG, IgA, IgE) and **affinity maturation** take place.
s3309 **T-dependent and T-independent antigens.** Protein antigens require the help of a T helper and give a full response (IgG, memory). Polysaccharide antigens (for example, the pneumococcal capsule) provoke a T-independent response: mainly IgM, with weak memory.

s3361 **Interferons:** IFN-α (leukocytes), IFN-β (fibroblasts) — **type I**, direct antiviral defense; IFN-γ — **type II**, produced by T and NK cells, activates macrophages and increases MHC expression. s3363 Independent work 2: Immunodeficiency states
s3364 **Primary (congenital) immunodeficiency** — the result of a genetic defect; it usually appears in childhood with recurrent and unusual infections. Depending on the impaired link:
s3365 **B-system (humoral)** defect — recurrent purulent infections with encapsulated bacteria;
s3366 **T-system (cellular)** defect — viral, fungal and intracellular infections;
s3367 **combined** defects — the most severe course;
s3368 **phagocytosis** defects — recurrent abscesses (the NBT test in topic 13);
s3369 **complement** defects — susceptibility to *Neisseria* infections.
s3370 **Secondary (acquired) immunodeficiency** — much more common; causes: viral infections (the most important — HIV), protein-energy malnutrition, severe chronic diseases, tumors and chemotherapy, irradiation, long-term glucocorticoid and immunosuppressive treatment, extensive surgical interventions, old age, chronic stress.
s3371 **Laboratory assessment:** complete blood count and leukocyte differential, immunoglobulin class levels, lymphocyte subpopulations (flow cytometry), phagocytic activity and the NBT test, complement components, and genetic testing when necessary.
