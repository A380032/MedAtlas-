s1218 Independent work: Modern methods of rapid diagnosis of bacteria
s1219 The classic bacteriological method is reliable, but it takes **3–4 days**. Modern methods can give an answer within hours.
s1220 **1. Polymerase chain reaction (PCR).** A specific fragment of the pathogen’s DNA (or RNA — via reverse transcription) is amplified in vitro. Steps: **denaturation** (≈95 °C) → **primer binding (annealing)** (50–65 °C) → **elongation** (72 °C); the cycle is repeated 30–40 times and the target sequence multiplies exponentially. **Real-time PCR** measures the result during amplification by fluorescence and gives a quantitative assessment.
s1221 *Advantages:* very sensitive and specific; indispensable for pathogens that do not grow or grow slowly (chlamydia, mycoplasma, tuberculosis, viruses); gives a result even after antibiotics have been started.
s1222 *Limitations:* does not distinguish live from dead microbes; does not give full antibiotic susceptibility (detects only known resistance genes); sensitive to contamination.
s1223 **2. Sequencing the 16S ribosomal RNA gene.** The 16S rRNA gene is present in all bacteria; within it, regions common to all species (conserved) alternate with species-specific (variable) ones. Primers are placed on the common regions to amplify the gene, then its sequence is read and compared with a database. This method is used to identify **uncultivable or unusual** microorganisms, and also to study the composition of the microbiota.
s1224 **3. Electromigration methods.** **Capillary zone electrophoresis** — separates particles by charge and size in a thin capillary; **capillary isoelectric focusing** — separates proteins by their isoelectric point. They allow microbial proteins and nucleic acids to be analyzed with high accuracy.
s1225 **4. Mass spectrometry (MALDI-TOF).** The spectrum of proteins in a colony is compared with a reference database, and the species is identified **in a few minutes**. It has now become the main method of identification in many laboratories.
s1226 Rapid methods **complement, not replace,** classic culture: a live culture is still needed for full assessment of antibiotic susceptibility and for epidemiological analysis.

s1357 **Constitutive** enzymes — “permanent staff” (always working); **inducible** — “on call” (synthesis begins when the substrate arrives). The lac operon is the classic example of induction.
s1358 Aggression enzymes are themselves pathogenicity factors: **plasmacoagulase** (*S. aureus*) forms a fibrin shell that protects against phagocytosis and is the main test for distinguishing *S. aureus* from *S. epidermidis*; **hyaluronidase** “dissolves” the intercellular space of the tissue and makes spread easier (the “spreading factor”); **lecithinase** destroys the cell membrane.
s1359 Pigment and odor are only **auxiliary** signs. They speed up identification, but a final answer is not given without biochemical and serological confirmation.
s1360 Remember Kligler as a “**traffic light**”: **yellow = acid** (sugar fermented), **red = alkaline** (not fermented), **black = H₂S**, **crack/bubble = gas**.
s1361 Constructive metabolism (anabolism)
s1362 **Constructive metabolism** is the process of synthesizing cell components (protein, nucleic acid, polysaccharide, lipid); energy is expended. It is closely linked to catabolism: catabolism provides **ATP and intermediate metabolites** (carbon skeletons), and anabolism uses them. Reactions common to both directions are called **amphibolic pathways** (for example, the Krebs cycle).
s1363 The bacterial cell synthesizes very quickly: under favorable conditions the entire cell content doubles in 20–30 minutes. The direction and rate of synthesis are controlled by **enzymes**.
s1364 Bacterial enzymes
s1365 An **enzyme** is a biological catalyst of protein nature. Enzymes serve as the main feature in the identification of bacteria, because **each species has its own characteristic set of enzymes** and it is passed on from generation to generation.

s1389 Methods of biochemical identification
s1390 For identification, the pure culture is tested for **saccharolytic** (carbohydrate breakdown), **proteolytic** (protein breakdown), **oxidation–reduction** and other enzyme activities.
s1391 5.1. Saccharolytic activity
s1392 **Hiss media (the “variegated series”)** — each tube contains one carbohydrate (glucose, lactose, maltose, sucrose, mannitol) + an indicator + a gas-collecting float (Durham tube). If the carbohydrate is broken down, acid forms → the indicator changes color (**A — acid**); if gas is also released, a bubble is seen in the tube (**AG — acid + gas**).
s1393 **Kligler medium (two-sugar iron medium)** — a medium that gives a lot of information in one tube. It contains glucose (little), lactose (much), an iron salt and an indicator; the tube consists of a slant (aerobic) and a butt (anaerobic) part.
