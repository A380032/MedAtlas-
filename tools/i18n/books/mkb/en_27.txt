s6116 Retroviruses and HIV
s6117 **Retroviruses** (*Retroviridae*) are enveloped viruses containing **two copies of single-stranded positive-sense RNA**; their main feature is the enzyme **reverse transcriptase (revertase)**: it synthesizes DNA from RNA, and this DNA is then **integrated** into the host chromosome (**provirus**).
s6118 **HIV (human immunodeficiency virus)** is the causative agent of AIDS (acquired immunodeficiency syndrome); there are two types: **HIV-1** (widespread worldwide) and **HIV-2** (mainly West Africa, with a slower course).
s6119 **Structure:**
s6120 **Genome** — two copies of RNA; the main genes: *gag* (internal proteins — p24 and others), *pol* (enzymes: reverse transcriptase, integrase, protease), *env* (envelope glycoproteins — **gp120 and gp41**);
s6121 **Conical capsid** (p24 — an important diagnostic antigen);
s6122 **Supercapsid** — from the host cell membrane; **gp120** binds to the receptor, **gp41** provides membrane fusion.
s6123 Figure 29.1
s6124 The structure of HIV (HIV-1 and HIV-2): the envelope, gp120 and gp41 glycoproteins, matrix protein, p24 capsid protein, RNA and reverse transcriptase. Source: Muhamedov I.M. et al., 2019.
s6125 **Target cells:** **CD4⁺ T lymphocytes** (the main ones), macrophages, dendritic cells, microglia. The virus binds to the **CD4 molecule** (the main receptor) and to **chemokine coreceptors** (CCR5, CXCR4).
s6126 **Replication cycle:** adsorption (gp120 + CD4 + coreceptor) → membrane fusion (gp41) → **reverse transcription** (RNA → DNA) → formation of the provirus with the help of **integrase** → transcription and translation → maturation of proteins by **protease** → release by **budding**.
s6127 Figure 29.2
s6128 The replicative cycle of HIV: entry into the cell, reverse transcription, integration, formation of new virions. Source: Aliyev Sh.R. et al., 2013.
s6129 **Routes of transmission:** sexual; parenteral (blood, syringe, medical and nonmedical manipulations); **vertical** (pregnancy, delivery, breastfeeding). It is **not transmitted** through household contact, air or insects.
s6130 **Pathogenesis:** the virus gradually destroys CD4⁺ cells → cell-mediated immunity is deeply impaired → **opportunistic infections** and tumors develop. Stages of the disease: acute retroviral syndrome → the **latent (asymptomatic) period** (lasting years) → persistent lymphadenopathy → the **AIDS stage**.
s6131 **AIDS indicator conditions:** Pneumocystis pneumonia, toxoplasma encephalitis, cryptococcal meningitis, disseminated forms of candidiasis, tuberculosis and atypical mycobacterioses, cytomegalovirus retinitis, Kaposi sarcoma and others.

s6178 3.2. Hepatitis D virus (HDV)
s6179 **Features:** a **defective** virus — it cannot multiply independently and uses **HBsAg** as its envelope. Therefore it occurs only together with HBV:
s6180 **coinfection** — HBV and HDV are acquired at the same time;
s6181 **superinfection** — HDV is added to a chronic HBV carrier; it usually runs more severely and increases the risk of cirrhosis.
s6182 **Diagnosis:** anti-HDV (IgM/IgG), HDV RNA (PCR) — tested in HBsAg-positive patients.
s6183 **Prophylaxis:** vaccination against hepatitis B — indirectly also protects against D (D does not develop without B).
s6184 3.3. Hepatitis C virus (HCV)
s6185 **Features:** *Flaviviridae*; enveloped, **single-stranded positive-sense RNA**; high genetic **variability** (many genotypes and quasispecies) — therefore **there is no vaccine** and immunity does not protect.
