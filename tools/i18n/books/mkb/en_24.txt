s5327 2.2. Serological diagnosis
s5328 **Two groups of tests:**
s5329 **Nontreponemal (lipoidal) tests** — **MPR, RPR, VDRL**; cardiolipin is used as the antigen; they detect **reagins** in the body.
s5330 - *Advantage:* cheap, fast, **quantitative (with a titer)** — therefore used for **monitoring the effect of treatment** (the titer should fall);
s5331 - *Disadvantage:* **false-positive** results are possible (pregnancy, autoimmune diseases, some infections, old age).
s5332 **Treponemal tests** — **TPHA, ELISA, immunoblot, IF**; the antigen is the treponeme itself or its proteins.
s5333 - *Advantage:* high specificity — a **confirmatory test**;
s5334 - *Disadvantage:* they remain positive for many years even after treatment → **unsuitable for monitoring treatment**.
s5335 **The Wassermann reaction (CFT)** — the historical classic method; it has now been replaced by modern tests.

s5338 Gonorrhea — *Neisseria gonorrhoeae*
s5339 **Morphology:** a gram-negative **diplococcus** with concave facing sides (“coffee bean”); in **acute gonorrhea it lies inside leukocytes in the pus** — a classic diagnostic sign. Nonmotile, nonspore-forming; it has pili and a microcapsule.
s5340 Figure 25.3
s5341 Gonococci: gram-negative diplococci inside leukocytes in a pus smear. Source: Muhamedov I.M. et al., 2019.
s5342 **Cultural properties:** a fastidious aerobe; **serum or chocolate agar**, **5–10% CO₂**; **oxidase positive**; it ferments **only glucose** (the difference from the meningococcus — topic 18). Very unstable in the external environment (sensitive to drying and cold).
s5343 **Pathogenicity factors:** **pili** (adhesion; because of antigenic variability immunity is not durable), **Opa proteins**, LPS (lipooligosaccharide), **IgA protease**, beta-lactamases (in some strains).
s5344 **Clinical picture:** in men — acute urethritis (purulent discharge, pain on urination); in women — often **with few or no symptoms** (cervicitis), so it is detected late and causes complications (pelvic inflammatory disease, infertility, ectopic pregnancy); in newborns — **blennorrhea** (conjunctivitis of the eye); in the disseminated form — arthritis, skin rash.
s5345 **Material:** a urethral, cervical canal, rectal or throat swab, conjunctival discharge.
s5346 **Bacterioscopy** — Gram and methylene blue: **gram-negative diplococci inside leukocytes**. Sensitivity is high in men with acute urethritis; low in women and in the chronic form.
s5347 **Bacteriological** — serum/chocolate agar + CO₂; identification: oxidase, carbohydrates (glucose only), **antibiogram** (important because resistance is widespread).
s5348 **Molecular (NAAT/PCR)** — currently the most sensitive method, especially in women, in the chronic form and in extragenital localization; it can also be done on urine.
s5349 **In the chronic form** — provocation methods (physiological, chemical, dietary) were used; in current practice **molecular methods** have largely taken their place.
s5350 Independent work: Other urogenital infections
s5351 4.1. Urogenital chlamydiosis
s5352 **Causative agent:** *Chlamydia trachomatis* (serovars D–K) — an obligate intracellular parasite (topic 4).
s5353 **Clinical picture:** often **asymptomatic or with few symptoms** — a “silent” infection; urethritis, cervicitis, pelvic inflammatory disease, infertility, reactive arthritis; conjunctivitis and pneumonia in newborns.
s5354 **The key to diagnosis:** the material must contain **epithelial cells** (a scraping, not the discharge itself). Methods: **PCR/NAAT (the main one)**, direct immunofluorescence, ELISA; cell culture — the gold standard, but rarely used in practice.
s5355 4.2. Urogenital mycoplasmosis
s5356 **Causative agents:** *Mycoplasma hominis*, *M. genitalium*, *Ureaplasma urealyticum*.
s5357 **Features:** no cell wall → **naturally resistant to beta-lactams** (topic 4); some species also occur in the normal flora, so finding them does not always mean disease. *M. genitalium* — a definite pathogen (urethritis, cervicitis).
s5358 **Diagnosis:** **PCR** (the main one), growth in special media (by urease and arginine), quantitative assessment.
s5359 4.3. Trichomoniasis
s5360 **Causative agent:** *Trichomonas vaginalis* — a flagellated protozoan; pear-shaped, with 4 free flagella and an undulating membrane; **it does not form cysts**.
s5361 Figure 25.4
s5362 *Trichomonas vaginalis* — a flagellated, pear-shaped protozoan (under the electron microscope). Source: Muhamedov I.M. et al., 2019.
s5363 **Clinical picture:** in women — foamy, yellowish-green discharge, itching; in men often asymptomatic (carriage).
s5364 **Diagnosis:** **warm wet mount** — seeing the motile parasite immediately (topic 4); a stained smear (Romanowsky–Giemsa), culture in special medium, **PCR** (the most sensitive), antigen tests.
s5365 4.4. Chancroid
s5366 **Causative agent:** *Haemophilus ducreyi* — a gram-negative coccobacillus; in smears it lies as a **“school of fish” or in chains**.
s5367 **Clinical picture:** a **painful**, soft ulcer with a purulent base and painful suppurating lymphadenitis — these signs are decisive in differential diagnosis from the **hard chancre of syphilis** (painless, dense).
s5368 **Diagnosis:** a smear from the ulcer discharge (Gram), culture on specially enriched medium (fastidious, difficult), PCR.

s5388 Practical skill: the examination algorithm when syphilis is suspected
s5389 **Determine the clinical period:** ulcer (primary), rash (secondary), late signs (tertiary).
s5390 **In the primary period:** cleanse the ulcer with physiological saline and take tissue fluid from its base → **examine it immediately in dark field** (a live, unstained preparation). If necessary — PCR.

s5393 - nontreponemal (+) and treponemal (+) → active or past infection (assessed with the clinical picture and history);

s5395 - nontreponemal (−), treponemal (+) → past/treated infection or the late period.
s5396 **Monitoring of treatment:** only by the dynamics of the **nontreponemal test titer**.
s5397 **Epidemiological work:** examination and registration of sexual partners — by the set procedure.

s5506 **Brill–Zinsser disease** — the recrudescence of epidemic typhus after years: the rickettsiae persist latent in the body and become active when immunity falls. Therefore isolated cases may occur even in a louse-free area.

s5509 General concept of vector-borne infections
s5510 **Vector-borne infections** are diseases in which the causative agent is transmitted by **arthropods (vectors)**. Vectors: lice, fleas, ticks, mosquitoes, horseflies.
s5511 **Two groups:**
s5512 **Anthroponoses** — the source is a human (epidemic typhus, epidemic relapsing fever);
s5513 **Zoonoses (natural-focal)** — the source is animals (endemic typhus, Lyme disease, tick-borne relapsing fever, Q fever).
s5514 **Natural focality** (the doctrine of E.N. Pavlovsky) — the causative agent, the vector and the reservoir animals circulate in a certain landscape even without human participation; a person falls ill on entering the focus.
s5515 Rickettsiae and typhus
s5516 **Rickettsiae** (topic 4) are small, pleomorphic gram-negative bacteria; **obligate intracellular parasites** (they multiply mainly in endothelial cells); they do not grow on artificial media.

s5534 **Pathogenesis of epidemic typhus:** the rickettsiae multiply in the **endothelium** of blood vessels → the vessel wall is damaged, thrombi and **vasculitis** develop → a skin rash (roseolar-petechial), damage to the nervous system and internal organs, severe intoxication.
s5535 **Clinical picture:** acute onset, high fever, severe headache, insomnia, excitement; from day 4–5 a **roseolar-petechial rash** (on the trunk and the inner surfaces of the limbs); cardiovascular and nervous system disturbances.
s5536 **The particularity of Q fever:** *C. burnetii* is **highly resistant** in the external environment (it forms a spore-like form); it is transmitted mainly by the **airborne route** (dust, animal birth products), a rash is usually absent; the clinical picture — fever, atypical pneumonia, hepatitis; in the chronic form, endocarditis.
s5537 **Laboratory diagnosis of rickettsioses:**
s5538 **Serological (the main one)** — CFT, **PHA**, **ELISA** (IgM/IgG); the dynamics of the titer in paired sera. The historical method — the **Weil–Felix reaction** (a cross-reaction with *Proteus* OX antigens; low specificity, no longer used in practice).
s5539 **PCR** — in blood and biopsy material; important in the early period.
s5540 **Bacterioscopy** — Romanowsky–Giemsa, the **Zdrodovsky** method (rickettsiae red, the cell cytoplasm blue).
s5541 **Isolating the pathogen** — chick embryo, cell culture, laboratory animal; because of the **high hazard** only in special laboratories.
