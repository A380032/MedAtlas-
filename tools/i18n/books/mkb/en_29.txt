s6438 In catheter- or prosthesis-associated infections, bacteria inside the biofilm are much more resistant to antibiotics. Therefore treatment is often ineffective without **removing the foreign body** or replacing it.
s6439 Practical skill: collecting material from a vesicular rash
s6440 The **newest, still unruptured vesicle** of the rash is chosen (it contains the highest amount of virus).
s6441 The skin is wiped with an antiseptic and one waits **until it is dry** (so that the antiseptic does not get into the sample).
s6442 The vesicle is opened with a sterile needle or the tip of a scalpel; the fluid is collected with a swab, and a **scraping from the base of the vesicle** is also taken (cells are needed).
s6443 The material is placed in **virological transport medium**; for PCR a dry swab may also be used (according to the laboratory’s requirements).
s6444 The sample is stored at **2–8 °C** and delivered quickly.
s6445 In parallel, a **Tzanck preparation** can be made (to see multinucleated giant cells).
s6446 The requisition states: the date the rash began, its stage, its spread, vaccination and travel history, and immune status.
s6447 **Safety:** gloves and protective equipment; when monkeypox is suspected — the set procedure for isolation and packaging.
