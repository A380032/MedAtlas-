s1447 **Checking purity:** a smear is prepared from the growth on the slant and stained by the Gram method. All cells must have the same morphology and tinctorial property.
s1448 **Biochemical identification:** the Hiss series, Kligler, indole, H₂S, urease and other tests are set up; modern laboratories use ready-made test systems or automated analyzers.
s1449 **Serological identification:** a **slide agglutination** reaction with diagnostic sera (dividing enteric bacteria into serovars — topic 22).
s1450 **Antibiogram:** set up by disk diffusion or another method (topic 9).
s1451 If necessary, additional tests: typing with phages, determination of toxigenicity, biological test.
s1452 **Day 4** — all the results are put together and the final report is given: **the species of the causative agent (if necessary — the serovar) and its susceptibility to antibiotics.**
s1453 Biochemical results must be read **at the specified time**. Kligler is read after 18–24 hours: if read too early, the breakdown of lactose is not seen; if read too late, the acid in the slant turns alkaline and a lactose-negative result may be misinterpreted as falsely “lactose-positive.”
s1454 Independent work: Forms of energy production and types of fermentation
s1455 **Forms of cell energy:** **ATP** (the chemical form) and the **proton gradient across the membrane (proton-motive force)**. The respiratory chain pumps protons out through the membrane, and the gradient formed is converted into ATP by **ATP synthase** (chemiosmotic theory). The proton gradient is also used directly for flagellar rotation and active transport.
s1456 **Ways of producing energy:** substrate-level phosphorylation (in fermentation, in a direct reaction), oxidative phosphorylation (in the respiratory chain), photophosphorylation (in phototrophs).
s1457 **Pathways of carbohydrate fermentation:** glycolysis (EMP), the pentose phosphate pathway, the Entner–Doudoroff pathway.

s1598 Distribution of microbes in the environment. The microbiota of water, air and soil. Basics of sanitary microbiology
s1599 To know the composition of the microbiota of water, air, soil and dwellings and its effect on human health;

s1601 to master the methods of determining the total microbial count (TMC) and the coli index;
s1602 Independent work: to know the principles of sanitary-bacteriological assessment of milk and meat products.
s1603 **The coli index and the coli titer are inversely related**: the larger the index, the smaller the titer, that is, the more polluted the water. Coli titer (mL) ≈ 1000 / coli index.
s1604 In sanitary examination, looking for the pathogen itself takes a long time and is often ineffective (pathogens are present in small numbers). That is why an **indirect indicator** — coliforms — is used: where they are found, the conclusion is drawn that intestinal pathogens may also be present.
s1605 Pouring agar that is **hotter than 45 °C** kills the microbes, and the TMC comes out falsely low. Placing the plate upside down prevents condensation drops from “spreading” the colonies.
s1606 General concept of the ecology of microorganisms
s1607 Microorganisms are distributed in all layers of the biosphere. They play a decisive role in **biogeochemical cycles** (the cycles of nitrogen, carbon, sulfur and phosphorus), break down organic residues and maintain soil fertility. From the medical point of view, however, the external environment can be a **source of infection and a factor of transmission**.
s1608 Relationships of microorganisms in the external environment: **symbiosis** (mutualism), **commensalism**, **neutralism**, **antagonism** (one species inhibits another — the natural basis of antibiotics and bacteriocins), **parasitism**.
s1609 Soil microbiota
s1610 Soil is the environment richest in microorganisms (billions of cells in 1 g of fertile soil). They are mainly **saprophytes**: bacteria, actinomycetes, fungi, algae, protozoa. The most microbes are at a depth of 10–20 cm; at the surface they decrease under the action of UV rays and drying.
s1611 **Function:** mineralization of organic residues, nitrogen fixation (*Azotobacter*, nodule bacteria), nitrification and denitrification, cellulose breakdown.
s1612 **Medical significance.** Most pathogenic microorganisms die quickly in soil, but **spore forms** persist for a long time; for this reason soil is a natural reservoir for the causative agents of tetanus, gas gangrene and anthrax. The causative agents of intestinal infections enter the soil with feces and can persist for several days to weeks.
s1613 **Sanitary indicators:** the total number of microbes, the **coli titer** and **perfringens titer** (the smallest amount of soil in which *E. coli* and *C. perfringens* are found), the number of thermophilic bacteria. A small coli titer is a sign of fresh fecal contamination, a small perfringens titer — of old contamination.
s1614 Water microbiota
s1615 The composition of microbes in water depends on the source: **artesian waters** are almost sterile; **open water bodies** (river, lake) are contaminated from soil, precipitation and wastewater. Water has a capacity for self-purification (sunlight, sedimentation, microbial antagonism, grazing by protozoa), but it is limited.
s1616 **Waterborne infections:** cholera, typhoid and paratyphoid fevers, dysentery, escherichiosis, leptospirosis, tularemia, hepatitis A and E, poliomyelitis, rotavirus gastroenteritis, giardiasis and amebiasis.

s1630 Air microbiota
s1631 Air is not a favorable environment for microbes: there are no nutrients, it dries out, and sunlight acts on it. Therefore the microbes in the air are temporary and float together with **dust particles and droplets**.
s1632 **Two kinds of aerosol:**
s1633 **droplet (large-droplet) phase** — formed by coughing and sneezing, settles quickly; ensures transmission at close range;
s1634 **droplet-nuclei and dust phase** — fine, stays suspended for a long time; resistant pathogens (tubercle bacillus, streptococcus, fungal spores) spread by this route.
s1635 **Airborne infections:** influenza and other acute respiratory viral infections, COVID-19, measles, chickenpox, whooping cough, diphtheria, meningococcal infection, tuberculosis, streptococcal tonsillitis.
s1636 **Sanitary indicators:** per 1 m³ of air, the **total microbial count**, and the number of **hemolytic streptococci** and **Staphylococcus aureus** (an indicator of contamination from the human respiratory tract). In enclosed rooms the indicators are standardized according to the summer–winter season and the density of people; the requirements are strictest for operating rooms and children’s institutions.
s1637 **Methods of taking an air sample:** sedimentation (Koch’s method — leaving an open Petri dish for a certain time; simplified, approximate) and **aspiration** (the Krotov apparatus and similar devices — blowing a certain volume of air onto the surface of the medium; quantitative and accurate).
s1638 Basics of sanitary microbiology
s1639 **Sanitary microbiology** is the branch that assesses the microbiological state of environmental objects (water, air, soil, food, objects, hands) and their danger to human health.
s1640 **Two main directions:**
s1641 Determining the **total microbial count** — the overall level of contamination of the object.
s1642 Determining **sanitary indicator microorganisms** — **indirect** evidence of contamination with human or animal excretions.
s1643 **Requirements for a sanitary indicator microorganism:**
s1644 it must live permanently in the human (animal) body and be excreted with discharges;
s1645 it must not multiply in the external environment, but survive for no shorter a time than pathogens;
s1646 the method of detection must be simple and reliable;
s1647 there must be no natural “native” representative of it in the external environment.

s1667 Independent work: Sanitary-bacteriological assessment of milk and meat products
s1668 **Milk.** After milking, milk contains natural microflora; the main cause of spoilage is the multiplication of lactic acid bacteria (souring). Sanitary indicators: **TMC (CFU/mL)**, coliforms (coli titer), somatic cell count, absence of pathogens (Salmonella, *S. aureus*, Listeria). The **reductase test** is a method for rapidly assessing the number of microbes in milk by the time it takes methylene blue or resazurin to decolorize: the faster the color disappears, the more microbes there are.
s1669 Infections that can be transmitted through milk: brucellosis, tuberculosis, salmonellosis, listeriosis, staphylococcal poisoning, campylobacteriosis. **Pasteurization** (for example, 72 °C for 15 s or 63 °C for 30 minutes) destroys vegetative pathogens.
s1670 **Meat.** The muscle tissue of a healthy animal is practically sterile; contamination occurs during slaughter, processing and storage. Indicators: TMC per 1 g, coliforms, sulfite-reducing clostridia, absence of Salmonella and Listeria in 25 g. In assessing freshness, organoleptic and chemical tests (ammonia, hydrogen sulfide reaction) are also used.
