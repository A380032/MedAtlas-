s3408 Practical skill: a stepwise approach to assessing immune status
s3409 **History and clinical picture:** frequently recurring infections, unusual pathogens, severe course, poor response to antibiotics, family history.
s3410 **First-stage tests:** complete blood count (absolute numbers of lymphocytes and neutrophils), immunoglobulin classes (IgG, IgM, IgA), the NBT test or phagocytic indices.
s3411 **Second-stage tests:** lymphocyte subpopulations (CD3, CD4, CD8, CD19, CD16/56) and the **CD4/CD8 ratio**, complement components, the response of lymphocytes to mitogens.
s3412 **Special investigations:** HIV test, genetic analysis, antibody response to specific antigens (assessing the response to vaccination).
s3413 **Interpretation:** the results are compared with **age-appropriate norms** and assessed together with the clinical picture.

s3490 In a patient, the CD4⁺ cell count on flow cytometry has fallen sharply and the CD4/CD8 ratio has dropped to 0.4. Infections caused by unusual pathogens have been found in the patient.
s3491 What condition does this picture suggest?
s3492 Profound deficiency of cellular immunity; selective loss of CD4⁺ cells is very characteristic of HIV infection. For confirmation, HIV-specific testing (screening ELISA + a confirmatory method, viral load) is done.
s3493 A 2-year-old child has recurrent pneumococcal otitis and pneumonias. Immunoglobulin levels are low and the response to polysaccharide vaccine is weak.
s3494 Which link is impaired and which vaccine is preferable in this situation?
s3495 The **humoral (B-system)** link is impaired. Polysaccharide vaccines are ineffective at this age and in this condition; **conjugate** vaccines are preferable because they give a T-dependent response. The treatment plan is determined by the physician.

s3528 **MRSA** (methicillin-resistant *S. aureus*) is an important problem in hospital and community settings. The resistance is linked to an altered penicillin-binding protein, so **all beta-lactams** are considered ineffective (topic 9). Treatment is chosen on the basis of the antibiogram.
s3529 Group A streptococcal antigens resemble antigens of heart, joint and kidney tissue (**cross-reactive antigens**). That is why **rheumatic fever** or **acute glomerulonephritis** may develop 2–4 weeks after tonsillitis. For this very reason, complete treatment of streptococcal tonsillitis is important.
s3530 Remember the complications of streptococcus: **“High ASO — had tonsillitis.”** The antistreptolysin O (ASO) titer shows a past group A streptococcal infection and helps in the diagnosis of rheumatic fever.
s3531 *P. aeruginosa* is resistant to many disinfectants and persists in wet places (sinks, respiratory equipment, solutions). That is why infection control in hospitals is directed at the regimen related to exactly these wet environments and instruments.
s3532 Staphylococci (*Staphylococcus*)
s3533 **Morphology:** gram-positive cocci (0.5–1.5 µm); because division occurs in different planes, they form **grape-like clusters**; nonmotile, do not form spores, may have a microcapsule.
s3534 Figure 16.1
s3535 Staphylococci: gram-positive cocci forming typical grape-like clusters (pure culture, Gram stain). Source: Muhamedov I.M. et al., 2019.
s3536 **Cultural properties:** facultative anaerobe; grows on ordinary media, **resistant to a high concentration of NaCl (7.5–10%)** — this property was the basis for creating a selective medium (egg-yolk salt agar). Colonies are round, smooth, convex; *S. aureus* forms a **golden-yellow pigment**. On blood agar it often produces beta-hemolysis.

s3550 **Pathogenicity factors:**
s3551 **Enzymes:** **plasmacoagulase** (a fibrin envelope — protection from phagocytosis, the main marker of *S. aureus*), hyaluronidase, fibrinolysin, lecithinase, DNase, beta-lactamase;

s3584 **Pathogenicity factors of group A streptococcus:** **M protein** (the main virulence factor — antiphagocytic, type-specific), capsule (hyaluronic acid), **streptolysin O and S**, **erythrogenic (pyrogenic) toxin** (the rash in scarlet fever), streptokinase, hyaluronidase, DNase.
s3585 *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*
s3586 **Morphology:** gram-negative, straight or slightly curved rod; **monotrichous** (a single polar flagellum) — motile; nonspore-forming, with a slime layer.
s3587 Figure 16.3
s3588 *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* — gram-negative motile rods. Source: Muhamedov I.M. et al., 2019.
s3589 **Cultural and biochemical properties:** **obligate aerobe**; grows on ordinary media, not demanding of nutrients; **oxidase-positive** (the main difference from Enterobacteriaceae); does not break down lactose. **Pyocyanin** (blue-green) and pyoverdine pigments stain the medium; it has a characteristic **hyacinth-like odor**.
s3590 **Pathogenicity factors:** exotoxin A (stops protein synthesis), elastase, proteases, phospholipase C, pyocyanin, LPS, **biofilm formation**.
s3591 **Clinical significance:** a typical **opportunistic and hospital-acquired** pathogen. It causes infection in burn patients, in patients on artificial ventilation, in patients with catheters and in the immunocompromised: suppuration of wounds (blue-green pus), pneumonia, urinary tract infection, otitis, eye infections, sepsis.
s3592 Laboratory diagnosis
s3593 **Material:** pus, wound discharge, sputum, blood (in sepsis), urine, cerebrospinal fluid, puncture fluid; in hospital surveillance — a nasal swab (staphylococcal carriage).
s3594 **Bacterioscopic method** — a Gram-stained smear: gram-positive clustered cocci (staphylococcus), chained cocci (streptococcus), gram-negative rods (*Pseudomonas*). It gives a preliminary direction.
s3595 **Bacteriological method (main):**
s3596 **Day 1:** the material is inoculated onto blood agar and a selective medium (egg-yolk salt agar for staphylococcus).
s3597 **Day 2:** the colonies are studied (hemolysis, pigment, odor), a smear is made, and a pure culture is collected.
s3598 **Day 3:** differential tests and antibiogram.
s3599 **Day 4:** final report.
