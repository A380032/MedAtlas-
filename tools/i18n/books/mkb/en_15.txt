s3409 Practical skill: a stepwise approach to assessing immune status
s3410 **History and clinical picture:** frequently recurring infections, unusual pathogens, severe course, poor response to antibiotics, family history.
s3411 **First-stage tests:** complete blood count (absolute numbers of lymphocytes and neutrophils), immunoglobulin classes (IgG, IgM, IgA), the NBT test or phagocytic indices.
s3412 **Second-stage tests:** lymphocyte subpopulations (CD3, CD4, CD8, CD19, CD16/56) and the **CD4/CD8 ratio**, complement components, the response of lymphocytes to mitogens.
s3413 **Special investigations:** HIV test, genetic analysis, antibody response to specific antigens (assessing the response to vaccination).
s3414 **Interpretation:** the results are compared with **age-appropriate norms** and assessed together with the clinical picture.

s3529 **MRSA** (methicillin-resistant *S. aureus*) is an important problem in hospital and community settings. The resistance is linked to an altered penicillin-binding protein, so **all beta-lactams** are considered ineffective (topic 9). Treatment is chosen on the basis of the antibiogram.
s3530 Group A streptococcal antigens resemble antigens of heart, joint and kidney tissue (**cross-reactive antigens**). That is why **rheumatic fever** or **acute glomerulonephritis** may develop 2–4 weeks after tonsillitis. For this very reason, complete treatment of streptococcal tonsillitis is important.
s3531 Remember the complications of streptococcus: **“High ASO — had tonsillitis.”** The antistreptolysin O (ASO) titer shows a past group A streptococcal infection and helps in the diagnosis of rheumatic fever.
s3532 *P. aeruginosa* is resistant to many disinfectants and persists in wet places (sinks, respiratory equipment, solutions). That is why infection control in hospitals is directed at the regimen related to exactly these wet environments and instruments.
s3533 Staphylococci (*Staphylococcus*)
s3534 **Morphology:** gram-positive cocci (0.5–1.5 µm); because division occurs in different planes, they form **grape-like clusters**; nonmotile, do not form spores, may have a microcapsule.

s3586 *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*
s3587 **Morphology:** gram-negative, straight or slightly curved rod; **monotrichous** (a single polar flagellum) — motile; nonspore-forming, with a slime layer.
s3588 Figure 16.3
s3589 *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* — gram-negative motile rods. Source: Muhamedov I.M. et al., 2019.
s3590 **Cultural and biochemical properties:** **obligate aerobe**; grows on ordinary media, not demanding of nutrients; **oxidase-positive** (the main difference from Enterobacteriaceae); does not break down lactose. **Pyocyanin** (blue-green) and pyoverdine pigments stain the medium; it has a characteristic **hyacinth-like odor**.
s3591 **Pathogenicity factors:** exotoxin A (stops protein synthesis), elastase, proteases, phospholipase C, pyocyanin, LPS, **biofilm formation**.
s3592 **Clinical significance:** a typical **opportunistic and hospital-acquired** pathogen. s3593 Laboratory diagnosis
s3594 **Material:** pus, wound discharge, sputum, blood (in sepsis), urine, cerebrospinal fluid, puncture fluid; in hospital surveillance — a nasal swab (staphylococcal carriage).
s3595 **Bacterioscopic method** — a Gram-stained smear: gram-positive clustered cocci (staphylococcus), chained cocci (streptococcus), gram-negative rods (*Pseudomonas*). It gives a preliminary direction.
s3596 **Bacteriological method (main):**
s3597 **Day 1:** the material is inoculated onto blood agar and a selective medium (egg-yolk salt agar for staphylococcus).
s3598 **Day 2:** the colonies are studied (hemolysis, pigment, odor), a smear is made, and a pure culture is collected.
s3599 **Day 3:** differential tests and antibiogram.
s3600 **Day 4:** final report.

s3636 Table 16.4. Brief characteristics of anaerobic cocci
s3637 **General features:** members of the normal microflora; against a background of injury, circulatory disturbance, surgery or reduced immunity they cause an **endogenous** infection. They usually occur as part of a **mixed (polymicrobial)** infection.
