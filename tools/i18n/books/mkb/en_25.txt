s5558 Table 26.2. The two forms of relapsing fever
s5559 **The “relapse” mechanism:** during the fever period borreliae are numerous in the blood; the body produces antibodies against them and they disappear (the apyrexia period). But a new population with a **changed antigenic structure (antigenic variation)** multiplies and produces the next wave of fever. For this reason the disease runs with **recurrent attacks of fever**.
s5560 In relapsing fever the **blood smear is taken at the peak of fever** — exactly when borreliae are numerous in the blood. During apyrexia the smear is negative.
s5561 **Bacterioscopic (the main one)** — a **thick drop** and a thin blood smear taken **at the peak of fever**, stained with Romanowsky–Giemsa; live borreliae can also be seen in dark field.
s5562 **Biological test** — was used to distinguish the endemic form from the epidemic form (infecting mice).
s5563 **Serological and molecular** methods — additional.
s5564 Independent work 1: Lyme disease (Lyme borreliosis)
s5565 **Causative agent:** the *Borrelia burgdorferi* group; the vector is **Ixodes ticks**; the reservoir is rodents and large mammals; a natural-focal zoonosis.
s5566 **Clinical stages:**
s5567 **Early localized** — at the site of the tick bite a **migrating annular erythema** (a spot with a pale center and red edge that keeps expanding) — the most characteristic sign;
s5568 **Early disseminated** — after several weeks to months: the nervous system (neuroborreliosis — neuritis, meningitis), the heart (atrioventricular block), migrating joint and muscle pain;
s5569 **Late (chronic)** — chronic arthritis, acrodermatitis chronica atrophicans, chronic damage to the nervous system.
s5570 **If there is a typical erythema — the diagnosis is made clinically**, and serology is not awaited (antibodies may not yet be present in the early stage);
s5571 **In later stages — two-tier serology:** first **ELISA (screening)**, and with a positive or equivocal result **immunoblot (confirmation)** (topic 14);
s5572 PCR — important in joint fluid and biopsy material;
s5573 Culture — difficult and rarely used in practice.
s5574 Independent work 2: Leptospirosis

s5590 Practical skill: examination when a vector-borne infection is suspected
s5591 **Epidemiological history:** travel to a natural focus, **a tick/louse/flea bite**, contact with livestock, swimming in a water body, occupation.
s5592 **Determine the syndrome:** fever + rash → rickettsiosis; **recurrent waves of fever** → relapsing fever; **migrating erythema** at the site of a tick bite → Lyme disease; fever + calf muscle pain + jaundice → leptospirosis; fever + atypical pneumonia + contact with livestock → Q fever.

s5594 - in relapsing fever — **a thick drop and smear at the peak of fever**;
s5595 - in leptospirosis — **blood** in week 1, **urine** from week 2, **serum** in parallel;
s5596 - in rickettsioses — **paired sera** and PCR in the early period.

s5599 **Epidemiological measure:** identifying the focus, vector control, monitoring of contacts.

s5711 Influenza virus (*Orthomyxoviridae*)
s5712 **Structure:** a medium-sized (80–120 nm), **enveloped (with a supercapsid)** virus; the genome is **single-stranded, negative-sense, segmented RNA** (8 segments in types A and B). The nucleocapsid has **helical** symmetry.

s5715 **Main antigens:**
s5716 **Internal antigens** — nucleoprotein (NP) and matrix protein (M): they determine the **type (A, B, C)** and are stable;
s5717 **Surface antigens (spikes):**
s5718 - **Hemagglutinin (H)** — provides binding to the cell receptor (sialic acid) and agglutinates erythrocytes; **the main protective antigen** — neutralizing antibodies form against it;
s5719 - **Neuraminidase (N)** — provides the release of new virions from the cell and their spread through mucus; the target of several antiviral drugs.
s5720 **Types:** A (in humans and animals; capable of pandemics), B (mainly in humans; epidemics), C (mild course).

s5730 **Pathogenesis:** the virus multiplies in the ciliated epithelium of the respiratory tract → epithelial necrosis and disruption of the clearance mechanism → viremia and intoxication (toxic effect, increased vascular permeability) → conditions for **secondary bacterial complications**.
s5731 **Clinical picture:** sudden onset, high fever, severe intoxication (headache, muscle pain), tracheitis (dry cough, retrosternal burning); catarrhal signs are relatively few. Complications: viral and **bacterial pneumonia** (often pneumococcus, *S. aureus*, *H. influenzae*), otitis, sinusitis, myocarditis, encephalopathy.
s5732 2.2. Diagnosis of influenza
s5733 **Material:** a nasal and throat swab/wash — **in the first 3 days** of the illness; in severe cases — material from the lower respiratory tract.
s5734 **Rapid methods:**
s5735 - **PCR (real-time)** — currently the main method: it determines the type and subtype;
s5736 - **Immunochromatographic rapid antigen tests** — fast, but less sensitive;
s5737 - **Immunofluorescence** — detecting viral antigen in epithelial cells.
s5738 **Virological method:** infection of a **chick embryo** (into the amniotic/allantoic cavity) or of a cell culture → indication by the **hemagglutination reaction (HA)** → identification by the **hemagglutination inhibition test (HI)** (topic 12). Mainly for epidemiological surveillance and for choosing vaccine strains.
s5739 **Serological method:** HI, CFT, ELISA in **paired sera** — a ≥4-fold rise in titer; of retrospective importance.
s5740 **Prophylaxis:** an annually updated **seasonal vaccine** (inactivated, subunit or live), vaccination of risk groups; nonspecific measures — hand hygiene, a mask, ventilation.
