s5100 **Occupational risk groups:** livestock breeders, shepherds, veterinary workers, workers at meat-processing plants and in the hide-and-wool industry, hunters, laboratory workers.
s5101 Anthrax — *Bacillus anthracis*
s5102 **Morphology:** a large gram-positive rod (1–1.5 × 3–10 µm) with blunt (“cut-off”) ends; in smears it forms **chains** (“bamboo cane”); **nonmotile** (rare among bacilli); it forms a **capsule** (polypeptide) in the body and a **central spore** in the external environment.
s5103 Figure 24.1
s5104 The anthrax bacillus: large gram-positive rods, encapsulated chains in a blood smear. Source: Muhamedov I.M. et al., 2019.
s5105 **Cultural properties:** aerobe; grows on ordinary media; the colonies are **R-form** — rough, with thread-like projections at the edges (“**lion’s mane**” or “Medusa head” appearance); in gelatin it grows in the shape of an **“inverted fir tree.”**
s5106 **Capsule** (polypeptide) — antiphagocytic;
s5107 **Exotoxin** — three-component: **protective antigen (PA)**, **lethal factor (LF)** and **edema factor (EF)**. Each alone is inactive; the combination of two acts: PA + EF → edema, PA + LF → tissue necrosis and death.
s5108 **Cutaneous form (the most frequent)** — a spot at the entry site → papule → vesicle → it bursts, leaving a **black eschar ulcer (carbuncle)** in the center; edema around it; **painlessness** is characteristic;
s5109 **Pulmonary form** — inhalation of spores; severe course;
s5110 **Intestinal form** — from eating infected meat; severe course.
s5111 **Material:** discharge from the ulcer and fluid under the eschar, sputum, stool, blood; material from dead animals; samples of skin, wool and meat.
s5112 **Bacterioscopy** — Gram stain and capsule stain: encapsulated, blunt-ended chains.
s5113 **Bacteriological** — culture, colony appearance (R-form), nonmotility, the “string of pearls” test (under the action of penicillin the cells become spherical), **susceptibility to bacteriophage**.
s5114 **Biological test** — in a laboratory animal (in special laboratories).
s5115 **Ascoli precipitation reaction** — detecting the heat-stable antigen in materials such as **skin, wool and meat**; the material is boiled (the antigen persists even if the bacteria are dead), then a ring reaction is set up with precipitating serum.
s5116 **Skin allergy test (anthraxin)** and PCR.
s5117 Figure 24.2
s5118 Scheme of the microbiological examination in anthrax: bacterioscopic, bacteriological, biological and serological examinations. Source: Aliyev Sh.R. et al., 2013.
s5119 **Prophylaxis:** veterinary control and vaccination of livestock, destruction of material from sick animals by the prescribed procedure, vaccination of risk groups (live vaccine), occupational hygiene.
s5120 **Causative agents:** *Brucella melitensis* (sheep, goats — the most pathogenic for humans), *B. abortus* (cattle), *B. suis* (pigs), *B. canis* (dogs).
s5121 **Morphology:** very small gram-negative coccobacilli; nonmotile, nonspore-forming; with a microcapsule.
s5122 Figure 24.3
s5123 Brucellae — very small gram-negative coccobacilli. Source: Muhamedov I.M. et al., 2019.
s5124 **Cultural properties:** aerobes, **fastidious and slow-growing** — growth may take 5–30 days; some species (*B. abortus*) require **additional CO₂ (5–10%)** for growth; they are grown in liver-broth and special media.
s5125 **Routes of transmission:** unpasteurized milk and dairy products (especially fresh cheese, cream), direct contact with a sick animal and its birth products, aerosol (in laboratories and on livestock farms).
s5126 **Pathogenesis:** brucellae spread through the lymphatic pathways and **live inside macrophages** (incomplete phagocytosis) → **granulomas** form in organs of the reticuloendothelial system (lymph nodes, liver, spleen, bone marrow) → repeated release into the blood produces **undulant fever**. Delayed-type hypersensitivity develops.
s5127 **Clinical picture:** prolonged **undulant fever**, profuse sweating, joint and muscle pain, enlargement of the lymph nodes, liver and spleen; when it becomes chronic — arthritis, spondylitis, damage to the nervous system and reproductive organs.
s5128 **Bacteriological** — blood and bone marrow aspirate are cultured; because growth is slow, it is observed for **up to 30 days**. Because of the high hazard it is done only in a special laboratory.
s5129 **Serological (the main one in practice):**
s5130 - **Wright reaction** — extended agglutination (in a tube), with a titer;
s5131 - **Huddleson reaction** — rapid screening on a glass slide;
s5132 - **Coombs reaction** — detecting incomplete antibodies (in the chronic form);
s5133 - ELISA (IgM/IgG) — a modern method.
s5134 **Skin allergy test (Bürnet test)** — with brucellin; it shows sensitization and is important in the chronic form.
s5135 **PCR** — fast and safer.
s5136 Working with a Brucella culture is **highly hazardous for laboratory staff**: it is transmitted by aerosol. Therefore when brucellosis is suspected the laboratory is warned in advance and the work is carried out under a special regime.
s5137 Independent work: Plague and tularemia
s5138 5.1. Plague — *Yersinia pestis*
s5139 **Morphology:** a gram-negative, short, oval rod; it **stains bipolarly** (both ends darker — a “safety pin” appearance, clearly seen with Romanowsky–Giemsa); nonmotile, encapsulated.
s5140 Figure 24.4
s5141 The causative agent of plague — gram-negative rods that stain bipolarly. Source: Muhamedov I.M. et al., 2019.
s5142 **Epidemiology:** a natural-focal, **vector-borne** infection; the reservoir is rodents, the vector is **fleas**; in the pulmonary form it spreads **from person to person by the airborne route**.
s5143 **Pathogenicity factors:** the F1 capsular antigen, the V and W antigens, “plague toxin,” plasminogen activator, LPS; they ensure resistance to phagocytosis and rapid spread.
s5144 **Clinical forms:** **bubonic** (the most frequent — severe inflammation of the lymph node nearest the entry site and a painful “bubo”), septic and **pulmonary** form (the most dangerous, spreading rapidly).
s5145 **Diagnosis:** bubo aspirate, sputum, blood, lymph nodes → bacterioscopy (bipolar rods) → culture → typing with phages → biological test; rapid methods — immunofluorescence, antigen tests, PCR. All work is done under the **especially dangerous infection** regime.
s5146 **Prophylaxis:** epidemiological surveillance in natural foci, measures against rodents and fleas, vaccination of risk groups, quarantine measures.
s5147 5.2. Tularemia — *Francisella tularensis*
s5148 **Morphology:** a very small gram-negative coccobacillus; nonmotile, encapsulated.
s5149 Figure 24.5
s5150 The causative agent of tularemia (*Francisella tularensis*) — very small gram-negative coccobacilli. Source: Muhamedov I.M. et al., 2019.
s5151 **Epidemiology:** the reservoir is rodents and hares; the routes of transmission are **very varied**: vector-borne (ticks, horseflies), contact (the skin of a sick animal), alimentary (contaminated water and food), airborne (dust). **The infectious dose is very low** — which is why it poses a high risk for laboratory staff.
s5152 **Features:** does not grow on ordinary medium — it requires a special medium **supplemented with cysteine/cystine** (a medium enriched with egg yolk); lives intracellularly.
s5153 **Clinical forms:** bubonic, ulceroglandular, oculoglandular, oropharyngeal (anginal-bubonic), pulmonary and generalized forms.
s5154 **Diagnosis (mainly by indirect methods):**
s5155 **Serological** — agglutination reaction (Wright type), PHA, ELISA; the dynamics of the titer in paired sera;
s5156 **Skin allergy test (tularin)** — becomes positive early (from day 3–5 of the illness);
s5157 **PCR** and antigen tests;
s5158 **Bacteriological method** — only in special laboratories.
s5159 **Prophylaxis:** surveillance in natural foci, protection of water sources, measures against rodents, vaccination of risk groups with the **live vaccine**.

s5183 Practical skill: procedure when a zoonosis is suspected
s5184 **Establish the epidemiological history:** occupation, contact with animals, consumption of unpasteurized milk and fresh cheese, hunting, a tick or flea bite, travel to a natural focus.
s5185 **Assess the clinical syndrome:** a painless black-eschar ulcer → anthrax; undulant fever + sweating + joint pain → brucellosis; acute fever + a painful bubo → plague/tularemia.
s5186 **Warn the laboratory in advance** — this is essential for staff safety and helps to plan the examination correctly.
s5187 **Collect the material in the prescribed manner:** a sealed, unbreakable container, double packaging, a clearly completed requisition.
s5188 **Choose the examination method:** in especially dangerous infections preference is given to **serological and molecular** methods; culture work is done only in a specially authorized laboratory.
s5189 **Notify the epidemiological service** and begin the measures for the focus.

s5271 A patient who works at a hide-and-wool plant has a painless sore on the hand: a black eschar in the center, vesicles around it and wide edema. The smear shows large gram-positive, blunt-ended, chain-forming rods.
s5272 What are the probable diagnosis and the laboratory tactics?
s5273 Cutaneous anthrax. The laboratory is **warned in advance**; the material is collected in a special manner. The examination: bacterioscopy, culture and identification in a special laboratory, the **Ascoli reaction** on raw-material samples, PCR. The epidemiological service is notified.
s5274 A livestock breeder has had undulant fever for 2 months, profuse sweating and joint pain; the spleen and liver are enlarged. The patient ate cheese made from fresh (unpasteurized) milk.
s5275 What should be considered, and which examinations are informative?
s5276 Brucellosis. In practice **serological methods** are the main ones: Huddleson (rapid screening), **Wright** (with a titer), Coombs and ELISA; the skin allergy **Bürnet** test; PCR. A blood culture is possible but slow and hazardous — only in a special laboratory.
s5277 A patient who hunted in a natural focus developed acute fever and a painful enlarged lymph node in the armpit.
s5278 Which infections should be excluded, and what is the first measure?
s5279 Plague and tularemia should be excluded (both produce a bubo). The first measure is to **notify the laboratory and the epidemiological service immediately** and to collect the material in a special manner; the examination — bacterioscopy (bipolar rods), rapid antigen tests and PCR, and in tularemia additionally serology and the tularin test. Treatment is not delayed.

s5301 **“In the primary period — the microscope; in the secondary and later periods — serology.”** This is the basic rule of syphilis diagnosis.
s5302 Rule of interpretation: **screening — nontreponemal; confirmation — treponemal; monitoring of treatment — the dynamics of the nontreponemal test titer.** If only the treponemal test is positive, it may also be a past (treated) infection.
s5303 The gonococcus dies quickly after the material is collected, so inoculation is done **as quickly as possible** (at the patient’s bedside or in transport medium). The material must not be cooled.

s5305 Syphilis — *Treponema pallidum*
s5306 **Morphology:** a delicate spirochete; **8–14 uniform, regular coils**; length 6–14 µm, thickness 0.1–0.2 µm. It takes up ordinary dyes poorly (its name means “pale”); with **Romanowsky–Giemsa** it stains pale pink, with **Morozov’s method (silver impregnation)** black-brown. Its motility is varied: rotating, flexing, undulating.
s5307 Figure 25.1
s5308 *T. pallidum* in a smear from a hard chancre and in dark field: bacterioscopic methods in syphilis. Source: Muhamedov I.M. et al., 2019.
s5309 **Cultural properties:** **it does not grow on artificial nutrient media** (pathogenic strains). Therefore the classic bacteriological method is not used; diagnosis rests on **bacterioscopy and serology**.
s5310 **Resistance:** very unstable in the external environment — sensitive to drying, high temperature, antiseptics and soap; it survives briefly when moisture and temperature are maintained.
