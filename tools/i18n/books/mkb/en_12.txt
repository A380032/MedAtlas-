s2805 Factors of innate immunity
s2806 4.1. Barriers and humoral factors
s2807 **Mechanical barriers:** intact skin, mucous membranes, movement of the ciliated epithelium, the cough and sneeze reflex, the flow of urine.
s2808 **Chemical factors:** fatty acids and sweat acids of the skin, gastric hydrochloric acid, vaginal lactic acid, bile acids.
s2809 **Biological barrier:** the normal microflora (colonization resistance — topic 10).
s2810 **Humoral factors:** **lysozyme** (breaks down peptidoglycan; abundant in tears, saliva, mucus), **complement**, **interferons**, **C-reactive protein** and other acute-phase proteins, properdin, beta-lysins, lactoferrin.
s2811 4.2. Phagocytosis
s2812 **Phagocytosis** is the process by which a cell engulfs and digests particles; it was discovered by I.I. Mechnikov.
s2813 **Phagocytes:**
s2814 **Microphages** — neutrophils (abundant in the blood, arrive quickly, short-lived);
s2815 **Macrophages** — arise from monocytes; located in tissues (Kupffer cells in the liver, alveolar macrophages in the lung, osteoclasts in bone tissue, microglia in the brain).
s2816 **Stages of phagocytosis:**
s2817 **Chemotaxis** — the movement of a phagocyte toward the object along chemical signals (bacterial products, complement C5a, cytokines).
s2818 **Adhesion (attachment)** — sticking to the object; **opsonins** (IgG antibodies, complement C3b) sharply speed up this stage.
s2819 **Endocytosis (engulfment)** — enclosure with pseudopodia, formation of a **phagosome**.
s2820 **Formation of the phagolysosome** — fusion with a lysosome.
s2821 **Digestion (killing)** — oxygen-dependent mechanisms (the oxidative burst: superoxide, hydrogen peroxide, hypochlorite) and oxygen-independent mechanisms (lysozyme, defensins, lactoferrin, proteases).
s2822 **By outcome:**
s2823 **Complete phagocytosis** — the microbe is digested;
s2824 **Incomplete phagocytosis** — the microbe stays alive inside the phagocyte and even multiplies.

s2847 4.4. Interferons and NK cells
s2848 **Interferons (IFN)** are the main innate factor of antiviral defense; they are protein cytokines. Types: **IFN-α** (leukocyte) and **IFN-β** (fibroblast) — mainly antiviral; **IFN-γ** (immune) — mainly regulates immunity and activates macrophages.
s2849 **Mechanism of action:** interferon does not kill the virus directly; acting on **neighboring cells**, it switches on enzymes in them that stop the synthesis of viral nucleic acid and proteins — the cell enters a “resistant” state against the virus.
s2850 **NK (natural killer) cells** destroy virus-infected cells and tumor cells in an antigen-independent manner. They detect **a decrease in MHC class I molecules** on the surface of the target cell (“missing-self recognition”): many viruses and tumors try to hide MHC I, and NK cells kill exactly such cells. They release perforin and granzymes and trigger apoptosis.

s2871 Independent work: Theories of immunity and class I HLA molecules
s2872 **Development of the theories:**
s2873 **Phagocytic (cellular) theory** — I.I. Mechnikov (1883): the basis of defense is phagocytes.
s2874 **Humoral theory** — P. Ehrlich: defense is due to antibodies in the blood; the “side-chain” theory was the first attempt to explain antibody formation.
s2875 **Instructive (template) theories** — assumed that the antigen acts as a “template” in antibody synthesis; later rejected.
s2876 **Clonal selection theory** — F. Burnet (1957): every lymphocyte has **in advance** a receptor matching one specific antigen; the antigen **selects** the matching clone and expands it. This is the foundation of modern immunology and explains both immunological memory and tolerance to self (clones that respond to the body’s own tissue are eliminated during maturation).
s2877 The theories of Mechnikov and Ehrlich do not exclude each other — it was later confirmed that they are the two arms of immunity (cellular and humoral); both scientists shared the Nobel Prize in 1908.
s2878 **Major histocompatibility complex class I (HLA-A, -B, -C).** These molecules are encoded on chromosome 6 and are extremely **polymorphic** (almost every person has a unique set). Structure: a heavy chain (α1, α2, α3 domains) + **β₂-microglobulin**; the peptide-binding groove lies between the α1 and α2 domains and holds a peptide of 8–10 amino acids.
s2879 **Functions:**
s2880 presenting peptides synthesized inside the cell (viral, tumor-specific) to **CD8⁺ cytotoxic T lymphocytes**;
s2881 serving as a “self-recognition” signal for NK cells (if MHC I decreases, the NK cell kills the cell);
s2882 determining tissue compatibility in **transplantation** — donor and recipient are matched by HLA;
s2883 some HLA alleles are linked to susceptibility to particular diseases (for example, HLA-B27 and certain rheumatologic diseases).
s2884 A virus multiplies inside the cell, so the main defense against it is the **MHC I + CD8⁺ T lymphocyte** pathway. For this very reason, antibodies alone are not enough in intracellular infections.
