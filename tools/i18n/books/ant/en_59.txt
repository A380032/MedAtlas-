s7211 The **external jugular vein** is formed below the auricle from the posterior auricular vein and the posterior branch of the retromandibular vein, descends obliquely **over the sternocleidomastoid** (under the platysma) and drains into the subclavian vein; it bulges on coughing or in heart failure. The **anterior jugular veins** begin below the hyoid bone and unite in the suprasternal space by the **jugular venous arch**.
s7212 The **subclavian vein** is the continuation of the axillary vein; on the 1st rib it passes **in front of the anterior scalene muscle** (the artery lies behind the muscle); because it adheres to the fasciae its lumen is always open — the site for **central venous catheterization**.
s7213 The **azygos vein** (the continuation of the right ascending lumbar vein) and the **hemiazygos vein** (on the left) ascend along the vertebral column in the posterior mediastinum; the **posterior intercostal**, esophageal, bronchial and mediastinal veins drain into them. The hemiazygos vein crosses to the right at the level of T~VII~–T~X~ and drains into the azygos vein; the azygos vein arches over the right bronchus and drains into the superior vena cava at the level of T~IV~. They form an important **cava-caval** route between the superior and inferior venae cavae.

s7216 The **dural venous sinuses** are valveless venous channels between the layers of the dura mater whose walls do not collapse; they collect blood from the brain, the eye, the inner ear, the skull bones and the meninges and carry it mainly to the **internal jugular vein**. The **superior sagittal** (in the upper border of the falx cerebri; cerebrospinal fluid is absorbed into the blood through the arachnoid **granulations**), **inferior sagittal**, **straight** (receives the great cerebral vein) and **occipital** sinuses meet at the **confluence of sinuses** (at the level of the internal occipital protuberance); from it the **transverse** sinuses → the **sigmoid** sinuses → the jugular foramen → the internal jugular vein. s7219 **Veins of the upper limb**: the **superficial veins** — the **cephalic vein** (*v. cephalica*; lateral side of the forearm, lateral bicipital groove, through the deltopectoral groove to the axillary vein) and the **basilic vein** (*v. basilica*; medial side of the forearm, pierces the fascia in the middle of the arm to join the brachial vein); in the cubital fossa they are connected by the **median cubital vein** — the main site for **venipuncture** and intravenous injection. The **deep veins** — the paired radial, ulnar and brachial veins accompanying the arteries → the **axillary vein** → the subclavian vein.
s7220 3. The inferior vena cava system
s7221 The **inferior vena cava** (*v. cava inferior*) is the largest vein; it is formed by the union of the right and left **common iliac veins** at the level of L~IV~–L~V~ (to the right of and below the aortic bifurcation), ascends to the right of the aorta, lies in a groove on the posterior surface of the liver, passes into the thoracic cavity through the **opening in the central tendon** of the diaphragm (T~VIII~) and drains into the **right atrium**. Its tributaries:
s7222 **Parietal**: the **lumbar veins** (4 pairs; connected with the ascending lumbar veins), the **inferior phrenic veins**.
s7223 **Visceral**: the **testicular (ovarian) veins** (the right one drains into the inferior vena cava, **the left one into the left renal vein** at a right angle), the **renal veins** (the left one is long and passes in front of the aorta; it receives the left testicular and left suprarenal veins), the **suprarenal veins** (the right one directly), the **hepatic veins** (3–4; they carry the blood that arrived through the portal vein and hepatic artery out of the liver into the inferior vena cava on the posterior surface of the liver).
s7224 **Common iliac veins** = the **internal iliac vein** (pelvic walls and organs; the **rectal**, **vesical** and **prostatic/uterovaginal venous plexuses**) + the **external iliac vein** (the continuation of the femoral vein; the inferior epigastric vein).
s7225 **Veins of the lower limb**: **superficial** — the **great saphenous vein** (*v. saphena magna*; **in front of** the medial malleolus — the site for venesection; along the medial surface of the leg and thigh, it drains into the **femoral vein** through the **saphenous opening** 3–4 cm below the inguinal ligament) and the **small saphenous vein** (from behind the lateral malleolus, along the posterior surface of the leg to the **popliteal vein**); **deep** — the paired veins of the foot and leg → the **popliteal** → the **femoral vein**; they are connected by **perforating veins**.

s7228 4. The hepatic portal vein system
s7229 The **portal vein** (*v. portae hepatis*, 5–6 cm long) carries blood from the **unpaired organs** of the abdominal cavity (the stomach, the small and large intestine as far as the upper part of the rectum, the spleen, the pancreas, the gallbladder) **to the liver**. It is formed behind the head of the pancreas (at the level of **L~II~**) by the union of the **superior mesenteric** and **splenic veins** (the inferior mesenteric vein usually drains into the splenic vein); it ascends in the **hepatoduodenal ligament** behind the common bile duct and the proper hepatic artery and divides at the porta hepatis into right and left branches; within the liver: **interlobular veins** → **sinusoidal capillaries** → **central veins** → **hepatic veins** → the inferior vena cava. The **left and right gastric veins**, the **cystic vein** and the **paraumbilical veins** (along the round ligament of the liver) drain directly into it.

s7258 6. The fetal circulation
s7259 The fetus obtains oxygen and nutrients from the mother's blood through the **placenta**; its lungs do not function. The course of the blood:
s7260 Arterial (oxygen-rich) blood from the placenta comes through the **umbilical vein** (unpaired), within the umbilical cord, to the porta hepatis.
s7261 Part of the blood passes into the portal vein (to the liver), but most of it bypasses the liver through the **ductus venosus (of Arantius)** and drains into the **inferior vena cava**, where it mixes with venous blood from the lower part of the body.
s7262 Blood arriving in the right atrium from the inferior vena cava is directed by the **valve of the inferior vena cava** (Eustachian valve) through the **foramen ovale** into the **left atrium** → left ventricle → aorta; through the branches of the aortic arch the relatively oxygen-rich blood goes **to the head and upper limbs** (which is why they are relatively well developed).
s7263 Venous blood from the superior vena cava passes to the right atrium → right ventricle → pulmonary trunk; a small part of the blood goes to the lungs, and most of it passes through the **ductus arteriosus (of Botallo)** into the part of the aorta beyond the arch (the descending aorta).
s7264 The mixed blood in the descending aorta goes to the lower part of the body and returns to the placenta through the two **umbilical arteries** arising from the internal iliac arteries.

s7286 7. The lymphatic system
s7287 The **lymphatic system** absorbs tissue fluid to form **lymph** and returns it to the venous system; it carries **fats** (chylomicrons) from the intestine, filters the lymph in the lymph nodes (a barrier) and produces lymphocytes. Its components: **lymphatic capillaries** (closed at one end, with a wall of a single layer of endothelium; absent from the brain, cartilage, cornea, lens, epithelium, splenic parenchyma and bone marrow) → **lymphatic vessels** (with many valves, "beaded"; superficial and deep) → **lymph nodes** → **lymphatic trunks** → **lymphatic ducts** → the **venous angles**.
s7288 **Lymphatic trunks** (paired): **jugular** (head and neck), **subclavian** (upper limb), **bronchomediastinal** (thoracic organs), **lumbar** (lower limbs, pelvis, abdominal wall, paired organs); unpaired — the **intestinal trunk** (unpaired abdominal organs).
s7289 The **thoracic duct** (*ductus thoracicus*, 30–41 cm long) is formed at the level of T~XII~–L~II~ by the union of the right and left lumbar trunks (and the intestinal trunk); at its start is the dilated **cisterna chyli**; it passes through the **aortic hiatus** of the diaphragm into the posterior mediastinum, ascends between the aorta and the azygos vein, deviates to the left at the level of T~V~–T~VII~, forms an **arch** at the level of C~VI~–C~VII~ and drains into the **left venous angle**. It collects lymph from **3/4 of the body** — both lower limbs, the abdomen, the pelvis, the left half of the thorax, the left upper limb and the left half of the head; it has 7–9 valves.
s7290 The **right lymphatic duct** (10–12 mm; often absent — the trunks drain separately) is formed from the right jugular, right subclavian and right bronchomediastinal trunks; it drains into the **right venous angle** and collects lymph from the upper right **1/4 of the body**.
s7291 **Lymph nodes** (500–1000 in number, with a total mass of ≈1 % of body mass) are bean-shaped, 1–15 mm; 4–6 **afferent** lymphatic vessels enter on the convex side, and 2–4 **efferent** lymphatic vessels leave from the **hilum** on the concave side. They have a capsule, trabeculae, a **cortex** (lymphoid follicles — B lymphocytes), a **paracortical zone** (T lymphocytes, postcapillary venules) and a **medulla** (medullary cords); lymph passes through the **sinuses** (subcapsular → cortical → medullary → hilar sinus). Groups: the superficial and deep cervical, submandibular, submental, axillary, cubital, inguinal, popliteal, mediastinal, bronchopulmonary, abdominal, iliac and others.
