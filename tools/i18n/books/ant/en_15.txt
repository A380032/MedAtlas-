s1792 3 days ago a patient had the upper left first molar extracted. Now there is a feeling of heaviness in the left cheek, purulent discharge from the left side of the nose and fever. When drinking water, fluid comes out of the left nostril.
s1793 This is **odontogenic maxillary sinusitis** with an **oroantral fistula** (perforation). The roots of the upper first and second molars and the second premolar lie very close to the **floor of the maxillary sinus** (sometimes separated from it only by mucosa). When the tooth was extracted, the floor of the sinus was perforated and the oral cavity came to communicate with the maxillary sinus; fluid passes from the sinus through the **maxillary hiatus** into the middle nasal meatus and from there out through the nose. Infection entered the sinus and inflammation developed.
s1794 After a blow to the chin, a boxer cannot close his mouth completely, his teeth do not meet properly (the bite is disturbed), there is pain in front of his left ear, and numbness is felt in the left half of the lower lip.
s1795 Where in the mandible can fractures occur? What is the numbness of the lip related to?
s1796 With a blow to the chin, the mandible often breaks **in two places**: at the site of impact (the chin or canine region) and at the **neck of the condylar process** on the opposite or the same side (pain in front of the ear). Numbness of the lower lip is due to injury of the **inferior alveolar nerve**: it enters the canal through the mandibular foramen, runs within the body and emerges from the **mental foramen** as the mental nerve (lower lip and skin of the chin). Because the fracture line passes through the canal, the nerve has been crushed or stretched.
s1797 To treat a lower left molar, a dentist performed an inferior alveolar nerve block. After the injection the left half of the lower lip, the teeth and the left anterior 2/3 of the tongue became numb.
s1798 Where was the anesthetic injected, and why did the tongue also become numb?
s1799 The anesthetic was injected on the inner surface of the mandibular ramus, in the region of the **mandibular foramen and lingula** — where the inferior alveolar nerve enters the canal. Here the **lingual nerve** also passes slightly anterior and medial to the inferior alveolar nerve, so it is anesthetized too — numbness appears in the anterior 2/3 of the tongue. The inferior alveolar nerve supplies all the lower teeth of that side, and through the mental nerve the lower lip and the skin of the chin.

s1829 When the calvaria is fractured, the **inner table** breaks over a wider area and into more fragments than the outer table (it is more brittle) — so even with an injury that looks small from outside, bone fragments inside may damage the meninges and brain. Through the diploic veins, infection of the scalp can spread into the cranial cavity (emissary and diploic veins).
s1830 The cranial nerves leave the cranial base in the order **"anterior fossa — 1, middle — 2 to 6, posterior — 7 to 12"**: anterior fossa — CN I; middle fossa — II (optic canal), III, IV, V1, VI (superior fissure), V2 (foramen rotundum), V3 (foramen ovale); posterior fossa — VII, VIII (internal acoustic opening), IX, X, XI (jugular foramen), XII (hypoglossal canal).
s1831 Fractures of the cranial base present differently depending on which fossa is injured: **anterior fossa** — "raccoon eyes" (periorbital bruising), CSF leak from the nose, loss of smell; **middle fossa** — blood or CSF leaking from the ear, bruising over the mastoid process (Battle's sign), injury to CN VII and VIII (facial palsy, deafness); **posterior fossa** — bruising in the occipital region and neck, injury to CN IX–XII (disturbed swallowing and speech), the most dangerous because of proximity to the vital centers.
s1832 When a blunt blow (a ball, a fist) strikes the eye region, pressure inside the orbit rises sharply and the thinnest walls break — the **inferior wall** (into the maxillary sinus) or the **medial wall** (into the ethmoidal cells) — a **"blow-out" fracture** (*blow-out*). Orbital tissue and the inferior rectus muscle become trapped in the fracture, causing inability to look upward and double vision; injury to the infraorbital nerve causes numbness of the cheek and upper lip.
s1833 The middle nasal meatus is the "crossroads" of the paranasal sinuses: **three** sinuses open into it — the frontal, maxillary, and anterior and middle ethmoidal cells. So swelling of the mucosa in this meatus (rhinitis) can block the outflow of several sinuses at once and cause **sinusitis**.
s1834 Remember the **7** routes of the pterygopalatine fossa as "**C-O-N-M-I-P and L**": **C**ranial cavity (foramen rotundum), **O**rbit (inferior orbital fissure), **N**asal cavity (sphenopalatine foramen), **M**outh (greater palatine canal), **I**nfratemporal fossa (pterygomaxillary fissure), **P**harynx (pharyngeal canal) and the foramen **L**acerum region (pterygoid canal).
s1835 Because of the many communications of the pterygopalatine fossa, infections and tumors in this region (for example, juvenile nasopharyngeal angiofibroma in young people) can spread into the nasal cavity, orbit, infratemporal fossa and cranial cavity. Block of the pterygopalatine ganglion is used in some types of headache; maxillary nerve block is performed through the greater palatine canal in operations on the maxilla.
s1836 The shape of a newborn's head may change during delivery (the skull bones overlap one another — **molding**, caput succedaneum) — this resolves within a few days. In a child who always lies on the back, the occiput flattens (positional deformity); it must be distinguished from **craniosynostosis** (premature closure of sutures) — in craniosynostosis the head shape changes characteristically along a particular suture, and surgery is required.
s1837 1. Views of the skull from different sides
s1838 **From above** (*norma verticalis*) the skull is ovoid; it shows the **frontal**, two **parietal** and the **occipital** bones and the **coronal**, **sagittal** and **lambdoid** sutures joining them. The points where sutures meet are used in craniometry: **bregma** (intersection of the coronal and sagittal sutures — site of the anterior fontanelle) and **lambda** (intersection of the sagittal and lambdoid sutures — site of the posterior fontanelle).
s1839 **From the front** (*norma frontalis*) one sees the frontal bone, the openings (aditus) of the two orbits, between them the **piriform aperture** (*apertura piriformis*) of the nasal cavity, and below the alveolar processes of the maxilla and mandible with the teeth. **From behind** one sees the occipital squama, the external occipital protuberance, the nuchal lines, the lambdoid suture and the mastoid processes.

s1842 **From the side** (*norma lateralis*) the wide **temporal fossa** (*fossa temporalis*) is seen: it is bounded above by the **temporal line** and below by the **zygomatic arch**, and the temporalis muscle lies in it. On the wall of the temporal fossa, the H-shaped area where the parietal, frontal and temporal bones and the greater wing of the sphenoid meet — the **pterion** — is one of the thinnest places of the skull. Below the zygomatic arch lies the **infratemporal fossa** (*fossa infratemporalis*), and deeper to it the **pterygopalatine fossa** (*fossa pterygopalatina*).

s1845 2. The calvaria
s1846 The bones of the calvaria (*calvaria*) consist of two compact tables — the **outer** (*lamina externa*) and the more brittle **inner table** (*lamina interna, lamina vitrea* — "glassy") — and the spongy substance between them, the **diploe**; the diploe contains **diploic canals** through which veins pass. On the inner surface, along the midline, there is the **groove for the superior sagittal sinus**; beside it are **granular foveolae** (*foveolae granulares*) formed by arachnoid granulations, as well as **arterial grooves** (branches of the middle meningeal artery) and impressions of the cerebral gyri — **digitate impressions**.

s1849 3. The internal cranial base
s1850 The **internal cranial base** (*basis cranii interna*) is divided into three step-like fossae: the **anterior**, **middle** and **posterior cranial fossae**. The boundary between the anterior and middle fossae is the posterior borders of the lesser wings of the sphenoid and the tuberculum sellae; the boundary between the middle and posterior fossae is the superior borders of the petrous parts of the temporal bones and the dorsum sellae.
