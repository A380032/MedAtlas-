s6100 2. External structure and position of the heart
s6101 The **heart** (*cor*) is a hollow, four-chambered muscular organ; it is cone-shaped and roughly the size of its owner's fist. It is 12–13 cm long, 9–10.5 cm wide and 6–7 cm anteroposteriorly, and in adults weighs **≈250–350 g** (more in men). The heart lies in the **middle mediastinum**, within the pericardium: **2/3 to the left of the midline** and 1/3 to the right. Its longitudinal axis is oblique — running from above downward, from right to left and from back to front.
s6102 The **base** (*basis cordis*) faces upward, to the right and backward; the atria and the great vessels (aorta, pulmonary trunk, venae cavae and pulmonary veins) are here.
s6103 The **apex** (*apex cordis*) faces downward, to the left and forward; it lies in the left 5th intercostal space, 1–1.5 cm medial to the midclavicular line (the apex beat).
s6104 **Surfaces**: the anterior **sternocostal** (mainly the right ventricle), the inferior **diaphragmatic** (mainly the left ventricle) and the lateral **pulmonary** surfaces; the **right** (sharp) and **left** (blunt) borders.
s6105 **Grooves**: the **coronary sulcus** (the boundary between the atria and ventricles; the coronary arteries and coronary sinus lie in it), the **anterior** and **posterior interventricular sulci** (the projection of the interventricular septum); they meet to the right of the apex at the **notch of the cardiac apex**.
s6106 The **auricles** are anterior projections of the atria; the right auricle embraces the aorta and the left auricle the pulmonary trunk from the left.

s6111 3. The chambers of the heart
s6112 The heart is divided by the **interatrial** and **interventricular septa** into right (venous blood) and left (arterial blood) halves; each half consists of an **atrium** and a **ventricle**, which communicate through the **atrioventricular orifices**.

s6137 4. The valves of the heart
s6138 The valves are **folds (duplications) of the endocardium** that ensure one-way blood flow. **Atrioventricular (cuspid) valves**: the **right — tricuspid** (anterior, posterior and septal cusps) and the **left — bicuspid, mitral** (anterior and posterior cusps). The bases of the cusps are attached to the **fibrous ring**, and their free edges are connected by **chordae tendineae** to the **papillary muscles** — when the ventricle contracts (systole) the cusps close, and the chordae prevent them from everting into the atrium.
s6139 **Semilunar valves** — at the orifices of the **pulmonary trunk** and **aorta**, each with **3** pocket-like cusps (in the pulmonary trunk — anterior, right, left; in the aorta — the **posterior (noncoronary)**, **right** and **left coronary** cusps). Each cusp has a **nodule** in the middle of its free edge and **lunules** at the sides; between the cusp and the vessel wall lies a **sinus**. The **right and left coronary arteries** arise from the aortic sinuses. When the ventricles relax (diastole), the returning blood fills the pockets and closes the valve.

s6142 5. The heart wall and the fibrous skeleton
s6143 The **endocardium** is the inner layer: endothelium and connective tissue; it lines the chambers, valves and chordae tendineae.
s6144 The **myocardium** is the middle and thickest layer: **striated cardiac muscle tissue** (cardiomyocytes joined by intercalated discs form a functional syncytium; it contracts involuntarily). The **atrial** myocardium has 2 layers: superficial (common to both atria, transverse) and deep (separate for each, longitudinal and encircling the venous openings). The **ventricular** myocardium has 3 layers: outer oblique and inner longitudinal (common; at the apex they pass into each other forming the **vortex cordis**; the papillary muscles and trabeculae derive from the inner layer) and a middle circular layer (separate for each ventricle). The atrial and ventricular myocardium are separated by **fibrous rings** — they are connected only through the conducting system.
s6145 The **epicardium** is the outer layer: the visceral layer of the serous pericardium (mesothelium + connective tissue, beneath which lie fat and the coronary vessels).
s6146 The **cardiac skeleton** — the right and left **fibrous rings**, the aortic and pulmonary rings, the **right and left fibrous trigones** between them, and the membranous part of the interventricular septum; it supports the valves and myocardium and acts as an electrical insulator.
s6147 6. The conducting system of the heart
s6148 The **conducting system** consists of **atypical cardiomyocytes** (few myofibrils, much sarcoplasm) that generate impulses automatically in the heart and conduct them to the myocardium:
s6149 The **sinoatrial node** (Keith–Flack; *nodus sinuatrialis*) — in the wall of the right atrium, **between the opening of the superior vena cava and the right auricle** (the upper part of the sulcus terminalis, under the epicardium); the **1st-order pacemaker**, 60–80 impulses per minute.
s6150 The interatrial bundles (anterior — Bachmann's, middle, posterior) carry the impulse to the atria and to the lower node.
s6151 The **atrioventricular node** (Aschoff–Tawara; *nodus atrioventricularis*) — in the lower part of the interatrial septum, in front of the opening of the coronary sinus (Koch's triangle); it delays the impulse slightly (so that the atria contract before the ventricles); 40–60 impulses.
s6152 The **atrioventricular bundle** (bundle of His) — pierces the right fibrous trigone, runs along the membranous part of the interventricular septum and in its muscular part divides into the **right and left bundle branches** (the right branch reaches the anterior papillary muscle via the septomarginal trabecula).
s6153 The **Purkinje fibers** (subendocardial branches) spread into the ventricular myocardium, first to the papillary muscles.

s6156 7. Blood supply and innervation of the heart
s6157 The **right coronary artery** (*a. coronaria dextra*) arises from the right aortic sinus and runs under the right auricle along the coronary sulcus to the right and backward; its branches: the **conus branch**, the **sinoatrial nodal branch** (in ≈60% of cases), the **right marginal branch**, the **posterior interventricular branch** (along the posterior sulcus to the apex) and the atrioventricular nodal branch. It supplies the right atrium and ventricle, part of the posterior wall of the left ventricle, the posterior 1/3 of the interventricular septum and both nodes.
s6158 The **left coronary artery** (*a. coronaria sinistra*) arises from the left aortic sinus, passes between the pulmonary trunk and the left auricle and soon divides in two: the **anterior interventricular branch** (along the anterior sulcus to the apex; septal branches — the anterior 2/3 of the interventricular septum; diagonal branches) and the **circumflex branch** (along the coronary sulcus to the left and backward; the left marginal branch). The left coronary artery supplies the left atrium, most of the left ventricle, the anterior part of the septum and part of the anterior wall of the right ventricle. The coronary arteries fill during diastole (in systole their openings are partly covered by the cusps and the myocardium compresses the vessels).

s6163 **Cardiac veins**: the main ones drain into the **coronary sinus** (in the posterior part of the coronary sulcus, opening into the right atrium) — the **great cardiac vein** (in the anterior interventricular sulcus, then in the coronary sulcus), the **middle cardiac vein** (in the posterior interventricular sulcus), the **small cardiac vein** (in the right coronary sulcus), the **posterior veins of the left ventricle** and the **oblique vein of the left atrium**. The **anterior veins of the right ventricle** and the **smallest veins** (Thebesian) open directly into the chambers. Lymph flows from networks in the heart wall to the mediastinal nodes.
s6164 **Innervation**: **sympathetic** fibers — from the cervical ganglia of the sympathetic trunk (superior, middle and inferior cervical cardiac nerves) and from thoracic ganglia II–V (they accelerate and strengthen cardiac contraction and dilate the coronary arteries); **parasympathetic** — the cardiac branches of the **vagus nerve** (slow the rhythm). They form the **superficial cardiac plexus** below the aortic arch and the **deep cardiac plexus** between the aortic arch and the tracheal bifurcation; from these arise the coronary plexuses and the plexuses in the heart wall (with cardiac ganglia). **Sensory** fibers carry pain impulses via the sympathetic nerves to spinal cord segments T~I~–T~V~.
s6165 8. The pericardium
s6166 The **pericardium** (pericardial sac) is a closed sac surrounding the heart. The **fibrous pericardium** is the outer dense layer; at its base it blends with the adventitia of the great vessels, below it is attached to the central tendon of the diaphragm, and in front to the sternum by the **sternopericardial ligaments**. The **serous pericardium** — its **parietal layer** lines the fibrous pericardium internally, and its **visceral layer** is the epicardium; they pass into each other at the base of the great vessels, and between them is the **pericardial cavity**, containing a small amount (20–50 mL) of serous fluid. Its parts: the **sternocostal**, **diaphragmatic** and right and left **mediastinal** parts (at the sides run the phrenic nerves and the pericardiacophrenic vessels; behind are the esophagus, thoracic aorta, and azygos and hemiazygos veins).
