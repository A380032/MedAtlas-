s5616 For spermatogenesis the testis must be in the scrotum, at a temperature ≈2–3 °C below body temperature (the dartos and cremaster muscles and the pampiniform plexus — a "heat exchanger" — regulate the temperature). In **cryptorchidism** spermatogenesis is impaired and the risk of tumors increases — surgery (orchiopexy) before 1 year of age is recommended. If the processus vaginalis remains patent — **congenital indirect inguinal hernia** or **hydrocele of the testis**. Dilation of the veins of the pampiniform plexus is **varicocele** (more often on the left, because the left testicular vein drains into the left renal vein at a right angle). The **cremasteric reflex** — elevation of the testis on stroking the inner surface of the thigh (L~I~–L~II~).
s5617 **Ectopic pregnancy** most often develops in the **ampulla** of the uterine tube; when the tube ruptures there is severe bleeding into the peritoneal cavity (the blood collects in the pouch of Douglas). Because the uterine tube opens into the peritoneal cavity, genital tract infections in women can lead to **pelvic peritonitis**. When the pelvic floor muscles weaken (multiple deliveries), **prolapse of the uterus and vaginal walls** occurs. The uterine artery crosses **in front of and above the ureter** in the parametrium — a risk of ureteric injury in hysterectomy.
s5618 During childbirth the perineal tissues are stretched and may **tear**; to prevent this an **episiotomy** (controlled incision of the perineum) is performed — the incision is usually made in a mediolateral direction to avoid injuring the perineal body and the anal sphincter. When the pelvic floor muscles weaken, urinary incontinence and prolapse of organs occur; special exercises (Kegel exercises) are recommended to strengthen them.
s5619 1. Development of the genital organs
s5620 In the early stages of development the genital organs are **the same in both sexes (indifferent)**. The **gonads** develop in the 4th–5th week from the epithelium and mesenchyme of the **genital ridge** on the medial surface of the mesonephros; the primordial germ cells migrate from the wall of the yolk sac. Two pairs of ducts form: the **mesonephric (Wolffian) duct** and the **paramesonephric (Müllerian) duct**. If a Y chromosome (SRY gene) is present, from the 7th week the gonad becomes a **testis**; the testis produces testosterone (for development of the Wolffian duct) and **anti-Müllerian hormone** (causing regression of the Müllerian ducts). If there is no Y chromosome, the gonad becomes an **ovary**, and the Wolffian duct regresses.

s5644 2. Male genital organs: the testis and epididymis
s5645 The **male internal genital organs** are the testis, epididymis, ductus deferens, seminal vesicles, ejaculatory duct, prostate and bulbourethral glands; the **external** organs are the penis and scrotum (the male urethra was considered in topic 22).

s5648 The **testis** (*testis*, Greek *orchis*) is a paired **mixed gland**: it produces **spermatozoa** and secretes **testosterone** (Leydig cells). It lies in the scrotum, the left testis slightly lower; it is oval, ≈4 cm long, ≈3 cm wide and ≈2 cm thick, and weighs 20–30 g. It has medial and lateral surfaces, anterior and posterior borders, and upper and lower poles; the epididymis is attached to its posterior border. It is covered externally by the strong **tunica albuginea**; at the posterior border this thickens to form the **mediastinum testis**, from which radial **septula** divide the testis into **250–300 lobules**. Each lobule contains 2–3 **convoluted seminiferous tubules** (70–80 cm long; their wall contains spermatogenic epithelium and Sertoli cells); they continue as the **straight tubules** → **rete testis** (in the mediastinum) → 12–15 **efferent ductules** into the epididymis.
s5649 The **epididymis** (*epididymis*) lies along the posterior border of the testis; it has a **head** (conical lobules formed by the efferent ductules), a **body** and a **tail**. Its single, highly coiled **duct** is **6–8 m** long; spermatozoa travel through it for ≈2 weeks, mature and acquire the ability to fertilize; they are stored in its tail. Rudiments are found on the testis and epididymis — the **appendix of the testis** (a remnant of the Müllerian duct) and the **appendix of the epididymis** (a remnant of the Wolffian duct).

s5654 3. The seminal ducts and the accessory genital glands
s5655 The **ductus deferens** (*ductus deferens*) is the continuation of the duct of the epididymis; it is **40–50 cm** long, with a thick muscular wall (it feels like a firm cord on palpation) and a lumen of ≈0.5 mm. Its parts: the **scrotal part**, the **funicular part** (in the spermatic cord), the **inguinal part** (in the inguinal canal) and the **pelvic part** — leaving the deep inguinal ring, it descends along the lateral pelvic wall, **crosses in front of and above the ureter**, and at the fundus of the bladder widens to form the **ampulla**. The ampulla joins the duct of the **seminal vesicle** to form the **ejaculatory duct** (≈2 cm); this pierces the prostate and opens on the **seminal colliculus** in the prostatic part of the urethra.
s5656 The **seminal vesicles** (*glandulae vesiculosae*) are paired, coiled tubular glands (≈5 cm long) lying behind the fundus of the bladder, lateral to the ampullae; they produce most of the seminal fluid (rich in fructose). The **prostate** (*prostata*) is a chestnut-shaped glandular-muscular organ (weighing ≈20 g) lying below the bladder and surrounding the initial part of the urethra; its **base** faces the bladder and its **apex** the urogenital diaphragm; its **anterior surface** faces the pubic symphysis, and its **posterior surface** is in contact with the rectal ampulla (it is palpated through the rectum). It has **right and left lobes** and between them an **isthmus (middle lobe)**; the ducts of 30–50 glandules open around the seminal colliculus; its muscle tissue takes part in the involuntary sphincter of the urethra. The **bulbourethral (Cowper's) glands** are pea-sized, lying behind the membranous part of the urethra within the urogenital diaphragm; their ducts open into the spongy part of the urethra.

s5659 4. Descent of the testis, the spermatic cord and the coverings of the testis
s5660 The testis develops in the abdominal cavity, in the lumbar region, and descends under the influence of the **gubernaculum testis** (*gubernaculum testis*): at 3 months it lies beside the deep inguinal ring, at **7–8 months** it passes through the inguinal canal, and **by the time of birth** it descends into the scrotum. Ahead of the testis the peritoneum bulges out to form the **processus vaginalis of the peritoneum**; the testis takes all the layers of the abdominal wall with it. After birth the upper part of the processus vaginalis closes, and its lower part forms the **tunica vaginalis** around the testis.
s5661 The **spermatic cord** (*funiculus spermaticus*) is a round cord (15–20 cm) extending from the deep inguinal ring to the upper pole of the testis; its contents: the **ductus deferens**, the **testicular artery**, the artery of the ductus deferens, the cremasteric artery, the **pampiniform venous plexus**, lymphatic vessels, nerves (testicular plexus, genital branch of the genitofemoral nerve) and the remnant of the processus vaginalis.

s5680 5. The penis and scrotum
s5681 The **penis** (*penis*) is the organ of urination and ejaculation; it has a **root** (attached to the pubic and ischial bones), a **body** (its upper surface — the dorsum of the penis) and a **glans** (with its corona and neck; at its tip the external urethral orifice). The penis consists of **two corpora cavernosa** (above; their posterior ends — the **crura** — attach to the ischiopubic rami) and **one corpus spongiosum** (below; the urethra runs through it; it ends behind in the **bulb** and in front in the **glans**). Each body is enclosed in a **tunica albuginea**; inside are endothelium-lined **caverns** — when they fill with blood, **erection** occurs. The skin of the penis is thin and mobile; over the glans it forms the fold of the **prepuce** (*preputium*) and, below, its **frenulum**. The penis is supported by the superficial and deep fasciae and the **suspensory ligaments**.
s5682 The **scrotum** (*scrotum*) is a skin-muscle sac lying behind and below the root of the penis; it is divided into two compartments by the **scrotal septum** (corresponding externally to the **scrotal raphe**); each compartment contains a testis, the epididymis and the initial part of the spermatic cord.
s5683 6. Female internal genital organs
s5684 The **female internal genital organs** are the **ovaries**, **uterine tubes**, **uterus** and **vagina**; the external organs make up the **female pudendum** (mons pubis, labia majora and minora, vestibule of the vagina, clitoris, vestibular glands and bulb).

s5687 The **ovary** (*ovarium*) is a paired **mixed gland**: the ova (oocytes) mature in it, and it secretes **estrogens** and **progesterone**. It lies on the lateral wall of the lesser pelvis, in the **ovarian fossa**; it is oval, 2.5–5 cm long and weighs 5–8 g. It has **medial** and **lateral surfaces**, a free and a **mesovarian border** (with the **hilum of the ovary**), and **tubal** and **uterine poles**. The ovary is held by its **mesovarium** (from the posterior layer of the broad ligament of the uterus), the **suspensory ligament of the ovary** (from the pelvic wall; containing the ovarian vessels) and the **proper ligament of the ovary** (to the angle of the uterus). It is not covered by peritoneum — its surface is covered by a simple "germinal" epithelium, beneath which lies the tunica albuginea; inside are the **cortex** (follicles) and **medulla** (vessels).
s5688 In the cortex there are **follicles** at different stages of maturity: **primordial**, **primary**, **secondary** and the mature **tertiary (Graafian follicle)**. Each month one Graafian follicle ruptures, and the ovum is released into the peritoneal cavity (**ovulation**); in place of the ruptured follicle the **corpus luteum** forms (if no pregnancy occurs, it becomes the **corpus albicans**). At birth the ovary contains ≈1–2 million primordial follicles, ≈300–400 thousand remain by puberty, and ≈400–500 ovulate during the entire reproductive period.

s5691 The **uterine tube** (*tuba uterina*, Fallopian tube) is 10–12 cm long; it lies in the upper border of the broad ligament of the uterus (in the **mesosalpinx**). Its parts: the **uterine part** (intramural; opens by the **uterine orifice**), the **isthmus** (narrow), the **ampulla** — the longest part, where **fertilization usually takes place** — and the **infundibulum**, which opens into the peritoneal cavity by the **abdominal orifice**, surrounded by **fimbriae**. Its wall: a mucosa with ciliated epithelium (longitudinal folds), a smooth muscle layer (peristalsis) and a serosa. The ovum is moved toward the uterus by ciliary action and peristalsis.
s5692 The **uterus** (*uterus*, Greek *metra*) is a pear-shaped, muscular unpaired organ; the fetus develops in it, and it expels the fetus at delivery. It is 7–8 cm long, ≈4 cm wide and weighs 40–50 g (more in women who have given birth). Its parts: the **fundus** (above the entry of the tubes), the **body**, the **isthmus** (*isthmus*) and the **cervix** (*cervix*) — with **supravaginal** and **vaginal parts**; in the vaginal part of the cervix is the **external os** (round in nulliparous women, a transverse slit in parous women). Inside is the triangular **uterine cavity** and the **cervical canal**. Its wall: the **endometrium** (mucosa; changes during the menstrual cycle), the **myometrium** (a thick smooth muscle layer) and the **perimetrium** (serosa). The uterus lies in the lesser pelvis between the bladder and rectum; its normal position is **anteflexion** (the body bent forward relative to the cervix) and **anteversion** (the whole uterus tilted forward).
