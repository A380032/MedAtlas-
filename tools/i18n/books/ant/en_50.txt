s6167 The **transverse sinus** — between the aorta and pulmonary trunk in front and the superior vena cava, right atrium and pulmonary veins behind; in surgery it is used to separate the aorta and pulmonary trunk and apply a clamp.
s6168 The **oblique sinus** — on the posterior surface of the heart, between the right and left pulmonary veins and the inferior vena cava, behind the left atrium.
s6169 The **anteroinferior sinus** — the lowest part, at the junction of the sternocostal and diaphragmatic parts; fluid collects here first.

s6195 **Radiographic anatomy** (PA projection): along the border of the central cardiovascular shadow there are **2 arcs on the right** — above, the superior vena cava (ascending aorta), below, the right atrium; and **4 arcs on the left** — the aortic arch, the pulmonary trunk, the left auricle (atrium) and the left ventricle. The concavity between the arcs of the left auricle and the pulmonary trunk is the **waist of the heart**. The position of the heart depends on body build: **oblique** in normosthenics (axis ≈45° to the horizontal), **transverse** in hypersthenics (25–30°) and **vertical** in asthenics ("drop heart," 50–60°).
s6196 10. Age-related features of the heart
s6197 The newborn's heart is **rounded** (the atria are large and the ventricles poorly developed), with a transverse dimension of 2.7–3.9 cm and a length of 3.0–3.5 cm; the right atrium is considerably larger than the left. The pectinate and papillary muscles are poorly defined, and the chordae tendineae are relatively thick and short.
s6198 Its mass in the newborn is **≈24 g** (≈0.8% of body weight, vs. ≈0.5% in adults); it increases 2 times by 1 year, 3 times by 4–5 years, increases 10 times by puberty and 15 times by 18–20 years. In the 1st year the atria grow faster; at 2–6 years the atria and ventricles grow equally; after 10 years the ventricles grow faster.
s6199 In the newborn the walls of both ventricles are almost equally thick (≈5 mm); after birth (once the pulmonary circulation starts working) the left ventricle thickens rapidly and by 2 years is 2 times as thick as the right; by 15 years the wall of the left ventricle thickens 2.5 times and that of the right only ≈0.3 times. From 2 years fat begins to accumulate under the epicardium.
s6200 The newborn's heart lies **high and transversely** (the diaphragm is high): the apex is in the left **4th intercostal space**, 1–2 cm **lateral** to the midclavicular line; until 1 year the upper border is in the 2nd intercostal space. At 2–3 years the heart takes an oblique position, and in adolescence it resembles that of adults. On radiographs the infant's cardiac shadow is spherical; in the elderly the heart is "lying," and the aortic shadow is distinct.
s6201 After birth the **foramen ovale** (within a few months; in ≈20–25% of adults it does not close completely) and the **ductus arteriosus (of Botallo)** close — the fetal circulation is considered in detail in topic 30.
s6202 In the newborn the pericardium is rounded, embraces the heart tightly, and its upper border reaches the level of the sternoclavicular joints; the heart rate in the newborn is ≈140/min and decreases with age.

s6299 A 58-year-old man complains of severe crushing retrosternal pain lasting 40 minutes; the pain radiates to the inner surface of the left arm and to the mandible. The ECG shows ST elevation in leads V~1~–V~4~ (infarction of the anterior wall of the left ventricle and the interventricular septum).
s6300 Which artery is affected? State its origin and course. Why does the pain radiate to the arm and jaw?
s6301 The affected artery is the **anterior interventricular branch of the left coronary artery**. The left coronary artery arises from the left sinus of the aortic bulb, passes between the pulmonary trunk and the left auricle and divides into the **anterior interventricular** and **circumflex** branches; the anterior interventricular branch runs along the anterior interventricular sulcus to the apex and supplies the anterior wall of the left ventricle, the anterior 2/3 of the interventricular septum (with the bundle branches) and the apex. Sensory fibers from the heart enter spinal cord segments **T~I~–T~V~** via the sympathetic nerves; the same segments (and C~VIII~) supply the skin of the inner surface of the arm, which is why the pain is **referred** there.
s6302 A young man with a stab wound to the left side of the chest beside the sternum in the 4th intercostal space was brought to the emergency department. His arterial pressure is 70/40 mmHg, the neck veins are distended and the heart sounds are muffled.
s6303 What condition has developed? Which part of the heart is most likely to have been injured? What does emergency treatment consist of, and how is it performed?
s6304 This is **cardiac tamponade** — blood has accumulated in the pericardial cavity, compressing the heart and preventing it from filling in diastole (Beck's triad: hypotension, distended neck veins, muffled heart sounds). The sternocostal surface is formed mainly by the **right ventricle**, so it is the part most often injured. Emergency treatment — **pericardiocentesis**: the needle is inserted from **Larrey's point** (the angle between the xiphoid process and the left 7th costal cartilage), directed upward and backward into the **anteroinferior sinus** of the pericardium (the lowest point, where blood collects first; the pleura and internal thoracic artery are not injured); then the wound is sutured surgically.
s6305 A 65-year-old patient complains of dizziness and brief fainting attacks. The heart rate is 36/min. The ECG shows the atria and ventricles contracting independently of each other (complete AV block). He has a history of inferior wall myocardial infarction.
s6306 Which part of the conducting system is affected? Explain the structure of the conducting system and the blood supply of this part.
s6307 The affected part is the **atrioventricular node** (Aschoff–Tawara) and/or the **bundle of His**. The conducting system: **sinoatrial node** (in the wall of the right atrium between the opening of the superior vena cava and the right auricle; the pacemaker) → interatrial bundles → **atrioventricular node** (in the lower part of the interatrial septum, in front of the opening of the coronary sinus) → **bundle of His** (pierces the fibrous skeleton; in the muscular part of the septum divides into right and left branches) → **Purkinje fibers**. The atrial and ventricular myocardium are separated by **fibrous rings**, so the impulse passes only through this bundle; if it is damaged, the ventricles contract at their own slow rhythm. In most people the atrioventricular node receives blood from the **right coronary artery** (from the region of the posterior interventricular branch) — inferior wall infarction also results from occlusion of this artery. Treatment — a pacemaker.

s6337 **Coarctation of the aorta** is a congenital narrowing, usually in the region of the aortic isthmus (near the ligamentum arteriosum): blood pressure is high in the arms and low in the legs, with weak pulses; blood reaches the lower body by **collateral routes** — subclavian → internal thoracic → intercostal arteries, and the dilated intercostal arteries produce **rib notching on the lower borders of the ribs** on radiographs. **Aortic aneurysm** and **dissecting aneurysm** most often affect the ascending aorta and the arch; an aneurysm of the arch compresses the left recurrent laryngeal nerve, causing hoarseness.
s6338 The **pulse** of the common carotid artery is felt at the anterior border of the sternocleidomastoid at the level of the thyroid cartilage; in bleeding the artery is pressed against the anterior tubercle of the transverse process of the 6th cervical vertebra — the **carotid tubercle** (Chassaignac's). Carotid sinus massage (vagal reflex) may stop an attack of tachycardia, but in the elderly may cause bradycardia and fainting. The **bifurcation** is the site where atherosclerotic plaques most often form (a cause of stroke); treatment — carotid endarterectomy.
