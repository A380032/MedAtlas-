s7004 Through the **suprapiriform foramen** to the buttock: the gluteus medius and minimus, the gluteus maximus
s7005 **Inferior gluteal artery**
s7006 Through the **infrapiriform foramen** (with the sciatic nerve): the gluteus maximus, the upper part of the posterior thigh muscles, the hip joint; the **artery accompanying the sciatic nerve**
s7007 **Obturator artery**
s7008 Along the lateral wall of the pelvis through the **obturator canal** to the medial surface of the thigh: the adductor muscles, the obturator externus, the hip joint (**artery of the ligament of the head of the femur**); its pubic branch anastomoses with the pubic branch of the inferior epigastric artery
s7009 **Umbilical artery**
s7010 The main branch in the fetus; after birth its distal part is obliterated and becomes the **medial umbilical ligament**; the **superior vesical arteries** arise from its patent proximal part
s7011 **Inferior vesical artery**
s7012 The fundus of the bladder, the lower part of the ureter, the prostate, the seminal vesicles (in women — the vagina)
s7013 **Artery of the ductus deferens** (in men)
s7014 Along the ductus deferens to the testis; anastomoses with the testicular artery
s7015 **Uterine artery** (in women)
s7016 At the base of the broad ligament of the uterus, beside the cervix, it crosses **over the ureter** ("water under the bridge") and ascends along the lateral border of the uterus; it gives off vaginal, ovarian and tubal branches; anastomoses with the ovarian artery
s7017 **Middle rectal artery**
s7018 The middle part of the rectum; anastomoses with the superior and inferior rectal arteries
s7019 **Internal pudendal artery**

s7025 3. The external iliac artery
s7026 The **external iliac artery** (*a. iliaca externa*) runs from the sacroiliac joint along the medial border of the psoas major to the inguinal ligament and, passing to the thigh through the **vascular lacuna**, takes the name **femoral artery**. Near the inguinal ligament it gives off two branches:
s7027 The **inferior epigastric artery** — ascends medial to the deep inguinal ring (within the **lateral umbilical fold**), rises inside the rectus sheath and anastomoses with the **superior epigastric artery** (from the internal thoracic artery) — a collateral between the subclavian and external iliac arteries. Its branches: the **pubic branch** (to the obturator artery), in men the **cremasteric artery**, in women the **artery of the round ligament of the uterus**.
s7028 The **deep circumflex iliac artery** — laterally behind the inguinal ligament along the iliac crest; the broad muscles of the abdominal wall and the iliacus.
s7029 4. The femoral artery
s7030 The **femoral artery** (*a. femoralis*) emerges in the middle of the vascular lacuna (lateral to the femoral vein, medial to the femoral nerve — **NAV**: nerve, artery, vein from lateral to medial), descends in the **femoral triangle** (between the inguinal ligament, the sartorius and the adductor longus), then enters the **adductor canal** (Hunter's canal; between the vastus medialis, the adductor magnus and the lamina vastoadductoria) and, passing through the **adductor hiatus** into the popliteal fossa, becomes the **popliteal artery**. Its branches:
s7031 The **superficial epigastric artery** — the skin of the anterior abdominal wall (as far as the umbilicus).
s7032 The **superficial circumflex iliac artery** — the skin over the iliac crest.
s7033 The **external pudendal arteries** — the skin of the scrotum or the labia majora.
s7034 The **deep artery of the thigh** (*a. profunda femoris*) — its largest branch; it arises from the posterolateral side 3–5 cm below the inguinal ligament; it gives off the **medial circumflex femoral artery** (the hip joint and **the main blood supply of the head of the femur**, the adductor muscles), the **lateral circumflex femoral artery** (ascending, transverse and descending branches — the quadriceps) and **3 perforating arteries** (which pierce the adductors to reach the posterior thigh muscles).
s7035 The **descending genicular artery** — arises in the adductor canal; branches to the genicular network and along the saphenous vein.

s7040 5. The popliteal artery and the genicular network
s7041 The **popliteal artery** (*a. poplitea*) lies deepest (closest to the bone) in the popliteal fossa: superficial to it is the **popliteal vein**, and most superficial is the **tibial nerve** of the sciatic nerve (**NVA** — from behind forward). At the lower border of the popliteus it divides into the **anterior** and **posterior tibial arteries**. Its branches forming the **arterial network of the knee joint** are the **superior lateral** and **superior medial**, **middle** (to the cruciate ligaments), **inferior lateral** and **inferior medial genicular arteries**; the descending genicular artery (from the femoral artery), the descending branch of the lateral circumflex femoral artery and the anterior and posterior tibial recurrent arteries also take part in the network. Muscular branches — the **sural arteries** (to the triceps surae).

s7065 7. Arterial anastomoses of the lower limb and age-related features
s7066 **Gluteofemoral anastomoses** (the "cruciate anastomosis"): the superior and inferior gluteal and obturator arteries (internal iliac) ↔ the medial and lateral circumflex femoral arteries and the 1st perforating artery (femoral artery). When the external iliac artery or the proximal part of the femoral artery is occluded, blood reaches the limb by this route.
s7067 **In the abdominal wall**: inferior ↔ superior epigastric arteries; deep circumflex iliac ↔ iliolumbar artery; obturator ↔ inferior epigastric (pubic branches).
s7068 The **genicular network**, the **malleolar networks** (medial and lateral malleolar branches), the **calcaneal network**; in the foot — the **plantar arch** and the deep plantar branch and perforating branches between it and the dorsalis pedis artery.
s7069 In the fetus the **umbilical arteries** are the main continuation of the internal iliac arteries and carry blood to the placenta; after birth they are obliterated and become the **medial umbilical ligaments** (their proximal parts remain as the superior vesical arteries). So in the newborn the internal iliac artery is thicker than the external one; with age, as the limb grows, the **external iliac artery** predominates.
s7070 In children the aortic bifurcation is lower (down to L~V~) and the angle between the common iliac arteries is smaller. The arteries of the lower limb lie more superficially in children; their length and diameter increase in step with the growth of the limb, especially rapidly during puberty.
s7071 In the elderly the arteries of the lower limb (especially the femoral and popliteal arteries) are the ones most often affected by atherosclerosis — "intermittent claudication," and in severe cases gangrene.
s7072 Practical skill: taking the pulse of the arteries of the lower limb
s7073 **Femoral artery** — palpate below the inguinal ligament, midway between the anterior superior iliac spine and the pubic symphysis; in bleeding, press here with the fist against the superior ramus of the pubis.
s7074 **Popliteal artery** — with the patient lying prone and the knee slightly flexed, palpate deeply in the middle of the popliteal fossa with the fingers of both hands.
