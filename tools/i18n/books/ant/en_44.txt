s5454 **Wall of the bladder**: **mucosa** — transitional epithelium (adapted to distension), forming folds in the empty bladder; **submucosa**; **muscular coat** — three layers of smooth muscle (outer and inner longitudinal, middle circular), together the **detrusor muscle** (*m. detrusor vesicae*), which expels urine; in the region of the neck the circular fibers form the **internal (involuntary) sphincter**; the outer layer is a serosa above and an adventitia elsewhere. s5478 The newborn's kidney is **relatively large**, rounded and **lobulated** (embryonic lobulation) — its surface is bumpy because the cortex is poorly developed; this disappears by 2–3 years. It is ≈4.2 cm long and weighs ≈12 g (both ≈25 g; ≈0.8% of body weight, vs. ≈0.5% in adults); the ratio of the thickness of the cortex to the medulla is 1:4 (1:2 in adults). The left kidney is larger than the right.
s5479 In the newborn the kidneys lie **lower**: the upper pole at the upper border of T~XII~ and the lower pole at the lower border of L~IV~; because the vertebral column grows rapidly, they rise relatively by 1 year and resemble those of adults after 7 years. In children the longitudinal axes of the kidneys are parallel; from 5–6 years the upper poles approach each other.
s5480 In the newborn the kidney **has no fatty capsule** — it appears in early childhood and is well defined by 7 years; the fascia is thin — the kidney is mobile and palpable in young children. The kidney grows rapidly in the 1st year and at 5–9 and 16–19 years, mainly through the cortex (the cortex thickens 4 times, the medulla 2 times). The number of nephrons at birth equals that of adults, but they are immature — the concentrating ability of the child's kidney is low.
s5481 In the newborn the renal pelvis is wide and ampullary and often lies **outside the kidney**; by 3 years it moves inside the kidney.
s5482 In the newborn the ureter is 5–7 cm long, tortuous and thin-walled, with poorly developed muscle and elastic fibers; it is ≈10 cm at 1 year, ≈15 cm at 4 years and 18–20 cm at puberty; its structure resembles that of adults by 8 years.
s5483 In the newborn the **bladder is fusiform and high** — it lies mainly in the **abdominal cavity** (its apex halfway between the umbilicus and the pubic symphysis) and is in contact with the peritoneum in front as well; its fundus is not formed, and its trigone lies frontally. Its capacity is **50–80 mL** in the newborn, 180–200 mL at 5 years and 500–700 mL at puberty; its shape is pear-like at 1.5–2 years, plum-like at 5 years, ovoid at 10 years and spherical in adolescence. With age the bladder descends into the lesser pelvis.
s5484 In the newborn boy the urethra is 5–6 cm long, in girls 1–3 cm; the muscle layer of the urethra and the **external (voluntary) sphincter are fully formed by 12–13 years**. Voluntary control of urination develops at 2–3 years.

s5575 A 35-year-old man presented with sudden severe colicky pain in the right loin radiating along the right side of the abdomen to the groin and testis; he is restless and cannot find a comfortable position. There are red blood cells in the urine. Ultrasound showed dilation of the right renal pelvis and a 6 mm stone in the ureter at the pelvic brim.
s5576 What is the condition? In which constrictions of the ureter do stones lodge? Why does the pain radiate to the groin and testis?
s5577 This is **renal colic** — a stone is lodged in the ureter (urolithiasis). The ureter has **three constrictions**: the junction of the pelvis with the ureter, the **entrance into the lesser pelvis** (where it crosses the iliac vessels) and the **intramural part** in the bladder wall (the narrowest). Because the stone blocks the ureter, the outflow of urine is impaired and the pelvis is dilated. The sensory fibers of the kidney and ureter go to segments **T~XI~–L~II~** — pain is referred to the areas supplied by these segments: the loin, the lateral abdominal wall, the groin and the testis (ilioinguinal and genitofemoral nerves).
s5578 A 22-year-old woman has had frequent, painful urination and suprapubic pain for the third time this year (acute cystitis). Her husband has not had this disease.
s5579 Why is cystitis much more common in women than in men? State the differences between the female and male urethras.
s5580 The female urethra is **short (2.5–4 cm), wide and almost straight**, and its external orifice lies in the **vestibule of the vagina**, close to the vagina and anus — microbes easily ascend into the bladder (ascending infection). The male urethra is **long (16–22 cm) and S-shaped**, consists of prostatic, membranous and spongy parts, has three constrictions and opens on the glans penis — there are more barriers to ascending infection. In women the urethra is fused with the anterior wall of the vagina and conveys only urine; in men it also conveys seminal fluid.
s5581 A thin 30-year-old woman who has lost weight complains of right loin pain that increases on standing and decreases on lying down. Urography performed in the upright position showed that the right kidney had descended by two vertebrae and the ureter was kinked.
s5582 What is the condition (nephroptosis)? Name the factors that hold the kidney in place, and explain why it occurs especially in thin women and on the right side.
s5583 This is **nephroptosis** — descent of the kidney. The **fixing apparatus** of the kidney: its coverings — especially the **fatty capsule** and the **renal fascia** — the renal vessels (artery and vein), intra-abdominal pressure and the tone of the anterior abdominal wall muscles, and the **renal bed** formed by the muscles. With sharp weight loss the fatty capsule diminishes; because the renal fascia is **open below**, the kidney slides downward. Because the right kidney lies lower owing to the liver and the right renal bed is shallower, nephroptosis is more often right-sided; in women the abdominal wall is weaker (pregnancies). Kinking of the ureter impairs urine flow.

s5613 Developmental anomalies: **hypospadias** (the external urethral orifice on the underside of the penis — the urethral folds have not fused), **epispadias** (on the upper surface), **cryptorchidism** (failure of the testis to descend into the scrotum — it remains in the abdominal cavity or the inguinal canal), **hermaphroditism** (pseudo- or true), and incomplete fusion of the Müllerian ducts — a **bicornuate**, **septate** or **double** uterus.
s5614 The route of sperm: **convoluted seminiferous tubules → straight tubules → rete testis → efferent ductules → duct of the epididymis → ductus deferens (ampulla) → ejaculatory duct → urethra (prostatic, membranous, spongy parts) → external orifice**. s5615 **Prostatic adenoma (benign hyperplasia)** — in older men the middle and periurethral parts of the prostate enlarge and compress the urethra: urination becomes difficult, and urinary retention may occur. **Prostate cancer** usually develops in the posterior peripheral part of the gland — it is detected as a hard nodule on **digital rectal examination**. **Vasectomy** (male sterilization) is ligation/division of the ductus deferens in the scrotum or spermatic cord.
