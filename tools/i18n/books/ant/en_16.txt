s1870 4. The external cranial base
s1871 The anterior part of the **external cranial base** (*basis cranii externa*) is formed by the hard palate and the alveolar arch of the maxilla, the middle part by the sphenoid bone and the inferior surfaces of the petrous parts of the temporal bones, and the posterior part by the occipital bone. **In the anterior part**: the hard palate (median and transverse sutures, incisive foramen, greater and lesser palatine foramina), behind it the posterior openings of the nasal cavity — the **choanae**, separated by the vomer; beside the choanae are the pterygoid processes of the sphenoid bone.
s1872 **In the middle part**: the foramen ovale and foramen spinosum, the spine of the sphenoid, the **foramen lacerum** (*foramen lacerum* — between the apex of the petrous part, the body of the sphenoid and the basilar part of the occipital bone), the external opening of the carotid canal, the jugular foramen, the styloid process and stylomastoid foramen, the mandibular fossa and articular tubercle, the external acoustic opening and the mastoid process. **In the posterior part**: the foramen magnum, the occipital condyles, the hypoglossal and condylar canals, the external occipital crest and protuberance, and the nuchal lines.

s1875 5. The orbit
s1876 The **orbit** (*orbita*) is a paired cavity shaped like a four-sided pyramid; its **base** — the **orbital opening** (*aditus orbitae*) — faces forward, and its **apex** faces backward and medially, toward the optic canal. The orbit contains the eyeball, its muscles, the lacrimal gland, vessels and nerves, and fatty tissue.

s1897 6. The nasal cavity
s1898 The **nasal cavity** (*cavitas nasi*) opens anteriorly by the **piriform aperture** (*apertura piriformis*) and communicates posteriorly through the **choanae** with the nasal part of the pharynx; the **nasal septum** (*septum nasi osseum* — perpendicular plate of the ethmoid bone and vomer) divides it into two halves. The **roof** is formed by the nasal bones, the nasal part of the frontal bone, the cribriform plate and the body of the sphenoid; the **floor** by the hard palate; the **lateral wall**, the most complex, by the maxilla (nasal surface and frontal process), the lacrimal bone, the ethmoidal labyrinth, the inferior nasal concha, the perpendicular plate of the palatine bone and the medial plate of the pterygoid process.
s1899 Three **nasal conchae** hang from the lateral wall: the **superior** and **middle** (parts of the ethmoid bone) and the **inferior** (an independent bone). Below them lie three **nasal meatuses**; the narrow space between the conchae and the septum is the **common nasal meatus**. Posterosuperior to the superior concha is the **sphenoethmoidal recess**. In the posterior part of the lateral wall the **sphenopalatine foramen** (*foramen sphenopalatinum*) connects the nasal cavity with the pterygopalatine fossa.

s1915 7. The infratemporal and pterygopalatine fossae
s1916 The **infratemporal fossa** (*fossa infratemporalis*) lies medial to the zygomatic arch and the ramus of the mandible; its **medial wall** is the lateral plate of the pterygoid process, its **anterior wall** the maxillary tuberosity, and its **roof** the infratemporal surface of the greater wing of the sphenoid; there is no bony wall below and laterally. It contains the pterygoid muscles, the maxillary artery, the pterygoid venous plexus and branches of the mandibular nerve. The fossa continues above into the temporal fossa, in front through the inferior orbital fissure into the orbit, and medially through the pterygomaxillary fissure into the pterygopalatine fossa; it communicates with the middle cranial fossa through the foramen ovale and foramen spinosum.
s1917 The **pterygopalatine fossa** (*fossa pterygopalatina*) is a narrow slit-like space, the medial continuation of the infratemporal fossa. Its **anterior wall** is the maxillary tuberosity, its **posterior wall** the base of the pterygoid process of the sphenoid, and its **medial wall** the perpendicular plate of the palatine bone. The fossa contains the **maxillary nerve**, the **pterygopalatine ganglion** and the terminal branches of the **maxillary artery**. It is an important "crossroads" communicating with most of the cavities of the skull.

s1935 Table 8.5. Age periods of the skull

s1943 Old age
s1944 The sutures ossify (synostosis), the bones become thin and brittle; teeth are lost, the alveolar processes are resorbed, and the lower part of the face shortens
s1945 **Sex differences:** the male skull is usually larger and more massive, with distinct prominences (superciliary arches, glabella, occipital protuberance, mastoid processes), a sloping forehead, a blunt superior orbital margin and a well-marked mandibular angle; the female skull has a more vertical forehead, well-defined frontal and parietal eminences, weak prominences and a sharp orbital margin.
s1946 **Craniometry.** To assess the shape of the skull, the **cranial (cephalic) index** is calculated: the greatest transverse diameter (between the two parietal eminences) is divided by the greatest longitudinal diameter (glabella — most prominent point of the occiput) and multiplied by 100. An index **below 75** is **dolichocephalic** (long head), **75–80** **mesocephalic** (medium), and **above 80** **brachycephalic** (short, broad head).
