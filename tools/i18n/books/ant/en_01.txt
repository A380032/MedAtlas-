s0023 Clinical significance
s0024 The conventional lines are used daily in clinical practice: in adults the apex beat is found in the 5th intercostal space 1–1.5 cm medial to the left **midclavicular line**; pleural puncture is usually performed along the **scapular** or **posterior axillary line**. That is why these lines are also needed in classes on percussion and auscultation.
s0025 Remember
s0026 If a bone is placed in a strong acid, the mineral salts dissolve and ossein remains — the bone keeps its shape but becomes so flexible that it can be tied in a knot. If a bone is burned, the organic matter burns away — the bone keeps its shape but becomes brittle and crumbles to powder when pressed. Thus, **organic matter gives elasticity, mineral matter gives hardness**.
s0027 Because children's bones are flexible and their periosteum is thick and strong, children often have **“greenstick” fractures** (*subperiosteal fractures*): the bone breaks, but the periosteum stays intact and the fragments are not displaced. The rich blood supply helps bones heal quickly, but also lets inflammation (osteomyelitis) spread quickly. With **vitamin D** deficiency, bone mineralization is impaired and **rickets** develops (bowing of the legs, the “rachitic rosary”). In the elderly, mineral content decreases, leading to **osteoporosis** and easy fractures (femoral neck, vertebral bodies).
s0028 Memory aid
s0029 Remember the number of vertebrae by mealtimes: **breakfast at 7 a.m.** (7 cervical), **lunch at 12** (12 thoracic), **dinner at 5** (5 lumbar). The sacrum also consists of **5** vertebrae, and the coccyx of **3–5**.
s0030 **7 processes** arise from the vertebral arch: **1** spinous + **2** transverse + **2** superior articular + **2** inferior articular processes. The vertebral foramina form the vertebral **canal**, while the notches of adjacent vertebrae form the **intervertebral foramen** — do not confuse them.
s0031 If the halves of the arch fail to fuse, the vertebral canal remains open posteriorly — **spina bifida** (most often in the lumbosacral region). The hidden form (*spina bifida occulta*) is asymptomatic and is often found incidentally on X-ray; in severe forms the meninges or the spinal cord itself herniate (meningocele, meningomyelocele). Taking **folic acid** before pregnancy and in its first weeks is important in preventing this defect.
s0032 The VII cervical vertebra is the main landmark for counting vertebrae: when the head is bent forward, the most prominent process at the lower part of the neck belongs to this vertebra. The spinous processes of the thoracic vertebrae are counted downward from it.
s0033 A strong blow to the top of the head (for example, diving head first into shallow water) can fracture the arches of the atlas — a **Jefferson fracture**. Sharp flexion or extension of the neck can cause a **fracture of the dens** of the axis. These injuries are life-threatening because they lie close to the upper spinal cord, i.e., to the respiratory center and the segments of the phrenic nerve (C~III~–C~V~). Therefore, a patient with a neck injury must never be moved without immobilizing the neck.
s0034 To distinguish a thoracic vertebra from the others, look for the **costal facets**. To identify which thoracic vertebra it is: a **whole superior + half inferior** facet — T~I~; **only a superior half** facet — T~X~; **one whole** facet on the body and **no** facet on the transverse process — T~XI~ and T~XII~.
s0035 In adults the spinal cord ends at the L~I~–L~II~ level; below it the vertebral canal contains only nerve roots (the “cauda equina”). Therefore, a **lumbar (spinal) puncture** is performed between the spinous processes of L~III~–L~IV~ or L~IV~–L~V~ — since the processes of the lumbar vertebrae are horizontal and the spaces between them are wide, the needle passes easily. Landmark: the line joining the highest points of the two iliac crests (Jacoby's line) passes through the L~IV~ process.
s0036 An anesthetic is injected into the epidural space through the **sacral hiatus** — **caudal (sacral) anesthesia**; this method is used especially in pediatric surgery and obstetrics. The two **sacral cornua**, palpable under the skin, serve as landmarks for finding the hiatus. In women the sacrum is wider, shorter and less curved — reflecting the adaptation of the pelvis to childbirth.
s0037 Caution
s0038 When counting vertebrae in a living person, do not rely on a single landmark: the C~VII~ process sometimes protrudes as much as that of T~I~. When the head is bent forward and backward, **the C~VII~ process moves, whereas that of T~I~ hardly moves** — this distinguishes them. Additional landmarks: the inferior angle of the scapula — T~VII~; the line of the iliac crests — L~IV~.
s0039 1. Anatomy as a science. Anatomical position, planes and directions
s0040 **Anatomy** (Greek *anatome* — cutting up, dissection) is the science that studies the form, structure and development of the human body in relation to the mutual position of the organs and the functions they perform. Anatomy is the foundation of medicine: making a diagnosis, performing surgery, injections and punctures, and reading X-rays and CT scans are impossible without knowledge of anatomy. Anatomy is taught in the I–III semesters at TMA and other medical universities.
s0041 All anatomical descriptions are given relative to the **anatomical position**: a person standing upright, head and gaze directed forward, arms hanging at the sides of the body with the **palms facing forward**, legs together and feet pointing forward. The forward-facing palms are important: in this position the radius is lateral and the ulna is medial.
s0042 The three main planes
s0043 The **sagittal plane** (Latin *sagitta* — arrow) passes through the body from front to back and divides it into right and left parts. The sagittal plane passing through the exact middle of the body is called the **median (midsagittal) plane** — it divides the body into equal right and left halves.
s0044 The **frontal plane** (Latin *frons* — forehead) runs parallel to the forehead and divides the body into anterior and posterior parts.
s0045 The **horizontal (transverse) plane** is perpendicular to both of the above planes and divides the body into upper and lower parts.

s0089 Conventional vertical lines on the body surface
s0090 Conventional lines are drawn to determine the surface projection of organs such as the heart, lungs, pleura and liver: the **anterior median line** (*linea mediana anterior*), the **sternal line** (*linea sternalis*), the **parasternal line** (*linea parasternalis*), the **midclavicular line** (*linea medioclavicularis*), the **anterior, middle and posterior axillary lines** (*linea axillaris anterior, media, posterior*), the **scapular line** (*linea scapularis*), the **paravertebral line** (*linea paravertebralis*) and the **posterior median line** (*linea mediana posterior*).
s0091 2. General concept of the skeleton
s0092 **Osteology** (Greek *osteon* — bone, *logos* — study) is the study of bones. The set of bones forming the solid framework of the body is called the **skeleton** (Greek *skeletos* — dried). The adult skeleton consists of **more than 200** (usually 206) separate bones, weighs 5–6 kg and makes up about 10 % of body weight in men and 8.5 % in women. The bones, their joints and the muscles together form the **musculoskeletal (locomotor) system**: bones and joints are its passive part, and muscles are its active part.
s0093 The skeleton is divided into two parts: the **axial skeleton** — the skull, vertebral column, ribs and sternum; and the **appendicular skeleton** — the bones of the upper and lower limbs together with their girdles.

s0134 Each bone has elevations on its surface — **apophyses** — where muscles, tendons, fasciae and ligaments originate or insert: a **tuber** (*tuber*), **tubercle** (*tuberculum*), **tuberosity** (*tuberositas*), **crest** (*crista*), **process** (*processus*). Bone surfaces are bounded by **borders** (*margo*); where blood vessels and nerves lie there is a **groove** (*sulcus*), where they pass through there is a **foramen** (*foramen*) and a **canal** (*canalis*), and where a joint is formed there is an **articular surface** (*facies articularis*) covered with cartilage. The **nutrient foramen** (*foramen nutricium*), through which a blood vessel enters the bone, is located on the diaphysis.
s0135 4. Structure and chemical composition of bone
s0136 The following parts are distinguished in a long tubular bone: the **diaphysis** (*diaphysis*) — a cylindrical or triangular body containing the **medullary cavity** (*cavitas medullaris*); the **epiphysis** (*epiphysis*) — the expanded ends, which bear articular surfaces covered with articular cartilage; the **metaphysis** (*metaphysis*) — the transition from the diaphysis to the epiphysis; in children the **epiphyseal (growth) cartilage** is located here, and the bone grows in length at its expense; the **apophysis** (*apophysis*) — an elevation near the epiphysis to which muscles attach (for example, the greater tubercle of the humerus).
s0137 There are two kinds of bone substance. **Compact substance** (*substantia compacta*) forms a thick layer in the diaphyses and a thin layer in the epiphyses and in flat and spongy bones. Its structural unit is the **osteon (Haversian system)**: 4–20 bone lamellae arranged concentrically around a central canal, through which blood vessels and nerves pass. The spaces between osteons are filled with **interstitial lamellae**, and **outer and inner circumferential lamellae** lie at the bone surfaces. **Spongy substance** (*substantia spongiosa*) consists of intersecting **bony beams (trabeculae)**; the trabeculae are arranged along the lines of compression and tension acting on the bone, which makes the bone light yet very strong.
