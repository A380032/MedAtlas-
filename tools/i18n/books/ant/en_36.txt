s4548 Clinically, the borders of the liver are determined by **percussion** (Kurlov's method); its enlargement (hepatomegaly) is seen in hepatitis, heart failure and tumors. In **cirrhosis of the liver** the pressure in the portal vein rises (portal hypertension) — fluid accumulates in the abdominal cavity (ascites), the spleen enlarges and the portocaval anastomoses dilate (esophageal varices, "caput medusae") (topic 30). Because the liver tissue is soft and rich in blood, it tears in abdominal injuries, causing severe internal bleeding.
s4549 **Projection of the fundus of the gallbladder** — the point where the right costal margin crosses the lateral border of the rectus (the tip of the 9th costal cartilage); in acute cholecystitis pressure at this point with deep inspiration is painful (Murphy's sign). In **gallstone disease** a stone may block the cystic duct or common bile duct, causing **jaundice**; lodged at the major papilla, it may make bile reflux into the pancreatic duct (because of the common ampulla), causing **acute pancreatitis**. In cholecystectomy the cystic artery is found in Calot's triangle (between the cystic duct, the common hepatic duct and the visceral surface of the liver).
s4550 A **tumor of the head of the pancreas** compresses the common bile duct, causing painless, progressively increasing **obstructive jaundice** and an enlarged, painless gallbladder (Courvoisier's sign). Because the gland lies posteriorly, in **pancreatitis** the pain radiates to the back ("girdle pain"). The superior mesenteric vessels passing through the notch of the gland and the portal vein behind it are considered the most dangerous area in surgery (pancreaticoduodenectomy).
s4551 1. The liver: general information and external structure
s4552 The **liver** (*hepar*, Greek *hepar*) is the **largest gland** in the body: in adults it weighs **≈1.5 kg** (≈2–3% of body weight), is reddish-brown and of soft consistency. It is a multifunctional organ: it produces **bile** (0.5–1 L per day), takes part in the metabolism of proteins (albumin, clotting factors), glycogen and lipids, **detoxifies harmful substances** (barrier function), stores glycogen, vitamins and iron, and in the fetus **forms blood**.
s4553 The liver has two surfaces: the **diaphragmatic surface** — smooth, convex, facing forward and upward, matching the dome of the diaphragm; and the **visceral surface** (facing the viscera) — uneven, facing downward and backward, bearing impressions of neighboring organs. In front the surfaces are separated by the sharp **inferior border** (with the notch for the gallbladder and the notch for the round ligament). The sagittally running **falciform ligament** on the diaphragmatic surface divides the liver into the **right** (larger) and **left** lobes; in its free lower edge lies the obliterated umbilical vein — the **round ligament of the liver**. Behind, the peritoneum forms the frontally running **coronary ligament**, whose ends are the **right and left triangular ligaments**. The area between the layers of the coronary ligament not covered by peritoneum — the **bare area** (*area nuda*) — adheres directly to the diaphragm.

s4556 On the **visceral surface** there are three grooves arranged in the shape of the letter "H". The **left sagittal groove** contains in its anterior part the **fissure for the round ligament** and in its posterior part the **fissure for the ligamentum venosum** (the remnant of the fetal ductus venosus of Arantius); it marks the border of the left lobe. The **right sagittal groove** forms in its anterior part the **fossa for the gallbladder** and in its posterior part the **groove for the inferior vena cava**. They are connected by the transverse groove — the **porta hepatis** (*porta hepatis*). The grooves separate two additional lobes from the right lobe: in front — the **quadrate lobe** (in front of the porta), behind — the **caudate lobe** (behind the porta, with its caudate and papillary processes).

s4568 2. Internal structure and blood circulation of the liver
s4569 The liver is covered externally by peritoneum (serosa; except for the bare area — it is a **mesoperitoneal** organ) and beneath it by a thin, strong **fibrous capsule** (Glisson's capsule). At the porta the fibrous capsule enters the liver together with the vessels and bile ducts, dividing it into lobes, segments and lobules.
s4570 **Segmental structure.** According to the branching of the portal vein, hepatic artery and bile ducts (C. Couinaud, 1957), the liver has **2 lobes**, **5 sectors** and **8 segments**. Each segment is supplied by a 3rd-order branch of the portal vein and the corresponding branches of the artery and bile duct, and is relatively independent — this allows **segmental resection** (removal of only the affected segment of the liver). The border between the functional right and left lobes does not run along the falciform ligament but along a plane passing through the **gallbladder fossa and the inferior vena cava** (Cantlie's line).

s4580 The **hepatic lobule** (*lobulus hepatis*) is the structural-functional unit of the liver: a hexagonal prism 1–2.5 mm in diameter; there are ≈500 thousand in the liver. In the center of the lobule lies the **central vein**, from which the **hepatic plates (cords)** — double rows of **hepatocytes** — radiate. Between the cords are **sinusoidal capillaries** (with Kupffer cells — macrophages — in their walls). At the boundaries of the lobules, where their edges meet, lie the **portal areas (triads)**: the **interlobular vein** (a branch of the portal vein), the **interlobular artery** and the **interlobular bile duct**. The **bile canaliculi** between the rows of hepatocytes carry bile to the periphery of the lobule — to the interlobular bile ducts.

s4583 The **blood circulation of the liver** is peculiar: blood reaches the liver from two sources and leaves by one route. Portal vein → segmental veins → interlobular veins → perilobular veins → **sinusoidal capillaries** (into which blood from the interlobular arteries also flows — mixed blood) → **central vein** → sublobular (collecting) veins → **hepatic veins** → inferior vena cava. Because the portal system has two capillary networks (in the intestinal wall and in the hepatic sinusoids), it is called the **wonderful venous network (rete mirabile) of the liver**. Bile flows in the direction opposite to the blood — from the center of the lobule to its periphery.

s4586 3. Topography of the liver
s4587 The liver lies mainly in the **right hypochondrium** and **epigastric** regions, and partly in the left hypochondrium; normally it does not project below the costal margin. **Skeletotopy**: its upper border is at the **4th intercostal space** (or 5th rib) in the right midclavicular line and at the 10th intercostal space in the right midaxillary line; on the left, at the 5th intercostal space in the parasternal line. Its lower border is at the 10th rib in the right midaxillary line, then runs along the **right costal margin**, leaves the margin at the junction of the 9th and 10th costal cartilages, crosses the epigastric region obliquely (in the midline, halfway between the xiphoid process and the umbilicus) and returns to the margin on the left at the junction of the 7th and 8th costal cartilages.
s4588 **Syntopy**: the diaphragmatic surface is in contact with the diaphragm (and through it with the right lung and pleura and the heart); the visceral surface with the **stomach** (left lobe), the **duodenum**, the **right kidney** and **suprarenal gland**, the **right colic flexure** (right lobe) and the **abdominal part of the esophagus**. From the liver arise the **hepatogastric** and **hepatoduodenal** ligaments (together — the **lesser omentum**) and the **hepatorenal ligament**.
s4589 4. The gallbladder and bile ducts
s4590 The **gallbladder** (*vesica biliaris (fellea)*) is a pear-shaped organ in which bile collects and is concentrated; it lies in the gallbladder fossa on the visceral surface of the liver. It is **8–12 cm** long, 4–5 cm wide and has a capacity of **40–70 mL**. Its parts: the **fundus** (projecting below the inferior border of the liver and touching the anterior abdominal wall), the **body** and the **neck** (facing the porta, through the infundibulum), which continues into the **cystic duct**. Its wall consists of a mucosa (folds; in the neck and cystic duct a **spiral fold** — Heister's valve), a smooth muscle coat and a serosa (on its inferior surface) or adventitia.
s4591 **Bile ducts**: bile canaliculi → interlobular bile ducts → **right and left hepatic ducts** → (uniting at the porta) **common hepatic duct** (4–5 cm) + **cystic duct** (3–4 cm) → **common bile duct** (*ductus choledochus*, 6–8 cm, 5–8 mm in diameter). The common bile duct descends in the hepatoduodenal ligament, behind the superior part of the duodenum and behind (or within) the head of the pancreas, joins the **pancreatic duct**, forming the **hepatopancreatic ampulla**, and opens on the **major papilla** in the descending part of the duodenum.

s4594 Bile flow is regulated by **sphincters**: the **sphincter of the common bile duct** (Boyden's), the **sphincter of the pancreatic duct** and the **sphincter of the ampulla** (the **sphincter of Oddi**). Between meals they are closed, and the bile continuously produced by the liver collects in the gallbladder through the cystic duct; when food (especially fatty food) enters the duodenum, the gallbladder contracts under the action of cholecystokinin, the sphincters open and bile flows into the intestine.

s4597 5. The pancreas
s4598 The **pancreas** (*pancreas*) is the second largest of the digestive glands; it is grayish-pink and lobulated, **14–18 cm** long and weighs **≈70–80 g**. It is a **mixed gland**: its **exocrine** part produces 1.5–2 L of **pancreatic juice** per day (trypsin, lipase, amylase and other enzymes, bicarbonates); its **endocrine** part — ≈1 million **islets of Langerhans** — secretes insulin, glucagon and somatostatin (topic 24).
s4599 The gland lies transversely behind the stomach on the posterior wall of the abdominal cavity at the level of **L~I~–L~II~**; the peritoneum covers only its anterior and partly its inferior surface (a **retroperitoneal** organ). Its parts: the **head** (L~I~–L~III~, within the "horseshoe" of the duodenum; below it the **uncinate process**), the **neck** (the junction of the head and body; behind it the superior mesenteric vein and splenic vein unite to form the **portal vein**; at its lower edge is the **notch** — through which the superior mesenteric artery and vein pass), the **body** (three-sided; anterosuperior, posterior and anteroinferior surfaces; on the anterosuperior surface the **omental tuberosity**) and the **tail** (reaching the hilum of the spleen).

s4602 **Syntopy**: in front — the **stomach** (through the omental bursa); behind — the **inferior vena cava**, **aorta**, **celiac plexus**, left kidney and suprarenal gland, and left renal vein; along its upper border runs the **splenic artery**, and on its posterior surface the **splenic vein**; the duodenum embraces its head; its tail touches the spleen. The **main pancreatic duct** (duct of Wirsung) runs through the whole gland from the tail to the head, joins the common bile duct and opens on the **major papilla**; the **accessory duct** (duct of Santorini), arising from the upper part of the head, opens on the **minor papilla**.
