s7296 8. Organs of the immune system
s7297 The organs of the **immune (lymphoid) system** produce lymphocytes and other immune cells that protect the body from genetically foreign substances and microorganisms. They appear early in fetal life and are mature in the newborn; their total mass (excluding the marrow) is 1.5–2 kg.

s7316 The **spleen** (*splen, lien*) lies under the left costal margin at the level of the **9th–11th ribs**, with its long axis along the 10th rib; it is 10–14 cm long and weighs ≈190 g in men and ≈150 g in women. It has **diaphragmatic** and **visceral** (gastric, renal and colic) surfaces, upper (notched) and lower borders, and a hilum; it is covered on all sides by peritoneum (gastrosplenic, splenorenal and phrenicosplenic ligaments). Inside are the **red pulp** (venous sinuses, ≈80 %) and the **white pulp** (periarterial lymphoid sheaths and lymphoid nodules). Blood arrives through the splenic artery and leaves through the splenic vein to the portal vein.

s7321 In the newborn the venous system is less mature than the arteries: the veins run straight, the venous plexuses are poorly defined, there are subcutaneous venous plexuses in the limbs, and the large subcutaneous veins become clearly visible only **after 2 years**. In early childhood the length and cross-section of the veins increase 2 times, and by 8–12 years they increase 7–8 times; the number of anastomoses increases up to 16 years.
s7322 In the newborn the superior vena cava has a diameter of 10–12 mm and is short; its point of entry is at the 1st intercostal space and descends to the level of the 2nd rib at 1–3 years and the 3rd rib at 4–10 years. In the newborn the inferior vena cava is formed at the level of L~III~–L~IV~ (L~IV~–L~V~ at puberty) and is short and wide (≈6 mm). In the newborn the portal vein is 16–44 mm long and 3–4 mm wide.
s7323 The fetal circulation changes after birth (Table 30.2); in the newborn the foramen ovale and the ductus arteriosus sometimes remain open for several days (the foramen ovale for up to 6–8 months).
s7324 In the newborn the lymph nodes are small, their capsule thin and their lymphoid nodules poorly formed; in childhood they grow rapidly and increase in number and size; in children the lymph nodes respond quickly to infection and enlarge; in the elderly the number of nodes decreases and they are replaced by fatty tissue.
s7325 In the newborn the **thymus** is relatively large (≈13 g), its upper border 1–2.5 cm above the manubrium; it grows rapidly in the first 3 years and is **largest at puberty** (≈37 g); it then undergoes **age-related involution** and is replaced by fatty tissue (≈6 g at 70–75 years). Until 10 years the cortex predominates; after that the medulla increases.
s7326 In the newborn **red bone marrow** is present in all bones; from 4–5 years it turns into yellow marrow in the diaphyses of the long bones, and by 20–25 years the medullary cavity is filled with yellow marrow; in adults the mass of the marrow is 2.5–3 kg.
s7327 In the newborn the **spleen** is rounded and lobulated and weighs ≈9.5 g; 24–28 g at 1 year, 2 times that at 6 years, and 165–171 g at 16–17 years. The (palatine) **tonsils** are largest at 8–13 years; the pharyngeal tonsil may enlarge in children (adenoids) and obstruct nasal breathing; after puberty the tonsils and lymphoid nodules shrink.

s7411 A 52-year-old man has been drinking alcohol for 15 years. He was brought in vomiting blood. On examination the abdomen is enlarged (ascites), there are dilated tortuous veins around the umbilicus, and the spleen is enlarged. Endoscopy shows ruptured varices in the lower esophagus.
s7412 What condition has developed? How is the portal vein formed? Explain the signs on the basis of the portocaval anastomoses.
s7413 This is **portal hypertension** resulting from **cirrhosis of the liver**. The **portal vein** is formed behind the head of the pancreas by the union of the **superior mesenteric** and **splenic veins** and carries blood from the unpaired abdominal organs to the liver; in cirrhosis flow through the liver is impeded, the pressure rises and blood flows into the caval veins through the **portocaval anastomoses**: 1) the **left gastric vein** (portal) ↔ the **esophageal veins** → the azygos and hemiazygos veins (superior vena cava) — esophageal varices, and hematemesis if they rupture; 2) the **paraumbilical veins** (along the round ligament of the liver to the portal vein) ↔ the superior and inferior epigastric veins — **"caput medusae"**; 3) the superior ↔ middle and inferior rectal veins — hemorrhoids; 4) the retroperitoneal anastomoses. **Splenomegaly** results from congestion in the splenic vein, and **ascites** from the increased hydrostatic pressure in the portal system and reduced protein synthesis by the liver.
s7414 In a premature newborn (32 weeks), on day 3 there is shortness of breath and a continuous "machinery" murmur (in systole and diastole) over the heart; the pulses in the legs are "bounding."
s7415 What defect is suspected? What is the function of this structure in the fetus, and how should it have closed after birth?
s7416 **Patent ductus arteriosus (of Botallo)**. Because the lungs do not function in the fetus, most of the blood in the pulmonary trunk passes through the **ductus arteriosus** into the **aorta** (the part beyond the aortic arch), bypassing the lungs. After birth, with the first breath, pulmonary vascular resistance falls, the pressure in the aorta exceeds that in the pulmonary trunk, blood oxygen rises and prostaglandins fall — the wall of the duct contracts, it closes **functionally in 1–2 days** and **anatomically in 8–10 days**, and becomes the **ligamentum arteriosum**. In premature infants this process is delayed; if the duct remains open, blood is shunted **from the aorta into the pulmonary trunk** (left-to-right shunt) — the lungs become congested and heart failure develops. Treatment — drugs that block prostaglandin synthesis (ibuprofen, indomethacin) or surgical/endovascular closure.
s7417 In a 48-year-old woman a hard, fixed, painless lymph node is palpated in the left supraclavicular fossa. The patient says that in recent months she has lost weight and her appetite has decreased.
s7418 Disease of which organs could be associated with enlargement of this node? Explain on the basis of the anatomy of the lymphatic system.
s7419 This is **Virchow's (Troisier's) node** — the left supraclavicular lymph node. It lies near the point where the **thoracic duct** drains into the **left venous angle** (the junction of the left internal jugular and subclavian veins). The thoracic duct begins at the **cisterna chyli** (from the lumbar and intestinal trunks) and collects lymph from the abdominal organs (stomach, intestines, pancreas, liver), the pelvic organs and the lower limbs. That is why **cancers of the abdominal organs**, especially **gastric cancer**, metastasize to this node through the lymphatic duct. In a woman tumors of the ovary and other pelvic organs are also considered. The patient needs endoscopy and abdominal investigations.
