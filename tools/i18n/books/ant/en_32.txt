s4020 **Epidemic parotitis** (mumps) is a viral infection with painful swelling of the parotid glands; because the gland lies within a dense fascial capsule, the swelling is very painful; complications — orchitis, pancreatitis, meningitis. **Sialolithiasis** (salivary stone disease) occurs most often in the duct of the **submandibular gland** (the duct is long and ascending, and its secretion thicker): the gland swells and hurts during meals. In operations on the parotid gland there is a risk of injuring the **facial nerve**, which passes through the gland.
s4021 Enlargement of the pharyngeal tonsil in children — **adenoids** — makes nasal breathing difficult (mouth open, "adenoid face") and, by blocking the opening of the auditory tube, causes frequent **otitis**. **Tonsillitis** (angina) is inflammation of the palatine tonsils; its complications are **peritonsillar abscess** and **retropharyngeal abscess**; from the latter pus can descend into the mediastinum. Foreign bodies such as fish bones become lodged in the piriform recesses.
s4022 1. General information on the internal organs
s4023 **Splanchnology** is the study of the internal organs. The internal organs include the **digestive**, **respiratory** and **urogenital** organs (in the broad sense also the endocrine glands and the cardiovascular system); they lie mainly in the body cavities — thoracic, abdominal and pelvic — as well as in the head and neck, and provide metabolism and reproduction. By structure the internal organs are divided into **tubular (hollow)** organs — esophagus, stomach, intestines, trachea, urinary bladder, etc. — and **parenchymatous** organs — liver, lungs, kidneys, salivary glands, etc.
s4024 The **digestive system** begins with the oral cavity and ends at the anus; it includes the oral cavity (teeth, tongue, salivary glands), pharynx, esophagus, stomach, small and large intestines, and the liver and pancreas. The total length of the alimentary canal is 8–10 m. In development the gut tube is divided into the **head gut** (mouth and pharynx), the **foregut** (esophagus, stomach, initial part of the duodenum, liver, pancreas), the **midgut** (small intestine, cecum, ascending colon and part of the transverse colon) and the **hindgut** (the rest of the large intestine). Most of it develops from **endoderm**; the epithelium of the oral cavity and the lower part of the rectum derive from **ectoderm**.

s4035 2. The oral cavity
s4036 The **oral cavity** (*cavitas oris*) is the initial part of the digestive system. It is bounded below by the **oral diaphragm** (mylohyoid muscles), above by the **hard and soft palate**, at the sides by the **cheeks** and in front by the **lips**; behind, it communicates with the pharynx through the **fauces** (*fauces*). The teeth and the alveolar processes of the jaws divide it into two parts: the **oral vestibule** (*vestibulum oris*) — the slit between the lips and cheeks and the teeth and gums — and the **oral cavity proper** (*cavitas oris propria*) — internal to the teeth, filled by the tongue when the mouth is closed.

s4039 The basis of the **lips** (*labia oris*) is the orbicularis oris; they are covered outside by skin and inside by mucosa, with the red **intermediate part** (vermilion) between them. In the midline the mucosa passes onto the gum, forming the **frenula of the upper and lower lips**; the skin of the upper lip bears the **philtrum** (*philtrum*). Small **labial glands** lie in the submucosa of the lips. The basis of the **cheek** (*bucca*) is the buccinator; the **buccal fat pad** lies between the skin and the muscle. On the mucosa of the cheek, opposite the upper **second molar**, is the **papilla of the parotid duct**. The **gums** (*gingivae*) are thick mucosa covering the alveolar processes and firmly attached to the periosteum; they encircle the necks of the teeth.
s4040 3. The palate
s4041 The **hard palate** (*palatum durum*) is the anterior 2/3 of the palate; its basis is formed by the palatine processes of the maxillae and the horizontal plates of the palatine bones. Its mucosa is firmly attached to the periosteum; in the middle is the **palatine raphe**, from which the **transverse palatine folds** run to both sides; in front, behind the incisors, is the **incisive papilla**.
s4042 The **soft palate** (*palatum molle*) is the posterior 1/3 of the palate; its basis is formed by the **palatine aponeurosis** and muscles, covered on both sides by mucosa. Its posterior part hangs down to form the **velum palatinum** (*velum palatinum*), with the **uvula** (*uvula*) in the middle. From the edges of the velum two pairs of arches descend: the anterior — the **palatoglossal arch** (to the root of the tongue), and the posterior — the **palatopharyngeal arch** (to the lateral wall of the pharynx). The **palatine tonsil** lies in the **tonsillar fossa** between the arches. The velum, the palatoglossal arches and the root of the tongue bound the **isthmus of the fauces** (*isthmus faucium*).

s4058 4. The teeth
s4059 The **teeth** (*dentes*) lie in the dental sockets (alveoli) of the maxilla and mandible and take part in biting and grinding food and in producing speech sounds. A tooth is attached to its alveolus by a **gomphosis** (dentoalveolar joint) by means of the **periodontium**. Each tooth has a **crown** (*corona dentis*) — the part visible in the oral cavity, a **neck** (*cervix dentis*) — the narrowing surrounded by the gum, and a **root** (*radix dentis*, 1–3). Inside the crown is the **pulp cavity**, which continues into the **root canal** and opens by a foramen at the root apex; the cavity is filled by the **pulp**, rich in blood vessels and nerves.
s4060 The main substance of a tooth is **dentin**; the dentin of the crown is covered by **enamel** (the hardest tissue in the body, ≈96% mineral), and that of the root by **cementum**. The crown has **lingual**, **vestibular** (labial or buccal), **contact** (facing neighboring teeth) and **occlusal (masticatory)** surfaces.

s4100 **Development.** The teeth develop from **ectoderm** (enamel) and **mesenchyme** (dentin, pulp, cementum, periodontium). In the 7th week of fetal life the epithelium of the jaw processes thickens to form the **dental lamina**, from which the **enamel organs** grow out; the mesenchyme forms the **dental papilla** (the source of dentin and pulp). The buds of the deciduous teeth and most permanent teeth appear during fetal life; the crowns of the deciduous teeth begin to calcify in the second half of fetal life. Deciduous teeth begin to erupt at **6–8 months** and number 20 by **2–2.5 years**; from **6–7 years** permanent teeth begin to replace them. The first permanent tooth is the **first molar** (at 6 years, behind the deciduous teeth, not in place of any deciduous tooth).
s4101 5. The tongue
s4102 The **tongue** (*lingua*, Greek *glossa*) is a mobile organ built of striated muscles and covered by mucosa; it takes part in mixing and swallowing food, taste and speech. The tongue has a **tip** (*apex*), **body** (*corpus*) and **root** (*radix*), an upper surface — the **dorsum of the tongue** (*dorsum linguae*), an inferior surface and **margins**. Along the dorsum runs the **median sulcus**, and at the junction of the body and root the V-shaped **terminal sulcus** (*sulcus terminalis*); at its apex lies the **foramen cecum of the tongue** (*foramen caecum*) — a remnant of the embryonic thyroglossal duct.

s4123 The **lingual septum** divides the tongue along the midline into two symmetrical halves. The muscles of the tongue are divided into two groups. The **intrinsic muscles** (arising and ending within the tongue) — the **superior and inferior longitudinal**, **transverse** and **vertical** muscles — change the shape of the tongue (shorten, narrow and flatten it, raise or lower its tip). The **extrinsic muscles** (arising from bone) change the position of the tongue: the **genioglossus** (*m. genioglossus*) draws the tongue forward and downward (protrudes it), the **hyoglossus** (*m. hyoglossus*) backward and downward, and the **styloglossus** (*m. styloglossus*) backward and upward. All the muscles of the tongue (except the palatoglossus) are supplied by the **hypoglossal nerve** (XII).

s4126 6. Glands of the oral cavity
s4127 The **minor salivary glands** (1–5 mm) lie in the mucosa and submucosa: the **labial**, **buccal**, **palatine** and **lingual** glands. By the nature of their secretion the glands are **serous** (protein-rich fluid), **mucous** and **mixed**. The **major salivary glands** — three pairs — lie outside the oral cavity, but their ducts open into the mouth. 1–1.5 L of saliva is secreted per day.
