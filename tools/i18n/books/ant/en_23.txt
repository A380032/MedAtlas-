s2695 There are two triangles of practical importance on the back. The **lumbar triangle** (Petit's triangle, *trigonum lumbale inferius*) is bounded below by the iliac crest, medially by the latissimus dorsi and laterally by the external oblique muscle of the abdomen; its floor is formed by the internal oblique — this is a weak spot in the abdominal wall (**lumbar hernias** may occur). The **triangle of auscultation** lies between the trapezius, the latissimus dorsi and the medial border of the scapula (rhomboid major); it widens when the arms are crossed in front and the scapulae are drawn apart, and here breath sounds are heard well because the muscle layer is thin.
s2696 8. Age-related changes of the muscles
s2697 In the newborn all skeletal muscles are formed but relatively poorly developed, making up **20–22%** of body weight; at 1–2 years this falls to ≈16–17%, and once the child begins to move actively it rises to ≈22% at 6 years and ≈27% at 8 years, reaching ≈36% in men and ≈33% in women in adulthood. The newborn's muscle fibers are thin (4–22 µm) with distinct cross-striation; fiber thickness increases to ≈90 µm by 18–20 years. In the newborn's muscles the vascular and nerve apparatus is well developed and connective tissue is scanty; after birth the tendinous part grows rapidly. The fasciae are thin and loose in the newborn and easily separated from the muscles; from the first month they develop according to muscle function.
s2698 The limbs of newborns and young children are cylindrical; as the muscles grow and subcutaneous fat decreases they become fusiform or conical. At **2–4 years** the long muscles of the back and the gluteus maximus grow rapidly (the child walks upright); the muscles holding the trunk upright develop intensively **after 7 years**, especially during adolescence.
s2699 **Age-related features of the back muscles.** In the newborn, of the superficial back muscles the trapezius is well developed; the latissimus dorsi is broad and thin, its aponeurosis passing into the muscular part without a clear boundary; the boundary between the muscular and tendinous parts of the rhomboids is not discernible; the serratus posterior muscles are thin, with poorly developed digitations. Of the deep muscles, the splenius capitis is well developed. In the erector spinae the middle part — the longissimus — is well developed, while the spinalis is poorly developed; the other deep muscles are weak, with thin tendons. The back muscles gradually strengthen as the child begins to hold the head up, sit and walk — in parallel with the formation of the curvatures of the vertebral column (see topic 9).

s2792 After an operation to remove a lymph node in the lateral region of the neck, a patient reports that the shoulder has drooped, that he cannot raise the arm above the head, and that he has difficulty shrugging the shoulder.
s2793 Which nerve and which muscle are injured? Explain the function of this muscle.
s2794 The **accessory nerve (XI)**, which runs superficially across the lateral triangle of the neck, has been injured, resulting in paralysis of the **trapezius**. The upper part of this muscle elevates the scapula and shoulder, the middle part retracts the scapula toward the spine, and the lower part depresses it; the upper and lower parts together rotate the scapula, turning the glenoid cavity upward — this is what makes it possible to raise the arm above the horizontal. With paralysis these movements are impaired.
s2795 A warehouse worker lifted a heavy box from the floor with straight legs, bending at the waist, and felt sudden pain in the lower back. In the lumbar region, rigidly tense muscle ridges are palpable on both sides of the vertebral column, and bending is sharply limited.
s2796 Which muscle is strained? What are its parts and function? What is the correct way to lift a load?
s2797 This is a strain and reflex spasm of the **erector spinae** (*m. erector spinae*). It consists of three parts: lateral — iliocostalis, middle — longissimus, medial — spinalis; contracting bilaterally it extends the vertebral column and holds the trunk upright. When a load is lifted while bent over, the lever arm in the lumbar region is very long, and a large load falls on the muscle and the discs. The correct method is to lift **with the knees bent**, the back straight and the load held close to the body.

s2830 The long thoracic nerve lies superficially on the surface of the serratus anterior in the axillary region and may be injured in breast operations (removal of axillary lymph nodes). The result is a **"winged scapula"** (*scapula alata*): when the patient pushes against a wall, the medial border and inferior angle of the scapula protrude backward, and raising the arm above the horizontal becomes difficult.
s2831 **Pleural puncture** (thoracentesis) and chest drainage are performed in the intercostal space **along the upper border of the lower rib** — because the intercostal neurovascular bundle lies in the groove on the lower border of the upper rib. s2832 Because the phrenic nerve arises from the cervical segments (C~III~–C~V~), in diseases of the organs below the diaphragm (liver, gallbladder, spleen) or when blood accumulates in the abdominal cavity, pain is referred **to the shoulder region** (C~IV~ dermatome) — **referred pain** (for example, to the right shoulder in acute cholecystitis, to the left shoulder when the spleen ruptures — Kehr's sign). In a **hiatal hernia** part of the stomach slides into the thoracic cavity, and heartburn occurs. s2833 During an attack of bronchial asthma or in chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, the patient sits **bracing the arms on a table or the knees** (the "orthopnea position"): once the shoulder girdle is fixed, the pectoralis major and minor and the serratus anterior swap their punctum fixum and punctum mobile, raise the ribs and assist inspiration. s2834 Umbilical hernia is common in infants and usually closes spontaneously by 3–5 years as the umbilical ring strengthens. Indirect inguinal hernia and hydrocele of the testis, by contrast, are related to failure of the **processus vaginalis** of the peritoneum to close — this is a congenital defect, usually treated surgically.
s2835 1. Muscles of the thorax: general information
s2836 By origin and function the thoracic muscles are divided into two groups. The **first group** consists of muscles that arise from the thoracic cage and insert on the bones of the shoulder girdle and the humerus: the **pectoralis major**, **pectoralis minor**, **subclavius** and **serratus anterior**. In development they have "migrated" to the chest from the limb bud; they mainly move the upper limb and shoulder girdle, and when the limb is fixed they act as accessory respiratory muscles. The **second group** consists of the intrinsic (autochthonous) muscles of the thoracic cage: the **external and internal intercostals**, the **innermost intercostals**, the **subcostals** and the **transversus thoracis**; they move the ribs and take part in inspiration and expiration. The **diaphragm** lies between the thoracic and abdominal cavities.
