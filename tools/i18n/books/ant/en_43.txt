s5411 Developmental anomalies: failure of the kidney to ascend — **ectopia** (lumbar, iliac or pelvic kidney); fusion of the lower poles of the two kidneys — **horseshoe kidney** (during ascent it is caught on the inferior mesenteric artery); **duplication** of the kidney and ureter; unilateral **agenesis**; if the urachus remains patent — leakage of urine from the umbilicus (urachal fistula). If the pubic bones and anterior abdominal wall fail to fuse — **bladder exstrophy**.
s5412 When the fatty capsule is sharply reduced (rapid weight loss) and the abdominal muscles weaken, **descent of the kidney (nephroptosis)** develops — because the fascia is open below, the kidney slides downward; the ureter kinks and urine flow is impaired. Because the kidney lies behind the peritoneum, it can be reached surgically **through the lumbar region** (without opening the peritoneal cavity). In kidney diseases pain is referred to the lumbar region, and along the ureter to the groin.
s5413 In **urolithiasis** stones most often lodge in the **three constrictions** of the ureter and cause severe colicky pain — **renal colic**: the pain radiates from the loin along the side of the abdomen to the groin, the testis (labium majus) and the inner surface of the thigh (segments T~XI~–L~II~). In women, during hysterectomy the ureter may be ligated, because it lies **beneath the uterine artery** ("water under the bridge").
s5414 **Bladder catheterization** is the passage of a catheter through the urethra into the bladder; in men it is more difficult because of the curves and constrictions of the canal. In urinary retention (for example, in prostatic hyperplasia) the full bladder bulges above the pubis and is not covered by peritoneum — so **suprapubic puncture** (cystostomy) is performed without injuring the peritoneal cavity. On **cystoscopy** the trigone of the bladder and the ureteric orifices are seen.
s5415 In women the urethra is short, wide and straight, and its external orifice is close to the vagina and anus — which is why **ascending urinary tract infections** (cystitis, pyelonephritis) are much more common in women than in men. In men, by contrast, the urethra is long and curved — care is needed during catheterization and cystoscopy (risk of injury to the membranous part).
s5416 1. The urinary system and its development
s5417 The **urinary system** (*systema urinarium*) removes metabolic waste products (urea, creatinine, uric acid, etc.), excess water and salts from the blood, forms urine and excretes it; in this way it regulates water-salt and acid-base balance, arterial pressure (renin) and hematopoiesis (erythropoietin). Its components: the **kidneys**, which form urine; the **calyces, pelvis and ureters**, which carry it away; the **urinary bladder**, where urine collects; and the **urethra**, which discharges it.
s5418 The **kidney** develops from the intermediate mesoderm (nephrotomes and the nephrogenic cord) **in three stages**: 1) the **pronephros** (*pronephros*) — appears in the cervical segments in the 3rd week and soon disappears, leaving only its duct; 2) the **mesonephros** (*mesonephros*, Wolffian body) — in the 4th week in the thoracolumbar segments; its duct (the **mesonephric, Wolffian duct**) becomes the seminal (genital) ducts in males; 3) the **metanephros** (*metanephros*) — from the 5th week: the **ureteric bud**, growing out of the mesonephric duct, gives rise to the ureter, pelvis, calyces and collecting ducts, and the **metanephrogenic tissue** to the nephrons. The definitive kidney first appears in the pelvis, then **ascends to the lumbar region** and rotates around its axis (the hilum turning from an anterior to a medial position). The **bladder** develops from the anterior part of the cloaca — the urogenital sinus; its apex is connected with the allantois (later the **urachus** — the median umbilical ligament).
s5419 2. External structure and topography of the kidney
s5420 The **kidney** (*ren*, Greek *nephros*) is a paired, bean-shaped, dark red organ; it is **10–12 cm** long, 5–6 cm wide, ≈4 cm thick, and weighs **120–200 g**. It has **anterior** (more convex) and **posterior** (flatter) **surfaces**, **superior** and **inferior poles (extremities)**, and a convex **lateral** and a concave **medial border**. In the middle of the medial border is the **renal hilum**, which continues internally into the **renal sinus**. The **renal artery** and nerves enter through the hilum, and the **renal vein**, the **pelvis (ureter)** and lymphatics leave; the structures from front to back are: **vein, artery, pelvis** ("VAP").

s5423 **Skeletotopy**: the kidneys lie on the posterior wall of the abdominal cavity, **behind the peritoneum** (retroperitoneal), on both sides of the vertebral column at the level of T~XI~–L~III~; their upper poles are close together and the lower poles farther apart. Because of the liver, the **right kidney** lies **≈2 cm lower** than the left. The upper pole of the left kidney is at the middle of T~XI~ and its lower pole at the upper border of L~III~; those of the right kidney are at the lower border of T~XI~ and the middle of L~III~. The **12th rib** crosses the left kidney across the middle of its posterior surface and the right kidney in the region of its upper pole. The hila are at the level of L~I~–L~II~.
s5424 **Syntopy**: through its coverings the posterior surface is in contact with the diaphragm, quadratus lumborum, psoas major and transversus abdominis; the **suprarenal gland** lies on the upper pole. In front of the **right kidney** are the liver (upper 2/3), the right colic flexure (below) and the descending part of the duodenum (at the medial border). In front of the **left kidney** are the stomach (above), the tail of the pancreas (in the middle) and loops of the jejunum (below), and at its lateral border the spleen and the left colic flexure.

s5427 **Coverings of the kidney** (from inside outward): 1) the **fibrous capsule** — thin and strong, easily stripped from a healthy kidney; 2) the **fatty capsule** — adipose tissue, especially thick on the posterior surface and at the hilum (the **perirenal fat body**); 3) the **renal fascia** — consisting of anterior and posterior layers; they unite at the lateral border and the upper pole, but are open below and medially (along the ureter and vessels). The **fixing apparatus** holding the kidney in place: the coverings (especially the fatty capsule and fascia), the renal vessels, intra-abdominal pressure and the tone of the abdominal muscles, and the renal bed (a depression formed by the muscles).

s5430 3. Internal structure of the kidney
s5431 On a frontal section of the kidney two substances are distinguished. The **cortex** (*cortex renalis*) is the outer layer, 4–7 mm thick and reddish; it comprises the **convoluted part**, containing the **renal corpuscles** and convoluted tubules, and the **medullary rays** (radiate part) entering from the medulla; the cortex extends between the pyramids to form the **renal columns**. The **medulla** (*medulla renalis*) consists of **10–15 renal pyramids**: their bases face the cortex and their apices — the **renal papillae** — face the renal sinus; 15–20 papillary ducts open on each papilla, forming the **area cribrosa**.

s5434 A **renal lobe** is one pyramid with the cortex surrounding it; each lobe contains ≈600 **cortical lobules** in the cortex. According to the distribution of the blood vessels, the kidney has **5 segments**: **superior**, **anterior superior**, **anterior inferior**, **inferior** and **posterior**; at the hilum the renal artery divides into anterior (4 segments) and posterior (the posterior segment) branches; the segmental arteries are **without anastomoses** (end arteries) — this allows segmental resection.
s5435 The **nephron** is the structural-functional unit of the kidney; each kidney contains **≈1 million** nephrons. Its parts: the **renal corpuscle** — the vascular **glomerulus** and the two-layered **glomerular capsule** surrounding it (Shumlyansky–Bowman capsule); the **proximal convoluted tubule**; the **nephron loop** (loop of Henle) — descending and ascending limbs; the **distal convoluted tubule**; this opens through a **connecting tubule** into a **collecting duct** (the collecting ducts are not part of the nephron). About 80% of the nephrons are **cortical** (with a short loop) and ≈20% **juxtamedullary** (the glomerulus at the corticomedullary junction, the loop extending deep into the medulla — important for concentrating urine).
s5436 **Renal circulation**: renal artery → segmental arteries → **interlobar** (between the pyramids) → **arcuate** (at the bases of the pyramids) → **interlobular** arteries → **afferent arteriole** → glomerular capillaries → **efferent arteriole** (narrower — hence the high pressure in the glomerulus) → peritubular capillaries → veins (of the same names) → renal vein. Because the glomerular capillaries lie between two arterioles, they are called the **wonderful vascular network (rete mirabile) of the kidney**. Each day **1500–1800 L** of blood pass through the kidneys, **150–180 L of primary urine** are filtered, and as a result of reabsorption in the tubules **1–1.5 L of secondary (final) urine** are formed.

s5439 4. The renal calyces, pelvis and ureter
s5440 Each renal papilla (or 2–3 of them) is embraced by a **minor calyx** (8–9 in all); 2–3 minor calyces unite to form a **major calyx** (superior, middle, inferior); the major calyces unite to form the funnel-shaped **renal pelvis** (*pelvis renalis*); the pelvis leaves the hilum, narrows and passes into the **ureter**. By shape the pelvis may be **ampullary** (embryonic), **branched** (mature) or intermediate (fetal). In the wall of the fornix of the minor calyces there is a ring of smooth muscle — the **fornical apparatus**; it regulates the outflow of urine and prevents its backflow (pyelorenal reflux).

s5443 The **ureter** (*ureter*) is the tube carrying urine from the pelvis to the bladder; it is **30–35 cm** long and 6–8 mm in diameter, and lies retroperitoneally. Its parts: the **abdominal part** — lies in front of the psoas major and is crossed anteriorly by the testicular (ovarian) vessels; the **pelvic part** — at the pelvic brim the right ureter passes in front of the external iliac vessels and the left in front of the common iliac vessels, then it descends along the pelvic wall (crossing the ductus deferens in males and passing **beneath the uterine artery** in females); the **intramural part** (1.5–2 cm) — pierces the bladder wall obliquely (acting as a valve against reflux). Its wall: mucosa (transitional epithelium, longitudinal folds), muscular coat (2 layers above, 3 below) and adventitia.

s5450 5. The urinary bladder
s5451 The **urinary bladder** (*vesica urinaria*) is an unpaired muscular organ in which urine collects; its capacity averages **500–700 mL** (the urge to void appears at 250–300 mL). Its parts: the **apex**, facing forward and upward (from it the **median umbilical ligament** — the remnant of the urachus — runs to the umbilicus), the **body**, the posteroinferior **fundus** and the narrowed lower **neck**, which passes into the urethra. The bladder lies in the lesser pelvis **behind the pubic symphysis** (with the prevesical fat between them — the space of Retzius); when full it rises above the symphysis and contacts the anterior abdominal wall **without intervening peritoneum**. **In males** behind it are the rectum, the seminal vesicles and the ampullae of the ductus deferentes, and below it the **prostate**; **in females** behind it are the cervix of the uterus and the vagina, and below it the urogenital diaphragm. The empty bladder is extraperitoneal; the full bladder is mesoperitoneal.
