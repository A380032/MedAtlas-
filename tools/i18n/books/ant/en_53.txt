s6573 In bleeding, the **subclavian artery** is pressed downward against the **1st rib** in the supraclavicular fossa, above the middle of the clavicle; its pulse is also taken here. In **thoracic outlet syndrome** (a cervical rib, hypertrophy of a scalene muscle) the artery and the brachial plexus are compressed in the interscalene space — numbness, pain and a weak pulse in the arm. If the subclavian artery is occluded proximal to the origin of the vertebral artery, blood for the arm is "stolen" **from the vertebral artery by reversed flow** — dizziness when the arm is exerted (**subclavian steal syndrome**). The **internal thoracic artery** is used in cardiac surgery for **coronary artery bypass grafting**.
s6574 The **pulse** of the brachial artery is felt in the medial bicipital groove and in the **cubital fossa** (medial to the aponeurosis) — **blood pressure** is measured here with the stethoscope of a sphygmomanometer (Korotkoff sounds). In bleeding the artery is pressed against the **humerus** in the medial groove. In children with a **supracondylar fracture of the humerus** a bone fragment may injure or compress the brachial artery — risk of ischemic contracture of the forearm muscles (**Volkmann's contracture**). If the brachial artery is ligated **below** the deep artery of the arm, blood circulates well through the cubital network.
s6575 The **radial artery** is the main site for taking the pulse (over the styloid process of the radius, lateral to the tendon of the flexor carpi radialis); it is also the site for **arterial blood sampling**, **coronary angiography** (transradial access) and the **arteriovenous fistula** for hemodialysis (radial artery + cephalic vein). Before puncturing the radial artery the **Allen test** is performed: the radial and ulnar arteries are compressed at the same time, then the ulnar artery is released — if the palm flushes quickly, collateral flow through the arches is adequate. In wounds of the palm both ends must be ligated, because the arches receive blood from both sides.
s6576 1. The subclavian artery
s6577 The **subclavian artery** (*a. subclavia*) arises on the right from the **brachiocephalic trunk** (behind the right sternoclavicular joint) and on the left directly from the **aortic arch** (so the left one is ≈4 cm longer and has a thoracic part). It arches over the cupula of the pleura, passes through the **interscalene space** (between the anterior and middle scalene muscles, together with the brachial plexus; the subclavian vein lies in front of the anterior scalene muscle), lies in the groove of the same name on the **1st rib** and at the lateral border of the 1st rib continues as the **axillary artery**. In relation to the anterior scalene muscle it is divided into **three parts** (Table 27.1). The main arterial trunk of the upper limb is named in sequence: subclavian → axillary → brachial → radial and ulnar arteries → palmar arches.

s6598 2. The axillary artery

s6616 3. The brachial artery
s6617 The **brachial artery** (*a. brachialis*) is the continuation of the axillary artery; it runs from the lower border of the pectoralis major **to the cubital fossa**. In the arm it lies in the **medial bicipital groove** (*sulcus bicipitalis medialis*); beside it are the **median nerve** (lateral above, crossing in front in the middle, medial below) and two brachial veins. In the cubital fossa it lies under the biceps **aponeurosis** and at the level of the **neck of the radius** (≈2 cm below the elbow joint) divides into the **radial** and **ulnar arteries**. Its branches:
s6618 **Deep artery of the arm** (the largest) — winds around the back of the humerus with the radial nerve in the **radial groove** and supplies the triceps; it ends as the **middle collateral** and **radial collateral arteries** (to the cubital network); it gives off a nutrient artery of the humerus and a deltoid branch.
s6619 **Superior ulnar collateral artery** — with the ulnar nerve, behind the medial epicondyle.
s6620 **Inferior ulnar collateral artery** — in front of the medial epicondyle.
s6621 Muscular branches and the **nutrient artery of the humerus**.

s6636 The **arterial network of the elbow joint** is formed by the union of the following arteries: from above — the **middle** and **radial collateral** arteries (from the deep artery of the arm) and the **superior** and **inferior ulnar collateral** arteries (from the brachial artery); from below — the **radial recurrent**, the **ulnar recurrent** (anterior and posterior branches) and the **recurrent interosseous** arteries. This network supplies the forearm with blood when the brachial artery is ligated.
s6637 5. Arteries of the hand
s6638 The **superficial palmar arch** is mainly the continuation of the **ulnar artery**, completed by the superficial palmar branch of the radial artery; it lies under the palmar aponeurosis, over the flexor tendons, at the level of the **distal border of the fully extended thumb** (Kaplan's line). 3 **common palmar digital arteries** arise from it; they divide into **proper digital arteries** to the adjacent borders of fingers II–V; a separate artery goes to the medial border of finger V.
