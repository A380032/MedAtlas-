s1266 As the child grows, the fontanelles are replaced by sutures. The bones of the cranial vault are joined mainly by serrated sutures: the **sagittal** (between the two parietal bones), **coronal** (between the frontal and parietal bones), **lambdoid** (between the parietal and occipital bones) and **squamous** (between the parietal and temporal bones). The serrations of the sutures appear from 3–4 years; after 30–40 years the sutures gradually ossify (synostosis) and begin to disappear — this is used in forensic medicine for approximate age estimation.

s1369 A 9-month-old child was brought to the pediatrician by his mother: for 2 days he has had diarrhea and vomiting, he urinates little and his lips are dry. On examination the anterior fontanelle is sunken below the level of the bone edges.
s1370 What does this state of the fontanelle indicate? Where is the anterior fontanelle located and when does it close?
s1371 A sunken fontanelle is a sign of **dehydration (exsiccosis)** — the body has lost fluid, and the intracranial volume and pressure have decreased. The anterior fontanelle lies **between the two frontal and two parietal bones**, where the sagittal, coronal and metopic sutures intersect, and is diamond-shaped; it closes at **1–1.5 years** (by 2 years). Urgent replacement of fluid losses (rehydration) is needed.
s1372 After a car accident, symmetric bruising appeared around both eyes of a patient (“spectacle sign”); when the patient bends forward, clear fluid drips from the nose, and he says he cannot smell.
s1373 Which bone and which structure are injured? What is the fluid flowing from the nose?
s1374 This is a **fracture of the anterior cranial fossa**, specifically of the **cribriform plate of the ethmoid bone** (hence the tearing of the olfactory nerves — anosmia). The clear fluid flowing from the nose is **cerebrospinal fluid (CSF)**: the meninges are torn, and the subarachnoid space communicates with the nasal cavity (rhinorrhea). There is a risk of infection (meningitis) — blowing the nose and nasal packing are forbidden.
s1375 A young man was hit in the temporal region by a baseball and lost consciousness for a few minutes, then came to and felt well. 3 hours later his headache worsened, he vomited and then lost consciousness again; the pupil on the side of the blow is dilated.
s1376 What complication has developed? What is its anatomical basis?
s1377 This is an **epidural hematoma**. In the temporal region (the pterion — where the frontal, parietal and temporal bones and the greater wing of the sphenoid bone meet) the bone is thin; the **middle meningeal artery** lies on the inner surface of the **sphenoidal angle** of the parietal bone. In a fracture the artery ruptures, and blood collects between the bone and the dura mater and compresses the brain. The period of regained consciousness is the “lucid interval.” Emergency trepanation and removal of the hematoma are required.

s1405 **Pituitary tumors** (adenomas) enlarge the sella turcica and thin its floor — this can be seen on a lateral skull X-ray and on MRI. When the tumor grows upward, it compresses the **optic chiasm** above the sella, and the patient loses the outer halves of the visual fields (**bitemporal hemianopia**). Pituitary tumors are often removed through the nose via the **sphenoidal sinus** (the transsphenoidal approach).
s1406 Remember the three foramina of the greater wing and the branches of the trigeminal nerve as **“R-O-S — 2-3-artery”**: foramen **R**otundum — the **2**nd branch of cranial nerve V, foramen **O**vale — the **3**rd branch, foramen **S**pinosum — the **artery** (middle meningeal artery). The first branch (the ophthalmic nerve) passes not through a foramen but through the **superior orbital fissure**.
s1407 Because the **mastoid antrum** communicates with the tympanic cavity, inflammation of the middle ear (otitis) may spread to the mastoid cells and cause **mastoiditis**: pain and swelling behind the ear, with the auricle pushed forward. The inflammation may spread through the thin walls of the cells to the sigmoid sinus (thrombosis) and to the posterior and middle cranial fossae (meningitis, brain abscess). Surgery here (antromastoidotomy) is performed in the triangle behind the suprameatal spine (Chipault's triangle).
s1408 **Course of the facial canal:** internal acoustic opening → horizontally forward → **geniculum** (the greater petrosal nerve branches off here) → backward along the medial wall of the tympanic cavity (below the lateral semicircular canal) → vertically downward → **stylomastoid foramen**. The canal lies very close to the middle ear, and its wall is sometimes very thin or dehiscent.
s1409 Because the facial nerve runs in a long, narrow bony canal, it becomes **compressed** in inflammation and edema — **peripheral facial nerve palsy** (Bell's palsy): one side of the face “droops,” the patient cannot close the eye or wrinkle the forehead, and the corner of the mouth drops. The nerve may also be injured in middle ear inflammation and surgery and in fractures of the temporal bone. Depending on the level of injury, additional signs (impaired taste — chorda tympani; reduced lacrimation — greater petrosal nerve) help locate the lesion.
s1410 In newborns and young children the mastoid process is undeveloped, so the **stylomastoid foramen lies very superficially** — the facial nerve emerges close to the skin. Therefore the facial nerve can easily be injured when obstetric forceps are used or an incision is made behind the ear. The short, straight auditory tube is the reason why middle ear inflammation is common in children.
s1411 In a fracture of the middle cranial fossa (through the pyramid), blood or cerebrospinal fluid leaks from the ear (**otoliquorrhea**), and after 1–2 days bruising appears behind the ear over the mastoid process (**Battle's sign**). If these signs appear, the ear must not be irrigated or packed.
s1412 1. The sphenoid bone: general structure
s1413 The **sphenoid bone** (*os sphenoidale*) is an unpaired bone in the center of the cranial base, wedged like a “wedge” between the occipital and frontal bones. It borders almost all the bones of the skull and resembles a bat in flight. The sphenoid bone consists of four parts: the **body** (*corpus*), the paired **lesser wings** (*alae minores*), the paired **greater wings** (*alae majores*) and the paired downward-directed **pterygoid processes** (*processus pterygoidei*).

s1416 The body and the sella turcica
s1417 The **body** is cube-shaped and contains the air-filled **sphenoidal sinus** (*sinus sphenoidalis*) — divided in two by a septum, it opens through an aperture on the anterior surface of the body (*apertura sinus sphenoidalis*) into the **sphenoethmoidal recess** of the nasal cavity (the region of the superior nasal meatus); therefore the sphenoid bone is also a pneumatic bone. On the upper — cerebral surface of the body is a saddle-like depression — the **sella turcica** (*sella turcica*). In its center is the **hypophyseal fossa** (*fossa hypophysialis*), which holds the endocrine gland the **hypophysis (pituitary gland)**; in front is the **tuberculum sellae** (*tuberculum sellae*), and behind the wall-like **dorsum sellae** (*dorsum sellae*); together with the basilar part of the occipital bone, the dorsum forms the **clivus** (*clivus*). In front of the tuberculum sellae runs the transverse **prechiasmatic sulcus** (*sulcus prechiasmaticus*), in which the optic chiasm lies, and on the lateral surfaces of the body runs the **carotid sulcus** (*sulcus caroticus*), in which the internal carotid artery lies. Around the sella are the **anterior, middle and posterior clinoid processes** (*processus clinoidei*).
s1418 The wings
s1419 The **lesser wings** arise from the anterosuperior part of the body by two roots; between the roots lies the **optic canal** (*canalis opticus*), through which the **optic nerve** (cranial nerve II) and the **ophthalmic artery** pass. The posterior border of the lesser wing is free, and its medial end terminates in the anterior clinoid process. Between the lesser and greater wings is the **superior orbital fissure** (*fissura orbitalis superior*), which opens into the orbit.
