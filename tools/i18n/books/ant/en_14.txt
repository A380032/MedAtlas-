s1672 **Processes:** the **frontal process** (*processus frontalis*) rises to join the frontal bone and bears the **anterior lacrimal crest**; the **zygomatic process** (*processus zygomaticus*) joins the zygomatic bone; the **alveolar process** (*processus alveolaris*) is directed downward, and its arched border bears 8 **dental alveoli** (sockets) for the upper teeth, with **interalveolar septa** between them and **alveolar yokes** on its outer surface; the **palatine process** (*processus palatinus*) is a horizontal plate that joins its fellow of the opposite side to form the anterior 2/3 of the hard palate; the **incisive canal** (*canalis incisivus*) opens in its anterior part.

s1677 3. The palatine, zygomatic and small facial bones
s1678 The **palatine bone** (*os palatinum*) is a paired, L-shaped bone consisting of two plates. The **horizontal plate** (*lamina horizontalis*) joins its fellow of the opposite side to form the **posterior 1/3 of the hard palate**; in the middle of its posterior border is the **posterior nasal spine**. The **perpendicular plate** (*lamina perpendicularis*) forms the lateral wall of the posterior part of the nasal cavity; its upper border bears the orbital and sphenoidal processes, with the **sphenopalatine notch** between them. Between the perpendicular plate and the maxilla and pterygoid process runs the **greater palatine canal**; it opens at the posterolateral angle of the hard palate by the **greater palatine foramen**. The **pyramidal process** projects backward and laterally from the junction of the plates.

s1683 The **zygomatic bone** (*os zygomaticum*) is a paired bone, the strongest in the lateral part of the face; it forms the cheek prominence and connects the facial bones with the neurocranium. It has **lateral**, **temporal** and **orbital surfaces** and three processes — the **frontal process** (to the frontal bone), the **temporal process** (which forms the **zygomatic arch** with the zygomatic process of the temporal bone) and the part that joins the maxilla. The bone is pierced by small foramina through which nerves pass.

s1686 The nasal and lacrimal bones, the inferior nasal concha and the vomer
s1687 The **nasal bone** (*os nasale*) is a paired, small quadrangular plate; the two join in the midline to form the bony part of the **bridge of the nose**. Above it joins the frontal bone and laterally the frontal process of the maxilla; its lower border continues into the nasal cartilages.
s1688 The **lacrimal bone** (*os lacrimale*) is a paired bone, the smallest and most delicate facial bone; it lies in the medial wall of the orbit. The **posterior lacrimal crest** on its lateral surface ends below in a **hamulus**; the **lacrimal groove** in front of the crest, together with the corresponding groove of the maxilla, forms the **fossa for the lacrimal sac**.
s1689 The **inferior nasal concha** (*concha nasalis inferior*) is a paired, independent thin curved plate; it attaches to the conchal crests of the maxilla and palatine bone and bounds the inferior nasal meatus from above. Its lacrimal process takes part in forming the nasolacrimal canal.
s1690 The **vomer** (*vomer*) is an unpaired, thin quadrangular plate; it forms the posteroinferior part of the nasal septum. Its upper border joins the body of the sphenoid bone by two **alae**, its anterosuperior border joins the perpendicular plate of the ethmoid bone, and its posterior border is free and separates the choanae.

s1693 4. The mandible
s1694 The **mandible** (*mandibula*) is the **only movable** unpaired bone of the skull; it forms the paired temporomandibular joint with the temporal bones. It has a horizontal horseshoe-shaped **body** (*corpus mandibulae*) and two **rami** (*rami mandibulae*) rising from it; at the junction of the body and ramus is the **angle of the mandible** (*angulus mandibulae*).
s1695 The thick lower border of the **body** is the **base** (*basis mandibulae*), and its upper border is the **alveolar part** (*pars alveolaris*), which bears 8 **dental alveoli** for the lower teeth on each side. On the **outer surface** of the body: in the middle — the **mental protuberance** (*protuberantia mentalis*) with the **mental tubercles** on either side; below the second premolar (or between the first and second premolars) — the **mental foramen** (*foramen mentale*), through which the mental nerve and vessels emerge; and the **oblique line** (*linea obliqua*), running obliquely backward into the ramus. On the **inner surface**: in the midline — the **mental spine** (*spina mentalis*, where the genioglossus and geniohyoid muscles attach); below and lateral to it — the **digastric fossa** (*fossa digastrica*); the **mylohyoid line** (*linea mylohyoidea*), running obliquely backward and upward; above it in front — the **sublingual fovea** (*fovea sublingualis*), and below it behind — the **submandibular fovea** (*fovea submandibularis*).

s1698 The upper part of the **ramus** is divided by the **mandibular notch** (*incisura mandibulae*) into two processes: the anterior **coronoid process** (*processus coronoideus*), to which the temporalis muscle attaches; and the posterior **condylar process** (*processus condylaris*), which consists of the **neck** (*collum*) and the ellipsoid **head** (*caput mandibulae*); on the anterior surface of the neck is the **pterygoid fovea**, to which the lateral pterygoid muscle attaches. The **mylohyoid groove** runs downward and forward from the foramen. In the region of the angle there is the **masseteric tuberosity** on the outer side and the **pterygoid tuberosity** on the inner side.

s1701 **Age-related features.** The mandible develops from the first branchial arch — mainly by the endesmal route from connective tissue around **Meckel's cartilage** (with additional cartilaginous foci in the condylar and coronoid processes). In the newborn the mandible consists of **two halves** joined in the midline by connective tissue, which ossify and fuse **during the 1st year**. In the newborn most of the body is occupied by tooth buds, the alveolar part and mental protuberance are not defined, the ramus is short, the **angle is obtuse (≈150°)** and the mental foramen lies near the lower border. With tooth eruption the alveolar part develops, the ramus lengthens and the angle decreases to **120–130°** in adults. **In the elderly**, after the teeth are lost, the alveolar part is resorbed (atrophy), the body becomes lower, the mental foramen moves near the upper border and the angle increases again (≈140°).

s1704 5. The hyoid bone
s1705 The **hyoid bone** (*os hyoideum*) is an unpaired, horseshoe-shaped bone; it lies in the anterior region of the neck between the mandible and the larynx (at the level of the III–IV cervical vertebrae) and does not articulate with other bones — it is suspended from the cranial base and the larynx by muscles and ligaments. It consists of a **body** (*corpus*), paired **greater horns** (*cornua majora*) and paired **lesser horns** (*cornua minora*). The suprahyoid and infrahyoid muscles and the muscles of the tongue attach to it; it serves as an important support in swallowing and speech.
s1706 Figure 7.11
s1707 Hyoid bone, left anterolateral view: *Corpus* — body; *Cornu majus* — greater horn; *Cornu minus* — lesser horn. Source: R.D. Sinelnikov. Atlas of Human Anatomy, vol. I (Uzbek edition).
s1708 **Age-related features.** The hyoid bone develops from the II and III branchial arches. Ossification centers appear in the body and greater horns just before birth, and in the lesser horns at puberty. The parts of the bone fuse with each other at **25–30 years** (sometimes later).
s1709 6. Age-related features of the facial skeleton
s1710 In the newborn the facial skeleton is very small relative to the neurocranium (≈1:8), because the jaws, teeth and paranasal sinuses have not yet developed. The **maxilla** is low, and most of its body is occupied by tooth buds, so the lower margin of the orbit lies close to the alveolar process; the **maxillary sinus** looks like a small groove. During infancy the bone grows rapidly, and alveoli and septa appear in the alveolar process. The eruption of the deciduous teeth (from 6 months) and especially **their replacement by permanent teeth** (6–12 years) enhance the vertical growth of the facial skeleton; the maxillary sinus enlarges rapidly after the permanent teeth erupt and at puberty resembles that of the adult.
s1711 The zygomatic, nasal and lacrimal bones are ossified at birth and grow in proportion with the skull as a whole; the processes of the inferior nasal concha are cartilaginous at birth and resemble those of the adult at ≈13 years. At puberty **individual and sex characteristics** appear in the facial bones (especially the jaws, chin and cheek prominences) — men have a more massive jaw and chin. In old age, with loss of the teeth, the alveolar processes are resorbed and the lower third of the face shortens.
