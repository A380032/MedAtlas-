s3887 9. Fasciae of the head
s3888 The **temporal fascia** arises from the superior temporal line and the epicranial aponeurosis, covers the temporalis and near the zygomatic arch splits into superficial and deep layers (attaching to the lateral and medial surfaces of the zygomatic arch, with fat between them). The **masseteric fascia** covers the masseter and continues posteriorly into the capsule of the **parotid gland**. The **buccopharyngeal fascia** covers the buccinator and passes onto the lateral wall of the pharynx. Between the buccinator and masseter lies the **buccal fat pad** (Bichat's fat pad).
s3889 10. Age-related features
s3890 In the newborn the mandible is small and the hyoid bone lies high, so the suprahyoid muscles are relatively long and the infrahyoid muscles short.
s3891 The neck muscles are thin in the newborn; the sternocleidomastoid, digastric and scalene muscles are relatively well developed. At 5–7 years all the neck muscles are well formed, resembling those of adults by 10–14 years; their development is completed at 20–25 years.
s3892 The cervical fasciae are very thin and loose in the newborn, and there is little loose tissue in the interfascial spaces; their volume increases considerably at 6–7 years and further during puberty.
s3893 In newborns and children aged 2–3 years the boundaries of the neck lie high, so the triangles also lie higher; they reach their adult position after 15 years.
s3894 In the newborn the muscles of facial expression, except the orbicularis oris and buccinator, are thin and weak; the **orbicularis oris** and **buccinator**, which take part in suckling, are well developed. The epicranial aponeurosis of the occipitofrontalis is poorly developed.
s3895 The muscles of mastication are poorly developed in the newborn; they thicken and strengthen as the deciduous teeth, especially the molars, erupt. During this period much fat accumulates in the temporal and buccal regions, giving the child's face its round shape. At 5–8 years the muscles and fasciae of the head are well developed.

s3980 The mother of a 3-week-old infant says that the baby's head is always tilted to the right with the face turned to the left. On examination a firm, painless, bean-sized mass is palpable on the right side of the neck along the sternocleidomastoid. The delivery was difficult, in breech presentation.
s3981 What is the disease? Which muscle is affected, and why is the head held in exactly this position?
s3982 **Congenital muscular torticollis** (*torticollis*). As a result of birth trauma, hemorrhage occurred into the **right sternocleidomastoid**, which became fibrotic and shortened. Contracting unilaterally, this muscle **tilts the head to its own side** and **turns the face to the opposite side** — which is why the head is tilted to the right and the face turned to the left. Early positional treatment, massage and stretching exercises are effective; if they fail, the muscle is lengthened surgically after 1 year of age.
s3983 On waking in the morning, a 40-year-old man noticed that the left half of his face had become "crooked": he cannot close his left eye or raise his left eyebrow, food collects in his left cheek when he eats, and the left corner of his mouth droops. There is no disturbance of limb movement.
s3984 Which nerve is injured? Explain the signs in terms of muscle function. How is this distinguished from central palsy?
s3985 **Peripheral palsy of the left facial nerve** (Bell's palsy) — all the **muscles of facial expression** on the left are paralyzed: the **occipitofrontalis** (the eyebrow cannot be raised, no forehead wrinkles), the **orbicularis oculi** (the eye does not close), the **buccinator** (food collects between the cheek and teeth), and the **levator anguli oris and zygomatic muscles** (the corner of the mouth droops). In central palsy (stroke) only the lower half of the face is affected and the forehead muscles are spared, because the upper part of the facial nucleus receives bilateral cortical innervation.
s3986 After sitting in the dentist's chair with the mouth wide open, a 25-year-old patient was unable to close the mouth: the mouth is half open, the mandible protrudes, a depression is palpable in front of the ear, and speaking and swallowing are difficult.
s3987 What has happened? Which muscles are involved in producing it?
s3988 This is an **anterior dislocation of the mandible** (at the temporomandibular joint). When the mouth opens wide, the **lateral pterygoid** pulls the head of the mandible and the disc forward; with excessive opening the head passes forward over the articular tubercle, and spasm of the elevators of the jaw — the **masseter, temporalis and medial pterygoid** — locks it in this position. That is why a depression is felt in front of the ear (in place of the articular fossa). Treatment — reduction by pressing the jaw downward and backward (the Hippocratic method).

s4017 **Cleft palate** ("wolf's mouth," *palatoschisis*) is failure of the palatine processes to fuse during fetal life; it often occurs together with **cleft lip** ("harelip"). In the infant, sucking is impaired and milk passes into the nose, and later speech is disturbed; it is corrected surgically. When the **vagus nerve** is injured, the soft palate droops on that side, and on saying "ah" the uvula deviates **toward the healthy side**.
s4018 Tooth eruption is an indicator of a child's **biological age** and development: in a child under 1 year, the number of deciduous teeth can be roughly estimated by the formula **(age in months − 4)**. Late eruption is seen in rickets, hypothyroidism and other diseases. **Caries** is demineralization of the enamel and dentin; if the process reaches the pulp — **pulpitis** (severe pain); if it extends beyond the root apex — **periodontitis**; and because the roots of the upper molars lie close to the maxillary sinus, **odontogenic maxillary sinusitis** may develop.
s4019 Innervation of the tongue: **motor** — hypoglossal nerve (XII); **general sensation** — anterior 2/3 by the lingual nerve (V~3~), posterior 1/3 by the glossopharyngeal nerve (IX); **taste** — anterior 2/3 by the chorda tympani of the facial nerve (VII), posterior 1/3 by the glossopharyngeal nerve (IX), the most posterior part of the root and the epiglottis by the vagus nerve (X). With unilateral injury of the hypoglossal nerve, the protruded tongue deviates **toward the injured side** (the healthy genioglossus pushes the tongue from its own side).
