s4769 3. Peritoneal ligaments, omenta and mesenteries
s4770 Where the peritoneum passes from the wall to an organ or from one organ to another, it forms two-layered folds — **ligaments**, **omenta** and **mesenteries**; blood vessels, nerves and lymphatics reach the organ between their layers.
s4771 **Ligaments of the liver**: from the diaphragm and anterior abdominal wall to the liver — the **falciform**, **coronary**, **right and left triangular** ligaments; from the liver to neighboring organs — the **hepatorenal**, **hepatogastric** and **hepatoduodenal** ligaments.
s4772 The **lesser omentum** (*omentum minus*) is a peritoneal duplication passing from the porta hepatis to the lesser curvature of the stomach (**hepatogastric ligament**) and the superior part of the duodenum (**hepatoduodenal ligament**). The common bile duct, proper hepatic artery and portal vein lie in the hepatoduodenal ligament (topic 18).
s4773 The **greater omentum** (*omentum majus*) is a **four-layered** peritoneal fold hanging like an apron from the greater curvature of the stomach and covering the loops of the small intestine in front (the two anterior layers descend, then turn back and ascend, fusing with the transverse colon and its mesentery). Its initial part is the **gastrocolic ligament**. Between its layers lie fat and lymphoid aggregates ("milky spots").
s4774 **Ligaments of the stomach**: the gastrophrenic, gastrosplenic and gastrocolic ligaments; from the spleen — the phrenicosplenic and splenorenal ligaments.
s4775 **Mesenteries**: the **mesentery of the small intestine** (root ≈15–18 cm, running obliquely from the left side of L~II~ to the right sacroiliac joint), the **transverse mesocolon** (dividing the peritoneal cavity into upper and lower compartments), the **sigmoid mesocolon** and the **mesoappendix**.

s4778 4. Compartments and bursae of the peritoneal cavity
s4779 The transverse colon and its mesentery divide the peritoneal cavity into **upper (supracolic)** and **lower (infracolic)** compartments (in some textbooks the lower compartment is divided into a **middle** compartment — from the mesentery to the pelvic inlet — and a **lower (pelvic)** compartment).

s4798 5. The lower compartment: gutters, sinuses and recesses
s4799 The **lower compartment** lies below the transverse mesocolon and continues into the cavity of the lesser pelvis; it contains most of the small and large intestines and the urinary and genital organs (in the pelvis). The ascending and descending colon and the root of the mesentery of the small intestine divide it into four parts:
s4800 The **right paracolic gutter** (right paracolic sulcus) — between the right lateral abdominal wall and the ascending colon; above it **communicates freely** with the hepatic bursa (the right subhepatic and subphrenic spaces), and below with the right iliac fossa and the lesser pelvis.
s4801 The **left paracolic gutter** (left paracolic sulcus) — between the descending colon and the left lateral abdominal wall; above it is bounded by the **phrenicocolic ligament** (it barely communicates with the upper compartment), and below it communicates with the lesser pelvis.
s4802 The **right mesenteric (infracolic) sinus** — between the root of the mesentery of the small intestine, the ascending colon and the transverse mesocolon; triangular in shape and relatively **closed**.
s4803 The **left mesenteric (infracolic) sinus** — between the root of the mesentery and the descending and sigmoid colon; below it is **open to the cavity of the lesser pelvis**.

s4809 6. The peritoneum of the anterior abdominal wall and the lesser pelvis
s4810 On the inner surface of the anterior abdominal wall, below the umbilicus, the parietal peritoneum forms **5 folds**: the unpaired **median umbilical fold** (containing the obliterated urinary duct — the **urachus**), the paired **medial umbilical folds** (the obliterated **umbilical arteries**) and the paired **lateral umbilical folds** (the **inferior epigastric** artery and veins). Between the folds there are fossae: between the median and medial folds — the **supravesical fossae**, between the medial and lateral folds — the **medial inguinal fossae** (site of direct inguinal hernia), and lateral to the lateral fold — the **lateral inguinal fossae** (deep inguinal ring; site of indirect inguinal hernia) (topic 12).

s4816 7. Spread of fluids in the peritoneal cavity
s4817 The parts of the peritoneal cavity communicate with one another, and fluid (blood, pus, intestinal contents) spreads by definite routes. For example, in appendicitis pus may ascend along the **right paracolic gutter** to the right subhepatic and **subphrenic space**, forming a **subphrenic abscess**, or descend into the **lesser pelvis**. When a gastric or duodenal ulcer perforates, the contents may run down the right paracolic gutter into the right iliac fossa, causing pain resembling appendicitis. Because the left paracolic gutter is closed above by the phrenicocolic ligament, on the left side processes spread upward less. Placing the patient in the semi-sitting (Fowler's) position directs pus into the pelvis and reduces the risk of a subphrenic abscess.

s4820 8. Development
s4821 The peritoneal cavity develops from the **coelom** (body cavity). The primitive gut tube is attached to the posterior abdominal wall by the **dorsal mesentery**, and the stomach and the initial part of the duodenum to the anterior wall by the **ventral mesentery**. After the liver grows into the ventral mesentery, it gives rise to the **lesser omentum** (the part between the liver and stomach) and the **falciform ligament** (the part between the liver and anterior abdominal wall). When the stomach rotates 90°, its dorsal mesentery (the **dorsal mesogastrium**) is drawn to the left and downward and hangs down as a double layer — this is how the **greater omentum** and **omental bursa** form; the posterior layers of the greater omentum fuse with the transverse colon and its mesentery by 3–4 months of postnatal life. After the gut rotates, the mesenteries of the ascending and descending colon and the duodenum adhere to the posterior abdominal wall (**fusion of the primary mesentery**), and these organs become meso- or retroperitoneal.
s4822 9. Age-related features
s4823 In the newborn the abdominal cavity is relatively large and the organs lie high; once the child begins to walk, the diaphragm descends, and because the thoracic organs grow rapidly, the abdominal cavity becomes relatively smaller.
s4824 In the newborn the peritoneum is **thin and transparent** — blood and lymph vessels show through it; there is **no fat** in the subperitoneal layer, mesenteries, ligaments or omenta. Fat appears at 2 years and increases considerably from 7 years; at 16–18 years the fat in the retroperitoneal space, omenta and mesenteries increases and the organs become more firmly fixed.
s4825 In the newborn the **greater omentum** is very short and thin and does not cover the loops of the small intestine; it is almost undeveloped until 2 years, and by 7 years it covers the anterior surface of the intestines. This is why inflammatory processes in young children are not walled off by the omentum.
s4826 In the newborn the root of the mesentery of the small intestine is almost **vertical**; with age it lengthens and becomes oblique. Because the ascending colon is undeveloped and the cecum lies high, the peritoneal recesses have a different shape — they are poorly developed and shallow.
s4827 The absorptive capacity of the peritoneum is high in children — toxins are absorbed rapidly, which is why peritonitis is severe in children.
s4828 Practical skill: demonstrating peritoneal structures on a specimen
s4829 On a specimen with the abdominal cavity opened, find the greater omentum, lift it and show the transverse colon and its mesentery — the boundary between the upper and lower compartments.
s4830 Show the lesser omentum between the liver and stomach and its hepatogastric and hepatoduodenal parts.
s4831 Insert a finger behind the hepatoduodenal ligament into the omental foramen; name the four boundaries of the foramen.
s4832 Displacing the loops of the small intestine to the left and then to the right, show the root of the mesentery, the right and left mesenteric sinuses, and the right and left paracolic gutters.
