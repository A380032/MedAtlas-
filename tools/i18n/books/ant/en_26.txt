s3133 The basic rule of innervation of the upper limb muscles: the **anterior group of the arm** — musculocutaneous nerve; **all posterior (extensor) muscles of the arm and forearm** — radial nerve; the **anterior group of the forearm** — median nerve (except the flexor carpi ulnaris and the medial half of the flexor digitorum profundus — ulnar nerve); the **muscles of the hand** — mainly the ulnar nerve (most of the thenar and lumbricals I–II — median nerve).
s3134 The **rotator cuff** is the "cuff" formed by the tendons of the supraspinatus, infraspinatus, teres minor and subscapularis blending with the capsule of the shoulder joint; it holds the head of the humerus in the glenoid cavity of the scapula (the main active stabilizer of the shoulder joint). The most frequently injured is the **supraspinatus tendon**: it passes under the acromion and is compressed when the arm is raised between 60° and 120° (**"painful arc" syndrome**, impingement). Tendon tears occur in elderly people and in athletes who often raise the arm overhead. Because the **axillary nerve** runs around the surgical neck of the humerus, it is injured in fractures in this region and in shoulder dislocation, leading to paralysis and atrophy of the deltoid.
s3135 **Tendon reflexes**: tapping the biceps tendon with a reflex hammer flexes the forearm (biceps reflex, segments C~V~–C~VI~), and tapping the triceps tendon extends it (triceps reflex, C~VII~–C~VIII~). Changes in the reflexes are used for localizing diagnosis in injuries of spinal cord segments and nerves. In elderly people the tendon of the long head of the biceps may rupture in the intertubercular groove — on flexion a "Popeye sign" (bulging of the muscle belly) appears in the lower part of the arm.
s3136 **Lateral epicondylitis** ("tennis elbow") is chronic strain of the common tendon of the extensor muscles arising from the lateral epicondyle (especially the extensor carpi radialis brevis); pain increases when the hand is extended against resistance. **Medial epicondylitis** ("golfer's elbow") is strain of the common flexor tendon. The **radial nerve** is injured in the radial groove in fractures of the shaft of the humerus — all the extensor muscles are paralyzed, and **"wrist drop"** appears (the hand and fingers cannot be extended).
s3137 Interosseous muscles: the **palmar** interossei **adduct** and the **dorsal** interossei **abduct** (mnemonic "PAD–DAB"); the axis line is the **3rd (middle) finger**. When the ulnar nerve is injured, the patient cannot hold a sheet of paper between the fingers (paralysis of the interossei), and when gripping paper between the thumb and index finger flexes the thumb (because the adductor does not work, the long flexor compensates — Froment's sign).
s3138 **Carpal tunnel syndrome** is compression of the median nerve in the tunnel (pregnancy, hypothyroidism, rheumatoid arthritis, prolonged computer work): numbness of fingers I–III and pain that worsens at night, and in late stages atrophy of the thenar and weakness of thumb opposition. Treatment involves dividing the flexor retinaculum. **The structure of the synovial sheaths** determines the spread of purulent tenosynovitis: pus in the sheath of the little finger or thumb can spread into the common sheath and the forearm (Pirogov's/Parona's space), forming a **V-shaped phlegmon**; in fingers II–IV, by contrast, the process is usually confined to the finger. **De Quervain's disease** is stenosing inflammation of the sheath of the tendons in the first extensor compartment (abductor pollicis longus and extensor pollicis brevis).
s3139 In the radial groove of the forearm, above the styloid process, the radial artery lies superficially and can be pressed against the bone, so the **pulse** is taken here. If pressure in the "anatomical snuffbox" is painful, a **fracture of the scaphoid** is suspected (this fracture may not be visible on initial radiographs and may have complications).
s3140 1. Muscles of the upper limb: general information
s3141 The muscles of the upper limb are divided into **muscles of the shoulder girdle** and **muscles of the free upper limb** — those of the arm, forearm and hand. The upper limb is an organ of labor, so its muscles are numerous and finely differentiated; muscles providing precise finger movements are especially well developed in the forearm and hand. The muscles of the arm and forearm are separated by fascial septa into **anterior (flexor)** and **posterior (extensor)** groups; each group is supplied mainly by one nerve — knowing this rule is the key to understanding nerve injuries.

s3174 3. Muscles of the arm
s3175 The muscles of the arm are divided into two groups by the **medial and lateral intermuscular septa** running from the brachial fascia to the humerus. The **anterior group** (flexors) — the **biceps brachii**, **coracobrachialis** and **brachialis** — are all supplied by the **musculocutaneous nerve**. The **posterior group** (extensors) — the **triceps brachii** and **anconeus** — are supplied by the **radial nerve**.

s3202 4. Muscles of the forearm
s3203 The forearm muscles are multiarticular — they act on the elbow, radioulnar, radiocarpal and finger joints. The anterior group lies in **four layers**, the posterior group in **two layers**.
