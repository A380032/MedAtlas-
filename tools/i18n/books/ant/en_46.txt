s5693 **Ligaments of the uterus**: the **broad ligament** — a peritoneal duplication passing from the lateral borders of the uterus to the pelvic walls (containing the tubes, ovaries, vessels and the **parametrium** connective tissue); the **round ligament** — from the angle of the uterus through the inguinal canal to the labium majus (maintains anteversion); the **cardinal (transverse cervical) ligaments** — between the cervix and the lateral pelvic walls; the **uterosacral** and **vesicouterine** ligaments. The **pelvic floor muscles** (especially the levator ani) play the main role in supporting the uterus.
s5694 The **vagina** (*vagina*) is a musculomembranous tube 8–10 cm long, flattened anteroposteriorly; above, around the cervix, it forms the **vaginal fornix** (anterior, posterior — the deepest — and lateral parts), and below it passes through the urogenital diaphragm and opens into the vestibule (in virgins partly closed by the **hymen**). In front are the bladder and urethra, behind — the rectum (and above, the pouch of Douglas). The mucosa has transverse **vaginal rugae** and longitudinal **columns**; there are no glands.

s5697 7. Female external genital organs
s5698 The **mons pubis** is an elevation over the pubic symphysis, rich in fat and covered with hair. The **labia majora** are paired skin folds (homologues of the scrotum); between them lies the **pudendal cleft**. The **labia minora** are thin skin folds inside the labia majora (without fat); in front they form the **prepuce** and **frenulum of the clitoris**; between them is the **vestibule of the vagina** — into which open the **external urethral orifice** (in front), the **vaginal orifice** (behind), the **greater vestibular (Bartholin's) glands** (beside the vaginal orifice) and the lesser vestibular glands. The **clitoris** is the homologue of the penis: two corpora cavernosa, a body and a glans (there is no corpus spongiosum — it has become the **bulb of the vestibule**; the bulb lies around the vaginal orifice, at the base of the labia majora).
s5699 8. The perineum. The urogenital and pelvic diaphragms
s5700 The **perineum** (*perineum*), in the broad sense, is the complex of soft tissues closing the outlet of the lesser pelvis; it is a diamond-shaped region bounded in front by the lower border of the pubic symphysis, behind by the tip of the coccyx, and at the sides by the inferior rami of the pubic and ischial bones, the ischial tuberosities and the sacrotuberous ligaments. The line joining the ischial tuberosities divides the diamond into two triangles: the anterior — the **urogenital region** (urogenital diaphragm) — and the posterior — the **anal region** (pelvic diaphragm). In the narrow sense the perineum is the region between the external genitalia and the anus; deep within it lies the **perineal body (central tendon of the perineum)** — a fibromuscular node to which many muscles attach.

s5733 In the newborn boy the testes are usually in the scrotum; the testis is small (≈10 mm long, ≈0.2 g), and the seminiferous tubules have no lumen and are little coiled. By 1 year its mass increases ≈3 times, after which it grows slowly; at puberty it grows rapidly, reaching 20 g by 18–20 years. s5734 In the newborn the epididymis is relatively large (≈20 mm); it grows slowly in the first 10 years, then rapidly. The ductus deferens is thin (≈0.6 mm); the seminal vesicles are very small and lie high (covered by peritoneum on all sides). The processus vaginalis closes in the first months of life.
s5735 In the newborn the prostate is round, lies high and weighs ≈0.8 g, consisting mainly of muscle and connective tissue; at puberty its glandular part develops and it comes to resemble that of adults; in old age it often undergoes hyperplasia. In the newborn the penis is 2–2.5 cm long, and the prepuce is long and covers the glans (physiological phimosis — normal up to 3–5 years); it grows slowly until puberty, then rapidly.
s5736 In the newborn girl the ovaries are relatively large, lie **above the pelvic inlet** (in the abdominal cavity), and are smooth and elongated; the number of follicles is greatest (≈1–2 million); later they descend into the lesser pelvis. At puberty, as a result of ovulations, the surface becomes uneven (scarred); after menopause they shrink.
s5737 In the newborn the uterus lies **high** (above the pubis, in the abdominal cavity), is cylindrical, 3–3.5 cm long and weighs 3–5 g; **its cervix is longer than its body** (2/3 of its length); under the influence of maternal hormones the endometrium is developed (sometimes in the first week of life there is a bloody vaginal discharge). After birth it shrinks somewhat, from 7 years it descends into the lesser pelvis, and at puberty its body becomes larger than the cervix and it takes on a pear shape.
s5738 In the newborn the uterine tubes are tortuous and relatively long (because the broad ligament is shorter than the tube); the vagina is short (≈2.5–3.5 cm), lies high and is arched. The labia majora do not completely cover the labia minora; the clitoris is relatively large. The external genitalia grow rapidly at puberty.
s5739 The perineal muscles and fasciae are poorly developed in children, with little fat; they develop strongly at puberty, especially in females.

s5833 The mother of a 1-year-old boy noticed that the right side of the scrotum is empty. On examination the right testis is not in the scrotum, and a small mobile mass is palpable in the region of the inguinal canal; the left testis is in place.
s5834 What is the condition? How and when does the testis normally descend into the scrotum? Why is treatment necessary?
s5835 This is right-sided **cryptorchidism** (undescended testis; here — in the inguinal canal). The testis develops in the lumbar region of the abdominal cavity and descends under the influence of the **gubernaculum**: at 3 months it lies beside the deep inguinal ring, **at 7–8 months it passes through the inguinal canal**, and by birth it descends into the scrotum, preceded by the **processus vaginalis of the peritoneum**. For spermatogenesis the testis must be at a temperature 2–3 °C below body temperature; in an undescended testis the spermatogenic epithelium is damaged and the **risk of tumors** increases; often there is also a hernia because of the patent processus vaginalis. Therefore surgery (orchiopexy) is recommended before 1 year of age.
s5836 A 68-year-old man complains of frequent urination at night, a weak urinary stream and a feeling of incomplete bladder emptying. On digital rectal examination the gland is enlarged and soft-elastic, and the median groove is flattened.
s5837 Which organ is enlarged? Why is urination impaired, and why can this organ be examined through the rectum?
s5838 The **prostate** is enlarged — **prostatic hyperplasia (adenoma)**. The prostate lies below the bladder and surrounds the **prostatic part of the urethra**; when it enlarges (especially its middle lobe and periurethral part) it compresses the urethra and obstructs the outflow of urine from the bladder — the stream weakens, residual urine remains, and frequent urges to void appear. The **posterior surface of the prostate is in contact with the rectal ampulla** (separated only by a thin fascia — Denonvilliers' fascia), so its size, consistency and the groove between its right and left lobes are easily palpated through the rectum.

s5841 The perineum consists of the soft tissues closing the outlet of the lesser pelvis; the line between the ischial tuberosities divides it into the **urogenital** triangle (the urethra and vagina pass through it; the superficial and deep transverse perineal, ischiocavernosus and bulbospongiosus muscles, the urethral sphincter) and the **anal** triangle (pelvic diaphragm — the levator ani and coccygeus, the external anal sphincter).
