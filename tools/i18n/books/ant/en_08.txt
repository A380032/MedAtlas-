s0933 The ischium and pubis
s0934 The body of the **ischium** forms the posteroinferior part of the acetabulum; the **ramus** (*ramus ossis ischii*) continues downward from it. The posterior border of the body bears the **ischial spine** (*spina ischiadica*); above it lies the **greater sciatic notch** (*incisura ischiadica major*), and below it the **lesser sciatic notch**. At the junction of the ramus and body is the thick **ischial tuberosity** (*tuber ischiadicum*), which serves as a support when sitting.
s0935 The **pubis** consists of a body and **superior** and **inferior rami**. The upper border of the superior ramus is a sharp crest — the **pecten of the pubis** (*pecten ossis pubis*) — which ends anteriorly in the **pubic tubercle** (*tuberculum pubicum*). Medially the two pubic bones face each other by their **symphyseal surfaces** (*facies symphysialis*). Together the pubis and ischium bound the large **obturator foramen** (*foramen obturatum*); at its upper border is the **obturator groove**, through which vessels and nerves pass.

s0938 **Age-related features.** The hip bone develops from three main ossification centers: in the body of the **ischium** in the 4th month of fetal life, the **pubis** in the 5th month and the **ilium** in the 6th month. In the newborn the three bones are separate and joined in the acetabulum by **Y-shaped cartilage**; the acetabulum is shallow and oblique. The rami of the ischium and pubis fuse at 7–8 years; the Y-shaped cartilage of the acetabulum ossifies at **14–16 years**. Additional centers in the iliac crest, spines and ischial tuberosity appear at puberty and fuse at **20–25 years**.
s0939 3. The pelvis as a whole: structure, sex differences and dimensions
s0940 The **pelvis** (*pelvis*) is formed by the two hip bones, the sacrum and the coccyx. The **terminal line** (*linea terminalis*) — a line passing through the sacral promontory, the arcuate lines, the pecten of the pubis and the upper border of the symphysis — divides it into two parts: above — the **greater pelvis** (*pelvis major*), bounded by the alae of the ilia, a support for the abdominal organs; below — the **lesser pelvis** (*pelvis minor*), the cavity containing the urinary bladder, rectum and internal genital organs — in women it is the **birth canal**. The **superior aperture** of the lesser pelvis (*apertura pelvis superior*) corresponds to the terminal line, and the **inferior aperture** (*apertura pelvis inferior*) is bounded by the coccyx, ischial tuberosities, inferior pubic rami and the symphysis.

s1009 4. The femur and patella
s1010 The **femur** (*femur*) is the longest and strongest tubular bone of the human skeleton. Its **proximal end** bears the spherical **head** (*caput femoris*); in its center lies the **fovea of the head** (*fovea capitis femoris*), to which the ligament of the head attaches. The head is joined to the body by the obliquely directed **neck of the femur** (*collum femoris*): the angle between the neck and the axis of the body is **≈130°** in adults (larger in children, smaller in the elderly). Where the neck passes into the body there are, laterally, the large **greater trochanter** (*trochanter major*) and, posteromedially, the **lesser trochanter** (*trochanter minor*); they are connected anteriorly by the **intertrochanteric line** (*linea intertrochanterica*) and posteriorly by the **intertrochanteric crest** (*crista intertrochanterica*). On the medial surface of the greater trochanter is the **trochanteric fossa** (*fossa trochanterica*).
s1011 The **body** (*corpus femoris*) is slightly convex forward and rounded; along its posterior surface runs the two-lipped **rough line** (*linea aspera*) — its **lateral and medial lips** continue upward to form the **gluteal tuberosity** (*tuberositas glutea*) and the **pectineal line** (*linea pectinea*), and below they diverge to bound the **popliteal surface** (*facies poplitea*). The **distal end** widens to form two condyles — the **medial** and **lateral condyles** (*condylus medialis et lateralis*); posteriorly they are separated by the deep **intercondylar fossa** (*fossa intercondylaris*), and anteriorly they are joined by the **patellar surface** (*facies patellaris*) for the patella. On the lateral surfaces of the condyles lie the **medial and lateral epicondyles**.

s1014 The **patella** (*patella*) is the largest **sesamoid bone** in the body; it develops within the tendon of the quadriceps femoris. It is triangular: its broad **base** (*basis patellae*) faces upward and its **apex** (*apex patellae*) downward; its posterior articular surface articulates with the patellar surface of the femur. Its ossification center appears at 2–6 years.
s1015 **Age-related features.** The primary center appears in the diaphysis at the end of the 2nd month of fetal life. In the newborn the femur is relatively short, its ends are cartilaginous, and the neck-shaft angle is large (≈150°). **The ossification center in the distal epiphysis appears just before birth** (in the 9th month of fetal life) — its presence is a **sign of fetal maturity** (Béclard's nucleus). A center appears in the head in the 1st year (at 3–6 months), in the greater trochanter at ≈4 years and in the lesser trochanter at ≈8 years. The trochanters fuse with the body at **16–18**, the head at **17–19**, and the distal epiphysis at **20–22 years** in women and **22–24 years** in men.
s1016 5. Bones of the leg
s1017 The **tibia** (*tibia*) lies on the medial side of the leg and bears the full body weight. Its **proximal end** widens to form the **medial and lateral condyles**; their upper **articular surfaces** (*facies articularis superior*) articulate with the condyles of the femur. Between the articular surfaces is the **intercondylar eminence** (*eminentia intercondylaris*) with two tubercles, and in front of and behind it the **anterior and posterior intercondylar areas** (where the cruciate ligaments and menisci attach). The posteroinferior surface of the lateral condyle bears an **articular surface** (*facies articularis fibularis*) for the head of the fibula.
s1018 The **body** is triangular; its **anterior border** (*margo anterior*) is sharp and palpable under the skin along its whole length; at its upper part is the **tibial tuberosity** (*tuberositas tibiae*), to which the quadriceps tendon (patellar ligament) attaches. The lateral border is the interosseous border; on the posterior surface the **soleal line** (*linea musculi solei*) runs obliquely. At the **distal end** are the downward-projecting **medial malleolus** (*malleolus medialis*) and the **inferior articular surface**, which articulates with the talus, and laterally the **fibular notch** (*incisura fibularis*) for the fibula.
s1019 The **fibula** (*fibula*) is a thin long bone on the lateral side of the leg; it bears almost no body weight and serves mainly as a site of muscle attachment and as the lateral wall of the ankle joint. At its proximal end are the **head** (*caput fibulae*) and its **apex** (*apex*), and at its distal end the **lateral malleolus** (*malleolus lateralis*), clearly palpable under the skin; the lateral malleolus extends **lower** than the medial malleolus. On the posterior surface of the malleolus is the **malleolar groove** (*sulcus malleolaris*), through which the tendons of the fibular muscles pass.

s1022 **Age-related features.** An ossification center also appears in the proximal epiphysis of the tibia **just before birth** (a sign of maturity), and in the distal epiphysis in the 2nd year; a secondary center appears in the tuberosity at 10–13 years. The distal epiphysis fuses with the diaphysis at **16–19**, and the proximal epiphysis at **19–24 years**. In the fibula the center of the distal epiphysis appears at ≈2 years and that of the head at 3–5 years; they fuse at **20–22** and **≈24 years**.
s1023 6. Bones of the foot
s1024 The skeleton of the foot (*ossa pedis*) consists of three parts: the **tarsal bones** (*ossa tarsi*) — 7, the **metatarsal bones** (*ossa metatarsi*) — 5, and the **phalanges of the toes** — 14.
s1025 Tarsal bones
s1026 The **proximal row** is formed by two large bones. The **talus** (*talus*) is the “link” between the leg and the foot: it has a **head**, **neck** and **body**; the upper part of the body — the **trochlea** (*trochlea tali*) — forms the ankle joint with the leg bones. No muscles attach to the talus. The **calcaneus** (*calcaneus*) is the largest bone of the foot; posteriorly it ends in the large **calcaneal tuberosity** (*tuber calcanei*) (where the Achilles tendon attaches), and medially it bears a process supporting the talus — the **sustentaculum tali** (*sustentaculum tali*).
s1027 **Distal row**: medially the **navicular bone** (*os naviculare*) — between the head of the talus and the three cuneiform bones; its medial-inferior surface bears a tuberosity. In front of it are the three **cuneiform bones** — **medial, intermediate and lateral** (*ossa cuneiformia*); laterally, in front of the calcaneus, is the **cuboid bone** (*os cuboideum*), whose inferior surface bears a groove for the tendon of the fibularis longus muscle.
s1028 Metatarsal bones and phalanges of the toes
s1029 The **metatarsal bones** (*ossa metatarsi I–V*) are short tubular bones (base, body, head). The I metatarsal is the shortest and thickest, the II the longest; the lateral side of the base of the V metatarsal bears a **tuberosity** (*tuberositas ossis metatarsi V*), palpable under the skin. The **phalanges of the toes** resemble those of the hand but are shorter; the big toe (*hallux*) has two phalanges and the other toes three each. Two sesamoid bones lie beneath the I metatarsophalangeal joint.
