s2377 The forearm bones are joined along their entire length by the **interosseous membrane of the forearm** (*membrana interossea antebrachii*, a syndesmosis), and at their distal ends by the **distal radioulnar joint** (pivot); in the latter a triangular **articular disc** lies below the head of the ulna. The proximal and distal radioulnar joints work as a **combined** joint: in pronation the radius rotates around the ulna and crosses it, and in supination the two lie parallel.
s2378 **Radiographic anatomy.** On an AP radiograph the spaces of the humeroulnar and humeroradial joints appear as a sharply outlined wavy band ≈3 mm wide; the olecranon is superimposed on the shadow of the medial edge of the humerus. On a lateral radiograph the trochlea, capitulum, olecranon and coronoid process are seen. **In children** 6 ossification centers appear one after another in the elbow region (capitulum ≈1 year, radial head ≈5, medial epicondyle ≈7, trochlea ≈10, olecranon ≈10, lateral epicondyle ≈11 years) — they are compared with the healthy side so as not to confuse them with fracture fragments.
s2379 **Age-related features.** In the newborn the ends of the bones forming the elbow joint are cartilaginous; the capsule encloses the joint leaving the epicondyles outside, and the ligaments are poorly developed.
s2380 4. The radiocarpal (wrist) joint and the joints of the hand
s2381 The **radiocarpal (wrist) joint** (*articulatio radiocarpalis*) is formed between the carpal articular surface of the radius and the articular disc (proximally) and the scaphoid, lunate and triquetrum of the proximal row (distally); the ulna does not take part directly. It is **ellipsoid** and biaxial: flexion and extension around the frontal axis, radial and ulnar abduction (deviation) around the sagittal axis, and circumduction. It is reinforced by the **radial and ulnar collateral ligaments** and the **palmar and dorsal radiocarpal ligaments**.
s2382 Other joints of the hand: the **midcarpal joint** (*articulatio mediocarpalis*) — between the proximal and distal rows, with an S-shaped joint space, moving together with the radiocarpal joint; the **intercarpal joints**; the **carpometacarpal joints** — plane and of little mobility for fingers II–V, **saddle-shaped for the thumb** (opposition of the thumb); the **metacarpophalangeal joints** — ball-shaped (but in practice biaxial: flexion-extension, abduction-adduction); the **interphalangeal joints** — hinge, uniaxial (flexion-extension), reinforced by collateral ligaments.

The capsule is tight on the palmar side — extension of the hand is somewhat limited.
s2386 5. Joints of the pelvic girdle
s2387 The **sacroiliac joint** (*articulatio sacroiliaca*) is formed between the auricular surfaces of the sacrum and ilium; it is **plane**, with very little movement (amphiarthrosis). The capsule is thick and tight; it is reinforced by the **anterior**, **posterior** and very strong **interosseous sacroiliac ligaments** (between the tuberosities). With age the joint often partly ossifies. The **pubic symphysis** (*symphysis pubica*) is formed by the **interpubic disc** between the symphyseal surfaces of the two pubic bones; the disc contains a slit-like cavity. It is reinforced above by the **superior pubic ligament** and below by the **arcuate pubic ligament**.
s2388 Intrinsic ligaments of the pelvis: the **sacrotuberous ligament** (*lig. sacrotuberale*) and the **sacrospinous ligament** (*lig. sacrospinale*) — they convert the sciatic notches into the **greater** and **lesser sciatic foramina** (*foramen ischiadicum majus et minus*); through the greater sciatic foramen pass the piriformis muscle, the sciatic nerve and the gluteal vessels, and through the lesser the obturator internus muscle and the pudendal vessels and nerve. The **obturator membrane** (*membrana obturatoria*) closes the obturator foramen, leaving only the obturator canal above. The **iliolumbar ligament** connects the 5th lumbar vertebra to the iliac crest.

s2391 6. The hip joint
s2392 The **hip joint** (*articulatio coxae*) is formed between the head of the femur and the acetabulum of the hip bone (its **lunate surface**). The fibrocartilaginous **acetabular labrum** (*labrum acetabuli*) is attached to the rim of the acetabulum; it deepens the socket and embraces more than half of the head; the **transverse acetabular ligament** bridges the acetabular notch. That is why the joint is **cotyloid** (*articulatio cotylica*) — a variety of ball-and-socket joint, multiaxial, but with a smaller range of motion than the shoulder joint and much greater stability.
s2393 The capsule is very strong; on the hip bone it attaches to the rim of the acetabulum, and on the femur in front to the **intertrochanteric line** and behind ≈1.5 cm medial to the intertrochanteric crest — so most of the femoral neck lies **inside** the capsule. The capsule is reinforced by three capsular ligaments: the strongest ligament in the body, the **iliofemoral ligament** (*lig. iliofemorale*, Bertin's ligament; withstands loads up to 300 kg, limits hyperextension of the femur and keeps the body from falling backward when standing), and the **pubofemoral** (*lig. pubofemorale*) and **ischiofemoral ligaments** (*lig. ischiofemorale*). Inside the capsule is the **ligament of the head of the femur** (*lig. capitis femoris*) — running from the fovea of the head to the acetabular fossa; it carries an artery that nourishes the head (especially important in children). Circular fibers in the layers of the capsule form the **zona orbicularis** around the neck.

s2398 Movements: flexion around the frontal axis (≈120° with the knee flexed) and extension (limited by the iliofemoral ligament, ≈15°), abduction and adduction around the sagittal axis, medial and lateral rotation around the vertical axis, and circumduction. **Radiographic anatomy:** on an AP radiograph the acetabulum appears as a socket ≈3×5 cm in size; between the head and the socket there is an arched lucent joint space; in the inferomedial part of the acetabulum is the "teardrop" shadow; Shenton's line (a continuous arc along the upper margin of the obturator foramen and the lower margin of the femoral neck) is normally unbroken.
s2399 7. The knee joint
s2400 The **knee joint** (*articulatio genus*) is the largest and most complex joint in the human body. It is formed by the femoral condyles, the superior articular surfaces of the tibia and the posterior surface of the patella (the fibula does not take part). It is a **compound** (three bones), **complex** (with menisci) joint, **bicondylar** in shape. The main movement is **flexion and extension** around the frontal axis; in the flexed position slight **rotation** around the vertical axis is also possible.
s2401 The **menisci** are crescent-shaped fibrocartilaginous plates between the incongruent surfaces of the femur and tibia; they are triangular in section, with a thick outer edge attached to the capsule and a thin inner edge. The **medial meniscus** (*meniscus medialis*) is **C**-shaped and attached to the medial collateral ligament, so it is less mobile and **tears more often**; the **lateral meniscus** is **O**-shaped and more mobile. Anteriorly the menisci are joined by the **transverse ligament of the knee**. The menisci distribute load, absorb shock and stabilize the joint.
s2402 The **cruciate ligaments** (*ligg. cruciata genus*) lie inside the joint but outside the synovial membrane: the **anterior cruciate ligament** runs from the inner surface of the lateral femoral condyle to the anterior intercondylar area of the tibia — it limits **forward** displacement of the tibia; the **posterior cruciate ligament** runs from the inner surface of the medial condyle to the posterior intercondylar area — it limits **backward** displacement of the tibia. Extracapsular ligaments: the **tibial collateral ligament** (medial; blended with the capsule and the medial meniscus), the **fibular collateral ligament** (lateral; separate from the capsule), in front — the **patellar ligament** (*lig. patellae*), a continuation of the quadriceps tendon, and behind — the **oblique** and **arcuate popliteal ligaments**.

s2407 In the knee joint the synovial membrane forms many folds (the fat-containing **alar folds**) and **bursae**; the largest is the **suprapatellar bursa** (*bursa suprapatellaris*), which communicates with the joint cavity and extends 5–6 cm above the patella. There are many other synovial bursae around the joint (prepatellar, infrapatellar and others).
s2408 **Radiographic anatomy.** On an AP radiograph the joint space between the femoral and tibial condyles appears as a clear, slightly curved lucent band **6–8 mm** wide — this corresponds to the thickness of the cartilages and menisci between the femur and tibia (the menisci are not visible on radiographs). The patella is superimposed on the shadow of the femur. On a lateral radiograph the patella, the oval shadow of the femoral condyles and the intercondylar eminence are seen.

s2411 **Age-related features.** In the newborn the articular surfaces are cartilaginous, and there are ossification centers in the distal epiphysis of the femur and the proximal epiphysis of the tibia (a sign of maturity). The patella is cartilaginous; the menisci are relatively thick and rich in blood vessels (in adults only their outer edges are supplied with blood — so a tear of the inner part does not heal). Development of the joint continues **until 18 years**.
s2412 8. Joints of the leg bones and joints of the foot
s2413 The leg bones are joined proximally by the **tibiofibular joint** (*articulatio tibiofibularis* — plane, of little mobility), along their entire length by the **interosseous membrane of the leg**, and distally by the **tibiofibular syndesmosis** (*syndesmosis tibiofibularis*); the syndesmosis firmly holds the "mortise" of the ankle joint.
s2414 The **ankle (talocrural) joint** (*articulatio talocruralis*) is formed between the inferior articular surface and medial malleolus of the tibia and the lateral malleolus of the fibula (together the **"mortise"**) and the **trochlea** of the talus. It is a **hinge** joint, uniaxial: around the frontal axis — **flexion** (toward the sole, plantar flexion) and **extension** (raising the foot, dorsiflexion). The trochlea of the talus is wider in front — when the foot is plantarflexed it sits more loosely in the mortise, so in this position slight sideways movement and **sprain injuries** occur more often.
s2415 Ligaments: on the medial side the strong, four-part **medial (deltoid) ligament** (*lig. mediale, deltoideum*); on the lateral side three separate ligaments — the **anterior and posterior talofibular** and the **calcaneofibular ligaments**. The lateral ligaments are weaker, so when the foot turns inward (supination injury) the **anterior talofibular ligament** is most often stretched or torn.
