s0880 A 65-year-old woman slipped on ice and fell on her outstretched palm. The distal forearm has a “dinner fork” deformity, and the styloid processes lie at the same level.

s0915 The **anterior superior iliac spine** is the main landmark for measuring leg length (from it to the medial malleolus), for the projection of the inguinal ligament and for surgical incisions of the abdominal wall. Bone marrow biopsies and trephine biopsies are taken from the **iliac crest** (its posterior part), and in children marrow puncture is also performed here. The **upper outer quadrant of the gluteal surface** is the safe site for intramuscular injection: the sciatic nerve passes through the lower inner quadrant.
s0916 Because the acetabulum is shallow in the newborn, **congenital hip dislocation (developmental dysplasia of the hip)** occurs. To detect it early, all newborns undergo ultrasound examination of the hip joint and special tests (Ortolani, Barlow); if detected early, it is fully cured with wide swaddling and a special splint.
s0917 Remember the subpubic angle with your fingers: the obtuse angle of the female pelvis resembles the angle between the **thumb and index finger** (≈90°), and the acute angle of the male pelvis the angle between the **index and middle fingers** (≈70°).
s0918 The **true conjugate** is not measured directly — it is calculated: **9 cm is subtracted from the external conjugate** or **1.5–2 cm from the diagonal conjugate** (20 − 9 = 11 cm; 13 − 2 = 11 cm). If the true conjugate is less than 11 cm, the pelvis is considered **contracted** and natural delivery may be difficult.
s0919 **Fracture of the femoral neck** is common in the elderly (especially in women with osteoporosis) from a fall on the side. The neck lies inside the joint capsule, and the vessels supplying the head run along the neck — in a fracture they are torn, and the risk of **avascular necrosis of the head** and nonunion of the fracture is high. Therefore, **joint replacement (arthroplasty)** is often performed. The patient's leg is externally rotated and shortened, and the greater trochanter is displaced upward.
s0920 Around the neck of the fibula the **common fibular nerve** (*n. fibularis communis*) lies just under the skin, pressed against the bone. A blow here, a tight cast or prolonged sitting with crossed legs can injure the nerve, leading to **“foot drop”** (inability to dorsiflex the foot and extend the toes, “steppage gait”). Because the anterior surface of the tibia lies directly under the skin, fractures here are often **open**.
s0921 In adolescents (10–15 years) the apophysis of the tibial tuberosity may become strained by the pull of the quadriceps muscle and form a painful bump — **Osgood–Schlatter disease** (common in adolescent athletes). The X-ray shows fragmentation of the apophysis — this must not be misinterpreted as a fracture.
s0922 There are 7 tarsal bones: **2 + 1 + 3 + 1** — posteriorly **2** large ones (talus and calcaneus), in the middle medially **1** (navicular), in front **3** cuneiform and laterally **1** cuboid. That is one fewer than the 8 bones of the hand: the pisiform has no “counterpart.”
s0923 If the ligaments and muscles supporting the arches weaken, **flatfoot** develops (longitudinal or transverse): rapid fatigue and pain in the foot and lower leg. In infants and young children the plantar fat pad is thick, so the foot looks flat — this is normal; the arch forms by 4–6 years. Therefore, flatfoot is usually diagnosed after 6–7 years (plantography, podometry).
s0924 1. The lower limb skeleton: general information
s0925 The lower limb skeleton consists of two divisions: the **pelvic girdle** (*cingulum membri inferioris*) — the paired hip bones, and the **free part of the lower limb** (*pars libera membri inferioris*) — the femur, patella, tibia and fibula, and the bones of the foot. The main function of the lower limb bones is to support the body weight with little expenditure of energy and to move the body in space. Therefore they are larger and more massive than the upper limb bones, their joints are stronger and their range of motion smaller. When standing, the body's center of gravity lies in the pelvis, in front of the II sacral vertebra.
s0926 In the newborn the lower limb is short relative to the upper limb, and the thigh is short. The lower limb grows especially quickly in the second period of childhood and at puberty; by puberty the thigh lengthens ≈4.5 times, the leg ≈4 times and the foot ≈3 times.
s0927 2. The hip bone
s0928 The **hip bone** (*os coxae*) is formed by the fusion of three bones — the **ilium** (*os ilium*), **ischium** (*os ischii*) and **pubis** (*os pubis*). Until 14–16 years they are joined by cartilage, after which they ossify into a single bone. Where the bodies of the three bones meet, the deep **acetabulum** (*acetabulum*), into which the head of the femur fits, is formed on the outer surface. The acetabulum is surrounded by a **rim** (*limbus acetabuli*); its articular part is the horseshoe-shaped **lunate surface** (*facies lunata*), its center is the rough **acetabular fossa** (*fossa acetabuli*), and on its inferomedial side is the **acetabular notch** (*incisura acetabuli*).

s0931 The ilium
s0932 The ilium consists of a **body** (*corpus ossis ilii*) and a broad **ala (wing)** (*ala ossis ilii*). The thickened upper border of the ala — the **iliac crest** (*crista iliaca*) — is S-shaped and bears an **outer lip**, **intermediate line** and **inner lip**, to which the abdominal muscles attach. Anteriorly the crest ends in the **anterior superior iliac spine** (*spina iliaca anterior superior*), below which lies the **anterior inferior spine**; posteriorly there are the **posterior superior** and **posterior inferior iliac spines**. On the outer — **gluteal surface** (*facies glutea*) of the ala, three rough lines — the **anterior, posterior and inferior gluteal lines** — delimit the origins of the gluteal muscles. The inner surface is concave — the **iliac fossa** (*fossa iliaca*); behind it lie the **auricular surface** (*facies auricularis*), which articulates with the sacrum, and the **iliac tuberosity**. On the inner surface the fossa is bounded below by the **arcuate line** (*linea arcuata*).
