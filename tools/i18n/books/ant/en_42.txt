s5248 The **pulmonary lobule** is a pyramidal part 1–1.5 cm in diameter supplied by a lobular bronchus (≈800 in each lung). Within the lobule the bronchus divides into 12–18 **terminal bronchioles**. The **acinus** (*acinus*) is the **structural-functional unit** of the lung: the branches of one terminal bronchiole — **respiratory bronchioles** (orders I–III, with alveoli in their walls) → **alveolar ducts** → **alveolar sacs** → **alveoli**. The two lungs contain ≈300–700 million alveoli; the alveolar wall is lined by **alveolocytes** (type I — gas exchange, type II — produce **surfactant**) and a network of capillaries. The thickness of the **air–blood barrier** is ≈0.5 µm. The aggregate of acini is called the **respiratory portion** (gas exchange), and the bronchial tree above it the **conducting portion**.

s5251 5. The pleura
s5252 The **pleura** (*pleura*) is the serous membrane enveloping the lungs and lining the walls of the thoracic cavity. The **visceral (pulmonary) pleura** adheres firmly to the lung tissue and also enters the fissures; at the **root of the lung** it passes into the **parietal pleura**, and below the root it forms the two-layered **pulmonary ligament**, which descends to the diaphragm. Parts of the **parietal pleura**: the **costal**, **diaphragmatic** and **mediastinal** pleura; above, over the apex of the lung, it forms the **cupula of the pleura**. Between the layers is the narrow **pleural cavity** (*cavitas pleuralis*) — it contains ≈1–2 mL of serous fluid, and its pressure is below atmospheric (**negative pressure**) — which is why the lung is stretched behind the wall of the thoracic cage.
s5253 Where the parts of the parietal pleura pass into one another, **pleural recesses (sinuses)** form — reserve spaces that the lung does not fully enter even in inspiration: the **costodiaphragmatic recess** (the largest and deepest, ≈6–8 cm in the midaxillary line; the lowest point), the **costomediastinal recess** (in front, larger on the left in the region of the cardiac notch) and the **phrenicomediastinal recess**.

s5278 7. The mediastinum
s5279 The **mediastinum** (*mediastinum*) is the complex of organs between the right and left mediastinal pleurae, together with the tissue between them. Its boundaries: **in front** — the sternum, **behind** — the thoracic vertebrae, **at the sides** — the mediastinal pleura, **below** — the diaphragm, **above** — the superior thoracic aperture. According to the international nomenclature, the mediastinum is divided by a conventional horizontal plane passing through the sternal angle (angle of Louis) and the disc between the 4th and 5th thoracic vertebrae into **superior** and **inferior** parts, and the inferior part, by the pericardium, into **anterior**, **middle** and **posterior** parts.

s5292 In the newborn the **trachea** begins at the level of the 3rd–4th cervical vertebrae and divides at the level of the **2nd–3rd thoracic vertebrae**; it is 3.2–4.5 cm long, funnel-shaped, ≈8–10 mm in diameter; its cartilages are soft and flexible, its membranous wall relatively wide, and its mucosa delicate, with few glands and rich in blood vessels. It grows rapidly in the first 6 months and during puberty; the bifurcation descends to the level of the 4th–5th thoracic vertebrae at 7 years and later to the 5th.
s5293 In the newborn the **main bronchi** are short (≈1.2 cm); the right bronchus diverges at ≈26° and the left at ≈49°; their cartilages are poorly developed. The branching of the bronchial tree at birth resembles that of adults; the bronchi grow rapidly in the 1st year and during puberty.
s5294 In the newborn the **lungs** are large relative to the thoracic cage and weigh ≈50–57 g (both); the upper lobes are small and the lower lobes large; the upper and middle lobes of the right lung are of equal size. There is much connective tissue and few elastic fibers — in children the lung tissue is less airy and swells easily (predisposition to pneumonia).
s5295 At birth the number of alveoli is ≈1/6–1/3 of that of adults; **up to 7–8 years** maturation of the bronchial tree and an increase in the number of alveoli continue; new alveoli form up to 12–15 years, after which they mainly grow in volume. The structure of the lung is completed at 15–25 years; the vital capacity increases 4 times by 1 year, 8 times by 8 years, 10 times by 12 years and 20 times by 20 years.
s5296 In the newborn the apex of the lung is at the level of the 1st rib, and the inferior border is one rib higher than in adults; with age they gradually descend. In the elderly the elasticity of the lungs decreases (age-related emphysema), and the apex descends lower.
s5297 In the newborn the **pleura** is very thin and mobile (little loose tissue) — which is why in children the mediastinal organs shift easily when fluid or air accumulates in the pleural cavity. The pleura resembles that of adults by 7 years.
s5298 In the newborn the **mediastinum** is wide and short (the diaphragm is high, the heart horizontal and the thymus large), occupying ≈1/2 of the thoracic cavity, and ≈1/3 at 1 month; by 3 years it resembles that of adults.

s5375 While playing, a 3-year-old child swallowed (inhaled) a nut; after a violent coughing fit the cough lessened somewhat, but shortness of breath persists. On auscultation breath sounds are diminished over the lower part of the right lung.
s5376 Where may the foreign body have gone, and why precisely there? What examination and treatment are required?
s5377 A foreign body most often enters the **right main bronchus**, and then the **right lower lobe bronchus**: the right main bronchus is **short and wide** and almost a **vertical continuation** of the trachea (deviating ≈20–25° from the tracheal axis), whereas the left bronchus is longer, narrower and diverges at ≈45°. If the bronchus is blocked, the part of the lung beyond it collapses (atelectasis) or becomes overinflated — breath sounds are diminished. Diagnosis and treatment — removal of the foreign body by **bronchoscopy**.
s5378 After a road traffic accident a patient has severe shortness of breath, lagging of the left side of the chest during breathing, a hyperresonant (box-like) percussion note and absent breath sounds; the trachea is displaced to the right.
s5379 What condition has developed? Which property of the pleura keeps the lung stretched, and why has the lung collapsed here? Why is the mediastinum displaced?
s5380 This is a **left-sided (tension) pneumothorax** — air has entered the pleural cavity. Normally, between the parietal and visceral pleura, **the pressure in the closed pleural cavity is below atmospheric (negative)**, and the lung is held stretched behind the wall of the thoracic cage. When air enters, the negative pressure is lost and the lung, owing to its own elasticity, **collapses**. In a valvular (tension) pneumothorax air accumulates and the pressure rises, and the **mediastinal** organs (heart, trachea) shift to the healthy side — this impairs the circulation. Emergency treatment — needle decompression in the 2nd intercostal space in the midclavicular line, followed by drainage.
s5381 In a patient with heart failure, fluid was found in the right pleural cavity. The physician performed pleural puncture in the 8th intercostal space along the posterior axillary line.
s5382 In which part of the pleural cavity does the fluid collect? Why is the puncture performed precisely at this site, and along which border of the rib?
s5383 Fluid collects first in the lowest and deepest part of the pleural cavity — the **costodiaphragmatic recess**; it is deepest (6–8 cm) in the region of the midaxillary and posterior axillary lines. Here the inferior border of the lung (9th rib in the posterior axillary line) lies above the border of the pleura (10th rib), so the needle enters the recess without injuring the lung; if the puncture is made too low, the diaphragm and liver may be injured. The needle is inserted **along the upper border of the lower rib** — the intercostal neurovascular bundle lies in the groove on the lower border of the upper rib.
