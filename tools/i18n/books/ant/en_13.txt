s1596 A 45-year-old woman complains that the outer parts of her visual field on both sides have “disappeared”; her menstrual cycle is disturbed, and milk is discharged from the breast. Brain MRI shows an enlarged sella turcica with a tumor in it.
s1597 From which organ has the tumor arisen? What is the anatomical cause of the visual disturbance? By which route can the tumor be reached?
s1598 This is a **pituitary tumor** (prolactinoma) — the pituitary gland lies in the **hypophyseal fossa** of the sella turcica. Growing upward, the tumor compresses the **optic chiasm**, which lies in the prechiasmatic sulcus above the tuberculum sellae: in the chiasm the fibers from the medial (nasal) halves of the retinas cross, and they serve the outer (temporal) halves of the visual fields — **bitemporal hemianopia**. The tumor is usually removed through the nose via the **sphenoidal sinus** (transsphenoidally), because the hypophyseal fossa lies directly above the sinus.
s1599 After a winter cold, a student woke up in the morning and noticed that the right side of his face had “frozen”: he cannot close his right eye or wrinkle his forehead, the right corner of his mouth droops, and his sense of taste has changed when eating.
s1600 Which nerve is affected? What is the course of its canal, and why is this nerve so often affected?
s1601 This is **peripheral palsy of the facial nerve (VII)** (Bell's palsy). The nerve passes through the long, narrow and bent **facial canal** in the pyramid of the temporal bone: from the fundus of the internal acoustic meatus → horizontally forward → geniculum → backward along the medial wall of the tympanic cavity → downward → **stylomastoid foramen**. With exposure to cold or a viral infection the nerve swells and, unable to expand in the bony canal, becomes compressed. The change in taste indicates that the nerve is damaged above the origin of the **chorda tympani**.
s1602 2 weeks after acute otitis media, a 5-year-old child's temperature rose again; redness and a painful swelling appeared behind the ear, and the auricle is pushed forward and downward.
s1603 What complication has developed? By which route has the inflammation spread, and where else can it spread?
s1604 This is **acute mastoiditis**. The inflammation of the middle ear has spread from the tympanic cavity via the **mastoid antrum** into the **cells of the mastoid process**. Through the thin walls of the cells the inflammation can spread to the **sigmoid sinus** (thrombosis), to the **posterior and middle cranial fossae** (meningitis, cerebellar or temporal lobe abscess), and also beneath the neck muscles. Treatment — antibiotics and, if necessary, antromastoidotomy.

s1634 The roots of the upper **second premolar and first and second molars** lie very close to the floor of the maxillary sinus and sometimes protrude into it. Therefore inflammation of these teeth may spread into the sinus causing **odontogenic maxillary sinusitis**, and extraction of a tooth may leave the sinus open (an oroantral fistula). Because the outlet of the sinus (hiatus) lies in its upper part, pus does not drain well on its own.
s1635 The maxillary sinus is the **largest** paranasal sinus. Openings of the sinuses into the nose: into the **middle nasal meatus** — the maxillary and frontal sinuses and the anterior and middle ethmoidal cells; into the **superior nasal meatus** — the posterior ethmoidal cells; into the **sphenoethmoidal recess** — the sphenoidal sinus; into the **inferior nasal meatus** — the nasolacrimal duct.
s1636 The hard palate is formed by the union in the midline of the right and left parts of the maxillae and palatine bones. If this union fails during fetal life, a **cleft palate** develops — the oral and nasal cavities communicate, the infant has difficulty sucking and food comes out of the nose; it is often combined with a cleft lip and is corrected surgically. In the region of the **greater palatine foramen**, palatal anesthesia is performed to anesthetize the upper teeth.
s1637 The **nasal bones** are the most frequently fractured facial bones (boxing, falls). **Deviation of the nasal septum** (at the junction of the vomer and the perpendicular plate) makes breathing difficult and is corrected surgically (septoplasty). When the lacrimal passages are blocked, **dacryocystitis** develops; because the lacrimal bone is very thin, during surgery a new passage between the lacrimal sac and the nasal cavity is made precisely through it (dacryocystorhinostomy).
s1638 In dentistry, **mandibular (inferior alveolar) nerve block** is used to anesthetize the lower teeth: the anesthetic is injected around the **mandibular foramen** (the region of the lingula) on the inner surface of the ramus — here the inferior alveolar nerve enters the canal. For the anterior teeth and the chin region the anesthetic is injected in the region of the **mental foramen**, and for the upper anterior teeth and upper lip in the region of the **infraorbital foramen**. The mandible most often fractures at the **angle**, the **canine region** and the **neck of the condylar process** (the bone is weaker at these sites).
s1639 The angle of the mandible changes over life like a **“U”**: large in the newborn (**≈150°**) → small in adults (**120–130°**) → large again in edentulous elderly people (**≈140°**). The reason is the load from the teeth and the masticatory muscles: when teeth are present and chewing is strong, the ramus stands more upright.
s1640 **Fracture of the hyoid bone** is an important sign in forensic medicine: it is often seen in cases of **strangulation** (manual compression of the neck). In young people the parts of the bone are joined by cartilage and are flexible, so it fractures less often, whereas in the elderly the fused, brittle bone fractures more easily.
s1641 On X-rays of children's facial bones, do not misinterpret the shadows of **tooth buds and unerupted permanent teeth** in the body of the jaw as a tumor or cyst. Knowing the eruption times of the teeth (deciduous — 6 months–2.5 years, permanent — 6–12 years, wisdom teeth — 17–25 years) is necessary for reading jaw X-rays correctly.
s1642 1. Bones of the facial skeleton: general information
s1643 The **facial skeleton** (*viscerocranium*) forms the skeleton of the orbits and the nasal and oral cavities and serves as a support for the masticatory and facial muscles. It includes **paired** bones — the **maxilla** (*maxilla*), **palatine bone** (*os palatinum*), **zygomatic bone** (*os zygomaticum*), **nasal bone** (*os nasale*), **lacrimal bone** (*os lacrimale*) and **inferior nasal concha** (*concha nasalis inferior*) — and **unpaired** bones — the **vomer** (*vomer*), **mandible** (*mandibula*) and **hyoid bone** (*os hyoideum*). The facial bones develop mainly from the visceral arches (maxilla, mandible, hyoid bone) and the nasal capsule; most ossify by the endesmal route.

s1667 2. The maxilla
s1668 The **maxilla** (*maxilla*) is a paired, central bone of the face; it articulates with almost all the bones of the face. It has a **body** and **four processes**. The **body** (*corpus maxillae*) contains the largest paranasal sinus — the **maxillary sinus** (*sinus maxillaris*, antrum of Highmore); it opens into the **middle nasal meatus** of the nasal cavity through the **maxillary hiatus** (*hiatus maxillaris*).
s1669 The body has four surfaces. The **anterior surface** (*facies anterior*) — below the infraorbital margin is the **infraorbital foramen** (*foramen infraorbitale*), and below it the **canine fossa** (*fossa canina*); its medial boundary — the **nasal notch** (*incisura nasalis*) — ends below in the **anterior nasal spine** (*spina nasalis anterior*). The **orbital surface** (*facies orbitalis*) is the floor of the orbit; from its posterior border the **infraorbital groove** begins, becoming a **canal** in front and opening by the above-mentioned foramen. The **infratemporal surface** (*facies infratemporalis*) faces backward; it bears the prominent **maxillary tuberosity** (*tuber maxillae*) and the **alveolar foramina**, through which vessels and nerves enter the molars. The **nasal surface** (*facies nasalis*) is the lateral wall of the nasal cavity; it bears the maxillary hiatus and, in front of it, the **lacrimal groove** (*sulcus lacrimalis*) — which, with the lacrimal bone and the inferior concha, forms the **nasolacrimal canal**.
