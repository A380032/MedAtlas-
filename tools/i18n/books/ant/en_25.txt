s2959 9. The inguinal canal
s2960 The **inguinal canal** (*canalis inguinalis*) is a slit **4–5 cm** long lying obliquely (from above and laterally, downward and medially) between the broad abdominal muscles above the medial half of the inguinal ligament. In males the **spermatic cord** passes through it (ductus deferens, testicular artery and veins, nerves), and in females the **round ligament of the uterus**; in both sexes the **ilioinguinal nerve** passes through the canal. In males the canal is wider and shorter.

s2999 10. Weak spots of the abdominal wall
s3000 Places in the abdominal wall where muscles are absent or thin are sites where **hernias** (*herniae*) occur: the **inguinal canal** (inguinal hernias), the **umbilical ring** (umbilical hernia — especially in infants), the **linea alba** (epigastric hernia — above the umbilicus, because there the linea alba is wide and thin), the **femoral canal** (femoral hernia — topic 14), the **lumbar triangle** (lumbar hernia), the muscle-free triangles of the diaphragm and the esophageal hiatus (diaphragmatic hernias). Increased intra-abdominal pressure (heavy lifting, chronic cough, constipation, pregnancy) causes hernias to appear.
s3001 11. Age-related features
s3002 In the newborn the pectoralis major is well defined; the tendinous part of the pectoralis minor is poorly developed; the serratus anterior is developed, but its digitations are not well separated.
s3003 Because the newborn's breathing is mainly of the **abdominal (diaphragmatic) type**, the intercostal muscles are weak; once thoracic breathing begins they thicken — the transverse dimension of some muscles increases 5–6 times by 12 years.
s3004 The newborn's diaphragm is relatively well developed: its mass makes up **5.3%** of the mass of all muscles (1–1.3% in adults); the dome is high, the central tendon small (12–15% of its area), and the sternocostal and lumbocostal triangles relatively large. Until 5 years the ribs lie horizontally, so the diaphragm stands high; at 7 years it resembles that of the adult.
s3005 The abdominal muscles and aponeuroses are weak in the newborn, and the abdomen protrudes; they grow rapidly after the child begins to walk. The tendinous intersections of the rectus lie higher in children; the posterior layer of the sheath is poorly developed.
s3006 In the newborn the width of the linea alba is 5–8 mm at the xiphoid process and 12–16 mm at the umbilicus; there are thin areas in its upper part and at the umbilicus. The transversalis fascia is thin; the preperitoneal fat appears from 2 years.
s3007 In the newborn the inguinal canal is short, wide and almost straight; at 1 year its length is 0.5–2.5 cm, and with age it lengthens and becomes oblique; the superficial ring lies higher and its crura are poorly developed. The structure of the canal is fully formed by 3 years.

s3094 After removal of the axillary lymph nodes for cancer of the left breast, a woman reports that she cannot raise her arm above her head. On examination, when the patient pushes against a wall, the medial border and inferior angle of the left scapula protrude backward.
s3095 Which nerve and muscle are injured? Why has raising the arm become difficult?
s3096 The **long thoracic nerve**, lying on the surface of the serratus anterior in the axillary region, has been injured, resulting in paralysis of the **serratus anterior** — a "winged scapula." This muscle presses the scapula against the thoracic cage and, together with the trapezius, rotates the inferior angle of the scapula laterally, turning the glenoid cavity upward; this is what allows the arm to be raised above the horizontal. When the muscle is paralyzed this rotation is impaired.
s3097 A newborn shows severe respiratory distress and cyanosis after birth; the abdomen is "sunken," no breath sounds are heard over the left half of the chest, but bowel sounds are heard there. A radiograph shows intestinal loops in the left thoracic cavity.
s3098 What defect should be suspected? Through which weak spot of the diaphragm did the intestines pass into the chest?
s3099 This is a **congenital diaphragmatic hernia**. Most often, owing to failure to close of the muscle-free **lumbocostal triangle** (Bochdalek's triangle) between the lumbar and costal parts of the diaphragm, mainly on the left side, abdominal organs pass into the thoracic cavity, compress the lung and prevent its development (hypoplasia). In the newborn these triangles are relatively large. It is treated surgically.
