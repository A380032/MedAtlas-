s6339 The **middle meningeal artery** lies in a groove on the inner surface of the bone in the temporal region (the **pterion** — the thin area where the frontal, parietal, temporal and sphenoid bones meet); if a blow to this area fractures the bone, the artery tears and an **epidural hematoma** forms between the dura mater and the bone (loss of consciousness after a "lucid interval") — emergency trephination is needed. **Nosebleeds** most often come from **Kiesselbach's area** in the anteroinferior part of the nasal septum, where the sphenopalatine, greater palatine, superior labial (from the facial artery) and anterior ethmoidal (from the internal carotid) arteries anastomose.
s6340 **Ischemic stroke** most often occurs in the territory of the **middle cerebral artery**; its thin **lenticulostriate branches** ("arteries of stroke") rupture in hypertension, and blood is shed into the region of the **internal capsule** — **hemiplegia** and hemianesthesia on the opposite side. **Saccular (berry) aneurysms** form at the branching points of the circle of Willis (most often the anterior communicating artery); if they rupture, **subarachnoid hemorrhage** results (a sudden "worst headache of my life"). An aneurysm of the **posterior communicating artery** compresses the oculomotor nerve (III) passing beside it, causing ptosis and a dilated pupil.
s6341 1. The aorta
s6342 The **aorta** is the largest unpaired arterial vessel of the systemic circulation; it begins from the left ventricle. It has three parts: the **ascending aorta**, the **aortic arch** and the **descending aorta** (which in turn is divided into the **thoracic** and **abdominal aorta**). Its wall is that of an **elastic-type** artery: the tunica media contains many elastic membranes, which stretch in systole and push the blood onward in diastole (the **compression chamber** function).

s6361 2. The common carotid artery
s6362 The **common carotid artery** (*a. carotis communis*) arises on the right from the brachiocephalic trunk and on the left directly from the aortic arch (so the left one is 2–2.5 cm longer and has a thoracic part). In the neck it ascends beside the trachea and esophagus, in front of the transverse processes of the cervical vertebrae, behind the sternocleidomastoid and omohyoid muscles, and **gives off no branches**. Lateral to it lies the **internal jugular vein**, with the **vagus nerve** behind and between them; all three are enclosed in the **carotid sheath** (cervical fascia) and form the main **neurovascular bundle** of the neck.
s6363 At the level of the **upper border of the thyroid cartilage** (C~IV~), in the **carotid triangle**, it divides into the **external** and **internal carotid arteries** (the **carotid bifurcation**). At the bifurcation the beginning of the internal carotid artery is dilated as the **carotid sinus** (baroreceptors — sense blood pressure; nerve IX), and behind the bifurcation lies the rice-grain-sized **carotid body** (glomus — chemoreceptors that sense O~2~ and CO~2~ in the blood).

s6401 4. The internal carotid artery
s6402 The **internal carotid artery** (*a. carotis interna*) is the main artery of the brain and the eyeball. In the neck it ascends vertically between the pharynx and the internal jugular vein and enters the cranium through the **carotid canal**. It has **four parts**:
s6403 **Cervical part** — up to the base of the skull; **gives off no branches**.
s6404 **Petrous part** — in the carotid canal of the temporal bone; gives off the caroticotympanic arteries and the artery of the pterygoid canal.
s6405 **Cavernous part** — inside the **cavernous venous sinus** (with nerve VI beside it and nerves III, IV, V~1~ and V~2~ in its lateral wall); it bends in an S shape, forming the **carotid siphon**; gives small branches to the pituitary gland, the trigeminal ganglion and the dura mater.
s6406 **Cerebral part** — pierces the dura mater and enters the subarachnoid space beside the optic nerve; gives off the **ophthalmic artery**, the **superior hypophyseal artery**, the **posterior communicating artery** and the **anterior choroidal artery**, and finally divides into the **anterior** and **middle cerebral arteries**.

s6412 5. Blood supply of the brain
s6413 The brain receives blood from two systems: the **carotid system** (both internal carotid arteries — ≈70–80 % of the blood reaching the brain) and the **vertebrobasilar system** (both vertebral arteries). The **vertebral artery** (from the subclavian artery; topic 27) ascends through the transverse foramina of vertebrae C~VI~–C~I~, bends backward above the atlas and enters the cranium through the **foramen magnum**. Inside the cranium it gives off the **anterior and posterior spinal arteries**, the **posterior inferior cerebellar artery** (its largest branch) and meningeal branches. The two vertebral arteries unite at the **lower border of the pons** to form the unpaired **basilar artery**; it lies in the basilar groove of the pons, gives off the **anterior inferior cerebellar**, **labyrinthine** (inner ear), **pontine** and **superior cerebellar arteries**, and at the upper border of the pons divides into the two **posterior cerebral arteries**.
