s1209 **Age-related features.** In the newborn the cranial vault is very large relative to the base (the neurocranium is ≈8 times larger than the facial skeleton, compared with ≈2 times in adults). In the first year the vault grows rapidly, and at 1 year its length reaches half that of an adult's head; at 20 years it takes its permanent shape. Until one year the vault bones are elastic, with no clear boundary between the outer table, diploe and inner table; the diploe forms from 4–5 years. Grooves for the meningeal vessels and venous sinuses appear on the inner surface of the vault bones from 5–6 months. The inner surface of the cranial vault bones has no periosteum — its role is performed by the dura mater.
s1210 2. The occipital bone
s1211 The **occipital bone** (*os occipitale*) forms the posteroinferior wall of the neurocranium. It consists of four parts surrounding the **foramen magnum** (*foramen magnum*) — through which the cranial cavity communicates with the vertebral canal (the medulla oblongata, the vertebral arteries and the spinal roots of the accessory nerve pass through it). Its parts are: posteriorly — the **occipital squama** (*squama occipitalis*), anteriorly — the **basilar part** (*pars basilaris*), and on the sides — the paired **lateral parts** (*partes laterales*).
s1212 In the center of the external surface of the **occipital squama** is the **external occipital protuberance** (*protuberantia occipitalis externa*), clearly palpable under the skin; the **external occipital crest** (*crista occipitalis externa*) descends from it to the foramen magnum. On either side of the protuberance run the **superior** and **inferior nuchal lines** (*linea nuchae superior et inferior*), to which muscles and ligaments attach, and above them the **highest nuchal line**. On the internal surface there is a cross-shaped elevation — the **cruciform eminence** (*eminentia cruciformis*); at its center is the **internal occipital protuberance**, from which the **groove for the superior sagittal sinus** runs upward, the **grooves for the transverse sinuses** run to either side and the **internal occipital crest** runs downward. The eminence divides the internal surface of the squama into four fossae: the occipital lobes of the cerebrum lie in the upper ones, and the cerebellar hemispheres in the lower ones.

s1215 On the inferior surface of the **lateral parts** lie the oval **occipital condyles** (*condyli occipitales*), which articulate with the atlas; the base of the condyles is pierced by the **hypoglossal canal** (*canalis nervi hypoglossi* — for cranial nerve XII), and behind the condyles is the inconstant **condylar canal** (*canalis condylaris* — for an emissary vein). On the outer border of the lateral part is the **jugular notch** (*incisura jugularis*) — together with the corresponding notch of the temporal bone it forms the **jugular foramen** (through which the internal jugular vein and cranial nerves IX, X and XI pass) — and behind it the **jugular process**.
s1216 The **basilar part** is a quadrangular segment in front of the foramen magnum; it fuses with the body of the sphenoid bone at **18–20 years**. Together with the body of the sphenoid its internal surface forms the sloping **clivus** (*clivus*), on which the pons and medulla oblongata lie. Its inferior surface bears the **pharyngeal tubercle** (*tuberculum pharyngeum*), to which the pharynx attaches, and along its lateral border runs the groove for the inferior petrosal sinus.

s1219 **Age-related features.** In the newborn the occipital bone consists of **four parts** around the foramen magnum, separated from each other by layers of cartilage. The squama and lateral parts fuse at **3–4 years**, and the basilar part with the lateral parts at **6–10 years**; growth of the bone ends at ≈20 years.
s1220 3. The parietal bone
s1221 The **parietal bone** (*os parietale*) is a paired, quadrangular flat bone, convex outward; it forms the upper lateral parts of the cranial vault. It has **four borders**: the **sagittal border** (*margo sagittalis*) — forms the sagittal suture with the opposite parietal bone; the **frontal border** (*margo frontalis*) — the coronal suture with the frontal bone; the **occipital border** (*margo occipitalis*) — the lambdoid suture with the occipital bone; and the **squamous border** (*margo squamosus*) — the squamous suture with the squama of the temporal bone. Between the borders **four angles** are distinguished: the **frontal**, **occipital**, **sphenoidal** and **mastoid angles**.
s1222 In the center of the **external surface** is the most prominent point — the **parietal tuber** (*tuber parietale*); below it run the arched **superior and inferior temporal lines**, to which the temporal fascia and muscle attach. Near the sagittal border there may be a **parietal foramen**, through which an emissary vein passes. The **internal surface** is concave; it bears **arterial grooves** (*sulci arteriosi*) left by the branches of the middle meningeal artery, half of the groove for the superior sagittal sinus along the sagittal border, and the groove for the sigmoid sinus in the region of the mastoid angle.

s1227 **Age-related features.** The parietal bone develops by the endesmal route from one or two ossification centers in the region of the parietal tuber. In the newborn it is quadrangular, with smooth edges and rounded, unossified angles — they bound the fontanelles. Vascular grooves appear on its inner surface at 3 years, and suture serrations on its edges at 4 years.
s1228 4. The frontal bone
s1229 The **frontal bone** (*os frontale*) forms the anterior part of the cranial vault and the roofs of the orbits. It consists of three parts: the vertical **frontal squama** (*squama frontalis*), the horizontal paired **orbital parts** (*partes orbitales*) and the **nasal part** (*pars nasalis*) between them.
s1230 On the external surface of the **squama** are the two **frontal tubers** (*tubera frontalia*), below them, above the orbital margins, the arched **superciliary arches** (*arcus superciliares*), and between them a smooth area — the **glabella**. The squama passes into the orbital part at the **supraorbital margin** (*margo supraorbitalis*); in its medial third is the **supraorbital notch (foramen)** (*incisura/foramen supraorbitale*), through which the nerve and vessels of the same name pass. Laterally the margin ends in the **zygomatic process** (*processus zygomaticus*), from which the temporal line runs upward. On the internal surface the **groove for the superior sagittal sinus** runs along the midline, and below it the **frontal crest** (*crista frontalis*).

s1233 The **orbital parts** are thin triangular plates; their inferior surface forms the roof of the orbit and their superior surface the floor of the anterior cranial fossa (where the frontal lobes of the brain lie, hence the impressions of the gyri). At the lateral angle of the inferior surface is the **fossa for the lacrimal gland** (*fossa glandulae lacrimalis*), and medially the **trochlear fovea**, to which the trochlea attaches. Between the two orbital parts is the **ethmoidal notch** (*incisura ethmoidalis*), into which the ethmoid bone fits. From the middle of the **nasal part** the **nasal spine** (*spina nasalis*) projects downward. In the thickness of the lower part of the squama lies the air-filled **frontal sinus** (*sinus frontalis*) — it is divided in two by a septum and opens into the **middle nasal meatus**; therefore the frontal bone is a **pneumatic** bone.

s1236 **Age-related features.** In the newborn the frontal bone consists of **two symmetric halves** joined in the midline by the **frontal (metopic) suture**; the frontal tubers are prominent, and there are no superciliary arches or frontal sinus. The two halves begin to fuse **in the 2nd year** and usually fuse completely **by 7–8 years**; in some people (about 5–10 %) the **metopic suture persists for life**. The frontal sinus appears in the 1st–2nd year of life, becomes clearly visible on X-rays after 7 years, and completes its development at ≈25 years.
s1237 5. The ethmoid bone
s1238 The **ethmoid bone** (*os ethmoidale*) is an unpaired, light pneumatic bone; it fits into the ethmoidal notch of the frontal bone and takes part in forming the anterior cranial base and the walls of the nasal cavity and orbits. Its parts:
s1239 The **cribriform plate** (*lamina cribrosa*) — a horizontal plate with many foramina; through them the **olfactory nerves** (cranial nerve I) pass from the nasal cavity into the cranial cavity. In the midline the **crista galli** (*crista galli*) projects upward from it — the falx cerebri of the dura mater attaches to it.
s1240 The **perpendicular plate** (*lamina perpendicularis*) — directed downward, it forms the upper part of the nasal septum.
s1241 The **ethmoidal labyrinth** (*labyrinthus ethmoidalis*) — paired, consisting of air-filled **ethmoidal cells** (*cellulae ethmoidales*: anterior, middle, posterior); they open into the superior and middle nasal meatuses. The lateral surface of the labyrinth — the thin **orbital plate** (*lamina orbitalis*) — forms the medial wall of the orbit; from its medial surface the **superior and middle nasal conchae** (*concha nasalis superior et media*) hang into the nasal cavity. Below the middle concha lie the **uncinate process** and the **ethmoidal bulla**.

s1244 **Age-related features.** In the newborn the ethmoid bone is cartilaginous, and only part of the labyrinth is ossified; the ethmoidal cells are present at birth but small — that is why in children **ethmoiditis** is the earliest sinusitis to develop. In the first year the cribriform plate ossifies and fuses with the labyrinth. At ≈3 years the nasal mucosa grows into the labyrinth cells and the cells enlarge; by puberty the bone resembles that of the adult.
s1245 6. The newborn's skull: fontanelles and sutures
s1246 In the newborn the bones of the cranial vault are not joined by sutures: between them there are wide **connective tissue membranes**. Where several bones meet, these membranes widen to form unossified areas — the **fontanelles** (*fonticuli cranii*). During delivery the fontanelles allow the skull bones to overlap, making it easier for the head to pass through the narrow birth canal, and in the first years they allow rapid growth of the brain.
