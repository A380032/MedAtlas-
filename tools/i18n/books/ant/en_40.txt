s4984 The **mucosa of the nasal cavity** is divided into two regions. The **olfactory region** covers the superior concha, part of the middle concha and the upper part of the septum; it contains the **olfactory cells** (primary sensory neurons) — their axons pass through the foramina of the cribriform plate to the olfactory bulb (the olfactory nerves, CN I). The **respiratory region** is the rest; it has pseudostratified ciliated epithelium and mucous glands, and in the submucosa, especially of the middle and inferior conchae, **cavernous venous plexuses** — these warm the air but also cause swelling of the mucosa.
s4985 4. The paranasal sinuses
s4986 The **paranasal sinuses** (*sinus paranasales*) are air-filled cavities in the skull bones; they communicate with the nasal cavity and are lined by its mucosa. They lighten the skull, give resonance to the voice, and warm and moisten the air. They are: the **maxillary sinus (antrum of Highmore)** (the largest, ≈15–20 mL), the **frontal sinus**, the **sphenoidal sinus** and the **ethmoidal cells (labyrinth)**.

s4989 5. The larynx: topography
s4990 The **larynx** (*larynx*) is part of the airway, an organ that produces the voice and protects the lower airways from foreign bodies. It lies in the anterior region of the neck below the hyoid bone and forms the **laryngeal prominence** (*prominentia laryngea*, "Adam's apple," well marked in men). In adults it lies at the level of the **4th–6th (7th) cervical vertebrae** (slightly higher in women). Above it is suspended from the hyoid bone, and below it continues into the trachea. **In front** — the infrahyoid muscles and cervical fascia; **at the sides** — the lobes of the thyroid gland and the neurovascular bundle of the neck; **behind** — the laryngeal part of the pharynx. In swallowing and phonation the larynx moves up and down.

s5035 8. The laryngeal cavity
s5036 The **laryngeal cavity** (*cavitas laryngis*) is hourglass-shaped: its upper and lower parts are wide and its middle narrow. It is entered from the pharynx through the **laryngeal inlet**, bounded in front by the epiglottis, at the sides by the **aryepiglottic folds** and behind by the arytenoid cartilages and the fold between them. On the lateral wall of the cavity there are two pairs of folds: above — the **vestibular folds** (false vocal cords), below — the **vocal folds** (containing the **vocal ligament** and the **vocalis muscle**). Between them lies the **laryngeal ventricle**, extending laterally.
s5037 The **laryngeal vestibule** — from the inlet to the vestibular folds; between the vestibular folds is the **rima vestibuli**.
s5038 The **intermediate (middle) part** — the shortest part, between the vestibular and vocal folds; between the right and left vocal folds is the **narrowest point** of the larynx — the **rima glottidis** (*rima glottidis*): the anterior **intermembranous** part (between the vocal ligaments, ≈2/3) and the posterior **intercartilaginous** part (between the bases of the arytenoid cartilages). Its length is 20–24 mm in men and 16–19 mm in women; its width is ≈5 mm in quiet breathing and up to ≈15 mm in deep breathing.
s5039 The **infraglottic cavity** (*cavitas infraglottica*) — below the vocal folds; it widens downward and passes into the trachea.
s5040 The laryngeal mucosa is lined mainly by **pseudostratified ciliated epithelium**, but the **vocal folds** and part of the epiglottis by **stratified squamous epithelium**; the vocal fold has no glands, and the mucosa adheres firmly to the ligament (it is whitish). Beneath the mucosa is the **fibroelastic membrane**: its upper part is the **quadrangular membrane** (its lower edge — the vestibular ligament), and its lower part is the **conus elasticus** (its upper free edge — the **vocal ligament**; in front it thickens to form the median cricothyroid ligament).

s5045 **Voice production.** During expiration the rima glottidis narrows and the vocal ligaments tense; air leaving the lungs makes them vibrate. The **pitch** of the voice depends on the tension, length and thickness of the ligaments, its **loudness** on the pressure of the airflow, and its **timbre** on the resonators (pharynx, mouth, nasal cavity and paranasal sinuses); speech sounds are shaped by the tongue, lips, palate and teeth (articulation).
s5046 The newborn's nose is short, flat and broad; its root is narrow, the dorsum and tip are not defined, and the cartilages are poorly developed; the nostrils are oval and horizontal, becoming round by 5 years.
s5047 In the newborn the nasal cavity is low (≈17.5 mm) and narrow; the conchae are relatively thick and do not reach the septum. **The inferior nasal meatus is almost absent** (the inferior concha touches the floor of the cavity), the middle meatus is poorly developed, and the superior meatus is present in only ≈30% of cases — the baby breathes mainly through the **common nasal meatus**. If the mucosa swells, nasal breathing easily stops — the baby has difficulty suckling. The nasal cavity enlarges 1.5 times by 10 years and 2 times by 20 years.
s5048 In the newborn, of the paranasal sinuses only the **maxillary sinus** (small, ≈5×10×3.5 mm) and the buds of the ethmoidal cells are present; the maxillary sinus occupies the body of the bone by 8–9 years. The **frontal sinus** begins to develop in the 2nd year (pea-sized at 5 years); the **sphenoidal sinus** develops from 3–4 years and is formed by 12–15 years; the ethmoidal cells resemble those of adults by 14 years. This is why frontal sinusitis is almost never seen in young children.
s5049 In the newborn the larynx is relatively large, funnel-shaped and **high** — at the level of the 2nd–4th cervical vertebrae; the epiglottis stands high and touches the root of the tongue — this allows the baby to breathe while suckling. The larynx gradually descends.
s5050 The cartilages of the newborn's larynx are thin and flexible; the laminae of the thyroid cartilage form an obtuse angle (there is no laryngeal prominence); the rima glottidis is ≈6.5 mm and the vocal ligaments are short. The mucosa is delicate and rich in blood and lymph vessels, and the submucosa of the infraglottic cavity is loose — in inflammation it swells rapidly, narrowing the laryngeal cavity (**false croup**).
s5051 The larynx grows rapidly at 1–3 years and during puberty. From 10–12 years in boys the angle between the laminae of the thyroid cartilage decreases (≈90°), the laryngeal prominence appears and the vocal ligaments lengthen — the **voice breaks (mutation)**; in girls the change is barely noticeable. In old age the laryngeal cartilages calcify (in men the thyroid and cricoid cartilages from 20 years).
s5052 Practical skill: palpating the larynx and examining the nasal cavity
