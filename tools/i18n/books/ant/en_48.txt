s5938 7. The pancreatic islets and the endocrine part of the gonads
s5939 The **pancreatic islets** (islets of Langerhans) are ≈1 million cell clusters scattered among the exocrine acini (0.1–0.3 mm in diameter; 1–2% of the mass of the gland), more numerous in the **tail** of the gland. Their cells: **β cells** (≈70%) — **insulin** (lowers blood glucose), **α cells** (≈20%) — **glucagon** (raises glucose), **δ cells** — **somatostatin**, **PP cells** — pancreatic polypeptide. The islets are densely surrounded by fenestrated capillaries. Insulin deficiency causes **diabetes mellitus**.

s5942 **Endocrine part of the gonads**: **in the testis** — the **interstitial (Leydig) cells** between the seminiferous tubules (produce testosterone under the action of LH) and the Sertoli cells (inhibin, anti-Müllerian hormone); **in the ovary** — the **granulosa** and **theca** cells of the follicular wall (estrogens) and, after ovulation, the **corpus luteum** (progesterone); in pregnancy the **placenta** also produces hormones. The sex hormones regulate the secondary sexual characteristics, the sexual cycle and reproductive function.
s5943 In the newborn the **pituitary** is pear-shaped and weighs ≈0.1–0.15 g; it grows rapidly in the 2nd year, at 4–5 years and at 11–12 years (onset of puberty); its mass increases 2 times by 10 years and 3 times by 15 years, and it is largest at 20 years (≈0.53–0.56 g); it is always larger in women. It grows together with the sella turcica.
s5944 In the newborn the **pineal gland** is round and small; it grows rapidly in the first year (≈100 mg) and reaches its definitive size (≈150 mg) **by 6 years**. It is active in childhood and involutes after puberty; brain sand begins to accumulate from 7–10 years.
s5945 In the newborn the **thyroid gland** is relatively large but structurally immature, weighing ≈1–3 g; it grows gradually until puberty to 10–14 g, grows rapidly at puberty to ≈25 g, and reaches 30–35 g after 20 years. In childhood the thyroid hormones are crucial for growth and **brain development**; in old age the gland shrinks.
s5946 In the newborn the **parathyroid glands** are very small (total mass 6–9 mg); they grow 3–4 times in infancy, then slowly, reaching 120–140 mg by 20 years; they are larger in women.
s5947 In the newborn the **suprarenal glands** are **relatively very large** (each ≈7–9 g — equal to those of adults; ≈1/3 of the mass of the kidney), mainly because of the fetal cortex. After birth the fetal zone rapidly involutes, and the gland shrinks ≈2 times during the 1st year; the cortical zones begin to re-form from 5 years and are completed at puberty; their mass is ≈4.6 g at 5 years, ≈6.6 g at 10 years, ≈8.6 g at 15 years and ≈13 g at 20 years (both). The medulla is poorly developed in the newborn.
s5948 In the newborn the **pancreatic islets** are relatively numerous (≈3.5% of the volume of the gland, ≈120 thousand); their number increases throughout life to 1 million, while their relative volume decreases to 1–2%.
s5949 The **endocrine activity of the gonads** increases during fetal life (in the testis — for sexual differentiation) and **at puberty** (activation of the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal axis); in women the ovarian hormones decline at menopause.

s6042 For a year a 45-year-old woman has complained of headache and deteriorating vision: she has lost her side vision (she does not notice cars coming from the side in the street). Her menstrual cycle is disturbed, and milk is being secreted from her breasts. MRI showed a tumor in the sella turcica growing upward.
s6043 Which gland is affected? What is the anatomical cause of the visual disturbance? Which hormone is being produced in excess?
s6044 A **pituitary adenoma** (prolactinoma). The pituitary lies in the hypophysial fossa of the sella turcica, above it is the diaphragma sellae, and in front of and above it lies the **optic chiasm**. The tumor, growing upward, compresses the middle part of the chiasm — the **crossing fibers coming from the medial (nasal) halves of the retinas of both eyes**; as a result the **temporal visual fields** of both eyes are lost — **bitemporal hemianopia**. The lactotroph cells of the adenohypophysis produce excess **prolactin**: milk secretion (galactorrhea) and disturbance of the menstrual cycle.
s6045 One day after total thyroidectomy the patient developed numbness around the lips and in the fingers, followed by painful spasms of the hands (the fingers in the "obstetrician's hand" position). The voice is hoarse.
s6046 Which structures have been injured? Explain their location and function.
s6047 1) The **parathyroid glands** have been removed or their blood supply disrupted — they lie on the **posterior surface** of the lobes of the thyroid gland, between the fibrous capsule and the fascial sheath (usually 4). They produce **parathyroid hormone**, raising blood calcium; when they are lost, **hypocalcemia** develops — neuromuscular excitability increases and **tetany** develops. 2) Hoarseness indicates injury to the **recurrent laryngeal nerve** — it runs in the groove between the trachea and esophagus, close to the inferior thyroid artery, in contact with the posteromedial surface of the lobes of the gland, and supplies the laryngeal muscles.
s6048 In recent months a 30-year-old patient has had paroxysmal headaches, palpitations, sweating and rises in arterial pressure up to 230/130 mmHg. CT showed a 4 cm tumor in the left suprarenal gland; urinary catecholamine metabolites are sharply elevated.
s6049 Which part of the suprarenal gland is affected? State the origin and hormones of this part. Explain the structure of the suprarenal gland.
s6050 This is a **pheochromocytoma** — a tumor of the chromaffin cells of the **suprarenal medulla**. The medulla develops from the **neural crest** (sympathetic neuroblasts) and is regarded as a modified sympathetic ganglion; it produces **epinephrine and norepinephrine** — their excess release causes paroxysmal hypertension, tachycardia and sweating. The suprarenal gland is enclosed externally by a fibrous capsule and consists of a **cortex** (mesodermal: zona glomerulosa — aldosterone, zona fasciculata — cortisol, zona reticularis — androgens) and a **medulla**. The left gland is crescentic, and its vein drains into the left renal vein.

s6075 In the pulmonary circulation **venous blood flows in the arteries** and **arterial blood in the veins** — a vessel is named not by the composition of its blood but by the direction of flow (from the heart or to the heart). In the liver there is an additional **portal system**: blood from the unpaired abdominal organs passes first through the hepatic capillaries via the portal vein.
s6076 When the conducting system is damaged, **heart blocks** develop: if the atrioventricular node or the bundle of His is affected (often in occlusion of the **right coronary artery** — it supplies both nodes), **AV block** results; in complete block the ventricles contract at their own rhythm (≈30–40/min), and fainting attacks (Morgagni–Adams–Stokes) occur; treatment — a **pacemaker** (its electrode is introduced into the right ventricle via the superior vena cava). Bundle branch block appears on the ECG as widening of the QRS complex.
s6077 **Myocardial infarction** develops with thrombosis of a coronary artery: if the **anterior interventricular branch** (the "widow-maker artery") is occluded — the anterior wall of the left ventricle, the septum and the apex are affected; the **circumflex branch** — the lateral and posterior walls of the left ventricle; the **right coronary artery** — the inferior (diaphragmatic) wall, often with rhythm and conduction disturbances. Cardiac pain (angina pectoris) radiates to the **left shoulder, the inner surface of the arm, the neck and the mandible** — because these are also supplied by segments T~I~–T~V~ (and C~VIII~) (**referred pain**). Treatment: angioplasty and stenting, coronary artery bypass grafting.
s6078 In **pericarditis** a friction rub of the layers is heard. Fluid or blood accumulating rapidly in the pericardial cavity (cardiac wound, aortic rupture) compresses the heart and prevents it from filling — **cardiac tamponade**. Emergency **pericardiocentesis** (puncture) is usually performed at **Larrey's point** — from the angle between the xiphoid process and the left 7th costal cartilage, directed upward and backward into the anteroinferior sinus.
s6079 Enlargement of the chambers appears on radiographs as bulging of particular arcs: in mitral stenosis the left atrium enlarges and the waist of the heart is flattened ("mitral configuration"); in aortic valve disease and arterial hypertension the left ventricular arc enlarges and the waist deepens ("aortic configuration"). Because the left atrium is close to the esophagus, when enlarged it displaces the esophagus backward (seen on contrast radiography), and **transesophageal echocardiography** shows the left atrium well.
s6080 1. The vascular system and the circulations
s6081 The **cardiovascular system** consists of the heart, arteries, veins and the **microcirculatory bed** between them (arterioles, precapillaries, capillaries, postcapillaries, venules). **Arteries** carry blood from the heart to the organs (regardless of its composition), and **veins** bring it from the organs to the heart. Blood vessels are absent in the epidermis, hair, nails, cornea, lens and articular cartilage.
