s3450 **Intramuscular injections** into the buttock are given into the **upper outer quadrant** of the buttock — the sciatic nerve and gluteal vessels emerge through the infrapiriform and suprapiriform foramina into the inferomedial part of the buttock. When the gluteus medius and minimus (or the superior gluteal nerve) are paralyzed, the pelvis **drops toward the opposite side** when the patient stands on the affected leg — **Trendelenburg's sign** (a "waddling gait" with bilateral injury). In spasm of the piriformis the sciatic nerve is compressed, causing pain in the buttock and back of the thigh (piriformis syndrome).
s3451 **Knee jerk** — when the patellar ligament is tapped, the quadriceps contracts and the leg extends (segments L~II~–L~IV~, femoral nerve). The **posterior thigh muscles** ("hamstrings") are the muscles most often strained and torn in athletes — being biarticular, during sprinting they are stretched and contract at the same time. Strain of the adductor muscles ("groin strain") is common in soccer players.
s3452 The order of structures passing behind the medial malleolus from front to back: tendon of the **tibialis posterior** → tendon of the **flexor digitorum longus** → **posterior tibial artery and veins** → **tibial nerve** → tendon of the **flexor hallucis longus**. An English mnemonic based on the initial letters: "**T**om, **D**ick **A**nd **V**ery **N**ervous **H**arry" (*tibialis posterior, flexor digitorum longus, arteria, vena, nervus, flexor hallucis longus*).
s3453 The **calcaneal (Achilles) tendon** is the strongest tendon in the body; its rupture (often during jumping or a sudden start) presents as inability to stand on tiptoe. Tapping the calcaneal tendon plantarflexes the foot — the **Achilles reflex** (S~I~–S~II~). The **common fibular nerve** lies superficially around the neck of the fibula and is injured by fractures, tight casts or prolonged sitting with crossed legs: the anterior and lateral groups are paralyzed, producing **"foot drop"** and a "steppage gait" (walking with the leg lifted high so that the toes do not catch the ground).
s3454 **Femoral hernia** occurs more often in women (the pelvis is wider, and the vascular lacuna and femoral ring are larger) and presents as a bulge **below** the inguinal ligament, medial to the femoral vein (an inguinal hernia bulges above the inguinal ligament, superomedial to the pubic tubercle). Because the femoral ring is narrow and rigidly bounded, femoral hernias are frequently **strangulated**. In the femoral triangle, slightly medial to the midpoint of the inguinal ligament, the pulse of the femoral artery is felt; here **arterial puncture** (coronary angiography, angiography) and catheterization of the femoral vein (medial to the artery) are performed.
s3455 1. Muscles of the lower limb: general information
s3456 The muscles of the lower limb are divided into **muscles of the pelvic girdle** and muscles of the free lower limb — those of the **thigh**, **leg** and **foot**. Unlike the upper limb, the lower limb is an organ of **support and locomotion**, so its muscles are large and strong, often multi-headed (quadriceps, triceps), and insert by short, strong tendons; they are adapted to carrying body weight, maintaining the upright posture and walking. The lower limb muscles make up **more than half** of the total muscle mass.
s3457 2. Muscles of the pelvis
s3458 The pelvic muscles arise from the pelvic bones and the vertebral column, insert on the proximal end of the femur and move the **hip joint** around all three axes. They are divided into **internal** (arising from the pelvic cavity) and **external** groups.

s3502 3. Muscles of the thigh
s3503 The thigh muscles surround the femur on all sides, act on the hip and knee joints and are divided into three groups: **anterior** (mainly knee extensors; **femoral nerve**), **medial** (adductors; **obturator nerve**) and **posterior** (thigh extensors and knee flexors; **sciatic nerve**). The groups are separated by the intermuscular septa of the fascia lata running to the femur.
