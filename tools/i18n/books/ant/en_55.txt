s6782 1. The thoracic aorta
s6783 The **thoracic aorta** (*pars thoracica aortae*) is the continuation of the aortic arch; it lies in the **posterior mediastinum** from T~IV~ to T~XII~: above, to the left of the esophagus; below (T~VIII~–T~IX~) it passes behind it; to its right are the thoracic duct and the azygos vein, to its left the hemiazygos vein and the left pleura. It passes into the abdominal cavity through the **aortic hiatus** of the diaphragm (T~XII~). Its branches are divided into two groups:

s6803 2. The abdominal aorta
s6804 The **abdominal aorta** (*pars abdominalis aortae*) lies from T~XII~ to L~IV~ in front of the bodies of the lumbar vertebrae, behind the peritoneum, slightly to the left of the midline; to its right is the **inferior vena cava**, and in front of it are the pancreas, the inferior horizontal part of the duodenum, the root of the mesentery of the small intestine and the **left renal vein** (which passes between the aorta and the superior mesenteric artery). At the level of L~IV~ (slightly below the level of the umbilicus) it divides into the **common iliac arteries**; its continuation is the thin **median sacral artery**.

s6834 3. The celiac trunk
s6835 The **celiac trunk** (*truncus coeliacus*) is a short (1–2 cm), thick unpaired trunk; it arises from the anterior surface of the aorta at the level of T~XII~, where the aorta emerges from the aortic hiatus of the diaphragm; above the upper border of the pancreas it divides into **three branches** (**Haller's tripod**):
s6836 The **left gastric artery** — upward and to the left to the cardia of the stomach, then to the right along the **lesser curvature**; it gives off esophageal branches; it anastomoses with the right gastric artery to form the **arterial arch of the lesser curvature**.
s6837 The **common hepatic artery** — to the right, along the upper border of the pancreas; it divides into the **proper hepatic artery** and the **gastroduodenal artery**. The **proper hepatic artery** runs to the porta hepatis in the hepatoduodenal ligament (to the left of the common bile duct, in front of the portal vein), gives off the **right gastric artery** and divides into the **right** (from which arises the **cystic artery**) and **left branches**. The **gastroduodenal artery** passes behind the superior part of the duodenum and gives off the **right gastro-omental artery** (greater curvature) and the **superior pancreaticoduodenal (anterior and posterior) arteries**.
s6838 The **splenic artery** (the largest, tortuous) — to the left along the upper border of the pancreas to the hilum of the spleen; it gives off **pancreatic branches**, the **short gastric arteries** (fundus of the stomach), the **left gastro-omental artery** (to the right along the greater curvature; with the right artery it forms the **arch of the greater curvature**) and splenic branches.

s6845 4. The superior and inferior mesenteric arteries
s6846 The **superior mesenteric artery** (*a. mesenterica superior*) arises from the aorta at the level of L~I~, descends behind the neck of the pancreas, passes between the gland and the inferior horizontal part of the duodenum (in front of the latter) and runs within the **root of the mesentery of the small intestine** toward the right iliac fossa. Its branches:
s6847 The **inferior pancreaticoduodenal arteries** — anastomose with the superior arteries (a route between the celiac trunk and the superior mesenteric artery).
s6848 The **jejunal** and **ileal arteries** (15–20) — arise from the left side and form **arterial arcades** with one another in the mesentery (1–5 tiers; more in the ileum); from the arcades **straight arteries** (vasa recta) run to the intestinal wall.
s6849 The **ileocolic artery** — the cecum (anterior and posterior cecal arteries), the **appendicular artery** (in the mesoappendix), the end of the ileum and the beginning of the ascending colon.
s6850 The **right colic artery** — the ascending colon.
s6851 The **middle colic artery** — in the transverse mesocolon; the transverse colon.

s6854 The **inferior mesenteric artery** (*a. mesenterica inferior*) arises from the aorta at the level of L~III~ and runs to the left and downward behind the peritoneum. Its branches: the **left colic artery** (descending colon and the left part of the transverse colon), the **sigmoid arteries** (2–3) and its terminal branch, the **superior rectal artery** (descends into the lesser pelvis and supplies the upper part of the rectum; anastomoses with the middle and inferior rectal arteries).
s6855 The **marginal artery of the colon** (artery of Drummond) is a continuous arcade formed along the intestine by the joined branches of all the colic arteries. The large anastomosis between the **middle and left colic arteries** is the **arc of Riolan** (it links the territories of the superior and inferior mesenteric arteries). The left flexure of the colon (splenic flexure) is the "critical point" at the border of the two territories: the collaterals are weak, and **ischemic colitis** most often occurs here.

s6860 5. Paired visceral branches and anastomoses
s6861 The **renal arteries** arise at the level of L~I~–L~II~; the **right renal artery is longer** and passes behind the inferior vena cava. At the renal hilum the artery lies behind the vein and in front of the pelvis (between the two); it divides into anterior and posterior branches, then into **segmental arteries** (5: superior, anterosuperior, anteroinferior, inferior, posterior). The segmental arteries are **end arteries** — they do not anastomose. **Accessory renal arteries** are common (≈25–30 %).
s6862 **Suprarenal arteries**: superior (from the inferior phrenic artery), middle (from the aorta), inferior (from the renal artery).
s6863 The **testicular arteries** arise at the level of L~II~, cross in front of the ureter behind the peritoneum, and pass through the **deep inguinal ring** in the **spermatic cord** to the testis; the **ovarian arteries** reach the ovary in the suspensory ligament of the ovary and anastomose with the ovarian branch of the uterine artery.
s6864 **Anastomoses**: celiac trunk ↔ superior mesenteric (superior and inferior pancreaticoduodenal arteries); superior ↔ inferior mesenteric (arc of Riolan, marginal artery); inferior mesenteric ↔ internal iliac (superior, middle and inferior rectal arteries); internal thoracic ↔ external iliac (superior and inferior epigastric arteries — anterior abdominal wall); intercostal ↔ lumbar arteries.
s6865 In the newborn the diameter of the aorta is 18–20 mm, and ≈4 cm at puberty. In children the descending aorta extends from T~IV~ to **L~V~** (in adults it bifurcates at L~IV~); it lengthens ≈4 times by 50 years, and the thoracic part grows faster than the abdominal part.
