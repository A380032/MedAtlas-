s3898 **Antitussives and expectorants (mucolytics) must not be given together**: the mucolytic thins the sputum and increases its volume, while the antitussive suppresses the reflex that expels it — sputum accumulates in the bronchi (**“bogging”**), and the risk of pneumonia and obstruction increases.
s3899 **In bronchial asthma, using a LABA as monotherapy without an ICS is forbidden** — the risk of death from asthma increases (the inflammation is not treated and the symptoms are masked). In addition, current GINA recommendations (since 2019) **do not recommend treating asthma with a SABA (salbutamol) alone** in adults and adolescents; every patient should receive ICS-containing treatment.
s3900 Theophylline has a **narrow therapeutic range** (effective blood concentration 5–15 mg/l, above 20 mg/l — toxic): nausea, vomiting, tachycardia, **arrhythmias**, **seizures**. It is metabolized by CYP1A2: **ciprofloxacin, clarithromycin and fluvoxamine** increase its concentration; smoking and rifampicin decrease it. Aminophylline (Euphylline) must be given intravenously **very slowly** (over at least 10–20 minutes). In modern recommendations, methylxanthines are only reserve drugs in asthma and COPD.
s3901 **The main rule of asthma control**: “Every asthmatic should receive an ICS”. Budesonide/formoterol in a single inhaler serves both as maintenance anti-inflammatory therapy and as a fast-acting bronchodilator — this reduces severe attacks and hospitalizations. Alternative approach: ICS (or ICS-LABA) maintenance therapy + salbutamol for attacks.
s3902 Treatment approaches are constantly being updated: older textbooks recommended starting asthma treatment with salbutamol and using aminophylline and cromones as maintenance drugs. Rely on the current GINA reports (updated every year) and the national clinical protocols of Uzbekistan.

s4013 5.2. M-cholinergic blockers
s4014 Ipratropium (short-acting, 6–8 hours) and tiotropium, glycopyrronium and umeclidinium (long-acting, 24 hours) block M~3~ receptors in the bronchi and reduce the bronchoconstrictor tone of the vagus nerve. s4017 6. Anti-inflammatory agents used in bronchial asthma
s4018 6.1. Inhaled glucocorticoids (ICS)
s4019 **ICS are the most effective maintenance therapy for bronchial asthma.** They reduce allergic inflammation in the bronchial wall (eosinophils, mast cells, T lymphocytes, cytokines), reduce bronchial hyperreactivity and restore the sensitivity of beta-2 receptors. Their effect develops gradually (days to weeks) — **they do not stop an attack**.
s4020 Preparations: budesonide, fluticasone propionate and furoate, beclometasone dipropionate, mometasone, ciclesonide. Combinations: **budesonide/formoterol**, beclometasone/formoterol, fluticasone/salmeterol, fluticasone furoate/vilanterol.
s4021 **Adverse effects: oral and pharyngeal candidiasis**, hoarseness (dysphonia) — prevention: rinsing the mouth after inhalation, a spacer; with prolonged use at high doses — systemic effects (slight growth retardation in children, osteoporosis, cataract), risk of pneumonia in COPD.
s4022 6.2. Other anti-inflammatory agents
s4023 **Montelukast** — a cysteinyl leukotriene (CysLT1) receptor antagonist; one tablet a day. Aspirin-induced asthma, exercise-induced asthma, asthma with allergic rhinitis. Adverse effects: neuropsychiatric disorders (sleep, mood, suicidal thoughts) — warning.
s4024 **Cromones** (sodium cromoglicate, nedocromil) — stabilize the mast cell membrane and prevent degranulation; prophylactic only; rarely used now.
s4025 **Systemic glucocorticoids** (oral prednisolone, intravenous methylprednisolone) — a short course (5–7 days) in severe attacks.
s4026 **Biologics** — in severe uncontrolled asthma: omalizumab (allergic), mepolizumab, benralizumab (eosinophilic), dupilumab, tezepelumab.

s4037 7.2. Emergency care in an attack of bronchial asthma
s4038 **Salbutamol** 2.5–5 mg via a nebulizer or 4–10 puffs (100 mcg) through a **spacer**, every 20 minutes in the first hour.
s4039 **Oxygen** (maintain SpO~2~ at 93–95%).
s4040 In a severe attack — **ipratropium** (together with salbutamol in a nebulizer).
s4041 **Systemic glucocorticoid** — prednisolone 40–50 mg orally (or methylprednisolone/hydrocortisone intravenously), for 5–7 days.
s4042 In a life-threatening attack — **magnesium sulfate** intravenously (2 g over 20 minutes), intensive care, intubation and mechanical ventilation.
s4043 **Sedatives are contraindicated** (they depress the respiratory center); beta-blockers are contraindicated.
s4044 7.3. COPD
s4045 In COPD, the main bronchodilators are **LAMAs (tiotropium)** and **LABAs**, often a **LAMA + LABA** combination. An ICS is added only in frequently exacerbating COPD with high blood eosinophils. **Stopping smoking** is the only effective measure that slows the progression of the disease. In exacerbations — a SABA + a short-acting M-cholinergic blocker, a systemic glucocorticoid (5 days), and an antibiotic if there are signs of bacterial infection.
