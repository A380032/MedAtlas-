s0965 8.2. Sample calculations
s0966 **Example 1.** An ampoule contains 2 ml of 2% lidocaine solution. How much lidocaine is in the ampoule?
s0967 2% = 20 mg/ml; 20 × 2 = **40 mg**.
s0968 **Example 2.** A patient needs 4 mg of dexamethasone. The ampoule contains a 4 mg/ml solution. How many ml are drawn up?
s0969 V = 4 / 4 = **1 ml**.
s0970 **Example 3.** 200 ml of a 5% solution must be prepared from a 40% glucose solution. How much of the 40% solution is taken?
s0971 C1 × V1 = C2 × V2 → 40 × V1 = 5 × 200 → V1 = **25 ml**; the remaining 175 ml of water for injection is added.
s0972 **Example 4.** A 1000 ml infusion must be given over 8 hours. The drip set gives 1 ml = 20 drops. What is the rate?
s0973 1000 × 20 / 480 ≈ **42 drops/minute**.
s0974 **Example 5.** A child weighing 12 kg is prescribed amoxicillin 25 mg/kg 2 times a day. The suspension is 250 mg/5 ml. How many ml are given at a time?
s0975 Dose: 12 × 25 = 300 mg; 250 mg — 5 ml, 300 mg — x; x = 300 × 5 / 250 = **6 ml**.
s0976 **Example 6.** How many tablets are needed for 500 ml of a 0.02% nitrofural (furacilin) solution (1 tablet = 20 mg)?
s0977 m = 500 × 0.02 / 100 = 0.1 g = 100 mg; 100 / 20 = **5 tablets**.

s1122 A nurse was about to give 10 ml of a 7.5% potassium chloride solution (in an ampoule) as an intravenous push to a patient with hypokalemia.
s1123 1) What is the nurse's error and what is the danger? 2) State the correct method of administration.
s1124 1) An intravenous push of concentrated potassium chloride sharply raises the blood potassium concentration and creates a risk of cardiac arrest (asystole, ventricular fibrillation). 2) Potassium chloride must always be diluted (e.g., in 400–500 ml of 0.9% NaCl or 5% glucose solution) and given slowly by drip (into a peripheral vein usually not more than 10 mmol per hour), under ECG and blood potassium monitoring. 10 ml of the 7.5% solution contains ≈ 750 mg KCl ≈ 10 mmol of potassium.
