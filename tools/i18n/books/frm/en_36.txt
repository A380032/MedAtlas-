s7264 **Monobactams** — **aztreonam**: only against gram-negatives (including Pseudomonas); **almost no cross-allergy with penicillins** (except for the side chain it shares with ceftazidime) — an alternative for gram-negative infections in severe penicillin allergy.

s7280 **“Penicillin allergy”** — about 10% of patients report it, but **more than 90%** of them are not actually allergic (often a viral rash in childhood or an adverse effect). A wrong label “condemns” the patient to less effective and more expensive antibiotics. The history must be clarified in detail: the type of reaction (urticaria, angioedema, anaphylaxis — true IgE-mediated; a mild rash — probably not) and its timing. In true severe allergy penicillins are contraindicated; **cross-reactivity with cephalosporins** depends mainly on **similar side chains** and is **less than 2%** (even less with the 2nd–3rd generations); in non-anaphylactic allergy, 3rd-generation cephalosporins are usually safe. Skin tests and a supervised drug challenge with an allergist make it possible to remove the “allergy” label.
s7281 3. Glycopeptides
s7282 **Vancomycin** (and teicoplanin) binds to the D-alanyl-D-alanine part of peptidoglycan precursors and blocks cell wall synthesis. Only against **gram-positive** bacteria (it does not cross the outer membrane of gram-negatives): **MRSA**, coagulase-negative staphylococci, enterococci, **C. difficile**, penicillin-resistant pneumococci.
s7283 Intravenously — MRSA infections (bacteremia, endocarditis, pneumonia, osteomyelitis), penicillin-resistant pneumococcal meningitis (with ceftriaxone).
s7284 **Orally** (not absorbed) — **C. difficile colitis** (severe and recurrent).
s7285 The dose is monitored by the minimum (**trough**) blood concentration or the AUC. **Adverse effects: “red man syndrome”** (histamine release with rapid infusion — flushing of the face, neck and chest, itching, hypotension; prevention — infusion over at least 60 minutes), **nephrotoxicity** (especially with aminoglycosides and piperacillin/tazobactam), ototoxicity, phlebitis.
s7286 4. Aminoglycosides
s7287 Table 17.5. Generations of aminoglycosides
s7288 Streptomycin, kanamycin, neomycin
s7289 Streptomycin — tuberculosis (reserve), plague, tularemia; neomycin — only topical and intestinal (very toxic)
s7290 Gentamicin, tobramycin, netilmicin
s7291 Gram-negatives, Pseudomonas (especially tobramycin)
s7292 Amikacin

s7294 **Mechanism.** They bind irreversibly to the **30S** ribosomal subunit and cause misreading of mRNA and synthesis of nonfunctional proteins — **bactericidal**, concentration-dependent, with a strong postantibiotic effect. Their entry into the bacterium is **oxygen-dependent** — **they do not act on anaerobes**; their activity decreases in an acidic environment (abscess).
s7295 **Pharmacokinetics:** not absorbed from the intestine (parenteral or topical); cross the BBB poorly; excreted almost unchanged by the **kidneys**.
s7296 **Uses:** severe gram-negative infections (sepsis, pyelonephritis, hospital-acquired pneumonia) — usually in combination with a beta-lactam; **enterococcal and streptococcal endocarditis** (synergy — the beta-lactam damages the cell wall and makes it easier for the aminoglycoside to enter); plague, tularemia; tuberculosis (amikacin); topical — eye drops (tobramycin), skin ointments.
s7297 5. Macrolides
s7298 **Preparations:** 14-membered — **erythromycin**, **clarithromycin**, roxithromycin; 15-membered (azalides) — **azithromycin**; 16-membered — josamycin, spiramycin, midecamycin.
s7299 **Mechanism. Binding to the 50S** subunit blocks translocation of the peptide chain — bacteriostatic (bactericidal at high concentrations).
s7300 **Spectrum:** gram-positive cocci (streptococci, pneumococci — resistance is growing), **atypical bacteria** (**Mycoplasma pneumoniae, Chlamydia, Legionella**), Bordetella pertussis, Campylobacter, H. pylori (clarithromycin), H. influenzae (azithromycin, clarithromycin), toxoplasma (spiramycin), mycobacteria (MAC — clarithromycin, azithromycin).
s7301 **Uses: community-acquired pneumonia** (atypical pathogens; in severe forms in combination with a beta-lactam), streptococcal tonsillitis and other infections **in penicillin allergy**, **whooping cough**, chlamydial infections (azithromycin 1 g once), H. pylori eradication (clarithromycin), campylobacteriosis, **trachoma**, travelers' diarrhea; azithromycin — long-term low-dose prevention of exacerbations in COPD and bronchiectasis (anti-inflammatory effect).
s7302 **Pharmacokinetics:** azithromycin reaches very high concentrations in tissues and inside cells, t½ — 68 hours; **short courses** (3–5 days).
s7303 **Adverse effects:** gastrointestinal disturbances (erythromycin is a **motilin agonist**, strongly so; it is also used as a prokinetic), **QT prolongation** and arrhythmias (especially with other QT-prolonging drugs), cholestatic hepatitis, ototoxicity (at high doses); in newborns, erythromycin and azithromycin — **pyloric stenosis**.
s7304 6. Tetracyclines
s7305 **Preparations:** tetracycline, **doxycycline** (preferred — well absorbed, 1–2 times a day, no dose change in renal failure), **minocycline**; glycylcyclines — **tigecycline** (reserve, multidrug-resistant bacteria), omadacycline, eravacycline.
s7306 **Mechanism. Reversible binding to the 30S** subunit stops the binding of aminoacyl-tRNA to the ribosome — bacteriostatic.
s7307 **Spectrum:** broad — gram-positives and gram-negatives, **intracellular and atypical pathogens**: rickettsiae (**drugs of choice**), chlamydiae, mycoplasmas, **borreliae (Lyme disease)**, brucellae (with streptomycin or rifampicin), Vibrio cholerae, anthrax, plague, tularemia, leptospirae, some protozoa (malaria prophylaxis — doxycycline), MRSA (doxycycline, minocycline), Propionibacterium acnes.
s7308 **Uses:** rickettsioses (epidemic typhus, Q fever), Lyme disease, brucellosis, chlamydial infections, atypical pneumonia, acne and rosacea, cholera, malaria prophylaxis, H. pylori (in a quadruple regimen).
s7309 7. Other antibiotics
s7310 7.1. Lincosamides
s7311 **Clindamycin** (and lincomycin) — act on the **50S** subunit; bacteriostatic. **Gram-positive cocci** (staphylococci, including some MRSA, streptococci) and **anaerobes** (Bacteroides, clostridia — except C. difficile). It penetrates bone tissue well and inhibits toxin synthesis.
s7312 **Uses:** skin and soft tissue infections, **osteomyelitis**, lung abscess and aspiration pneumonia, pelvic and abdominal anaerobic infections, odontogenic infections, **toxic shock syndrome** and necrotizing fasciitis (with penicillin — to stop toxin synthesis), streptococcal infections **in penicillin allergy**, acne (topical), bacterial vaginosis (vaginal cream), toxoplasmosis and pneumocystosis (with other drugs).
s7313 **Adverse effects: C. difficile colitis** (pseudomembranous colitis) — one of the antibiotics with the highest risk; diarrhea, rash.
s7314 7.2. Amphenicols
s7315 **Chloramphenicol (levomycetin)** — acts on the **50S** subunit; broad-spectrum, bacteriostatic; crosses the BBB well. Because of severe adverse effects it is now a **reserve** drug (bacterial meningitis — in severe penicillin allergy, enteric fever, rickettsioses — when there is no other option); topically — eye drops, ointments (Levomekol).
s7316 7.3. Polymyxins and others
s7317 **Polymyxin B** and **colistin (polymyxin E)** bind to the lipopolysaccharides and phospholipids of the gram-negative bacterial membrane and damage the membrane like a detergent — bactericidal. **The last reserve** in infections with gram-negative bacteria resistant to many drugs, including **carbapenems** (Acinetobacter, Pseudomonas, Klebsiella). Nephrotoxic and neurotoxic. Topically — eye and ear drops.
s7318 **Daptomycin** — against gram-positives (MRSA, VRE); membrane depolarization; inactivated by pulmonary surfactant — **ineffective in pneumonia**; myopathy (CK monitoring).
s7319 **Fusidic acid** — staphylococci; a topical cream for skin infections.
s7320 **Mupirocin** — topical (nasal ointment) — eradication of staphylococcal carriage (including MRSA), impetigo.
s7321 **Rifamycins** (rifampicin, rifabutin, rifaximin) — in Topic 18; rifaximin is not absorbed from the intestine (travelers' diarrhea, hepatic encephalopathy).

s7375 8.3. Antibiotic-associated diarrhea and C. difficile
s7376 Almost any antibiotic (especially **clindamycin, cephalosporins, fluoroquinolones, amoxicillin/clavulanate**) can disrupt the intestinal flora and lead to overgrowth and toxin production by **Clostridioides difficile** — from watery diarrhea to **pseudomembranous colitis** and toxic megacolon. Treatment: stop the causative antibiotic; **oral vancomycin** or **fidaxomicin** (metronidazole only in mild cases when nothing else is available); in recurrent cases — fecal microbiota transplantation. Loperamide and other antiperistaltic agents are contraindicated. Prevention — no unjustified antibiotics, hand hygiene (with soap; alcohol does not act on spores).
