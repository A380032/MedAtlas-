s7832 A 34-year-old man with pulmonary tuberculosis (mycobacteria in sputum, Xpert MTB/RIF — rifampicin-susceptible) is being treated with the HRZE regimen. In week 5 he developed nausea, vomiting, loss of appetite and yellowing of the sclerae. ALT is 6 times the upper limit of normal, bilirubin is raised. The patient drinks alcohol every day.
s7833 1) Condition? 2) Which drugs may be the cause? 3) Management.
s7834 1) Drug-induced hepatitis (during antituberculosis treatment). 2) Isoniazid, rifampicin and pyrazinamide are hepatotoxic; alcohol increases the risk; other causes (viral hepatitis) must be excluded. 3) Stop all hepatotoxic drugs; if the patient's condition and the severity of the disease require it — a temporary liver-sparing regimen (ethambutol + levofloxacin ± linezolid); when the enzymes and bilirubin approach normal, restart the drugs one by one (rifampicin first, then isoniazid; do not restart pyrazinamide and extend the continuation phase); abstinence from alcohol; DOT.
s7835 A 28-year-old woman is being treated for tuberculosis with a rifampicin-containing regimen. She takes a combined oral contraceptive and does not want to become pregnant.
s7836 What advice would you give?
s7837 Rifampicin is a strong CYP3A4 inducer; it speeds up the metabolism of estrogens and progestins and sharply reduces contraceptive effectiveness. During treatment and for at least 4 weeks after rifampicin is stopped, nonhormonal contraception (a copper intrauterine device, condoms) or a method not affected by inducers (depot medroxyprogesterone injection, a levonorgestrel intrauterine system) is recommended. She should also be warned that rifampicin colors urine and other fluids red and stains soft contact lenses.
s7838 A 68-year-old man has had severe burning pain along the intercostal spaces of the right half of the chest for 2 days, followed by clusters of blisters on reddened skin in the same area.
s7839 1) Diagnosis? 2) Treatment? 3) Prevention.
s7840 1) Herpes zoster (shingles) — reactivation of the varicella-zoster virus. 2) Valacyclovir 1 g 3 times a day for 7 days (or acyclovir 800 mg 5 times a day) — within 72 hours of the onset of the rash (over 50 years — to reduce the risk of postherpetic neuralgia); analgesics for pain (paracetamol, NSAIDs; for severe pain — gabapentinoids or a low-dose TCA); dose adjustment according to renal function. If the ophthalmic branch is involved — an urgent ophthalmologist. 3) Vaccination with the recombinant zoster vaccine for people over 50.
