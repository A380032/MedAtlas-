s4184 A 58-year-old man with COPD uses tiotropium once a day. Because of an exacerbation of bronchitis, the doctor prescribed ciprofloxacin; at home he has also been taking aminophylline (Euphylline) tablets for a long time. After 3 days he developed nausea, vomiting, palpitations and hand tremor.

s4233 Key note
s4234 Antiarrhythmic drugs. Antianginal drugs
s4235 Cardiac arrhythmias and ischemic heart disease (IHD) are the main forms of cardiovascular disease, which ranks first among causes of death. Atrial fibrillation, supraventricular tachycardia, an attack of angina and acute coronary syndrome are conditions that GPs and emergency physicians face every day. Paradoxically, antiarrhythmic drugs can themselves cause arrhythmias (proarrhythmic effect), so choosing them requires a deep knowledge of their mechanisms. In line with the 6th-semester laboratory class, this topic also covers **cardiotonic drugs (cardiac glycosides)**.

s4240 write prescriptions for drugs such as amiodarone, verapamil, adenosine, lidocaine, nitroglycerin, isosorbide mononitrate, amlodipine, ivabradine and digoxin;

s4243 Antiarrhythmics stop re-entry in two ways: **converting a unidirectional block into a bidirectional one** (slowing conduction even further — class I) or **prolonging the effective refractory period** (when the impulse returns, the tissue is still unexcitable — class III).
s4244 Vaughan Williams classes — **“Sodium, Beta, Potassium, Calcium”** (1, 2, 3, 4). To remember IA, IB, IC: **“IA — Affects both QRS and QT (prolongs both); IB — Best in ischemia; IC — Causes marked QRS widening”**.
s4245 **The CAST trial** (1989): in patients with ventricular extrasystoles after myocardial infarction, flecainide and encainide reduced the extrasystoles but **increased mortality** (proarrhythmic effect). Since then class IC drugs **are contraindicated in IHD, after infarction and in heart failure**. This trial is the classic proof of the principle “treat the patient, not the ECG”.
s4246 The adverse effects of amiodarone — **“Thyroid, lungs, liver, eyes, skin”** — all the “fatty” organs where it accumulates. Amiodarone increases the concentrations of warfarin and digoxin — their doses must be reduced.
s4247 Verapamil and diltiazem: 1) **contraindicated** in atrial fibrillation with **WPW syndrome** — by blocking the AV node they “direct” impulses to the ventricles through the accessory pathway and may cause ventricular fibrillation; 2) contraindicated in **wide-QRS tachycardia** (it may be ventricular tachycardia); 3) not given **intravenously together with beta-blockers** (risk of asystole); 4) heart failure (reduced ejection fraction) — a contraindication.
s4248 **Tolerance to nitrates** — with continuous use the effect decreases within 24–48 hours (depletion of sulfhydryl groups, neurohormonal activation). Prevention — **an 8–12-hour “nitrate-free interval” each day** (for example, sustained-release isosorbide mononitrate once a day in the morning; a patch is removed at night).
s4249 **Nitrates + phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitors (sildenafil, tadalafil, vardenafil)** — absolutely contraindicated: both increase cGMP and **life-threatening hypotension** develops. No nitrate is given for 24 hours after sildenafil or 48 hours after tadalafil. Other contraindications: hypotension (systolic BP < 90), right ventricular infarction, severe aortic stenosis, hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy, raised intracranial pressure.
s4250 **Short-acting nifedipine** (ordinary capsules) **increases the risk of death** in acute coronary syndrome because of abrupt vasodilation and reflex sympathetic activation; giving it sublingually in a hypertensive crisis is also not recommended. In angina and hypertension only long-acting forms (amlodipine, nifedipine GITS) are used.
s4251 The higher the **lipophilicity** (nonpolarity) of glycosides, the stronger their intestinal absorption, protein binding, hepatic metabolism and **cumulation**: **digitoxin > digoxin > strophanthin**. Because digoxin is excreted by the kidneys, **its dose is reduced in renal failure**.
s4252 Older textbooks presented cardiac glycosides as the main drugs for treating heart failure and recommended “digitalization” (loading) schemes. In modern recommendations, the main treatment of heart failure is ACE inhibitors/ARNIs, beta-blockers, mineralocorticoid antagonists and SGLT2 inhibitors (Topic 11); digoxin is an add-on drug used at low doses (blood concentration 0.5–0.9 ng/ml).
s4253 In glycoside toxicity **calcium preparations are contraindicated** (they worsen arrhythmias and cardiac arrest), and electrical cardioversion is avoided as far as possible (risk of ventricular fibrillation). Intravenous calcium preparations must not be given to a patient taking glycosides.
s4254 Conclusions of the experiment: cardiac glycosides have a **positive inotropic, negative chronotropic and dromotropic** effect; at toxic doses — arrhythmias and systolic arrest; **hypokalemia increases toxicity**. Verapamil is antiarrhythmic but negatively inotropic.
