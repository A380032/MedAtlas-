s1838 A 3-year-old child (15 kg) swallowed an unknown number of tablets from his grandfather's medicine box at home; 40 minutes have passed since the incident. The child is conscious and his condition is satisfactory for now. The box contained digoxin and amlodipine tablets.

s1881 Drugs acting on the cholinergic system appear in almost every area of clinical medicine: treatment of glaucoma and myasthenia gravis, postoperative intestinal atony, bradycardia, bronchial obstruction, intestinal and renal colic, urinary incontinence, Alzheimer's disease. Poisoning with organophosphorus compounds (insecticides) and atropine poisoning are classic emergencies. This topic is the foundation of autonomic nervous system pharmacology.

s1888 **Acetylcholine** is the mediator of all **preganglionic** fibers, all **parasympathetic postganglionic** fibers, **somatic** nerves and the sympathetic fibers innervating the sweat glands. Norepinephrine is the mediator of most **sympathetic postganglionic** fibers only.
s1889 M-cholinomimetic effect — **“DUMBELS”**: **D**iarrhea, **U**rination, **M**iosis, **B**radycardia and **B**ronchorrhea/**B**ronchospasm, **E**mesis (vomiting), **L**acrimation, **S**alivation and **S**weating (secretion).
s1890 General **contraindications** to M-cholinomimetics and anticholinesterase agents: bronchial asthma, bradycardia and AV block, ischemic heart disease, gastric ulcer, epilepsy, pregnancy (uterine tone increases), **mechanical** obstruction of the urinary and intestinal tracts (peritonitis, intestinal obstruction).
s1891 **Quaternary ammonium compounds** (neostigmine, pyridostigmine, metocinium, ipratropium, hyoscine butylbromide) carry a permanent positive charge: they are **poorly absorbed** from the intestine, **do not cross the BBB** and do not act on the CNS. **Tertiary amines** (physostigmine, galantamine, atropine, scopolamine) are well absorbed and act **on the CNS**.
s1892 **Myasthenic and cholinergic crisis** — both present with muscle weakness and respiratory failure. In a myasthenic crisis, the anticholinesterase drug is **insufficient**; in a cholinergic crisis — **excessive** (DUMBELS signs: miosis, salivation, bradycardia, diarrhea, fasciculations). They used to be distinguished by the edrophonium test; now the airway is secured first and anticholinesterase agents are temporarily stopped.
s1893 **Causes of death in OPC poisoning** — respiratory failure: bronchorrhea and bronchospasm + paralysis of the respiratory muscles + depression of the respiratory center.
s1894 Treatment of OPC poisoning — **“A-R-D”**: **A**tropine (blocks M), **R**eactivator (restores the enzyme), **D**iazepam (stops seizures). Atropine does not eliminate the N effect (muscle paralysis) — only a reactivator can do this.
s1895 **Dose dependence** of atropine effects: the most sensitive are the salivary, bronchial and sweat glands (0.5 mg) → eye and heart (1–2 mg) → bladder and intestine (2–5 mg) → gastric secretion (the highest dose; that is why atropine is ineffective in gastric ulcer and has many adverse effects). At doses above 10 mg — CNS disturbances.
s1896 A pupil dilated by atropine stays dilated for **7–10 days** (and even longer); for diagnostic purposes, short-acting tropicamide (4–6 hours) or cyclopentolate (up to 24 hours) is now preferred. **Contraindications** to atropine and other M-cholinergic blockers: **angle-closure glaucoma**, prostatic hypertrophy (urinary retention), tachyarrhythmias, intestinal atony and paralytic ileus, severe heart failure, hot climate and febrile children (hyperthermia).
s1897 Atropine poisoning, in the classic English phrase: **“Hot as a hare, dry as a bone, red as a beet, blind as a bat, mad as a hatter, full as a flask”** (hyperthermia, dry skin, flushing, mydriasis and inability to see near objects, delirium, urinary retention).
s1898 Eye muscles: **“Circular — parasympathetic, radial — sympathetic”**. The circular (sphincter) muscle constricts the pupil through M receptors; the radial (dilator) muscle dilates it through alpha-1. M blockers cause **both mydriasis and cycloplegia**; phenylephrine — mydriasis only.
s1899 Conclusion of the experiment: atropine abolishes only spasm induced **through M-cholinergic receptors** (**neurotropic antispasmodic**). Papaverine and drotaverine act directly on the smooth muscle cell (**myotropic antispasmodics**) and relax spasm of any cause. Platyphylline has both mechanisms.

s1923 1.2. How the cholinergic synapse works
s1924 **Synthesis:** in the cytoplasm of the nerve ending, acetylcholine (ACh) is formed from choline and acetyl-CoA with the participation of **choline acetyltransferase**. Choline is taken back up into the cell by a special carrier (hemicholinium blocks this process).
s1925 **Storage:** ACh is collected into vesicles (vesamicol blocks this).
s1926 **Release:** when a nerve impulse arrives, Ca2+ enters, the vesicles fuse with the membrane and release ACh into the synaptic cleft (exocytosis). **Botulinum toxin** cleaves SNARE proteins and blocks ACh release.
s1927 **Action on receptors:** ACh stimulates the postsynaptic M or N cholinergic receptors.
s1928 **Inactivation:** in the synaptic cleft, **acetylcholinesterase** (true cholinesterase) breaks ACh down into choline and acetic acid within a few milliseconds. Blood plasma and the liver contain **butyrylcholinesterase** (pseudocholinesterase).

s1999 2. M-cholinomimetics
s2000 **M-cholinomimetics** — drugs that stimulate M-cholinergic receptors **directly**. They reproduce the muscarinic effects of ACh.
s2001 2.1. Pilocarpine
s2002 **Pilocarpine** — an alkaloid of the South American plant Pilocarpus jaborandi, a tertiary amine. It is used mainly **topically in ophthalmology**.
s2003 **Miosis** — the pupillary constrictor muscle contracts.
s2004 **Lowering of intraocular pressure** — the iris becomes thinner and stretched, the anterior chamber angle opens, and outflow of fluid into Schlemm's canal improves; the ciliary muscle contracts and pulls the trabecular meshwork.
s2005 **Spasm of accommodation** — the eye adjusts to near vision, and distance vision temporarily worsens. **Uses:** glaucoma (especially in an attack of angle-closure glaucoma), retinal artery thrombosis, constriction of a pupil dilated by atropine; orally — for **xerostomia** (dry mouth) in Sjögren's syndrome and after radiation.
s2006 **Acute attack of angle-closure glaucoma** — an emergency: severe eye pain, redness, sharp loss of vision, “colored halos”, headache, vomiting. Treatment: pilocarpine 1–2% drops (every 15 minutes), acetazolamide orally or intravenously, osmotic diuretics, beta-blocker drops (timolol); then laser iridotomy. **M-cholinergic blockers (atropine) are absolutely contraindicated** in such patients.
s2007 2.2. Aceclidine and bethanechol
s2008 **Aceclidine** — a synthetic M-cholinomimetic. It is less irritating than pilocarpine. It is used as eye drops in glaucoma and given subcutaneously for postoperative **atony of the intestine and bladder**.
s2009 **Bethanechol** — is not broken down by cholinesterase and acts mainly on the intestine and bladder; for postoperative urinary retention.
s2010 2.3. Muscarine poisoning
s2011 Some mushrooms (Inocybe and Clitocybe species; little in fly agaric) contain **muscarine**. Poisoning begins after 15–30 minutes: profuse salivation, sweating, lacrimation, miosis, bronchorrhea, bronchospasm, nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, colicky abdominal pain, bradycardia, hypotension. **Antidote — atropine** (intravenously or intramuscularly, until the signs disappear).
s2012 3. Anticholinesterase agents (indirect cholinomimetics)
s2013 **Anticholinesterase agents** inhibit acetylcholinesterase. As a result, ACh is not broken down in the synapses, it accumulates, and the action on **all** cholinergic receptors (M and N) increases. Their effect therefore consists of **muscarinic** (DUMBELS) and **nicotinic** (fibrillary twitching of skeletal muscles, improved neuromuscular transmission, at high doses — depolarizing block) components.
