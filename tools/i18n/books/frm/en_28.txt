s5819 A 68-year-old man takes furosemide 80 mg/day, digoxin and enalapril for chronic heart failure and atrial fibrillation. In recent days he has felt weakness, muscle cramps, nausea and an irregular heartbeat. Potassium 2.9 mmol/l, magnesium low.

s5853 Thromboses are the main cause of myocardial infarction, ischemic stroke, deep vein thrombosis and pulmonary embolism. Antithrombotic drugs (antiplatelet agents, anticoagulants, fibrinolytics) save millions of lives, but they are also among the drugs that cause the most serious adverse effects (bleeding). Anemia — especially iron deficiency anemia — is also widespread among women and children in Uzbekistan. This topic covers drugs that affect blood coagulation, hemostasis and hematopoiesis.

s5862 **Vitamin K-dependent clotting factors** — **II, VII, IX, X** (and proteins C and S). To remember them: **“1972”** — 10, 9, 7, 2. Warfarin disrupts the synthesis of exactly these factors.
s5863 Arterial and venous thrombi: in arteries (high velocity) — a **platelet-rich “white” thrombus** → **antiplatelet agents** (prevention of infarction and stroke); in veins (slow flow) — a **fibrin- and red cell-rich “red” thrombus** → **anticoagulants** (deep vein thrombosis, PE, atrial fibrillation).
s5864 **The antiplatelet dose of aspirin is 75–100 mg/day** (low dose). At high doses (> 300 mg), prostacyclin synthesis in the endothelium is also inhibited and gastrointestinal adverse effects rise sharply, while the antiplatelet effect does not increase. In acute coronary syndrome and stroke, a **loading dose** of 150–300 mg is chewed.
s5865 **The antidote to heparin is protamine sulfate**: a strongly basic protein that binds heparin directly and inactivates it (chemical antagonism). **1 mg of protamine neutralizes 100 units of heparin** (the amount of heparin given in the last 2–3 hours is taken into account). It neutralizes LMWH only partly (≈ 60%).
s5866 **Warfarin has very many interactions** (narrow therapeutic range, metabolism via CYP2C9 and CYP3A4, 99% protein binding): **enhance its effect (bleeding risk)**: amiodarone, fluconazole and other azoles, metronidazole, many antibiotics (co-trimoxazole, fluoroquinolones, macrolides — they also reduce the intestinal flora), NSAIDs and aspirin (pharmacodynamic), SSRIs, acute alcohol intake, liver diseases; **reduce its effect**: rifampicin, carbamazepine, phenytoin, phenobarbital, St John's wort, foods rich in vitamin K (spinach, cabbage, broccoli, green tea — intake should be kept stable). Whenever a new drug is prescribed, the INR should be checked frequently.
s5867 **Absolute contraindications to thrombolysis**: a history of intracerebral hemorrhage at any time; ischemic stroke in the last 3–6 months (other than the acute stroke); a brain tumor or vascular malformation; active internal bleeding (except menstruation); suspected aortic dissection; severe trauma, head injury or major surgery in the last month; uncontrolled AH (> 185/110 mm Hg). The main complication is **bleeding, especially intracerebral hemorrhage**; antidote — tranexamic acid, cryoprecipitate (fibrinogen).
s5868 **Acute iron poisoning** is one of the common causes of fatal poisoning in children (sweet tablets!): vomiting (with blood), diarrhea, then a “false improvement”, followed by shock, metabolic acidosis and liver failure. **Antidote — deferoxamine** (intravenously). Activated charcoal does not bind iron.
s5869 In B12 deficiency, giving **folic acid** alone improves the anemia but **worsens the neurological disorders**. In megaloblastic anemia, the B12 level should be measured first, or both should be given together.
s5870 1. The hemostatic system
s5871 **Hemostasis** is the system that stops bleeding when a vessel is damaged and keeps the blood fluid. It consists of three stages:
s5872 **Vascular-platelet hemostasis.** Collagen and von Willebrand factor under the damaged endothelium cause platelet **adhesion**. Activated platelets release **thromboxane A2** (TXA2), **ADP** and serotonin and recruit other platelets. The final common pathway is activation of the **GP IIb/IIIa** receptors on the platelet membrane, which link platelets to each other through fibrinogen (**aggregation**) — a white thrombus forms.
s5873 **Coagulation hemostasis.** The cascade of plasma clotting factors: tissue factor (with factor VII) → **factor Xa** → prothrombin (II) → **thrombin** (IIa) → fibrinogen → **fibrin** → factor XIIIa stabilizes the fibrin — a red thrombus.
s5874 **Fibrinolysis.** Under the influence of tissue plasminogen activator (t-PA), plasminogen is converted into **plasmin**, which breaks down fibrin.

s5916 2. Antiplatelet agents
s5917 2.1. Acetylsalicylic acid (aspirin)
s5918 **Mechanism.** It **irreversibly acetylates** **cyclooxygenase-1 (COX-1)** in platelets → TXA2 synthesis stops. Platelets have no nucleus and cannot synthesize new enzyme, so the effect lasts for **the whole life of the platelet** (7–10 days). Endothelial cells, in contrast, resynthesize COX and restore prostacyclin production.
s5919 **Uses:** acute coronary syndrome, stable IHD, ischemic stroke and TIA, peripheral artery disease — **secondary prevention**; after coronary stenting (with a P2Y12 blocker — dual antiplatelet therapy); prevention of preeclampsia (in high-risk pregnant women). In **primary prevention** (in people without cardiovascular disease), the risk of bleeding may outweigh the benefit — it is now **not routinely recommended**.
s5920 **Adverse effects:** gastrointestinal erosions and ulcers, **bleeding**, bronchospasm (**“aspirin-induced asthma”** — NSAID-exacerbated respiratory disease), **Reye syndrome** in children (during a viral infection — liver and brain damage; contraindicated for fever under 16 years).

s5943 3. Anticoagulants
s5944 3.1. Unfractionated heparin (UFH)
s5945 **Heparin** is a sulfated glycosaminoglycan formed in mast cells; a natural anticoagulant. The preparation is obtained from porcine intestinal mucosa.
s5946 **Mechanism.** Heparin binds to **antithrombin III** and increases its activity **1,000-fold**. The complex inactivates **thrombin (IIa)** and **factor Xa** (as well as IXa, XIa and XIIa). To inactivate thrombin, the heparin molecule must be long (18 or more saccharides) — it binds antithrombin and thrombin simultaneously like a “bridge”.
s5947 It acts **in vitro and in vivo**; the effect begins **immediately** (intravenously).
s5948 It is not absorbed from the intestine — only **intravenously** or **subcutaneously** (intramuscular injection is contraindicated because of the risk of hematoma).
s5949 Short action (t½ ≈ 1–1.5 hours); nonlinear pharmacokinetics, variable individual response.
s5950 **Laboratory monitoring: activated partial thromboplastin time (aPTT)** — prolonged to 1.5–2.5 times normal. **Uses:** acute coronary syndrome, PE, deep vein thrombosis, cardiopulmonary bypass, hemodialysis, the hypercoagulable phase of DIC, anticoagulation **in severe renal failure** (instead of LMWH), situations requiring rapid reversal.
