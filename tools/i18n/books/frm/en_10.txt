s2043 **Myasthenia gravis** — an autoimmune disease in which autoantibodies against NM cholinergic receptors are formed: pathological fatigability of the extraocular muscles (ptosis, diplopia), the muscles of swallowing and speech, and the limb muscles. s2044 3.2. Irreversible anticholinesterase agents — organophosphorus compounds (OPCs)
s2045 OPCs **phosphorylate** the esteratic site of acetylcholinesterase; the bond is very strong. Over time, the enzyme–OPC complex **“ages”** (an alkyl group is cleaved off) and can then no longer be restored even by reactivators. Enzyme activity recovers only when new enzyme is synthesized (weeks).
s2046 Of the OPCs, only **Armin** and **ecothiopate** (in glaucoma) have been used as drugs; their main importance lies in **poisoning**: agricultural insecticides (trichlorfon, malathion, dichlorvos, parathion), household insecticides and chemical warfare agents (sarin, soman, tabun, VX).
s2047 3.3. Acute OPC poisoning
s2048 OPCs are rapidly absorbed through the skin, mucous membranes, and the respiratory and gastrointestinal tracts.

s2063 4. M-cholinergic blockers (M-cholinolytics, antimuscarinic agents)
s2064 **M-cholinergic blockers** block M-cholinergic receptors **competitively** and eliminate the effects of ACh and M-cholinomimetics. Their effect resembles “switching off” the parasympathetic innervation.
s2065 4.1. Atropine
s2066 **Atropine** — an alkaloid of plants of the nightshade family: belladonna (Atropa belladonna), henbane (Hyoscyamus niger), jimsonweed (Datura stramonium). A tertiary amine; it nonselectively blocks all types of M-cholinergic receptors.

s2091 4.2. Atropine poisoning
s2092 Poisoning is often seen in children who eat the berries and seeds of belladonna, jimsonweed and henbane, and also with drug overdose.
s2093 **Clinical picture:** dryness of the skin and mucous membranes, difficulty swallowing, hoarseness, **flushing** of the skin of the face and neck, **hyperthermia** (especially in children), marked **mydriasis** and photophobia, inability to see near objects, **tachycardia**, urinary retention, intestinal atony; CNS excitation — restlessness, speech and motor excitation, **hallucinations**, delirium, seizures; in severe cases — coma, paralysis of the respiratory center.
s2094 Gastric lavage (useful even late, because atropine slows intestinal motility), activated charcoal.
s2095 **Antidote — physostigmine** (a tertiary amine that enters the CNS) 0.5–2 mg intravenously, slowly; it eliminates central and peripheral signs. Neostigmine acts only on peripheral signs.
s2096 For agitation and seizures — **diazepam** (phenothiazines are contraindicated — they themselves have M-blocking activity).
s2097 For hyperthermia — physical cooling (ice, wet sponging).
s2098 For urinary retention — catheterization; for respiratory disturbance — mechanical ventilation.

s2153 5. Laboratory class: studying the antispasmodic effect of M-cholinergic blockers
s2154 This section corresponds to the 5th-semester class “Laboratory study of the antispasmodic effect of M-cholinergic blockers”.
s2155 **Essence of the experiment.** An isolated segment of intestine (usually guinea pig ileum) is placed in warm (37 °C) oxygenated Tyrode's solution, and its contractions are recorded.
s2156 Baseline contractions are recorded.
s2157 **Acetylcholine** is added to the bath — the intestine contracts strongly (tone and amplitude increase).
s2158 After washing, **atropine** is added to the bath and then acetylcholine is given again — the contracting effect of ACh **weakens or disappears**.
s2159 It is shown that the block can be overcome by increasing the ACh dose — a sign of **competitive antagonism** (the dose–effect curve shifts to the right, Emax is unchanged).
s2160 For comparison, a spasm caused by **barium chloride** (a direct myotropic contractor) is not abolished by atropine but is abolished by **papaverine** (a myotropic antispasmodic).

s2197 Skill 1. Algorithm of emergency care for a patient poisoned with OPCs
s2198 Protect yourself (gloves, mask) and remove the patient from the contaminated environment.
s2199 Take off the clothes, wash the skin with soap and plenty of water.
s2200 Clear the airway and give oxygen; if necessary, intubate (without suxamethonium).
s2201 Establish intravenous access; **atropine 2–4 mg intravenously**, doubling the dose every 5–10 minutes until atropinization (dry bronchi, HR > 80).
s2202 Give a **cholinesterase reactivator** (pralidoxime or dipyroxime) as early as possible.
s2203 For seizures — diazepam 10 mg intravenously.
s2204 If ingested (after the airway has been secured) — gastric lavage, activated charcoal.
s2205 Determine serum cholinesterase activity and observe for at least 24–48 hours (delayed conditions, “intermediate syndrome”).
