s2835 2.5. Beta-adrenomimetics
s2836 **Isoprenaline (Isadrin)** — a beta-1 and beta-2 agonist. Tachycardia and bronchodilation. Rarely used now (temporarily in AV block, in bradycardia).
s2837 **Beta-2 adrenomimetics** — the main drugs for bronchial asthma and COPD (in detail in topic 9):
s2838 **short-acting** (4–6 hours; for relieving attacks): salbutamol, fenoterol, terbutaline;
s2839 **long-acting** (12 hours): salmeterol, formoterol (formoterol has a rapid onset); **ultra-long-acting** (24 hours): indacaterol, olodaterol, vilanterol;
s2840 **tocolytics** (relaxing the uterus when there is a risk of preterm labor): hexoprenaline (Ginipral), salbutamol. **Adverse effects:** hand tremor (beta-2 of skeletal muscles), tachycardia and arrhythmias (at high doses they also act on beta-1), **hypokalemia**, hyperglycemia, headache; tachyphylaxis.
s2841 2.6. Indirect sympathomimetics
s2842 **Ephedrine** — an alkaloid of the ephedra plant; it has a **mixed** action: it enhances norepinephrine release from the nerve ending (the main action) and also stimulates alpha and beta receptors directly. It resembles epinephrine but acts more weakly and **longer**; it is active orally; it stimulates the CNS. With repeated doses, **tachyphylaxis** develops (norepinephrine stores are depleted). Uses: hypotension during spinal anesthesia, narcolepsy (historically). As a precursor of narcotic substances (methamphetamine), it is under strict control.
s2843 **Amphetamine**, **methylphenidate** — CNS stimulants (topic 8).
s2844 **Tyramine** — found in cheese, wine and smoked products; in patients taking MAO inhibitors it is not broken down, releases a large amount of norepinephrine and causes a **hypertensive crisis** (“cheese reaction”).
s2845 3. Adrenergic blockers
s2846 3.1. Alpha-blockers
s2847 Figure 6.3
s2848 Sites of action of adrenergic blockers: phentolamine, Tropaphen — alpha-1 and alpha-2; prazosin — alpha-1 (teaching material of the TMA Department of Pharmacology)
s2849 **Nonselective alpha-1 and alpha-2 blockers** (phentolamine, Tropaphen, phenoxybenzamine): they dilate the vessels and lower BP. Because presynaptic alpha-2 receptors are also blocked, norepinephrine release increases and **tachycardia** is marked.
s2850 **Phentolamine** — short-acting; hypertensive crises during the diagnosis and surgery of **pheochromocytoma**, norepinephrine extravasation.
s2851 **Phenoxybenzamine** — irreversible (covalent) block; preparation of pheochromocytoma for surgery.
s2852 **Dihydroergotoxine, nicergoline** (derivatives of ergot alkaloids) — for cerebral and peripheral circulatory disorders.
s2853 **Selective alpha-1 blockers** (prazosin, doxazosin, terazosin): little reflex tachycardia (alpha-2 remains open), slightly improve the lipid profile.
s2854 **Uses:** arterial hypertension (as an add-on drug, especially together with prostatic hyperplasia), **benign prostatic hyperplasia** (urination improves), Raynaud's syndrome.
s2855 **“First-dose phenomenon”** — marked **orthostatic hypotension** and fainting after the first dose. Prevention: start with a small dose, **at bedtime**. **Uroselective alpha-1A blockers** (tamsulosin, silodosin, alfuzosin) act selectively on the smooth muscle of the prostate and bladder neck; they have little effect on BP. Used for prostatic hyperplasia and to ease the passage of urinary stones. Adverse effects: retrograde ejaculation, “floppy iris” syndrome during cataract surgery.
s2856 In a patient with **pheochromocytoma** (a catecholamine-producing tumor), a beta-blocker must not be given **without an alpha-blocker**: beta-2 vasodilation is lost and alpha vasoconstriction is left unopposed — **a severe hypertensive crisis**. An alpha-blocker (phenoxybenzamine, doxazosin) is given first, and only then is a beta-blocker added for tachycardia.

s2948 4. Sympatholytics
s2949 **Sympatholytics** reduce norepinephrine stores in adrenergic nerve endings or stop its release; they do not act on receptors.
s2950 **Reserpine** — an alkaloid of Rauwolfia; it blocks the uptake of norepinephrine, dopamine and serotonin into vesicles (VMAT), the mediators are broken down by MAO, and the stores are depleted. The hypotensive effect develops slowly (over 1–2 weeks). Adverse effects: **depression** (risk of suicide), parkinsonism, nasal congestion, bradycardia, exacerbation of gastric ulcer (parasympathetic predominance). It is hardly used now; it remains in some combination tablets (Adelphane).
s2951 **Guanethidine (Octadine)** — blocks norepinephrine release; does not enter the CNS. It causes severe orthostatic hypotension. It is of historical importance.
