s6331 3. Thyroid hormones and antithyroid drugs
s6332 3.1. Thyroid hormones
s6333 The thyroid gland produces **thyroxine (T4)** and **triiodothyronine (T3)**. T4 is converted in the periphery into the more active T3. Through nuclear receptors they regulate basal metabolism, heat production, growth and development (especially of the CNS), and cardiac function.
s6334 **Levothyroxine** (L-thyroxine) is the drug of choice for treating **hypothyroidism** (replacement therapy). T½ ≈ 7 days — once a day; the dose is titrated according to **TSH** (every 6–8 weeks). The full replacement dose in adults is ≈ 1.6 mcg/kg/day; **in the elderly and in IHD it is started at a small dose** (12.5–25 mcg) so as not to provoke myocardial ischemia. It is taken **on an empty stomach**, 30–60 minutes before breakfast, separately from other drugs (calcium, iron, PPIs, antacids). The dose requirement increases in pregnancy.
s6335 Other uses: congenital hypothyroidism (in newborns — early start is crucial for mental development), after thyroidectomy, TSH suppression in thyroid cancer.
s6336 **Adverse effects (in overdose)** — drug-induced thyrotoxicosis: tachycardia, atrial fibrillation, angina, weight loss, tremor, osteoporosis.

s6359 4. Insulin and glucose-lowering drugs
s6360 4.1. Physiology of insulin
s6361 **Insulin** is the hormone of the pancreatic **beta cells**; it consists of A and B chains (51 amino acids). Glucose enters the beta cell and generates ATP → **ATP-dependent K+ channels close** → depolarization → Ca2+ enters → insulin is released.
s6362 Insulin acts through a **tyrosine kinase receptor**:
s6363 it increases glucose entry into muscle and fat cells (GLUT4 transporters move to the membrane);
s6364 it enhances glycogen synthesis in the liver and inhibits **gluconeogenesis and glycogenolysis**;
s6365 it inhibits lipolysis and enhances fat synthesis (stops the formation of **ketone bodies**);
s6366 it increases protein synthesis;
s6367 it shifts **potassium into cells** (activation of Na+/K+-ATPase).
s6368 4.2. Insulin preparations
s6369 Modern preparations are **recombinant human insulin** and its **analogs** (with a modified amino acid sequence and improved pharmacokinetics). The concentration is usually **100 U/ml** (U-100).

s6406 4.3. Hypoglycemia
s6407 **Hypoglycemia** is glucose **below 3.9 mmol/l** (clinically significant — below 3.0). Causes: an excessive dose of insulin or a sulfonylurea, a missed meal, physical exertion, alcohol, renal failure.
s6408 **Signs:** adrenergic — sweating, tremor, palpitations, hunger, anxiety; neuroglycopenic — headache, confusion, unusual behavior, visual disturbances, seizures, coma. **Beta-blockers mask the adrenergic signs.**

s6456 5.1. Inflammatory mediators and the arachidonic acid cascade

s6513 5.3. Paracetamol
s6514 **Paracetamol (acetaminophen)** is an analgesic and antipyretic; its anti-inflammatory effect is very weak. Its mechanism is not fully clear: it inhibits COX mainly in the CNS (in the periphery, the high peroxide levels in the inflammatory focus inactivate it) and acts on the serotonergic and cannabinoid systems. It has almost no effect on the gastric mucosa, platelets or kidneys — it is the **first-choice** analgesic-antipyretic **in children, pregnant women and the elderly** and in gastric diseases.
s6515 **Dose:** 500–1000 mg for adults, **no more than 4 g a day** (2–3 g with liver disease, alcoholism, starvation, low body weight); **10–15 mg/kg** for children, every 4–6 hours, no more than 60 mg/kg a day.
