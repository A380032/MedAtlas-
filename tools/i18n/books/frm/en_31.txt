s6338 3. Thyroid hormones and antithyroid drugs
s6339 3.1. Thyroid hormones
s6340 The thyroid gland produces **thyroxine (T4)** and **triiodothyronine (T3)**. T4 is converted in the periphery into the more active T3. Through nuclear receptors they regulate basal metabolism, heat production, growth and development (especially of the CNS), and cardiac function.
s6341 **Levothyroxine** (L-thyroxine) is the drug of choice for treating **hypothyroidism** (replacement therapy). T½ ≈ 7 days — once a day; the dose is titrated according to **TSH** (every 6–8 weeks). The full replacement dose in adults is ≈ 1.6 mcg/kg/day; **in the elderly and in IHD it is started at a small dose** (12.5–25 mcg) so as not to provoke myocardial ischemia. It is taken **on an empty stomach**, 30–60 minutes before breakfast, separately from other drugs (calcium, iron, PPIs, antacids). The dose requirement increases in pregnancy.
s6342 Other uses: congenital hypothyroidism (in newborns — early start is crucial for mental development), after thyroidectomy, TSH suppression in thyroid cancer.
s6343 **Adverse effects (in overdose)** — drug-induced thyrotoxicosis: tachycardia, atrial fibrillation, angina, weight loss, tremor, osteoporosis.

s6369 4. Insulin and glucose-lowering drugs
s6370 4.1. Physiology of insulin
s6371 **Insulin** is the hormone of the pancreatic **beta cells**; it consists of A and B chains (51 amino acids). Glucose enters the beta cell and generates ATP → **ATP-dependent K+ channels close** → depolarization → Ca2+ enters → insulin is released.
s6372 Insulin acts through a **tyrosine kinase receptor**:
s6373 it increases glucose entry into muscle and fat cells (GLUT4 transporters move to the membrane);
s6374 it enhances glycogen synthesis in the liver and inhibits **gluconeogenesis and glycogenolysis**;
s6375 it inhibits lipolysis and enhances fat synthesis (stops the formation of **ketone bodies**);
s6376 it increases protein synthesis;
s6377 it shifts **potassium into cells** (activation of Na+/K+-ATPase).
s6378 4.2. Insulin preparations
s6379 Modern preparations are **recombinant human insulin** and its **analogs** (with a modified amino acid sequence and improved pharmacokinetics). The concentration is usually **100 U/ml** (U-100).

s6417 4.3. Hypoglycemia
s6418 **Hypoglycemia** is glucose **below 3.9 mmol/l** (clinically significant — below 3.0). Causes: an excessive dose of insulin or a sulfonylurea, a missed meal, physical exertion, alcohol, renal failure.
s6419 **Signs:** adrenergic — sweating, tremor, palpitations, hunger, anxiety; neuroglycopenic — headache, confusion, unusual behavior, visual disturbances, seizures, coma. **Beta-blockers mask the adrenergic signs.**

s6466 5. Anti-inflammatory drugs
s6467 5.1. Inflammatory mediators and the arachidonic acid cascade
s6468 **Arachidonic acid** is released from cell membrane phospholipids by **phospholipase A2**. It is metabolized by two pathways:
s6469 **the cyclooxygenase (COX)** pathway → prostaglandin G2/H2 → **prostaglandins** (PGE2 — pain, fever, inflammation, gastric protection, renal blood flow; PGI2 — prostacyclin), **thromboxane A2**;
s6470 **the lipoxygenase** pathway → **leukotrienes** (LTB4 — chemotaxis; LTC4, D4, E4 — bronchospasm, vascular permeability).

s6482 5.2. Nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs)
s6483 **Mechanism.** By inhibiting COX, they reduce prostaglandin synthesis. As a result:
s6484 **anti-inflammatory** effect (vascular permeability and edema decrease);

s6487 **antiplatelet** effect — TXA2 in platelets decreases (aspirin — irreversibly).
