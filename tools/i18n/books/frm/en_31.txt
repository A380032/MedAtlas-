s6327 3. Thyroid hormones and antithyroid drugs
s6328 3.1. Thyroid hormones
s6329 The thyroid gland produces **thyroxine (T~4~)** and **triiodothyronine (T~3~)**. T~4~ is converted in the periphery into the more active T~3~. Through nuclear receptors they regulate basal metabolism, heat production, growth and development (especially of the CNS), and cardiac function.
s6330 **Levothyroxine** (L-thyroxine) is the drug of choice for treating **hypothyroidism** (replacement therapy). T½ ≈ 7 days — once a day; the dose is titrated according to **TSH** (every 6–8 weeks). The full replacement dose in adults is ≈ 1.6 mcg/kg/day; **in the elderly and in IHD it is started at a small dose** (12.5–25 mcg) so as not to provoke myocardial ischemia. It is taken **on an empty stomach**, 30–60 minutes before breakfast, separately from other drugs (calcium, iron, PPIs, antacids). The dose requirement increases in pregnancy.
s6331 Other uses: congenital hypothyroidism (in newborns — early start is crucial for mental development), after thyroidectomy, TSH suppression in thyroid cancer.
s6332 **Adverse effects (in overdose)** — drug-induced thyrotoxicosis: tachycardia, atrial fibrillation, angina, weight loss, tremor, osteoporosis.

s6355 4. Insulin and glucose-lowering drugs
s6356 4.1. Physiology of insulin
s6357 **Insulin** is the hormone of the pancreatic **beta cells**; it consists of A and B chains (51 amino acids). Glucose enters the beta cell and generates ATP → **ATP-dependent K^+^ channels close** → depolarization → Ca^2+^ enters → insulin is released.
s6358 Insulin acts through a **tyrosine kinase receptor**:
s6359 it increases glucose entry into muscle and fat cells (GLUT4 transporters move to the membrane);
s6360 it enhances glycogen synthesis in the liver and inhibits **gluconeogenesis and glycogenolysis**;
s6361 it inhibits lipolysis and enhances fat synthesis (stops the formation of **ketone bodies**);
s6362 it increases protein synthesis;
s6363 it shifts **potassium into cells** (activation of Na^+^/K^+^-ATPase).
s6364 4.2. Insulin preparations
s6365 Modern preparations are **recombinant human insulin** and its **analogs** (with a modified amino acid sequence and improved pharmacokinetics). The concentration is usually **100 U/ml** (U-100).

s6402 4.3. Hypoglycemia
s6403 **Hypoglycemia** is glucose **below 3.9 mmol/l** (clinically significant — below 3.0). Causes: an excessive dose of insulin or a sulfonylurea, a missed meal, physical exertion, alcohol, renal failure.
s6404 **Signs:** adrenergic — sweating, tremor, palpitations, hunger, anxiety; neuroglycopenic — headache, confusion, unusual behavior, visual disturbances, seizures, coma. **Beta-blockers mask the adrenergic signs.**

s6451 5. Anti-inflammatory drugs
s6452 5.1. Inflammatory mediators and the arachidonic acid cascade
s6453 **Arachidonic acid** is released from cell membrane phospholipids by **phospholipase A~2~**. It is metabolized by two pathways:
s6454 **the cyclooxygenase (COX)** pathway → prostaglandin G~2~/H~2~ → **prostaglandins** (PGE~2~ — pain, fever, inflammation, gastric protection, renal blood flow; PGI~2~ — prostacyclin), **thromboxane A~2~**;
s6455 **the lipoxygenase** pathway → **leukotrienes** (LTB~4~ — chemotaxis; LTC~4~, D~4~, E~4~ — bronchospasm, vascular permeability).

s6467 5.2. Nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs)
s6468 **Mechanism.** By inhibiting COX, they reduce prostaglandin synthesis. As a result:
s6469 **anti-inflammatory** effect (vascular permeability and edema decrease);

s6472 **antiplatelet** effect — TXA~2~ in platelets decreases (aspirin — irreversibly).
