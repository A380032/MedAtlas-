s4527 4.4. Laboratory class: effects of drugs on the isolated heart
s4528 Experiment (frog heart, Straub method, or isolated heart):
s4529 The frog heart is connected to a cannula and perfused with Ringer's solution; contractions are recorded with a kymograph or a sensor.
s4530 Normal contractions are recorded; then a **“heart failure model”** is created (for example, with a low-calcium solution — the contractions weaken).
s4531 **Strophanthin** or **digoxin** is added to the solution: the **contraction amplitude increases** (positive inotropy), systole is strong and short, diastole is prolonged, the rhythm slows.
s4532 When the glycoside dose is increased, a **toxic effect** is seen: arrhythmias, incomplete contractions and **arrest of the heart in systole**. If K^+^ in the solution is reduced, the toxic effect appears sooner.
s4533 **Studying the antiarrhythmic effect**: against the background of an arrhythmia (induced with calcium chloride or epinephrine), lidocaine or verapamil is added and restoration of the rhythm is observed; verapamil also reduces the force of contraction (negative inotropy).
