s6926 4.1. Sulfonamides and co-trimoxazole
s6927 **Mechanism.** Bacteria synthesize folic acid themselves from **PABA** (para-aminobenzoic acid) (humans obtain it from food). Sulfonamides are structurally similar to PABA — they **competitively inhibit dihydropteroate synthetase**. **Trimethoprim** inhibits **dihydrofolate reductase** at the next step. Together (**co-trimoxazole**) they produce sequential blockade, synergy and a bactericidal effect.
s6928 **Uses of co-trimoxazole:** urinary tract infections (if local resistance is low), **Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia** (treatment and prevention — the drug of choice in HIV infection), nocardiosis, toxoplasmosis, skin infections caused by MRSA, shigellosis. **Sulfasalazine** — ulcerative colitis and rheumatoid arthritis (5-aminosalicylic acid is released in the intestine). **Sulfacetamide** eye drops (conjunctivitis), **silver sulfadiazine** — burns.

s6964 4.3. Nitrofurans
s6965 **Nitrofurantoin** reaches therapeutic concentrations **only in urine** (low in blood). The **first-line** drug for **uncomplicated cystitis** (in women) (5 days), prevention of recurrent urinary tract infection. Resistance develops rarely. Ineffective and toxic at GFR < 30–45 ml/min. Adverse effects: nausea, brown urine; with long-term use — **pulmonary reactions** (acute pneumonitis, chronic fibrosis), peripheral neuropathy, hepatitis; hemolysis in G6PD deficiency and in newborns.
s6966 **Nifuroxazide** — not absorbed from the intestine, used in bacterial diarrhea (in CIS countries). **Furazolidone** — intestinal infections, giardiasis; inhibits MAO, **disulfiram-like reaction with alcohol**. **Furazidin (Furagin)** — urinary tract.
s6967 4.4. Nitroimidazoles
s6968 In the cells of anaerobic microorganisms and protozoa, the nitro group of **metronidazole** is **reduced**, forming free radicals that damage DNA (this process does not occur in aerobic cells — the basis of its selective action).
s6969 **Spectrum: anaerobic bacteria** (Bacteroides fragilis, Clostridioides difficile, Fusobacterium), **protozoa** (Trichomonas vaginalis, Giardia lamblia, Entamoeba histolytica), Gardnerella (bacterial vaginosis), H. pylori.
s6970 **Uses:** anaerobic infections (abdominal, pelvic, abscesses — often with other antibiotics), **trichomoniasis** (both partners are treated), bacterial vaginosis, amebiasis, giardiasis, mild C. difficile colitis (vancomycin/fidaxomicin are now preferred), H. pylori eradication, prophylaxis in colorectal surgery.
s6971 **Adverse effects: metallic taste in the mouth**, nausea, dark urine, **disulfiram-like reaction with alcohol** (facial flushing, tachycardia, vomiting — no alcohol during treatment and for 48–72 hours afterward), peripheral neuropathy and encephalopathy (with long-term and high-dose use), enhances the effect of warfarin; caution in the 1st trimester of pregnancy.
s6972 4.5. Oxazolidinones and others
s6973 **Linezolid** — active against gram-positive bacteria, including **MRSA** and **vancomycin-resistant enterococci (VRE)**; oral bioavailability ≈ 100%. A reserve antibiotic. Adverse effects: **myelosuppression** (thrombocytopenia — with use for more than 2 weeks), peripheral and optic neuropathy, lactic acidosis; inhibits MAO — **serotonin syndrome with SSRIs**, hypertension with tyramine.
s6974 **Nitroxoline** (5-NOK) — urinary tract infections; colors the urine bright yellow.
s6975 **Fosfomycin trometamol** — **a single dose in uncomplicated cystitis** (3 g), first line; also allowed in pregnant women.
