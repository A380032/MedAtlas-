s4318 **Procainamide (Novocainamide)** — given intravenously to restore sinus rhythm in atrial fibrillation and in ventricular tachycardia. Adverse effects: hypotension, QT prolongation, with prolonged use a **lupus-like syndrome** (especially in slow acetylators), agranulocytosis.
s4319 **Lidocaine** — selectively blocks sodium channels in ischemically damaged (depolarized) ventricular myocardium; it has almost no effect on the atria. Only **intravenously** (strong first-pass effect). Uses: **ventricular arrhythmias** (especially in acute ischemia), defibrillation-resistant ventricular fibrillation when amiodarone is not available. Toxicity — CNS signs (Topic 3).
s4320 **Class IC** (flecainide, propafenone) — for restoring rhythm in atrial fibrillation and preventing it (the **“pill-in-the-pocket”** approach), in patients **without** structural heart disease.
s4321 2.3. Class II — beta-blockers
s4322 By reducing sympathetic stimulation, they slow sinus node automaticity and AV conduction. They are proven antiarrhythmics **that reduce the risk of sudden death after myocardial infarction**. Uses: sinus tachycardia, rate control in atrial fibrillation, supraventricular tachycardias, catecholamine-dependent ventricular arrhythmias, long QT syndrome. **Esmolol** — ultrashort-acting, intravenous.
s4323 2.4. Class III — potassium channel blockers
s4324 **Amiodarone** — the most effective and broad-spectrum antiarrhythmic; it has properties of all four classes (blockade of K+, Na+ and Ca2+ channels and beta receptors). It may also be used in structural heart disease and heart failure.
s4325 **Pharmacokinetics:** highly lipophilic, a very large volume of distribution; **t½ — 40–60 days**; the effect develops over weeks — treatment is started with a **loading dose**.
s4326 **Uses:** atrial fibrillation (restoring and maintaining rhythm), ventricular tachycardia, defibrillation-resistant ventricular fibrillation **in cardiac arrest** (300 mg intravenously, then 150 mg).

s4347 2.5. Class IV — calcium channel blockers
s4348 **Verapamil** and **diltiazem** block the calcium current in the sinus and AV nodes and slow AV conduction. Uses: **supraventricular tachycardias** involving the AV node (if adenosine is ineffective), rate control in atrial fibrillation and flutter.
s4349 2.6. Other antiarrhythmics
s4350 **Adenosine** — via A1 receptors briefly (for seconds) stops conduction in the AV node; t½ < 10 seconds. **The drug of choice for terminating AV nodal re-entrant tachycardia**: 6 mg intravenously as a **rapid bolus** (into a proximal vein, immediately “flushed” with 20 ml of saline); if there is no effect — 12 mg. Adverse effects (brief): chest discomfort, facial flushing, dyspnea, brief asystole. Contraindicated in **bronchial asthma**; theophylline reduces its effect, dipyridamole enhances it.
s4351 **Magnesium sulfate** — the drug of choice for **torsades de pointes ventricular tachycardia** (long QT) (2 g intravenously); hypomagnesemia, glycoside arrhythmias.
s4352 **Digoxin** — slows AV conduction by increasing vagal tone; rate control in atrial fibrillation combined with heart failure (Section 6).
s4353 **Atropine** — in sinus bradycardia and AV blocks (Topic 4).
s4354 2.7. Proarrhythmic effect of antiarrhythmics
s4355 Almost all antiarrhythmics can themselves cause new or more severe arrhythmias: classes IA and III — QT prolongation and **torsades de pointes**; IC — ventricular tachycardia (especially in ischemia); classes II and IV, digoxin — bradycardia and AV block. Factors that increase the risk of proarrhythmia: **hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia**, bradycardia, other QT-prolonging drugs (macrolides, fluoroquinolones, antipsychotics, ondansetron), renal failure, female sex.

s4380 3. Antianginal drugs
s4381 3.1. Pathogenesis of angina
s4382 **Angina** is retrosternal pain caused by a mismatch between the myocardial oxygen **demand** and its **supply** through coronary blood flow. Causes: atherosclerotic narrowing of the coronary arteries (exertional angina), coronary spasm (vasospastic — Prinzmetal angina), thrombus (acute coronary syndrome).
s4383 Antianginal drugs work in two ways:
s4384 **reducing the myocardial oxygen demand** — lowering the heart rate, contractile force, preload (venous return) and afterload (BP);
s4385 **increasing the oxygen supply** to the myocardium — dilating the coronary vessels, relieving spasm, prolonging diastole (coronary perfusion takes place in diastole).

s4406 3.2. Organic nitrates
s4407 **Mechanism.** In the body, nitrates release **nitric oxide (NO)**. In smooth muscle cells NO activates guanylate cyclase → **cGMP** ↑ → myosin light chains are dephosphorylated → the vessel wall relaxes.

s4451 3.4. Other antianginal drugs
s4452 **Ivabradine** — by selectively inhibiting the If current in the sinus node, it **reduces only the heart rate**; it does not affect contractility or BP. Effective only **in sinus rhythm** (not in atrial fibrillation). Used when a beta-blocker cannot be used or is insufficient. Adverse effects: **phosphenes** (flashes of light in vision), bradycardia.
s4453 **Trimetazidine** — reduces beta-oxidation of fatty acids and shifts metabolism to glucose oxidation (saves oxygen). An add-on drug; it may cause parkinsonism and movement disorders.
s4454 **Ranolazine** — inhibits the late sodium current and improves diastolic relaxation; slightly prolongs QT.
s4455 **Nicorandil** — a potassium channel opener and NO donor; may cause oral and intestinal ulcers.
s4456 3.5. Prognosis-improving treatment
s4457 The treatment of angina has two goals: **reducing symptoms** (the antianginal drugs above) and **preventing myocardial infarction and death**:
s4458 **antiplatelet agents** — acetylsalicylic acid 75–100 mg/day (or clopidogrel);
s4459 **statins** — high-intensity (atorvastatin, rosuvastatin), to the target level of LDL cholesterol;
s4460 **ACE inhibitors** (or ARBs) — with concomitant hypertension, diabetes or heart failure;
s4461 **beta-blockers** — after infarction and in heart failure;
s4462 elimination of risk factors: stopping smoking, controlling BP and glucose, physical activity.
s4463 **Care in an attack of angina**: stop physical activity, sit down; **nitroglycerin** 0.5 mg sublingually (or 1 spray dose); if the pain does not pass in 5 minutes — a second dose and **call an ambulance**; up to 3 doses in total. If the pain lasts more than 15–20 minutes — acute coronary syndrome is suspected: chew aspirin 150–300 mg (if there is no allergy), ECG, urgent hospitalization.
s4464 4. Cardiotonic drugs (cardiac glycosides and non-glycoside inotropes)
s4465 This section corresponds to the 6th-semester class “Laboratory study of the effects of antiarrhythmic and cardiotonic drugs on the heart”.
s4466 4.1. Cardiac glycosides
s4467 **Cardiac glycosides** are substances obtained from plants, whose molecule consists of an **aglycone (genin)** — a steroid nucleus and a lactone ring — and a **sugar** part. Sources: foxglove (Digitalis lanata — digoxin, Digitalis purpurea — digitoxin), strophanthus (strophanthin), lily of the valley (Corglycon), spring adonis.
s4468 **Mechanism of action.** Glycosides inhibit **Na+/K+-ATPase** in the cardiomyocyte membrane → Na+ increases inside the cell → Ca2+ efflux via the Na+/Ca2+ exchanger decreases → **Ca2+ increases** in the sarcoplasmic reticulum and cytoplasm → contraction is strengthened.
