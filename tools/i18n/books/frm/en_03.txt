s0443 **Coated** tablets: Rp.: Tabulettas Omeprazoli obductas 0.02 N. 14.
s0444 **Retard**, enteric-coated and film-coated tablets **must not be split, chewed or crushed**: if the coating is damaged, the whole dose may be absorbed at once and cause a toxic effect (nifedipine retard, morphine retard), or the active substance may be destroyed by gastric acid (omeprazole, pancreatin). The patient should be reminded of this in the signatura: “swallow without chewing”.
s0445 6.4. Dragées (Dragee)
s0446 **Dragée** — a spherical dosed dosage form made in a factory by coating sugar granules layer by layer with drug and auxiliary substances (dragée coating). Its mass is 0.1–0.5 g. The name dragée is not declined in Latin.
s0447 **PRESCRIPTION 1.21. DRAGÉE** Rp.: Dragee Drotaverini 0.04
s0448 D.t.d. N. 20 S. Take 1 dragée 3 times a day when in pain.
s0449 6.5. Capsules (Capsulae)
s0450 **Capsule** — a shell that holds powdered, granular, pasty or liquid drugs. The capsule masks the taste and smell of the drug, protects it from light and air, and protects the gastric mucosa from the irritant effect of the drug.
s0451 **Gelatin capsules** (capsulae gelatinosae): hard (with a cap — operculatae) and soft (elastic — elasticae; for oily solutions).
s0452 **Starch capsules** (capsulae amylaceae, cachets) — hardly used now.
s0453 **Enteric (glutoid) capsules** — dissolve only in the intestine.

s0462 6.6. Pills (Pilulae) and other solid forms
s0463 **Pill** (Pilula, ae f) — a spherical dosage form weighing 0.1–0.5 g; it is prepared in the pharmacy from a special mass. If its mass is more than 0.5, it is a **bolus**. Today tablets and capsules have almost completely replaced them.

s0614 Case 1
s0615 A GP found that a 4-year-old child (weight 16 kg) has a temperature of 39 °C during an acute respiratory infection. The child is vomiting and refuses to take medicine by mouth. The physician wants to prescribe paracetamol.
s0616 1) Which dosage form is most appropriate? 2) Calculate the single dose (the single dose of paracetamol is 10–15 mg/kg). 3) Write a prescription.
s0617 1) Because the child is vomiting, a rectal suppository is appropriate: the drug partly bypasses the liver and is absorbed even when oral intake is impossible. 2) 16 kg × 15 mg/kg = 240 mg ≈ 0.25 g. 3) Prescription: ANSWER TO CASE 1 Rp.: Suppositorium cum Paracetamolo 0.25 D.t.d. N. 10 S. Insert 1 suppository into the rectum when the temperature is above 38.5 °C; not more than 4 times a day, at least 6 hours apart.
s0618 Case 2
s0619 A student wrote the following prescription: Rp.: Neomycinum sulfas 1% 50 / Vaselinum / D.S. for application.
s0620 Find the errors in the prescription and write the correct version.
s0621 Errors: 1) the substance name is in the nominative case — it should be in the genitive (Neomycini sulfatis); 2) the mass should be “50.0”, not “50”; 3) if the base is petrolatum and there is a single active substance, the abbreviated form is sufficient; in the extended form, the amount of active substance should be calculated in grams (0.5) and “ad” and “M.f. unguentum” written; 4) the signatura is vague — where and how many times to apply is not stated. Correct version: ANSWER TO CASE 2 Rp.: Unguenti Neomycini sulfatis 1% — 50.0 D.S. Apply a thin layer to the infected wound surface 2 times a day.
s0622 Case 3
s0623 A 68-year-old patient needs a nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug for knee joint pain caused by gonarthrosis. The history includes a gastric ulcer and arterial hypertension.
s0624 1) Which dosage form is safer and why? 2) Write a prescription.
s0625 1) In an elderly patient with a history of gastric ulcer, a systemic NSAID (tablet) increases the risk of gastrointestinal bleeding and of a rise in blood pressure. A topical gel creates a sufficient concentration in the joint area, very little is absorbed into the blood, and the risk of systemic adverse effects is much lower. 2) Prescription: ANSWER TO CASE 3 Rp.: Geli Diclofenaci 1% — 50.0 D.S. Rub a thin layer into the knee joint area 3–4 times a day; do not use for more than 14 days.

s0681 Converting a percentage into a ratio: **1% = 1:100**, **0.1% = 1:1000**, **0.02% = 1:5000** (furacilin), **0.01% = 1:10,000**. The second number of the ratio = 100 / percentage.

In **officinal solutions** (Cordiamine, Validol, dilute hydrochloric acid) the concentration is **not indicated** — only the name and the amount are written: Rp.: Validoli 5 ml.
s0683 The standard ratios are used only when the amount of raw material is not given in the prescription (S.S. Azizova, department manual). When the amount of raw material is calculated from the single dose (e.g., thermopsis herb 0.6 — 180 ml), the ratio follows from the prescription itself. Infusions of foxglove, thermopsis and ipecacuanha are hardly ever prescribed in current practice — they have been replaced by standardized tablets and syrups.
