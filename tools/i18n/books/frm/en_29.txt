s5951 Adverse effects:
s5952 **bleeding**;
s5953 **heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (HIT)** — immune (antibodies to the heparin-PF4 complex), on days 5–14; platelets fall by more than 50% and **paradoxical thrombosis** (arterial and venous) develops. Treatment: stop all heparins, switch to a non-heparin anticoagulant (argatroban, bivalirudin, fondaparinux);
s5954 with long-term use — **osteoporosis**, alopecia, hyperkalemia (inhibition of aldosterone synthesis), allergic reactions.

s5984 3.3. Indirect anticoagulants — warfarin
s5985 **Mechanism.** Warfarin inhibits **vitamin K epoxide reductase (VKORC1)** → the active (reduced) form of vitamin K is not regenerated → gamma-carboxylation of factors **II, VII, IX and X** in the liver is disrupted → inactive factors are formed.
s5986 It acts **only in vivo** (not in vitro).
s5987 The effect begins **with a delay** — 3–5 days (the existing factors must be broken down; t½ of factor VII is 6 hours, of factor II 60 hours). Therefore, **in acute thrombosis, warfarin is started together with heparin (LMWH)**, and the heparin is stopped when the INR has been in the target range on 2 consecutive days.
s5988 In the first days of treatment, **protein C** (short t½) falls quickly — a temporary **hypercoagulable** state, rarely **warfarin skin necrosis** (in protein C deficiency).
s5989 **Laboratory monitoring: prothrombin time and INR** (international normalized ratio). For most indications the target INR is **2.0–3.0**; with a mechanical mitral valve — 2.5–3.5. **Uses:** atrial fibrillation (especially with mitral stenosis and **mechanical heart valves** — in these cases direct oral anticoagulants are **contraindicated**), deep vein thrombosis and PE (treatment and secondary prevention), antiphospholipid syndrome.
s5990 **Warfarin has very many interactions** (narrow therapeutic range, metabolism via CYP2C9 and CYP3A4, 99% protein binding):
s5991 **enhance its effect (bleeding risk)**: amiodarone, fluconazole and other azoles, metronidazole, many antibiotics (co-trimoxazole, fluoroquinolones, macrolides — they also reduce the intestinal flora), NSAIDs and aspirin (pharmacodynamic), SSRIs, acute alcohol intake, liver diseases;
s5992 **reduce its effect**: rifampicin, carbamazepine, phenytoin, phenobarbital, St John's wort, foods rich in vitamin K (spinach, cabbage, broccoli, green tea — intake should be kept stable).
s5993 Whenever a new drug is prescribed, the INR should be checked frequently.
s5994 **Adverse effects: bleeding** (the most important), skin necrosis, “purple toe” syndrome, **teratogenicity** (in the 1st trimester warfarin embryopathy — hypoplasia of the nose and bones; in the 3rd trimester — fetal bleeding) — **contraindicated in pregnancy**.

s6006 3.4. Direct oral anticoagulants (DOACs)
s6007 **Rivaroxaban, apixaban, edoxaban** — direct inhibitors of **factor Xa**; **dabigatran** — a direct inhibitor of **thrombin** (the prodrug dabigatran etexilate).
s6008 **Advantages (over warfarin):** fixed doses, **no routine laboratory monitoring needed**, rapid onset and offset of action (hours), no food interactions, few drug interactions, **lower risk of intracerebral hemorrhage**.
s6009 Disadvantages and limitations:
s6010 **contraindicated with mechanical heart valves and moderate-to-severe mitral stenosis** (warfarin is used);
s6011 dependent on renal function (especially dabigatran — 80% renal): dose adjustment by GFR, not used in severe renal failure;
s6012 contraindicated in pregnancy and breastfeeding;
s6013 interactions with strong CYP3A4 and P-glycoprotein inhibitors/inducers;
s6014 a missed dose means rapid loss of protection (adherence is important). **Antidotes:** dabigatran — **idarucizumab** (a monoclonal antibody fragment); Xa inhibitors — **andexanet alfa** (if unavailable — prothrombin complex concentrate).
s6015 **Uses:** stroke prevention in nonvalvular atrial fibrillation, deep vein thrombosis and PE (treatment and prevention), prevention after orthopedic surgery; low-dose rivaroxaban — with aspirin in IHD and peripheral artery disease.

s6063 5. Drugs that stop bleeding (hemostatics)
s6064 5.1. Antifibrinolytics
s6065 **Tranexamic acid** and **aminocaproic acid** are lysine analogs; they block the binding of plasminogen to fibrin and inhibit fibrinolysis.
s6066 **Uses of tranexamic acid: massive bleeding in trauma** (within the first 3 hours — reduces mortality), **postpartum hemorrhage**, menorrhagia, dental procedures (in hemophilia), orthopedic and cardiac surgery, overdose of fibrinolytics. Contraindications: active thrombosis, DIC (hypercoagulable phase); seizures at high doses.
s6067 5.2. Coagulants
s6068 **Vitamin K** — **phytomenadione** (K1, natural, fat-soluble; oral and slow intravenous) and **menadione (Vikasol, K3, synthetic)**. Uses: warfarin overdose, vitamin K deficiency (biliary obstruction, obstructive jaundice, prolonged antibiotic therapy, malabsorption), **prevention of hemorrhagic disease of the newborn** (phytomenadione 1 mg intramuscularly after birth). The effect begins in 6–24 hours (the factors must be synthesized). Rapid intravenous injection of phytomenadione causes anaphylactoid reactions. Vikasol may cause hemolysis and jaundice in newborns — phytomenadione is preferred.
s6069 **Prothrombin complex concentrate** (factors II, VII, IX, X) — rapid treatment of severe warfarin-related bleeding.
s6070 **Clotting factor concentrates**: factor VIII (hemophilia A), factor IX (hemophilia B), von Willebrand factor; **desmopressin** increases the release of factor VIII and von Willebrand factor in mild hemophilia A and von Willebrand disease.
s6071 **Local hemostatics**: hemostatic collagen sponge, thrombin, hydrogen peroxide, calcium alginate, epinephrine (topical).
s6072 **Etamsylate** reduces capillary permeability and increases platelet adhesion; widely used in CIS countries, with a limited evidence base.
s6073 6. Drugs affecting hematopoiesis (brief)
s6074 6.1. Iron preparations
s6075 **Iron deficiency anemia** is the most common anemia (pregnant women, women with heavy periods, children, chronic blood loss).
s6076 Figure 14.4
s6077 Absorption, distribution, storage and excretion of iron (teaching material of the TMA Department of Pharmacology)
s6078 Iron is absorbed mainly in **the duodenum and the upper jejunum**, in the divalent (**Fe2+**) form; ascorbic acid improves absorption; antacids, PPIs, tetracyclines, calcium and tea (tannins) reduce it. In the blood it is carried by transferrin and stored as ferritin and hemosiderin.
s6079 **Oral preparations** (first choice): iron(II) sulfate, fumarate, gluconate; iron(III) hydroxide polymaltose complex. The dose is based on **elemental iron**: 100–200 mg a day for adults (or 60–100 mg every other day — better absorption, fewer adverse effects). After hemoglobin normalizes, treatment continues for another **3 months** to replenish stores.
s6080 **Adverse effects**: nausea, epigastric pain, constipation or diarrhea, **black discoloration of the stool** (must be distinguished from bleeding); liquid preparations stain tooth enamel.
s6081 **Parenteral (intravenous) preparations** — ferric carboxymaltose, iron sucrose, ferric derisomaltose: when oral preparations are not tolerated or are ineffective, in malabsorption, chronic kidney disease, heart failure, the 2nd–3rd trimesters of pregnancy. Anaphylactic reactions are possible — give where resuscitation is available.
s6082 6.2. Vitamin B12 and folic acid
s6083 **Cyanocobalamin, hydroxocobalamin (B12)** — in **megaloblastic (pernicious) anemia**, especially with intrinsic factor deficiency (atrophic gastritis, gastric resection), and with long-term metformin and PPI use; only parenterally or orally at high doses. B12 deficiency causes **neurological disorders** (subacute combined degeneration of the spinal cord).
s6084 **Folic acid** — folate deficiency anemia; **when planning pregnancy** (400 mcg/day from 1–3 months before pregnancy and in the 1st trimester) — **prevention of neural tube defects**; during treatment with methotrexate.
s6085 6.3. Stimulants of erythropoiesis and leukopoiesis
s6086 **Erythropoietins** (epoetin alfa and beta, darbepoetin) — anemia of **chronic kidney disease**, chemotherapy-induced anemia. Hemoglobin is not raised above 120 g/l (risk of thrombosis, stroke, AH); iron stores must be adequate.
s6087 **Granulocyte colony-stimulating factors** (filgrastim, pegfilgrastim) — neutropenia after chemotherapy.

s6112 Skill 1. Starting and monitoring warfarin treatment
s6113 Determine the baseline INR, complete blood count, and liver and kidney function.
s6114 Start warfarin at 2.5–5 mg a day (2.5 mg in the elderly, in liver disease and with low body weight).
s6115 In acute thrombosis, give it together with LMWH for at least 5 days, until the INR is above 2.0 on 2 consecutive days.
s6116 Check the INR on days 3–5, then every 2–3 days, and once stable — every 4 weeks.
s6117 Explain to the patient: take the drug at the same time every day, eat foods rich in vitamin K in stable amounts, tell the doctor about new drugs, signs of bleeding (blood in the urine, black stool, bleeding gums, bruising).
