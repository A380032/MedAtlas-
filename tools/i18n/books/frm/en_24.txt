s5140 Drugs acting on the digestive system
s5141 Gastrointestinal diseases are among the most common reasons for visiting a GP: dyspepsia, gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD), gastric and duodenal ulcer, constipation, diarrhea, nausea and vomiting, biliary tract and liver diseases. Proton pump inhibitors are among the most prescribed drugs in the world, but they are often used without reason and for too long. Eradication of Helicobacter pylori has radically changed the treatment of peptic ulcer disease. This topic covers the main groups of digestive system pharmacology based on modern recommendations.

s5150 PPIs block only **actively working** pumps. Therefore they are taken **30–60 minutes before meals** (usually before breakfast) — when food activates the pumps, the drug is at its peak blood concentration. The full effect develops in 3–5 days; a PPI is not a quick “switch-off” (symptomatic, as-needed) drug.
s5151 Antacids reduce the absorption of many drugs (**tetracyclines, fluoroquinolones**, iron preparations, digoxin, levothyroxine, bisphosphonates). Other drugs are taken **at least 2 hours before or 4 hours after** an antacid.
s5152 The effectiveness of eradication is checked **at least 4 weeks** after it (and 2 weeks after stopping the PPI) with a **urea breath test** or a stool antigen test. A serological test (antibodies) is unsuitable for assessing the result of eradication — antibodies persist for months.
s5153 The drug according to the cause of vomiting: **“Chemo — Ondansetron, Sea — Dimenhydrinate and scopolamine, Slow stomach — Metoclopramide/domperidone, Delayed chemo — Aprepitant”**.
s5154 Laxatives are **contraindicated** in **intestinal obstruction**, an acute abdomen (suspected appendicitis), and abdominal pain of unknown cause. In treating constipation, diet (fiber), fluids and physical activity come first; of the drugs — bulk-forming and osmotic laxatives. Regular long-term use of contact (stimulant) laxatives is not recommended.
s5155 Choleretic drugs (especially cholekinetics) are contraindicated in **gallstone disease** — a stone may become lodged in the bile duct and lead to obstructive jaundice and pancreatitis. They are also not used in acute cholecystitis, acute hepatitis and pancreatitis. The effectiveness of most herbal choleretics has not been proven in modern studies.
s5156 Hepatoprotectors are very widely prescribed in CIS countries, but the effectiveness of most of them (silymarin, essential phospholipids) **has not been proven** in high-quality clinical trials and they are not included in international recommendations. In liver diseases the main focus is on **treating the cause**: antiviral therapy in viral hepatitis, abstinence from alcohol in alcoholic disease, weight loss in fatty liver disease, **N-acetylcysteine** in paracetamol poisoning.
s5157 The diagnosis of **“dysbacteriosis”** and prescribing probiotics to treat it on the basis of stool analysis is widespread in CIS countries, but international medicine does not recognize it as a separate disease.
