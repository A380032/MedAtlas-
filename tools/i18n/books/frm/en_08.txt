s1639 4.4. Systemic toxicity of local anesthetics
s1640 When the anesthetic accidentally enters a **vessel** or the dose is exceeded, a systemic toxic effect develops (LAST — local anesthetic systemic toxicity).
s1641 **CNS signs** (first): numbness of the lips and tongue, a metallic taste in the mouth, tinnitus, dizziness, restlessness, speech disturbance, muscle twitching → **seizures** → CNS depression, coma, respiratory arrest.
s1642 **Cardiovascular signs:** bradycardia, AV block, hypotension, ventricular arrhythmias, **cardiac arrest** (typical of bupivacaine — it dissociates from sodium channels very slowly).
s1643 **Allergic reactions** are typical mainly of esters (PABA metabolite). Allergy to amides is very rare; it is usually due to a preservative (methylparaben) or sulfites (in epinephrine-containing solutions).
s1644 4.5. Astringents
s1645 **Astringents** **partly and reversibly denature** proteins on the surface of a mucous membrane or wound and form a thin protective film. As a result, sensory nerve endings are protected, pain decreases, the vessels constrict, and exudation and inflammation decrease.
s1646 **Organic:** tannin (5–10% solution — for rinsing the mouth, in burns), oak bark decoction, infusions of chamomile, sage and St John's wort, blackcurrant berries.
s1647 **Inorganic (metal salts):** basic bismuth nitrate, bismuth tripotassium dicitrate (De-Nol — for gastric ulcer, topic 12), zinc sulfate (eye drops), zinc oxide, aluminum potassium sulfate (alum), lead acetate, silver nitrate (astringent at low concentration, caustic at high concentration).
s1648 The effect of metal salts depends on concentration: at **low** concentration — astringent, at **moderate** — irritant, at **high** — caustic (necrosis). For example, silver nitrate 0.05–0.1% is astringent, 2–5% is caustic (excess granulation tissue, warts).
s1649 4.6. Coating agents
s1650 **Coating agents** form colloidal solutions in water, cover the mucous membrane and protect it from irritation. They have no resorptive effect.
s1651 starch mucilage, flaxseed mucilage, marshmallow root mucilage, salep;
s1652 aluminum hydroxide, magnesium trisilicate, Almagel (topic 12). Uses: gastritis, gastric ulcer, enteritis, colitis; together with irritant drugs (chloral hydrate) — to soften their local effect; in poisoning — to reduce absorption.

s1671 4.8. Irritants
s1672 **Irritants** stimulate sensory nerve endings in the skin and mucous membranes. Their effect is mainly **reflex**:
s1673 **“Distracting” effect** — excitation from the skin competes in the spinal cord with pain impulses coming from internal organs (**gate control theory**), thereby reducing pain; endorphins and enkephalins are released.
s1674 **Trophic effect** — blood circulation and trophism improve in the internal organs belonging to the same segment as the irritated skin area (according to the **Zakharyin–Head zones**).
s1675 **Reflex stimulating effect** — ammonia vapor stimulates the trigeminal nerve endings in the nasal mucosa and reflexly stimulates the respiratory and vasomotor centers (in fainting).

s1710 5.2. Practical application of clinical pharmacokinetics
s1711 **Knowing the time to reach steady state has practical importance.** The t½ of digoxin is ≈ 36–48 hours — at an ordinary dose, steady state is reached after 7–10 days; if a rapid effect is needed, a **loading dose** is given. The t½ of amiodarone is several weeks, so treatment is started with a loading dose, and its effect persists for months after it is stopped. In contrast, drugs with a short t½ (adenosine — less than 10 seconds) are used only as a bolus, for an immediate effect.
s1712 Skill 1. Algorithm for calculating the maximum volume of a local anesthetic
s1713 Determine the patient's weight (kg).
s1714 Choose the maximum dose (mg/kg) of the anesthetic for the solution without or with epinephrine.
s1715 Maximum dose (mg) = weight × mg/kg (but not exceeding the absolute maximum dose).
s1716 Convert the solution concentration to mg/ml (percentage × 10).
s1717 Maximum volume (ml) = maximum dose (mg) / concentration (mg/ml). **Example:** a 60 kg patient, 1% lidocaine without epinephrine. 60 × 4.5 = 270 mg; 1% = 10 mg/ml; 270 / 10 = **27 ml**.
s1718 Skill 2. Calculating the time to reach steady-state concentration
s1719 Determine the t½ of the drug.
s1720 Time to reach Css ≈ 4–5 × t½.
s1721 Time for complete elimination after the drug is stopped ≈ 4–5 × t½. **Example:** t½ = 6 hours → Css after 24–30 hours.

s1832 A 55-year-old patient (70 kg) came to outpatient surgery to have a lipoma removed from the forearm. The surgeon wants to use 1% lidocaine with epinephrine for infiltration anesthesia. After 25 ml had been injected during the procedure, the patient reported numbness of the tongue, a metallic taste in the mouth and ringing in the ears, and then his hands began to tremble.
s1833 1) What complication has developed? 2) What is the maximum dose for this patient? Has the dose been exceeded? 3) What emergency measures are needed?
s1834 1) Systemic toxicity of the local anesthetic (early CNS signs) — the solution probably partly entered a vessel. 2) The maximum dose of lidocaine with epinephrine is 7 mg/kg → 70 × 7 = 490 mg ≈ 49 ml of 1% solution. The 250 mg injected did not exceed the maximum dose, so the main cause is intravascular injection (injection without an aspiration test). 3) Stop the injection, call for help, give 100% oxygen, establish intravenous access; if seizures begin — midazolam or diazepam; if the condition worsens — 20% lipid emulsion 1.5 ml/kg bolus and infusion; ECG and BP monitoring. Prevention: an aspiration test before each injection, injecting the solution slowly in small portions.
s1835 A 32-year-old woman is taking rifampicin for tuberculosis. She also takes a combined oral contraceptive. Two months after the start of treatment, pregnancy was diagnosed.
s1836 1) What is the pharmacological cause of the pregnancy? 2) What measures should have been taken?
s1837 1) Rifampicin is a strong inducer of hepatic microsomal enzymes (CYP3A4); it accelerated the metabolism of estrogens and progestins and lowered their blood concentrations — the contraceptive became ineffective. 2) Non-hormonal contraception (intrauterine device, condom) should have been recommended during treatment with rifampicin and for at least 4 weeks after it. This example shows the need to check for pharmacokinetic interactions every time a prescription is written.
