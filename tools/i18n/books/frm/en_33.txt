s6800 Antiseptic and disinfectant agents. Synthetic antibacterial agents
s6801 The fight against infections is waged in two directions: destroying microorganisms in the environment and on the skin surface (**antisepsis and disinfection**) and treating infection inside the body (**chemotherapy**). Proper hand disinfection is considered the most effective measure for preventing hospital-acquired infections. Synthetic antibacterial agents (fluoroquinolones, sulfonamides, nitrofurans, nitroimidazoles) are widely used to treat urinary tract, intestinal and anaerobic infections. Antibiotic **resistance** is a global health problem of the 21st century; that is why knowing the principles of rational use of antimicrobials is mandatory for every doctor.

s6809 The main difference between antiseptic and chemotherapeutic agents is **selective action (selectivity)**. Antiseptics are protoplasmic poisons: they act on microbial and human cells **nonselectively**, so they are used only on surfaces. Chemotherapeutic agents act on targets specific to microbes and cause little harm to the macroorganism — they are used systemically.
s6810 Common incorrect practices: 1) applying iodine and brilliant green **into an open wound** — this damages tissue and slows healing (only around the wound); 2) using boric acid in children — it is absorbed through the skin and may cause severe poisoning; 3) pouring alcohol onto an open wound. The most important thing in cleaning minor wounds is **irrigation with plenty of clean water or saline**.
s6811 Laboratory conclusions: the effect of antiseptics depends on **concentration and contact time**; **organic matter** (blood, pus, protein) reduces the activity of most antiseptics — so a wound must first be cleaned mechanically.
s6812 **Antimicrobial resistance** is a global threat that causes millions of deaths a year. Factors that worsen it: unjustified use of antibiotics in viral infections (ARVI, influenza), over-the-counter sales, wrong dose and duration, antibiotics in animal farming. The WHO **AWaRe** classification divides antibiotics into “Access” (can be widely used), “Watch” (use with caution) and “Reserve” groups.
s6813 Sources of PABA reduce the effect of sulfonamides: **pus and necrotic tissue** (much PABA) and drugs that form PABA — **procaine (Novocaine), benzocaine (Anesthesin)**. That is why sulfonamides are ineffective when used together with procaine or in a purulent wound.
s6814 Because of **serious adverse effects of fluoroquinolones** (FDA and EMA warnings), they are **not recommended as first choice** in uncomplicated cystitis, acute bronchitis and mild sinusitis: **tendinitis and tendon (Achilles) rupture** — especially in people over 60 and together with **glucocorticoids**; peripheral neuropathy (may be irreversible), CNS disorders (dizziness, insomnia, psychosis, **seizures** — especially with NSAIDs); **QT prolongation** and arrhythmias; aortic aneurysm and dissection; dysglycemia (hypo- and hyperglycemia), photosensitivity, C. difficile colitis; contraindicated **in children and adolescents** (damage to growing joint cartilage) and **in pregnancy and breastfeeding** (except for special indications).
s6815 The spectrum of metronidazole — **“GET GAP on the Metro”**: **G**iardia, **E**ntamoeba, **T**richomonas, **G**ardnerella, **A**naerobes, **P** — H. pylori. In short: **“Anaerobes and protozoa — metronidazole”**.
s6816 1. Basic concepts
s6817 **Antisepsis** — destroying microorganisms on the skin, mucous membranes and wound surfaces or stopping their multiplication.
s6818 **Disinfection** — destroying pathogenic microorganisms on objects in the environment (instruments, rooms, waste, clothing).
s6819 **Sterilization** — complete destruction of all microorganisms, including spores (autoclave, dry heat, ethylene oxide, glutaraldehyde).
s6820 **Chemotherapeutic agents** — agents that act selectively inside the body on microorganisms, parasites or tumor cells.
s6821 Types of antimicrobial action: **bacteriostatic** (stops multiplication) and **bactericidal** (kills). The type of action depends on concentration, the type of microbe and the conditions of the environment.
s6822 2. Antiseptic and disinfectant agents
s6823 2.1. Requirements for antiseptics and factors affecting their activity
s6824 An ideal antiseptic should be broad-spectrum, fast-acting, not lose its activity in the presence of organic matter (blood, pus), not damage tissue, not be absorbed, not cause allergy, and be inexpensive and stable. No such substance exists — each group has its own advantages and disadvantages.
s6825 Factors affecting activity: **concentration** and contact time, temperature, the pH of the environment, **organic matter** (blood, pus and proteins inactivate most antiseptics), the type of microbe (spores, mycobacteria and non-enveloped viruses are the most resistant).

s6871 2.3. Features of the main antiseptics
s6872 **Ethyl alcohol** — the **70%** concentration is the most effective: 96% alcohol rapidly coagulates the proteins on the cell surface, forms a protective layer and does not penetrate inside; at 70%, the presence of water improves denaturation. It acts on bacteria, fungi and enveloped viruses; **it does not act on spores**. It is not used on open wounds (pain, tissue necrosis). Alcohol-based antiseptics with added glycerin do not dry the skin of the hands.
s6873 **Iodine preparations.** Alcoholic iodine solution — on the skin and around wounds; irritating, allergenic, and with long-term use — **iodism** (rhinitis, rash, increased salivation) and thyroid dysfunction. **Povidone-iodine** (an iodophor — a complex of iodine with polyvinylpyrrolidone) releases iodine slowly, irritates little, has a broad spectrum (including spores) and is widely used for surgical field antisepsis. It is not applied to large areas in newborns and pregnant women (iodine absorption — hypothyroidism).
s6874 **Chlorhexidine** — a potent antiseptic with a long (residual) effect; it “sticks” well to the skin. The **chlorhexidine + alcohol** combination is **the most effective** for antisepsis of the surgical field and the central venous catheter site. Its activity decreases with organic matter and soap. It must not come into contact with the eye, the middle ear or the meninges (it damages them); with prolonged use as a mouthwash it stains the teeth.
s6875 **Hydrogen peroxide 3%** — under the action of tissue catalase it releases oxygen, forms **foam** and cleans the wound mechanically; its antiseptic effect is weak and short. It helps stop capillary bleeding. Injecting it into deep closed cavities is dangerous (gas embolism).
s6876 **Potassium permanganate** — a strong oxidizing agent; depending on the concentration it is antiseptic (0.01–0.1%), astringent and caustic (2–5%). In some poisonings (alkaloids, phosphorus) a 0.02–0.1% solution is used for gastric lavage; the **crystals** must be completely dissolved — they burn the mucosa.
s6877 **Brilliant green 1–2%** — active against gram-positive bacteria (staphylococci); skin wounds, pyoderma, the umbilical wound. **Furacilin 0.02%** (1:5000) — irrigation of wounds and cavities; weak activity, many microorganisms are resistant.
s6878 2.4. Hand disinfection
s6879 According to the WHO, a large share of hospital-acquired infections is transmitted through the hands of health workers. **The 5 moments for hand hygiene** (WHO): 1) before touching a patient; 2) before an aseptic procedure; 3) after contact with body fluids; 4) after touching a patient; 5) after touching the patient's surroundings. If the hands are not visibly soiled, an **alcohol-based hand rub** (3–5 ml, rubbed for 20–30 seconds) is more effective than washing with soap; if they are visibly soiled, or after working with C. difficile or norovirus — soap and water (alcohol does not act on spores).
s6880 2.5. Laboratory class: studying the mechanism of action of antiseptics
s6881 This section corresponds to the 6th-semester class “Laboratory study of the mechanism of action of antiseptic agents”.
s6882 **Experiment 1 (protein denaturation):** solutions of ethyl alcohol (70% and 96%), phenol, formaldehyde, silver nitrate and copper sulfate are added to egg white solution in test tubes — **precipitation (coagulation)** of the protein is observed. Conclusion: most antiseptics denature the proteins of the microbial cell.
s6883 **Experiment 2 (effect of oxidizing agents):** when hydrogen peroxide is added to blood or a piece of meat, **foam** (oxygen) forms under the action of catalase; potassium permanganate solution becomes colorless on contact with organic matter (manganese(IV) oxide precipitates). Conclusion: the action of oxidizing agents is short and ends quickly in an organic environment.
s6884 **Experiment 3 (effect of organic matter and concentration):** antiseptics of different concentrations are applied to a microbial culture (paper disk method) and the zones of growth inhibition are measured; when serum is added, the zones shrink.

s6903 3.3. Basic principles of antimicrobial therapy
s6904 **Establishing the indication** — when a bacterial infection is likely (antibiotics do not act on viral infections).
s6905 **Identifying the pathogen** — where possible, take cultures before the antibiotic; first **empirical** therapy (according to the likely pathogens and local resistance), then switching to **targeted** therapy according to the results (de-escalation).
s6906 **Penetration of the drug into the site of infection** (urine, CNS, bone, prostate).
s6907 **Patient factors**: age, pregnancy, renal and hepatic function, allergy history, interactions.
s6908 **Optimal dose, route and duration** — the shortest effective course possible.
s6909 **Assessing effectiveness at 48–72 hours**.
s6910 Combinations only when indicated (tuberculosis, severe sepsis, H. pylori, prevention of resistance).
