s3158 General anesthetics. Neuroleptics. Anxiolytics
s3159 This topic brings together three important groups that depress the central nervous system (CNS). **General anesthetics** made surgery painless and safe. **Neuroleptics (antipsychotics)** revolutionized the treatment of schizophrenia and other psychoses. **Anxiolytics (tranquilizers)** are among the most frequently prescribed drugs, but they carry a high risk of dependence. The GP must recognize the adverse effects of these drugs (neuroleptic malignant syndrome, extrapyramidal disorders, benzodiazepine dependence) and provide help in poisoning.

s3167 Components of modern general anesthesia: **hypnosis** (loss of consciousness), **analgesia**, **amnesia**, **muscle relaxation** and **suppression of unwanted autonomic reflexes**. No single drug can safely provide all of these, so **combined (balanced) anesthesia** is used.
s3168 The therapeutic range of anesthesia (the difference between the surgical stage and stage IV) is relatively narrow for inhalational anesthetics. The excitement stage is dangerous (aspiration of vomit, arrhythmias), so in modern anesthesia, anesthesia is started with fast-acting intravenous agents (**induction**), and stage II is hardly seen.
s3169 **“Sevo is a child's friend”**: sevoflurane is pleasant and non-irritating, so it is the main agent for mask induction in children. **“Halothane is the enemy of the heart and liver”**: arrhythmias and hepatitis.
s3170 Main criteria of the depth of anesthesia in the laboratory: the **righting reflex** (consciousness), **response to pain**, the **corneal reflex**, **muscle tone**, and **respiratory rate and pattern**. A sharp slowing of breathing is a sign of the danger of passing into stage IV.
s3171 The 4 dopamine pathways — **“Limbic — Cortex — Substantia nigra — Pituitary”**: meso**limbic** (psychosis decreases), meso**cortical** (negative symptoms), nigrostriatal — **substantia nigra** (movement disorders), tuberoinfundibular — **pituitary** (prolactin increases). The therapeutic effect is only in the first; the others are a source of adverse effects.
s3172 **Neuroleptic malignant syndrome (NMS)** — a rare but life-threatening complication (mortality up to 10%): **high fever**, **“lead-pipe” muscle rigidity**, impaired consciousness, autonomic instability (tachycardia, fluctuating BP, sweating), a **sharp rise in blood creatine kinase**, leukocytosis, rhabdomyolysis and renal failure. **Treatment:** stop the neuroleptic immediately, intensive care, cooling, fluids, **dantrolene** and/or **bromocriptine** (a dopamine agonist), benzodiazepines.
s3173 **Benzodiazepines** increase the **frequency** of chloride channel opening, while **barbiturates** increase its **duration**. At large doses, barbiturates open the channel even without GABA — that is why their therapeutic range is narrow and they can paralyze the respiratory center.
s3174 Benzodiazepines are prescribed in **short courses** (not more than 2–4 weeks). In chronic anxiety disorders, the first choice is **antidepressants** (SSRIs) and psychotherapy. When long-term benzodiazepines are stopped, the dose must be reduced gradually over several weeks to months. **Combination with opioids** is one of the main causes of overdose deaths.
