s2663 A 19-year-old man was given sevoflurane anesthesia and suxamethonium for intubation during knee arthroscopy. After 20 minutes, rigidity of the masticatory muscles, tachycardia of 150/min, a sharp rise in end-tidal CO~2~ and a rise in temperature to 39.5 °C were observed.
s2664 1) Your diagnosis? 2) Pathogenesis? 3) Emergency treatment.
s2665 1) Malignant hyperthermia. 2) Because of a hereditary (RYR1) defect, suxamethonium and the inhalational anesthetic triggered uncontrolled release of calcium from the sarcoplasmic reticulum in skeletal muscle — hypermetabolism, muscle rigidity, excessive production of CO~2~ and heat, rhabdomyolysis, hyperkalemia. 3) Stop sevoflurane immediately, hyperventilate with 100% oxygen, dantrolene 2.5 mg/kg intravenously (repeated), physical cooling, correction of acidosis and hyperkalemia, support of diuresis; observation in intensive care; warn the patient and his family about future anesthesia.
s2666 A 52-year-old man has smoked 1 pack of cigarettes a day for 30 years. He had a myocardial infarction two years ago. He wants to stop smoking, but previous attempts have failed (irritability, insomnia, strong craving to smoke).
s2667 1) What pharmacotherapy would you offer? 2) What causes the withdrawal symptoms?
s2668 1) Together with counseling — varenicline (12 weeks) or cytisine (25 days), or combined nicotine replacement therapy (patch + gum). In stable ischemic heart disease, nicotine replacement therapy is much safer than smoking (no carbon monoxide or other harmful substances from smoke). 2) Chronic exposure to nicotine changes the number and sensitivity of N-cholinergic receptors in the CNS, and dopamine release in the mesolimbic system becomes nicotine-dependent; when nicotine is stopped, the dopamine deficit causes irritability, depression, insomnia and strong craving. Partial agonists stimulate the receptors moderately and soften these symptoms.

s2710 Remember the beta receptor numbers by the number of organs: **beta-1 — 1 heart**, **beta-2 — 2 lungs**. Alpha-1 — “**constriction**” (vessels, sphincters), alpha-2 — “**brake**” (self-limitation, central sympathetic tone).
s2711 **Dale's phenomenon (vasomotor reversal).** If epinephrine is given against the background of an alpha-blocker, BP **does not rise but falls**: the alpha receptors are blocked and only beta-2-mediated vasodilation remains. Therefore epinephrine is not given for hypotension caused by alpha-blockers or neuroleptics (chlorpromazine has alpha-blocking properties) — **norepinephrine** or phenylephrine is chosen.
s2712 **“Renal-dose dopamine”** (1–3 mcg/kg/min) was formerly widely prescribed to prevent acute renal failure. Clinical studies have shown that it does not protect the kidneys or reduce mortality; it is now **not recommended** for this purpose. In septic shock, dopamine causes more arrhythmias than norepinephrine, so norepinephrine is preferred.
s2713 Using decongestants for **more than 5–7 days** leads to **tachyphylaxis** and **“rebound”** swelling — **drug-induced rhinitis** develops (naphazoline dependence). In young children, imidazolines are absorbed into the blood and depress the CNS: drowsiness, **bradycardia, hypothermia, coma** (naphazoline poisoning). Only pediatric concentrations (xylometazoline 0.05%) are used in children; naphazoline 0.1% is contraindicated in children.
s2714 **The three beta-blockers that reduce mortality in heart failure — “BMC”**: **B**isoprolol, **M**etoprolol succinate, **C**arvedilol (+ nebivolol in the elderly). Cardioselective ones run alphabetically **“A–M”**: **A**tenolol, **B**isoprolol, **B**etaxolol, **E**smolol, **M**etoprolol (all begin with letters from A to M) + nebivolol.
s2715 **A beta-blocker must not be stopped abruptly** — “rebound” syndrome: the number of receptors has increased (up-regulation), and when the drug is stopped, sensitivity to catecholamines rises sharply — tachycardia, angina attacks, **myocardial infarction**, hypertensive crisis. The dose is reduced gradually over 1–2 weeks.
s2716 **Beta-blocker poisoning** (bradycardia, hypotension, cardiogenic shock, hypoglycemia, seizures): atropine (often of little effect), **glucagon** (activates adenylate cyclase independently of beta receptors), high-dose insulin + glucose, catecholamines (epinephrine, isoprenaline), cardiac pacing; in propranolol poisoning (wide QRS) — sodium bicarbonate.
s2717 Older textbooks gave reserpine and Octadine (guanethidine) as the main drugs for hypertension. In modern recommendations (topic 11), they are **not used**: they have many adverse effects, and a reduction in cardiovascular complications has not been proven.
s2718 1. The adrenergic synapse
s2719 1.1. Synthesis, release and inactivation of catecholamines
s2720 **Synthesis** (in the nerve ending): **tyrosine** → (tyrosine hydroxylase — the slowest, rate-limiting step) → **DOPA** → (DOPA decarboxylase) → **dopamine** → (dopamine beta-hydroxylase in the vesicles) → **norepinephrine**. In the adrenal medulla, norepinephrine is converted into **epinephrine** by phenylethanolamine N-methyltransferase.
s2721 **Storage:** norepinephrine is stored in vesicles together with ATP and chromogranins (VMAT carrier; **reserpine** blocks it).
s2722 **Release:** Ca^2+^-dependent exocytosis when a nerve impulse arrives.
s2723 **Action on receptors:** postsynaptic alpha and beta receptors, presynaptic alpha-2 (negative feedback — reduces release) and beta-2 (enhances release) receptors.
s2724 **Inactivation:** mainly (70–90%) by **neuronal reuptake** (NET, “uptake 1”; blocked by cocaine and tricyclic antidepressants); the rest — breakdown by the enzymes mitochondrial **MAO** (in the nerve ending) and **COMT** (in the effector cell and the liver). Metabolites — vanillylmandelic acid and metanephrines (measured in the diagnosis of pheochromocytoma).

s2787 2.1. Epinephrine (adrenaline)
s2788 **Epinephrine** — a hormone of the adrenal medulla; it directly stimulates all **alpha and beta** receptors. It is destroyed in the gastrointestinal tract — it is used only parenterally, by inhalation and topically. Its action is short (intravenously — 5 minutes, subcutaneously — 30 minutes) because of reuptake and breakdown by COMT/MAO.

s2814 2.2. Norepinephrine (noradrenaline)
s2815 **Norepinephrine** stimulates alpha-1, alpha-2 and beta-1 receptors; **its effect on beta-2 is very weak**. As a result, all vessels constrict and **systolic and diastolic BP rise**. Although it directly stimulates beta-1 receptors in the heart, **reflex bradycardia** develops through the baroreceptors.
s2816 **Uses: septic shock** (the vasopressor of first choice), other types of shock and severe hypotension (after adequate fluid replacement); only **by intravenous drip**, under BP monitoring.
s2817 **Complication:** if the solution leaks out of the vessel — **tissue necrosis**. Treatment: infiltration of the area with **phentolamine** (an alpha-blocker) solution.
