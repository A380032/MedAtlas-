s2298 A 45-year-old farmer was brought to the emergency department 2 hours after spraying insecticide in a cotton field. Complaints: blurred vision, shortness of breath, nausea. s2301 A 4-year-old child ate jimsonweed seeds growing in the village. An hour later the child is restless and hallucinating; the skin is dry and red, temperature 39.2 °C, the pupils are dilated and unreactive to light, HR 160/min, and he has not urinated for 6 hours.

s2346 Drugs acting on N-cholinergic receptors
s2347 N-cholinergic receptors play a decisive role in two places: **in the autonomic ganglia** (NN) and **at the neuromuscular junction of skeletal muscles** (NM). The drugs acting on these receptors are the main drugs of modern anesthesiology (muscle relaxants), smoking cessation drugs (cytisine, varenicline) and ganglionic blockers of historical importance. Working with muscle relaxants requires full control of breathing and circulation, so knowing their mechanism and complications is mandatory for every physician.

s2356 Older textbooks recommended cytisine (Cytiton) and lobeline as **reflex respiratory analeptics** (carbon monoxide poisoning, asphyxia). In modern practice, respiratory failure is treated with oxygen and mechanical ventilation; analeptics are considered ineffective and dangerous. The main current importance of cytisine is **smoking cessation**.
s2357 Ganglionic blockers **“switch off the sympathetic and parasympathetic systems at once”**: in the vessels — loss of sympathetic tone (hypotension); in most other organs — loss of parasympathetic tone (atropine-like effects). That is why they have many adverse effects.
s2358 After a ganglionic blocker has been given, the patient must lie **horizontally for 2 hours**; if he gets up suddenly, orthostatic collapse develops. Today ganglionic blockers are hardly used to treat hypertension — they have been replaced by modern antihypertensive drugs with fewer adverse effects (topic 11).
s2359 **Hofmann elimination — “ATRA and CIS”**: **atra**curium and **cis**atracurium break down spontaneously in the blood independently of organ function — the agents of choice in renal and hepatic failure.
s2360 **Neostigmine does not reverse a suxamethonium block but, on the contrary, enhances it** — because it accumulates ACh and also inhibits pseudocholinesterase. There is no specific antagonist of a depolarizing block; its effect wears off quickly on its own.
s2361 **Treatment of malignant hyperthermia:** immediately stop all triggering agents (inhalational anesthetics, suxamethonium), hyperventilation with 100% oxygen, **dantrolene** intravenously (2.5 mg/kg, repeated up to 10 mg/kg; blocks calcium release from the sarcoplasmic reticulum), physical cooling, correction of acidosis and hyperkalemia.
s2362 1. N-cholinergic receptors
s2363 The N-cholinergic receptor is a **ligand-gated ion channel** (ionotropic receptor). It consists of five subunits (alpha, beta, gamma or epsilon, delta), with a channel in the center that conducts Na+ and K+ ions. When two molecules of acetylcholine bind to **the two alpha subunits**, the channel opens and the membrane depolarizes.

s2387 2.1. Nicotine
s2388 **Nicotine** — an alkaloid of tobacco (Nicotiana tabacum), a liquid tertiary amine; it is absorbed very quickly from the skin, mucous membranes and lungs. As a drug, it is used only in **nicotine replacement therapy** (smoking cessation).
s2389 **Biphasic action:** at small doses it **stimulates** N-cholinergic receptors, at large doses it **blocks** them through prolonged depolarization.

s2400 **The harm of smoking** is due mainly not to nicotine but to **more than 4000** substances in tobacco smoke (tars, benzpyrene and other carcinogens, carbon monoxide, cyanides): lung cancer, COPD, atherosclerosis, myocardial infarction, stroke, impaired fetal development.
s2401 **Acute nicotine poisoning** (when children swallow cigarettes or nicotine liquid, e-cigarette liquid): nausea, vomiting, salivation, abdominal pain, diarrhea, dizziness, tachycardia, then bradycardia and hypotension, seizures, **paralysis of the respiratory muscles**. Treatment: activated charcoal, respiratory support, diazepam for seizures, atropine for M signs.
s2402 2.2. Cytisine and lobeline
s2403 **Cytisine** — an alkaloid of thermopsis and laburnum (Cytisus laburnum, the “golden rain” shrub); it stimulates N-cholinergic receptors, but more weakly and more selectively than nicotine. **Tabex** tablets — for smoking cessation: by partly occupying nicotinic receptors, it softens withdrawal symptoms and reduces the “pleasure” obtained from smoking.
s2404 **Lobeline** — an alkaloid of lobelia. It was formerly used as a reflex respiratory analeptic; it is hardly used now.

s2422 3. Ganglionic blockers
s2423 **Ganglionic blockers** block the NN-cholinergic receptors in the autonomic ganglia and simultaneously stop impulse transmission in **sympathetic and parasympathetic** ganglia; they also reduce epinephrine release from the adrenal medulla.
