s7609 1.4. Drugs for treating drug-resistant tuberculosis

s7629 **Hepatotoxicity of antituberculosis drugs** is the most common reason for stopping treatment. Liver enzymes are measured before treatment; in risk groups (alcoholism, hepatitis B and C, HIV, old age, pregnancy) they are monitored regularly. If symptoms appear (nausea, vomiting, loss of appetite, jaundice, right upper quadrant pain) or transaminases rise more than 3-fold with symptoms (more than 5-fold without symptoms), **all hepatotoxic drugs are stopped**, and once the enzymes normalize they are restarted one by one.
s7630 2. Antiviral drugs
s7631 2.1. Stages of viral replication and drug targets
s7632 Viruses are obligate intracellular parasites — they use the mechanisms of the host cell. That is why it is difficult to create drugs that act selectively on viruses; drugs target **virus-specific enzymes and processes**.

s7650 2.2. Anti-influenza drugs
s7651 **Neuraminidase inhibitors** — **oseltamivir** (oral), zanamivir (inhaled): by inhibiting neuraminidase they stop the release of new virions from the infected cell and their spread. They act on influenza A and B. Effective when started **within 48 hours** of symptom onset — they shorten the illness by ≈ 1 day and reduce complications and hospitalizations **in risk groups** (the elderly, pregnant women, children under 2, chronic diseases, immunosuppression); in severe and hospitalized patients at any time. Prophylaxis — after contact. Adverse effects: nausea, vomiting; rarely neuropsychiatric disorders in children. Zanamivir — bronchospasm in asthma and COPD.
s7652 **Baloxavir marboxil** inhibits the cap-dependent endonuclease of the viral polymerase; a single dose.
s7653 **Amantadine, rimantadine** — influenza A only; almost all modern strains are **resistant** — not recommended (amantadine is used in Parkinson's disease).
s7654 The most effective prevention of influenza is **annual vaccination**, especially for risk groups and health workers. Antiviral drugs do not replace vaccination.
s7655 2.3. Drugs against herpesviruses
s7656 **Acyclovir** is a guanosine analog and a **prodrug**: viral **thymidine kinase** (only in the infected cell) converts it into the monophosphate, and then cellular kinases phosphorylate it to the triphosphate. Acyclovir triphosphate inhibits the viral **DNA polymerase** and, by being incorporated into the DNA chain, stops its elongation. It has almost no effect on uninfected cells — **high selectivity**, low toxicity.

s7675 2.4. Drugs against viral hepatitis
s7676 **Hepatitis B** (chronic): **tenofovir** (tenofovir disoproxil fumarate or alafenamide) and **entecavir** — nucleoside/nucleotide analogs; highly effective, with very little resistance; usually taken **long term** (often for life); they suppress the virus and reduce the risk of cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma. Tenofovir is also used **in pregnancy** (to prevent mother-to-child transmission). Pegylated interferon alfa — a finite course in selected patients. Abrupt withdrawal — a severe flare of hepatitis.
s7677 **Hepatitis C**: **direct-acting antivirals (DAAs)** — **sofosbuvir** (an NS5B polymerase inhibitor) + **velpatasvir** or daclatasvir (NS5A inhibitors), glecaprevir/pibrentasvir; an **8–12-week** course eliminates the virus (cure) in **more than 95%** of patients. Pangenotypic regimens do not require genotyping. Screening for hepatitis B before treatment is mandatory (risk of HBV reactivation); sofosbuvir with amiodarone — severe bradycardia.
s7678 2.5. Drugs against HIV infection (antiretroviral therapy)
s7679 HIV is a retrovirus; its RNA is converted into DNA by **reverse transcriptase** and integrated into the host genome by **integrase**; new viral proteins mature with the help of **protease**.

s7695 Special situations
s7696 **Prevention: post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP)** — a 28-day course of ART **within 72 hours** (ideally within 2 hours) after a risky contact (including a needlestick injury in a health worker); **pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP)** — tenofovir/emtricitabine in high-risk groups; prevention of mother-to-child transmission — ART in pregnancy.
s7697 2.6. Drugs against COVID-19
s7698 **Nirmatrelvir/ritonavir** — an inhibitor of the main protease (Mpro) of SARS-CoV-2; ritonavir slows its metabolism. In patients **at high risk** of severe disease, **within 5 days** of symptom onset. **Ritonavir is a strong CYP3A4 inhibitor**: dangerous interactions with statins, antiarrhythmics, DOACs and some anticonvulsants.
s7699 **Remdesivir** — an RNA polymerase inhibitor; intravenous; in hospitalized patients and in high-risk outpatients.
s7700 **Molnupiravir** — introduces mutations into the viral RNA; when there are no other options; contraindicated in pregnancy.
s7701 Anti-inflammatory treatment in severe COVID-19 — **dexamethasone** (reduces mortality in patients requiring oxygen), tocilizumab, baricitinib; anticoagulants (thrombosis prevention).
s7702 2.7. Interferons
s7703 **Interferons** are proteins produced by cells in response to a virus; they induce an antiviral state in cells (enzymes that break down viral RNA, inhibition of protein synthesis) and enhance the immune response.
s7704 **Interferon alfa** (pegylated) — chronic hepatitis B (selected patients), some tumors (hairy cell leukemia, melanoma); **interferon beta** — multiple sclerosis; **interferon gamma** — chronic granulomatous disease.
s7705 **Adverse effects** (parenteral): flu-like syndrome (fever, myalgia), depression and suicidal thoughts, cytopenias, autoimmune thyroid disease, fatigue.
