s5202 2. Drugs affecting gastric secretion
s5203 2.1. Regulation of gastric acid secretion
s5204 The **parietal cells** of the stomach produce hydrochloric acid. Their secretion is regulated by three main stimulants:
s5205 **acetylcholine** (vagus nerve) — via M3 receptors;
s5206 **gastrin** (antral G cells) — via CCK2 receptors and by releasing histamine from enterochromaffin-like cells;
s5207 **histamine** (enterochromaffin-like cells) — via **H2 receptors** (cAMP ↑) — the main final stimulant. All pathways converge on **H+/K+-ATPase (the proton pump)** — the **final common step** of acid secretion.

s5210 2.2. Proton pump inhibitors (PPIs)
s5211 **Preparations:** omeprazole, esomeprazole, pantoprazole, lansoprazole, rabeprazole.
s5212 **Mechanism.** PPIs are **prodrugs**, unstable in acid, so they are produced in **enteric-coated** capsules or tablets. After absorption from the intestine they reach the parietal cell via the blood; in the acidic environment of the secretory canaliculi they are converted into the active **sulfenamide**, which binds **irreversibly and covalently** to the SH groups of H+/K+-ATPase. Acid secretion falls by more than 90%; the effect lasts until new pumps are synthesized (more than 24 hours), although t½ is only 1–2 hours.
s5213 **Uses:** GERD and reflux esophagitis, gastric and duodenal ulcer, **H. pylori eradication** (with antibiotics), prevention and treatment of NSAID gastropathy, Zollinger–Ellison syndrome, ulcer bleeding (intravenously), functional dyspepsia.
s5214 **Adverse effects (with long-term use):** headache, diarrhea or constipation; increased risk of **Clostridioides difficile infection** and pneumonia; reduced absorption of **vitamin B12, magnesium, iron and calcium** (hypomagnesemia, osteoporosis, bone fractures), interstitial nephritis; hypergastrinemia; **rebound acid hypersecretion** on abrupt withdrawal.
s5215 **Omeprazole and esomeprazole inhibit CYP2C19** and may reduce the conversion of **clopidogrel** into its active metabolite. If a patient taking clopidogrel needs gastroprotection, **pantoprazole** is preferred. In every patient taking a PPI long term, the indication should be reviewed regularly and, where possible, the dose reduced or the drug stopped (“deprescribing”).
s5216 2.3. H2-histamine receptor blockers
s5217 **Preparations:** famotidine (the main one), ranitidine (withdrawn from the market in many countries in 2020 because of N-nitrosodimethylamine (NDMA) contamination), cimetidine (outdated).
s5218 By competitively blocking the H2 receptors of parietal cells, they reduce basal secretion, especially at **night**. They are weaker than PPIs, and tolerance develops quickly. Uses: mild GERD, dyspepsia, an add-on to a PPI (nocturnal acid “breakthrough”). **Cimetidine** — a CYP450 inhibitor (many interactions), antiandrogenic effect (gynecomastia) — no longer used.
s5219 2.4. Antacids
s5220 **Antacids** **chemically neutralize** hydrochloric acid in the stomach. The effect begins quickly (minutes) but is short (30–60 minutes on an empty stomach; up to 3 hours if taken 1 hour after a meal).

s5235 2.5. Gastroprotectors
s5236 **Bismuth tripotassium dicitrate (De-Nol)** — forms a protective **film** on the ulcer surface in an acidic environment, increases the synthesis of prostaglandins and bicarbonate, and **acts against H. pylori** (in quadruple regimens). Adverse effect: **darkening of the stool and tongue** (harmless); with long-term use at large doses — encephalopathy (contraindicated in renal failure).
s5237 **Sucralfate** — forms a sticky paste in an acidic environment and coats the ulcer base. Constipation; reduces the absorption of other drugs.
s5238 **Misoprostol** — a prostaglandin E1 analog: reduces acid secretion and increases the production of mucus and bicarbonate. **Prevention of NSAID gastropathy**. Adverse effects: diarrhea; **uterine contractions — absolutely contraindicated in pregnancy** (it causes abortion; it is used for this purpose in obstetrics).
s5239 2.6. Eradication of Helicobacter pylori
s5240 H. pylori is the main cause of gastric and duodenal ulcer, chronic gastritis, gastric cancer and MALT lymphoma. Eradication sharply reduces ulcer recurrence.

s5256 3. Emetic and antiemetic drugs
s5257 3.1. Mechanism of vomiting
s5258 The vomiting center is located in the medulla oblongata and receives impulses from:
s5259 **the chemoreceptor trigger zone** (in the floor of the fourth ventricle, outside the blood-brain barrier; D2, 5-HT3 and NK1 receptors) — circulating toxins, drugs (cytostatics, opioids, glycosides), metabolites;
s5260 **the gastrointestinal tract** — vagal afferents (5-HT3; cytostatics release serotonin from enterochromaffin cells);
s5261 **the vestibular apparatus** (M1, H1 receptors) — motion sickness;
s5262 the cerebral cortex (fear, smells, “anticipatory vomiting”).

s5265 3.2. Emetics
s5266 **Apomorphine** stimulates D2 receptors in the trigger zone; given subcutaneously. **Ipecacuanha** irritates the stomach reflexly. s5308 4. Drugs affecting gastrointestinal motility
s5309 4.1. Prokinetics
s5310 **Metoclopramide** (D2 blocker + 5-HT4 agonist), **domperidone** (peripheral D2 blocker), **itopride** (D2 blocker + anticholinesterase) — speed up gastric emptying and increase the tone of the lower esophageal sphincter. Uses: gastroparesis (diabetic), functional dyspepsia, reflux (as an add-on). **Erythromycin** at a small dose stimulates motilin receptors (in gastric bleeding before endoscopy).

s5313 **Neurotropic**: hyoscine butylbromide (Buscopan), platyphylline (Topic 4).
s5314 **Peppermint oil** capsules — in irritable bowel syndrome.

s5350 4.4. Antidiarrheal drugs
s5351 **The main treatment of acute diarrhea is rehydration.** Oral rehydration solution (the WHO low-osmolarity ORS: sodium chloride, potassium chloride, sodium citrate, glucose) sharply reduces dehydration and death: glucose enhances the absorption of sodium and water in the intestine (sodium-glucose cotransport, SGLT1). In children, **zinc** preparations (10–14 days) are recommended together with ORS.
s5352 **Loperamide** — a peripheral mu-opioid agonist that hardly crosses the BBB; it slows peristalsis and increases anal sphincter tone. Symptomatic in acute nonspecific diarrhea in adults. **Contraindicated in bloody diarrhea, high fever** (dysentery, invasive infection), C. difficile colitis and **in children under 2** (toxic megacolon, ileus). At large doses — cardiotoxicity (QT prolongation).
s5353 **Adsorbents** — diosmectite (Smecta), activated charcoal (Topic 3).
s5354 **Racecadotril** — an enkephalinase inhibitor; antisecretory effect.
s5355 **Probiotics** (Lactobacillus rhamnosus GG, Saccharomyces boulardii) — slightly shorten the duration of acute diarrhea in children; prevention of antibiotic-associated diarrhea.
s5356 In acute diarrhea, **antibiotics** are used only when indicated: dysentery (bloody stool + fever), cholera, severe travelers' diarrhea, immunodeficiency. Most acute diarrheas (viral) do not require antibiotics.
s5357 5. Drugs affecting liver and biliary function
s5358 5.1. Choleretics and cholagogues
s5359 **Choleretics** enhance bile formation in the liver: those containing bile acids (Allochol, Cholenzym), synthetic (hymecromone, osalmid), herbal (corn silk, immortelle, rose hip — Cholosas).
s5360 **Cholekinetics** enhance gallbladder contraction and relaxation of the sphincter of Oddi: magnesium sulfate, sorbitol, xylitol, cholecystokinin.
s5361 **Cholespasmolytics** relax biliary spasm: drotaverine, papaverine, hymecromone, M-cholinergic blockers.
s5362 5.2. Ursodeoxycholic acid (UDCA)
s5363 **UDCA** is a hydrophilic bile acid. Mechanism: it reduces cholesterol saturation of bile (**dissolves cholesterol stones**), displaces toxic hydrophobic bile acids and protects hepatocytes and cholangiocytes, choleresis and immunomodulation.
s5364 **Uses: primary biliary cholangitis** (the main drug), small (< 15 mm) radiolucent cholesterol stones (with preserved gallbladder function), intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy, cholestatic liver diseases, prevention of stone formation after bariatric surgery.
s5365 5.3. Hepatoprotectors
s5366 Hepatoprotectors are a heterogeneous group intended to protect liver cells from harmful influences: **ademetionine** (Heptral), **silymarin** (milk thistle — Karsil), **essential phospholipids** (Essentiale), ornithine aspartate (Hepa-Merz — reduces ammonia in hepatic encephalopathy).
