s0285 4. Classification of dosage forms
s0286 **Dosage form** — a convenient state given to a medicinal product for use. The dosage form determines the rate of absorption, bioavailability, onset and duration of action of the drug.

s0300 This topic covers **solid** and **soft** dosage forms; liquid dosage forms are studied in topic 2.
s0301 5. Soft dosage forms (Formae medicamentorum molles)
s0302 Soft dosage forms include **ointments, pastes, creams, gels, liniments, suppositories and plasters**. They are widely used in dermatology, ophthalmology, proctology, gynecology and surgery; some (nitroglycerin ointment, hormonal patches) are used for a **resorptive** (systemic) effect.
s0303 Any soft dosage form consists of two parts:
s0304 **the active substance (basis)** — has the therapeutic effect;
s0305 **the form-giving substance, the base (constituens)** — forms the dosage form and holds the active substance.
s0306 5.1. Ointments (Unguenta)
s0307 **Ointment** (Unguentum, i n) — a non-dosed dosage form of soft consistency intended for application to the skin and mucous membranes. The amount of active substance is usually **up to 25%** (if more — a paste).

s0327 Ointments are prescribed in **two** ways:
s0328 **Abbreviated form** — if the ointment is officinal or has a single active substance with a petrolatum base. After Rp.: the name of the dosage form in the genitive (Unguenti), the substance name, its concentration and the total amount are written.
s0329 **PRESCRIPTION 1.1. TETRACYCLINE EYE OINTMENT** Rp.: Unguenti Tetracyclini ophthalmici 1% — 10.0
s0330 D.S. Apply inside the lower eyelid 3 times a day.
s0331 **PRESCRIPTION 1.2. OFFICINAL ZINC OINTMENT** Rp.: Unguenti Zinci 10% — 30.0
s0332 D.S. Apply to the affected skin area 2 times a day.
s0333 **Extended form** — for magistral ointments, or if the base is not petrolatum, or if there are two or more active substances. Each substance is written on a separate line, the base is brought up to the total mass with “**ad**”, and then **M.f. unguentum** is written.
s0334 **PRESCRIPTION 1.3. 1% NEOMYCIN OINTMENT ON A LANOLIN BASE, 50.0** Rp.: Neomycini sulfatis 0.5
s0335 Lanolini ad 50.0 M.f. unguentum D.S. Apply to the injured area 2 times a day.
s0336 Calculation: 100.0 of ointment contains 1.0 of neomycin; 50.0 of ointment — x; x = 50 × 1 / 100 = **0.5**.
s0337 Eye ointments are prepared sterile and prescribed in amounts of 5.0–10.0, skin ointments in amounts of 20.0–100.0. The word ophthalmicum is added to the name of an eye ointment.
s0338 5.2. Pastes (Pastae)
s0339 **Paste** (Pasta, ae f) — a thick ointment containing **25% to 65%** powdered substances (zinc oxide, talc, starch, white clay). Thanks to the large amount of powder, pastes have an **adsorbing, drying and anti-inflammatory** effect and stay on the skin surface for a long time. They are used for weeping dermatitis, eczema and diaper rash.
s0340 If the total powdered substances in the prescription are less than 25%, an indifferent powder (starch, talc) is added to obtain the required consistency.
s0341 **PRESCRIPTION 1.4. OFFICINAL ZINC PASTE** Rp.: Pastae Zinci 40.0
s0342 **PRESCRIPTION 1.5. MAGISTRAL PASTE (5% ANESTHESIN AND 25% ZINC OXIDE, 50.0)** Rp.: Anaesthesini 2.5
s0343 Zinci oxydi 12.5 Vaselini ad 50.0 M.f. pasta D.S. Apply to the itching skin area.
s0344 5.3. Creams (Cremores) and gels (Gela)
s0345 **Cream** — a soft dosage form that is more liquid than an ointment and contains water (emulsion base). It is well absorbed into the skin, cosmetically convenient (not shiny) and convenient for use in skin folds and on the face. **Gel** — a viscous dosage form based on gelling agents such as gelatin, agar-agar and carbomer; it is quickly absorbed by the skin and has a cooling effect. Both are officinal only and are therefore written in the **abbreviated** form.
s0346 **PRESCRIPTION 1.6. HYDROCORTISONE CREAM** Rp.: Cremoris Hydrocortisoni 1% — 15.0
s0347 D.S. Apply a thin layer to the affected skin area 2 times a day.
s0348 **PRESCRIPTION 1.7. DICLOFENAC GEL** Rp.: Geli Diclofenaci 1% — 50.0
s0349 D.S. Apply to the painful joint area 3 times a day.
s0350 5.4. Liniments (Linimenta)
s0351 **Liniment** (Linimentum, i n) — a dosage form in the form of a **liquid ointment** that melts at body temperature. Vegetable oils (sunflower, cottonseed, castor, peach oil) are used as the base. It is shaken before use. It is used for joint and muscle pain, burns and wounds. Officinal liniments are written in the abbreviated form, magistral liniments in the extended form.
s0352 **PRESCRIPTION 1.8. OFFICINAL SYNTHOMYCIN LINIMENT** Rp.: Linimenti Synthomycini 10% — 25.0
s0353 D.S. Apply to the wound surface under a dressing.
s0354 **PRESCRIPTION 1.9. MAGISTRAL LINIMENT (5% TURPENTINE, 10% METHYL SALICYLATE, 75.0)** Rp.: Olei Terebinthinae 3.75
s0355 Methylii salicylatis 7.5 Olei Helianthi ad 75.0 M.f. linimentum D.S. Rub into the painful joint.
s0356 **Vishnevsky's liniment** (balsamic liniment) — a classic magistral formulation of xeroform, birch tar and castor oil. It is still used to treat purulent wounds and abscesses.

s0374 Bases: **cocoa butter** (Oleum Cacao) — the most commonly used base, melts at 30–34 °C; gelatin-glycerin mass; polyethylene oxide bases; butyrol.
s0375 Rectal suppositories are used for a **local** (hemorrhoids, proctitis) and **resorptive** effect. A drug absorbed from the lower part of the rectum enters the inferior vena cava directly through the inferior and middle rectal veins, bypassing the liver. The drug is therefore protected from partial breakdown in the liver. This route is convenient in vomiting patients, young children (paracetamol suppositories) and unconscious patients.
s0376 Officinal suppositories are prescribed in the abbreviated form: Rp.: Suppositorium cum (with …) + the substance name **in the ablative** + dose.
s0377 **PRESCRIPTION 1.10. OFFICINAL PARACETAMOL SUPPOSITORIES (FOR A CHILD)** Rp.: Suppositorium cum Paracetamolo 0.25
s0378 D.t.d. N. 10 S. Insert 1 suppository into the rectum when the temperature is above 38.5 °C (not more than 4 times a day).
s0379 Compound officinal suppositories with a trade name: Rp.: Suppositoria “Anusolum” N. 10.
s0380 Magistral suppositories are written in the extended form: the dose of the active substance for one suppository, the amount of base, **M.f. suppositorium rectale (vaginale)**, then **D.t.d. N.** and the signatura.
s0381 **PRESCRIPTION 1.11. RECTAL SUPPOSITORIES WITH ANESTHESIN (MAGISTRAL)** Rp.: Anaesthesini 0.3
s0382 Olei Cacao 3.0 M.f. suppositorium rectale D.t.d. N. 10 in charta cerata S. Insert 1 suppository into the rectum when in pain.
s0383 5.6. Plasters (Emplastra) and transdermal therapeutic systems
s0384 **Plaster** (Emplastrum, i n) — a plastic mass that softens at body temperature and sticks to the skin, or a fabric coated with such a mass. Plasters may consist of the base only (adhesive plaster — for securing a dressing) or contain a drug (pepper and corn plasters).
s0385 **Transdermal therapeutic systems (TTS)** — multilayer patches that release the drug through the skin into the blood at a constant, controlled rate. Examples: nitroglycerin, fentanyl, nicotine, estradiol, rivastigmine and scopolamine patches. They bypass the liver and keep the blood concentration steady.
s0386 **PRESCRIPTION 1.12. ADHESIVE PLASTER** Rp.: Emplastri adhaesivi 3 cm × 500 cm
s0387 D.S. For securing a dressing.
s0388 **PRESCRIPTION 1.13. FENTANYL TRANSDERMAL SYSTEM** Rp.: Systematis therapeutici transdermalis Fentanyli 25 mcg/hour
s0389 D.t.d. N. 5 S. Apply one patch to the skin of the chest or shoulder every 72 hours.
s0390 6. Solid dosage forms (Formae medicamentorum durae)
s0391 Solid dosage forms include **powders, granules, tablets, dragées, capsules, pills, caramels, lozenges (troches), eye films** and medicinal herbal species.

s0405 6.1. Powders (Pulveres)
s0406 **Powder** (Pulvis, eris m) — a free-flowing solid dosage form. Types:
s0407 **by composition:** simple (simplices) and compound (compositi);
s0408 **by dosing:** divided into doses (divisi) and undivided (indivisi);
s0409 **by fineness:** coarse (grossi), fine (subtiles), very fine (subtilissimi).
s0410 Figure 1.3
s0411 Powder (TMA “Fundamentals of prescription writing”, 2020)
s0412 **Undivided powders** are prescribed for dusting (5.0–100.0) or for substances taken orally in large doses where an exact dose is not important. Undivided powders for oral use are measured with a teaspoon.
s0413 **PRESCRIPTION 1.14. SIMPLE UNDIVIDED POWDER FOR DUSTING** Rp.: Pulveris Streptocidi subtilissimi 20.0
s0414 D.S. Dust onto the wound surface.
s0415 **PRESCRIPTION 1.15. COMPOUND UNDIVIDED POWDER FOR ORAL USE** Rp.: Magnesii oxydi 40.0
s0416 Natrii hydrocarbonatis 10.0 M.f. pulvis D.S. Take 1/3 teaspoon 3 times a day 1 hour after meals.
s0417 **Divided powders** — each dose is wrapped in a separate paper. The optimal mass of one powder is **0.1–1.0 g** (usually 0.3–0.5 g). If the dose of a substance is less than 0.1, an **indifferent filler** — sugar (Saccharum), glucose or sodium bicarbonate — is added so that the mass of one powder reaches at least 0.3.
s0418 **PRESCRIPTION 1.16. CODEINE PHOSPHATE POWDERS (WITH A FILLER)** Rp.: Codeini phosphatis 0.015
s0419 Sacchari 0.3 M.f. pulvis D.t.d. N. 12 S. Take 1 powder 3 times a day when coughing.
s0420 Powders of hygroscopic, volatile or odorous substances are wrapped in **waxed** (in charta cerata) or **paraffined** (in charta paraffinata) paper: D.t.d. N. 12 in charta cerata.
s0421 6.2. Granules (Granula)
s0422 **Granules** — small uniform grains 0.2–3 mm in size. Substances with an unpleasant taste, irritants and antibiotics for children are often produced as granules; a suspension is prepared from them. All granules are officinal and are therefore written in the abbreviated form.
s0423 **PRESCRIPTION 1.17. ACETYLCYSTEINE GRANULES** Rp.: Granulorum Acetylcysteini 0.2
s0424 D.t.d. N. 20 S. Dissolve 1 sachet of granules in 1/2 glass of water and take 3 times a day after meals.
s0425 6.3. Tablets (Tabulettae)
s0426 **Tablet** (Tabuletta, ae f) — a dosed solid dosage form made in a factory by compressing drug and auxiliary substances. All tablets are **officinal**. Their mass is 0.05–1.0 g; if the diameter is more than 9 mm, a score line is made to ease splitting. Auxiliary substances (starch, talc, lactose, magnesium stearate) are **not written** in the prescription.
s0427 Figure 1.4
s0428 Tablets and capsules (TMA “Fundamentals of prescription writing”, 2020)
s0429 Types of tablets:
s0430 **by route of administration:** oral; sublingual (sublinguales, nitroglycerin); buccal; chewable; water-soluble (solubiles, effervescent); vaginal tablets; tablets for preparing solutions;
s0431 **by coating:** uncoated and coated (obductae); enteric-coated; film-coated;
s0432 **by drug release:** immediate and extended-release (retard, SR, CR, XL, MR);
s0433 **by dose:** forte (increased dose), mite (reduced dose). Tablets can be prescribed in **two ways**.
s0434 **First method** (without the name of the dosage form, with D.t.d.):
s0435 **PRESCRIPTION 1.18. PARACETAMOL TABLETS (METHOD 1)** Rp.: Paracetamoli 0.5
s0436 D.t.d. N. 20 in tabulettis S. Take 1 tablet when the temperature rises (not more than 4 times a day, at least 4 hours apart).
s0437 **Second method** (begins with the name of the dosage form in the accusative case — Tabulettas):
s0438 **PRESCRIPTION 1.19. PARACETAMOL TABLETS (METHOD 2)** Rp.: Tabulettas Paracetamoli 0.5 N. 20
s0439 D.S. Take 1 tablet when the temperature rises.
s0440 Tablets of **compound composition** are prescribed by trade name; the dose is not indicated, and the name is in quotation marks in the nominative case:
s0441 **PRESCRIPTION 1.20. COMPOUND TABLET WITH A TRADE NAME** Rp.: Tabulettas “Co-trimoxazolum 480” N. 20
s0442 D.S. Take 2 tablets 2 times a day after meals for 7 days.
