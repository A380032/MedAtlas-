s4736 The combination **ACE inhibitor + ARB** (or either of them + aliskiren) — “dual RAAS blockade” — **is not recommended**: it gives no additional benefit and increases the risk of hyperkalemia, hypotension and acute kidney injury.
s4737 Clonidine used to be one of the main drugs for AH in CIS countries. Modern recommendations **do not recommend it for regular treatment** (rebound, sedation, no proven organ-protective effect). Clonidine is also under strict control because it can be used for criminal purposes (combined with alcohol).
s4738 For most patients the target BP is **below 130/80 mm Hg** (individualized in those over 65 and in frail elderly people, usually systolic 130–139 mm Hg). In most patients, treatment is recommended to start with **a fixed low-dose combination of two drugs (in a single tablet)**; in low-risk grade 1 AH and in frail elderly people — with monotherapy.
s4739 In a hypertensive crisis, giving **short-acting nifedipine sublingually**, and lowering BP very quickly and sharply, is dangerous: cerebral, cardiac and renal ischemia (stroke, infarction) may develop. In an uncomplicated crisis, urgent hospitalization is usually not required — a calm environment, an oral drug and follow-up within 24–48 hours are enough.
s4740 **In acute hypotension, the cause must be identified first**: hypovolemia (blood loss, dehydration) — fluids; anaphylaxis — epinephrine; septic shock — fluids + norepinephrine; cardiogenic shock — inotropes; bradycardia — atropine; adrenal insufficiency — hydrocortisone. Vasopressors are ineffective and harmful in hypovolemia without adequate fluid replacement.
s4741 1. Regulation of blood pressure and classification of antihypertensive drugs
s4742 BP = **cardiac output (minute volume)** × **total peripheral vascular resistance**. The main systems that regulate it:
s4743 **the sympathetic nervous system** (vasomotor center, baroreceptors, alpha and beta receptors);
s4744 **the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS)**: renin → angiotensin I from angiotensinogen → ACE → **angiotensin II** (via AT1 receptors — strong vasoconstriction, aldosterone release, sympathetic activation, remodeling of the heart and vessels);
s4745 excretion of sodium and water by the **kidneys** (circulating volume);
s4746 local factors: NO, endothelin, prostacyclin, bradykinin, natriuretic peptides.

s4783 2. Drugs acting on the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system
s4784 2.1. ACE inhibitors
s4785 **Mechanism.** They inhibit angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE, kininase II):
s4786 **angiotensin II formation decreases** → vasodilation, reduced aldosterone release (sodium and water are excreted, potassium is retained), reduced sympathetic activity;
s4787 **bradykinin breakdown decreases** → bradykinin accumulates → release of NO and prostacyclin increases (additional vasodilation) — but **dry cough** and **angioedema** are also related to this. **Organ-protective effects:** reduction of left ventricular hypertrophy and vascular remodeling; **kidney protection** — by dilating the efferent arteriole they reduce intraglomerular pressure and **reduce proteinuria** (diabetic nephropathy).

s4818 2.2. Angiotensin II receptor blockers (ARBs, sartans)
s4819 **Mechanism. Selective blockade of AT1 receptors** — all the harmful effects of angiotensin II are abolished (including angiotensin II formed by pathways other than ACE). They do not affect bradykinin breakdown — so they **do not cause cough**, and angioedema is very rare.
s4820 **Uses:** the same as ACE inhibitors (AH, heart failure, diabetic nephropathy); in patients who cannot tolerate ACE inhibitors. **Losartan** increases uric acid excretion (AH with gout). **Telmisartan** is long-acting.
s4821 **Adverse effects and contraindications** are similar to those of ACE inhibitors: hyperkalemia, worsening of renal function (in renal artery stenosis), **contraindicated in pregnancy**.
s4822 2.3. Mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists and the renin inhibitor
s4823 **Spironolactone** and **eplerenone** block aldosterone receptors (in detail in Topic 13). Uses: **resistant AH** (BP still high on three drugs — the fourth drug), primary hyperaldosteronism, chronic heart failure (reduces mortality), ascites in liver cirrhosis. Adverse effects: **hyperkalemia**; with spironolactone — **gynecomastia**, menstrual disorders (antiandrogenic effect); eplerenone is more selective.
s4824 **Aliskiren** inhibits renin directly; now rarely used.
s4825 3. Calcium channel blockers
s4826 Their mechanism and classification were covered in Topic 10. In AH, mainly **long-acting dihydropyridines** (amlodipine, lercanidipine, nifedipine GITS) are used: they dilate arterioles and reduce peripheral resistance.
s4827 **Advantages:** metabolically neutral, effective in the elderly and in isolated systolic AH, good effect in Black patients, effective in stroke prevention, allowed in pregnancy (nifedipine).
s4828 **Adverse effects: ankle edema** (not reduced by a diuretic, reduced by an ACE inhibitor/ARB), headache, facial flushing, gingival hyperplasia; with verapamil — constipation and bradycardia.
s4829 4. Diuretics
s4830 Their mechanism is covered in detail in Topic 13. In AH, mainly **thiazide and thiazide-like** diuretics are used: at first they reduce the circulating volume, and later they lower peripheral resistance through a decrease in sodium in the vessel wall.
s4831 **Indapamide** and **chlorthalidone** (thiazide-like) — long-acting, with few metabolic adverse effects; proven to reduce cardiovascular complications.
s4832 **Hydrochlorothiazide** — often in combination tablets (12.5–25 mg).
s4833 **Loop diuretics** (furosemide, torasemide) are used in AH only with **renal failure** (GFR < 30 ml/min) and in heart failure with edema. **Metabolic adverse effects of thiazides:** hypokalemia, hyponatremia, **hyperuricemia** (gout attack), hyperglycemia, dyslipidemia, hypercalcemia — mainly at high doses.

s4855 6. Myotropic vasodilators
s4856 **Hydralazine** dilates arterioles directly; strong reflex tachycardia and fluid retention (given with a beta-blocker and a diuretic); with prolonged use — **drug-induced lupus** (in slow acetylators). Given intravenously in severe AH of pregnancy; in Black patients with heart failure — together with isosorbide dinitrate.
s4857 **Minoxidil** opens KATP channels; very potent; used in resistant AH; **hypertrichosis** — that is why its topical solution is used for alopecia.
s4858 **Sodium nitroprusside** — an NO donor; dilates arteries and veins; the effect begins immediately and ends 1–2 minutes after the infusion is stopped. Given **as an intravenous drip** through a light-protected line (it decomposes in light) in **hypertensive crisis** and aortic dissection (with a beta-blocker). With prolonged use — **cyanide toxicity** (antidote — sodium thiosulfate, hydroxocobalamin).
s4859 **Bendazol (Dibazol)** — a weak myotropic antispasmodic; used in CIS countries with papaverine for mild crises; its effectiveness does not meet modern standards.
s4860 **Magnesium sulfate** — **prevention of seizures in preeclampsia and eclampsia** (the main indication); its antihypertensive effect is weak.

s4910 7.3. Hypertensive crisis
s4911 **Hypertensive crisis** is a sharp rise in BP (usually > 180/120 mm Hg). Two types are distinguished:
s4912 **complicated (emergency) crisis** — with acute target-organ damage: hypertensive encephalopathy, stroke, acute coronary syndrome, pulmonary edema, aortic dissection, eclampsia, acute kidney injury. BP is lowered **stepwise** with **intravenous** drugs (in the first hour mean BP by no more than 25%; in aortic dissection — rapidly, to systolic < 120);
s4913 **uncomplicated crisis** — without organ damage; BP is lowered gradually over 24–48 hours with **oral** drugs.
