s3589 The effect of caffeine **depends on the initial state of the CNS**: it is strong in a tired person and weak in a rested one. Consuming more than 400 mg a day (about 4 cups of coffee) leads to anxiety, insomnia and tachycardia; when regular use is stopped abruptly, headache and lethargy (withdrawal) occur. Contraindications: anxiety disorders, insomnia, severe hypertension, tachyarrhythmias, glaucoma, gastric ulcer.
s3590 **TCA poisoning** — one of the most dangerous poisonings in suicide attempts (narrow therapeutic range: a 10–15-day supply can be fatal). Signs — **“3 C's”**: **C**oma, **C**onvulsions (seizures), **C**ardiotoxicity (wide QRS, arrhythmias, hypotension) + anticholinergic syndrome. **Treatment:** secure the airway, activated charcoal, intravenous **sodium bicarbonate** (if QRS > 100 ms — the sodium load and alkalinization reduce cardiotoxicity), benzodiazepines for seizures. Physostigmine and flumazenil are **contraindicated** (risk of seizures, asystole). A depressed patient should not be prescribed a large quantity of TCAs at one time.
s3591 **In children, adolescents and people under 25**, the risk of suicidal thoughts and behavior may increase in the first weeks of antidepressant treatment. The patient and relatives are warned, and frequent follow-up is arranged in the first month of treatment.
s3592 **Distinguishing serotonin syndrome from NMS**: serotonin syndrome — **rapid** (hours), **clonus and hyperreflexia**, diarrhea, mydriasis (a serotonergic drug); NMS — **slow** (days), **“lead-pipe” rigidity**, hyporeflexia (a neuroleptic).
s3593 **Basic rules of antidepressant treatment:** 1. The effect begins in **2–4 weeks**, the full effect in 6–8 weeks; the patient must be warned about this. 2. Start with a small dose and increase to the effective dose over 1–2 weeks. 3. If there is no effect after 4–6 weeks, increase the dose or switch to another group. 4. After remission, treatment is continued for at least **6–12 months** (in recurrent episodes — for years). 5. Withdrawal — **gradually** (over several weeks): abrupt cessation causes dizziness, “electric shock” sensations, nausea, anxiety and sleep disturbance. 6. Assess suicide risk; if there is a risk — refer to a psychiatrist.

s3642 3. Depression and antidepressants
s3643 3.1. The monoamine theory of depression
s3644 **Depression** — a disease with a depressed mood or loss of interest (anhedonia) lasting at least 2 weeks, accompanied by disturbed sleep and appetite, fatigue, feelings of guilt, reduced concentration and thoughts of death.
s3645 According to the **monoamine theory**, depression is associated with a deficiency of **serotonin** and **norepinephrine** (and also dopamine) in the CNS. Antidepressants increase the amount of these mediators in the synapse. However, although mediator concentrations rise within a few hours, the clinical effect appears only **after 2–4 weeks** — this is explained by adaptation of receptor sensitivity (down-regulation), neuroplasticity and increased production of BDNF (brain-derived neurotrophic factor).
