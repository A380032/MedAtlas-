s0684 **Oily solutions, suspensions and ordinary emulsions must not be given intravenously** — risk of fat and particle embolism. **Alcoholic solutions are not given subcutaneously** — tissue necrosis. **Calcium chloride is given only intravenously** (necrosis when given intramuscularly or subcutaneously). **A concentrated potassium chloride solution is not given as an intravenous push** — only diluted, slowly by drip (risk of cardiac arrest).
s0685 Ceftriaxone diluted with lidocaine is given **only intramuscularly**; for intravenous use it is dissolved in water for injection. Lidocaine is not used in patients allergic to lidocaine or with impaired cardiac conduction. Giving ceftriaxone in the same line with calcium-containing solutions (Ringer's solution), or simultaneously with calcium solutions in newborns, is forbidden (precipitate formation in the lungs and kidneys).
s0686 **Rules for using a metered-dose aerosol inhaler:** 1) remove the cap and shake; 2) breathe out fully; 3) seal the lips tightly around the mouthpiece; 4) press the canister once while starting a slow, deep breath; 5) hold the breath for **5–10 seconds**; 6) breathe out slowly. Rinsing the mouth after a glucocorticoid inhaler is mandatory (to prevent oral candidiasis).
s0687 **“Multiply the percentage by ten — you get mg/ml”**: 1% = 10 mg/ml, 2% = 20 mg/ml, 0.1% = 1 mg/ml, 10% = 100 mg/ml, 40% glucose = 400 mg/ml.
s0688 The most common error in calculations is **mixing up units** (mg and g, mcg and mg). Before starting a calculation, convert all quantities to one unit. For “high-alert” drugs such as insulin, heparin and potassium chloride, it is recommended that a second staff member independently check the calculation.

s0759 2.2. Solutions for oral use
s0760 Solutions for oral use are dosed with a spoon or in drops. Solutions taken by the spoon are usually prescribed **3 times a day for 4 days — 12 doses in total** (the solution does not keep long).
s0761 Calculation algorithm:
s0762 Number of doses: times a day × days (e.g., 3 × 4 = 12).
s0763 Amount of substance: single dose × number of doses.
s0764 Volume of solution: spoon volume × number of doses (tablespoon: 15 × 12 = 180 ml; dessert spoon: 10 × 12 = 120 ml; teaspoon: 5 × 12 = 60 ml). Example. Write a prescription for a sodium bromide solution with a single dose of 0.3, to be taken 1 tablespoon 3 times a day.
s0765 Calculation: 0.3 × 12 = **3.6**; 15 ml × 12 = **180 ml**; percentage: 3.6 × 100 / 180 = **2%**.
s0766 Solutions can be prescribed in **three forms**:
s0767 **PRESCRIPTION 2.1. EXTENDED FORM** Rp.: Natrii bromidi 3.6
s0768 Aquae destillatae ad 180 ml M.f. solutio D.S. Take 1 tablespoon 3 times a day.
s0769 **PRESCRIPTION 2.2. ABBREVIATED FORM (MASS – VOLUME)** Rp.: Solutionis Natrii bromidi 3.6 — 180 ml
s0770 D.S. Take 1 tablespoon 3 times a day.
s0771 **PRESCRIPTION 2.3. ABBREVIATED PERCENTAGE FORM** Rp.: Solutionis Natrii bromidi 2% — 180 ml
s0772 2.3. Drops for oral use
s0773 Potent and low-dose substances are given in drops. The single dose is usually **5–20 drops**; the prescription is written for **20 doses**. 1 ml of aqueous solution = 20 drops.
s0774 Example. Write a prescription for an atropine sulfate solution with a single dose of 0.0005, to be taken 10 drops at a time.
s0775 Calculation: 0.0005 × 20 = **0.01**; 10 drops × 20 = 200 drops = **10 ml**; percentage: 0.01 × 100 / 10 = **0.1%**.
s0776 **PRESCRIPTION 2.4. DROPS (PERCENTAGE FORM)** Rp.: Solutionis Atropini sulfatis 0.1% — 10 ml
s0777 D.S. Take 10 drops 2 times a day before meals.
s0778 3. Liquid forms prepared from medicinal plant raw material
s0779 3.1. Infusions and decoctions (Infusa et Decocta)
s0780 **Infusion** — prepared from the delicate parts of a plant (leaf — folium, flower — flos, herb — herba): the raw material is heated with boiling water in a water bath for **15 minutes**, then cooled for **45 minutes** and strained.
s0781 **Decoction** — prepared from coarse parts (bark — cortex, root — radix, rhizome — rhizoma): heated in a water bath for **30 minutes** and strained **while hot**. Exception: bearberry (Uva ursi) leaf is thick, so a decoction is made from it; conversely, an infusion is made from roots containing essential oils (valerian).

s0797 3.2. Tinctures and extracts
s0798 **Tincture** (Tinctura) — a clear, colored extract obtained from plant raw material with 40% or 70% ethyl alcohol. Because alcohol is a preservative, tinctures keep for a long time. All tinctures are **officinal**; they are dosed in drops.
s0799 **PRESCRIPTION 2.7. TINCTURE** Rp.: Tincturae Valerianae 30 ml
s0800 D.S. Take 20–25 drops 3 times a day.
s0801 Several tinctures can be prescribed as a mixture; in that case **M.D.S.** is used:
s0802 **PRESCRIPTION 2.8. MIXTURE OF TINCTURES** Rp.: Tincturae Valerianae
s0803 Tincturae Leonuri ana 15 ml M.D.S. Take 30 drops 3 times a day.
s0804 **Extracts** (Extracta) — concentrated extracts: liquid (fluida, 1:1), thick (spissa, moisture up to 25%) and dry (sicca, moisture up to 5%). Liquid extracts are given in drops; thick and dry extracts are given as part of tablets, capsules and suppositories.
s0805 **PRESCRIPTION 2.9. LIQUID EXTRACT** Rp.: Extracti Crataegi fluidi 25 ml
s0806 D.S. Take 20–30 drops 3 times a day before meals.
s0807 **Neogalenic preparations** — standardized plant preparations maximally purified from ballast substances; some are suitable for injection. Examples: Corglycon, strophanthin, Adonisid.
s0808 4. Mixtures, emulsions, suspensions, mucilages
s0809 4.1. Mixture (Mixtura)
s0810 **Mixture** — a liquid mixture of **3 or more** ingredients consisting of dissolved solids and various liquid dosage forms (solution, infusion, tincture, syrup). Mixtures that form a precipitate are shaken before use (mixturae agitandae). The word “Mixtura” is not written in the prescription; **M.D.S.** is placed at the end.
s0811 **PRESCRIPTION 2.10. MIXTURE (ADONIS INFUSION + SODIUM BROMIDE + CODEINE)** Rp.: Infusi herbae Adonidis vernalis 6.0 — 180 ml
s0812 Natrii bromidi 3.6 Codeini phosphatis 0.12 M.D.S. Take 1 tablespoon 3 times a day.
s0813 **Corrigents** — substances that improve the taste and smell of a drug: sugar syrups (Sirupus simplex, Sirupus Rubi idaei — raspberry syrup), aromatic waters (Aqua Menthae piperitae — peppermint water). **Mucilages** are added to irritant drugs: starch mucilage (Mucilago Amyli), marshmallow root mucilage (Mucilago radicis Althaeae). Syrups and mucilages are usually added in an amount of **1/3 of** the total volume.
s0814 4.2. Emulsions (Emulsa)
s0815 **Emulsion** (Emulsum, i n) — a milky dosage form consisting of two immiscible liquids (water and oil). Stability is provided by **emulsifiers**: gelatose, gum arabic, egg yolk, Tweens. The classic ratio for preparing an oil emulsion: **oil : emulsifier : water = 2 : 1 : 17**; if the amount of oil is not specified, the emulsion is prepared at a ratio of 1:10.
s0816 **PRESCRIPTION 2.11. OIL EMULSION (ABBREVIATED FORM)** Rp.: Emulsi olei Ricini 20.0 — 200 ml
s0817 D.S. Take all at once (as a laxative).
s0818 In modern practice, the most important emulsions are **emulsions for intravenous use**: propofol (for general anesthesia) and fat emulsions for parenteral nutrition. Oily solutions normally cannot be given intravenously, but special emulsions with very fine particles (less than 1 μm) are an exception.
s0819 4.3. Suspensions (Suspensiones)
s0820 **Suspension** (Suspensio, onis f) — solid drug particles that are insoluble in the liquid, held in suspension. It is used orally, topically and intramuscularly. **It must not be given intravenously.** The signatura must include “**Shake before use!**”.
s0821 **PRESCRIPTION 2.12. ANTIBIOTIC SUSPENSION FOR A CHILD** Rp.: Suspensionis Amoxicillini 250 mg/5 ml — 100 ml
s0822 D.S. Shake before use. Take 5 ml 3 times a day for 7 days.
s0823 Antibiotics for children are often produced as **powders or granules for oral suspension**. Boiled and cooled water is poured into the bottle up to the mark and shaken. The finished suspension is kept in the refrigerator for 7–14 days. Parents should be taught to use a dosing syringe or spoon: the volume of kitchen spoons is not standard.

s0849 5.1. Eye drops (Guttae ophthalmicae)
s0850 Requirements for eye drops: they must be **sterile**, **free of mechanical particles**, as far as possible **isotonic** (osmotic pressure equal to 0.9% NaCl) and close to the pH of tear fluid, and chemically stable. Eye drops are prescribed in 5–10 ml, enough for **20 applications**; 1–2 drops are instilled into each eye at a time.

s0856 5.2. Enemas and mucilages
